mine_take_interpolation_3p_2026-08-24.txt
===========================================

Follow-up to mine_take_probe_3p_2026-08-24.txt and
mine_take_sensitivity_3p_2026-08-24.txt. Those established, at 3 players,
that a scored Mine take (Iron/Coal/Oil) is outranked at EVERY one of 12,299
decision points by the champion vector (best rank ever 3rd, mean rank
17.87), and that no single WeightKey's multiplier -- swept 0 to 1e6, both
directions, individually and jointly for the five clamp-saturated keys --
ever brings it to rank 1. That leaves open whether a linear evaluator can
ever want a Mine at all. It can: the SAME code, run against
analysis/frozen/human_weights.json (read-only, never modified here), takes
Iron/Coal/Oil at a nonzero rate in independent self-play census data. This
file measures how far the champion vector has to move toward the human
vector -- along the straight line between them, and one coordinate at a
time -- before a scored Mine take starts winning.

Binary: rust/src/bin/takeprobe.rs, extended with two new static-rescoring
modes (--interpolate and its associated single-key adoption sweep). Only
this one file was edited; no bot behaviour changed anywhere.

Command (from rust/, release binary):

  ./target/release/takeprobe --games 200 --players 3 \
      --weights /private/tmp/opencensus/champ3p.json --threads 5 \
      --interpolate /Users/pt/tta-ai/analysis/frozen/human_weights.json

           in the file; missing keys resolve through load_weights the same
           way for both files)

Sample size: 200 self-play games, 3 players, all seats the champion vector
(mirrors behavcensus.rs's all-Weighted self-play). elapsed 10.0s.
  seen=24412  legal=12299 (0.504)  scored=12299 (1.000)

These reproduce mine_take_probe_3p_2026-08-24.txt's own numbers exactly (a
consistency check, not a new claim): this run measures the identical set of
12,299 scored Mine-take decision points, just rescored under two new
static perturbations instead of the earlier multiplier sweep.

METHOD (see takeprobe.rs's own doc comments on InterpCtx / rank_gap_at_t /
ProbeConfig for the implementation)
-----------------------------------
dot(w, f) is linear in w for a fixed, already-computed feature vector f (the
same freeze mine_take_sensitivity_3p_2026-08-24.txt's rank_under_perturbation
already relies on). So for w(t) = (1-t)*champion + t*human:

    dot(w(t), f) = (1-t)*dot(champion, f) + t*dot(human, f)

Two dot products per candidate, computed once per decision point, then every
t is a cheap blend -- no replayed self-play per t. Swept t = 0.00 to 1.00 in
steps of 0.01 (101 points total: this IS the "0.05 coarse plus 0.01 fine"
grid the task asked for, computed once, sliced two ways at report time,
since the per-t cost is negligible and there is no need to guess the
transition interval before running).

The single-key adoption sweep is a SEPARATE measurement: for every WeightKey
that differs between champion and human, set w[key] = human[key] with every
other coordinate left at the champion's value, and re-rank the same 12,299
decision points via the identical rank_under_perturbation identity already
used by --sensitivity's multiplier sweep. This is a different intervention
from a multiplier: a multiplier can only rescale a coordinate (it can never
flip its sign or move it off a clamp fence), whereas adopting the human's
actual value can do both.

FINDING
-------
Neither pattern named in the task's "most valuable findings" section is
what happened. This is not a gradual ramp (rank1 fraction sits at exactly
0.0000 across the ENTIRE interior of the sweep, t=0.00 through t=0.99 --
mean_gap does shrink smoothly and monotonically as t rises, from 190.44 to
3.53 at t=0.99, but that shrinking gap never once crosses zero before the
endpoint). And it is not an interior abrupt transition at some intermediate
t either -- the single jump the sweep finds sits at the very last step,
t=0.99 -> t=1.00, i.e. the unlock requires the FULL joint human vector, not
any partial blend of it, however close. Even at t=1.00 the Mine take only
reaches rank 1 on 6.45% of these 12,299 champion-trajectory decision points
(rank 1-or-2 on 17.67%) -- a real but modest unlock, not a landslide, and
noticeably lower than the human census's own reported Mine-take rate,
consistent with caveat 1 below (this measures the human WEIGHTS rescoring
CHAMPION-reached states, not human self-play).

The single-key adoption sweep rules out that this is a large number of
small individual differences that only add up in combination: 155 of 156
differing keys, adopted alone at the human's exact value, achieve EXACTLY
zero rank-1 fraction -- indistinguishable from --sensitivity's multiplier
sweep's own finding that 14 of 18 named keys never reach rank 1 at any
multiplier. The one exception is HandPotential, at 4.78% rank-1 alone --
matching mine_take_sensitivity_3p_2026-08-24.txt's own finding that
DELETING HandPotential outright (multiplier 0) also reached 4.78%, to four
significant figures. That match is itself informative: it says the human
vector's HandPotential coordinate behaves, for this measurement, almost
exactly like deleting the champion's -- i.e. adopting the human's actual
value is not doing anything a multiplier sweep could not already reach,
so HandPotential is NOT the coordinate the multiplier sweep structurally
could not have found. No other single-key swap does better than 0.0000,
including keys like ResourceStock/FoodStock/CultureRate that sit at the
champion's clamp fence and that a multiplier alone cannot move off of --
adopting the human's actual (non-clamped) value for any one of them,
individually, still never flips a single decision point to rank 1.

Put together: the capability the human vector has and the champion vector
lacks is not concentrated in one coordinate, or even in a small jointly-
acting handful reachable one substitution at a time. It requires
(something very close to) the full 156-coordinate joint difference at
once. This is consistent with, and sharpens, the earlier sensitivity
sweep's finding that deleting the entire five-key clamp block jointly still
yields exactly 0.0000: the effect really is distributed across the whole
vector, not hiding in an interaction the earlier per-key/per-block sweeps
missed. No single cause is identified; the multi-coordinate/joint-effect
explanation is what the data supports, and no coefficient is proposed.

CAVEATS
-------
1. This is a STATIC rescoring at a FIXED set of 12,299 decision points, all
   reached by champion-vs-champion self-play. It is not a policy evaluation
   and not a predicted win rate. A vector that ranks these particular Mine
   takes first would also have played different earlier moves and reached
   different states entirely -- states this method cannot see or score.
   The gap between this run's 6.45% rank-1-at-t=1.00 and the human census's
   own reported per-player-game Mine-take rate (0.60, tier II 0.272 / tier
   III 0.328) is exactly this effect: the census is human weights choosing
   their OWN trajectory; this table is human weights judging the champion's
   trajectory.
2. Per candidate_features'/linear_features' own contract, the feature
   vector f used for every dot product in this file (both t-blends and the
   single-key swaps) was computed ONCE per decision point with the champion
   vector as linear_features' `freeze` argument (the same freeze the actual
   champion-driven game already used to reach that state). freeze feeds
   horizon::rate_multiplier, which scales every RATE_KEYS-tagged feature.
   Neither the t-interpolation nor the single-key swap recomputes that
   scaling with the human vector as freeze -- only the weight side of the
   dot product changes, never the feature side. A "human-freeze" feature
   recomputation is a different (unmeasured) question.
3. The human vector is a diagnostic mirror, not a target. It exists to
   prove the champion's inability to want a Mine is a property of THIS
   vector, not of linear evaluators generally -- nothing here says the
   human vector, or any blend toward it, is a good direction to move the
   champion.
4. Single-key adoption swaps exactly one coordinate at a time; it was not
   re-run for every pair or larger subset, so a joint effect between two
   specific non-HandPotential keys smaller than the full 156-key set cannot
   be ruled out by this file alone -- only that no INDIVIDUAL key does it.
5. As in the sensitivity sweep, no weight's SIGN was flipped independently
   of what the human's own value happens to be; the single-key sweep
   inherits whatever sign the human vector carries for that key, it was not
   swept separately.

DO NOT FIT WEIGHTS TO THE HUMAN NUMBERS.

CORRECTION ADDED 2026-08-24 BY THE COORDINATOR: THE FINDING ABOVE DOES NOT
FOLLOW. THE ENDPOINT JUMP IS A SCALE ARTIFACT.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The two vectors are not on the same scale:

  champion  n=160  mean|w|=5.6313  max|w|=60.000  L2=183.357
  human     n=140  mean|w|=0.2014  max|w|= 2.781  L2=  6.157

A factor of ~30 in norm. In the blend (1-t)*champion + t*human the champion
term therefore dominates until the two contributions are comparable, which
happens at 1-t ~= 1/30, i.e. t ~= 0.97. The observed transition sits at
t=0.99->1.00. That is where the arithmetic puts it regardless of what the
two vectors mean, so the sweep locates the scale ratio and not a property
of the vectors' DIRECTIONS.

The file's own numbers corroborate this. mean_gap at t=1.00 is 1.8893 while
at t=0.00 it is 190.4358 -- the human vector's whole score spread is two
orders of magnitude below the champion's. The interior of the sweep was
never a mixture of two policies; it was the champion's policy with a small
perturbation added, right up to the last step.

THE SAME ARTIFACT INVALIDATES TABLE 2, AND THE FILE CONTAINS THE PROOF.
Adopting human[key] into the champion replaces a coordinate of typical
magnitude 5.63 with one of typical magnitude 0.20. That is not "adopting the
human's value", it is approximately DELETING the coordinate. Hence the
result the FINDING section flags as informative -- HandPotential adoption
0.0478 matching HandPotential deletion at m=0 to four significant figures --
is not a coincidence to be interpreted. It is the signature of the artifact.
Table 2 is a second, noisier copy of the multiplier sweep's m=0 column, and
its 155-of-156 zeroes carry no information the sensitivity sweep did not.

WHAT SURVIVES
  - The t=0.00 column, which reproduces the earlier probe exactly.
  - The t=1.00 row (rank1 0.0645, rank1or2 0.1767, mean_rank 6.63). A
    uniform rescaling of a weight vector cannot change an argmax, so the
    human vector's own ranking behaviour is scale-invariant and this row is
    unaffected. It stands as an independent confirmation that a linear
    evaluator CAN rank a Mine take first -- 6.45% of the time even when
    judging states it did not choose.
  - The method, the binary, and caveats 1 and 2, which are correct and
    important.

WHAT DOES NOT SURVIVE
  The conclusion that the capability "requires the full 156-coordinate joint
  difference at once" and is "distributed across the whole vector". This
  measurement cannot support that and no part of it should be cited for it.
  The question of whether a small set of coordinates is responsible remains
  OPEN. It was not answered here.

HOW TO REDO IT
  Interpolate between DIRECTIONS, not raw vectors: normalise each to unit L2
  first, then sweep. For the adoption sweep, adopt the human's value scaled
  by ||champion||/||human|| so that "adopt" means "take the human's relative
  emphasis on this coordinate" rather than "shrink this coordinate ~30x".
  Any future comparison of two weight vectors in this repo must state both
  norms before interpreting a blend.
