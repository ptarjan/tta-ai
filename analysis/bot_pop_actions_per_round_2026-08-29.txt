Bot population-increase ACTIONS per round, and the first like-for-like
comparison against the human journals
=======================================================================
2026-08-29. 2p, `behavcensus --games 200 --players 2 --threads 2`, frozen copy
of `experiments/rust_champion_2p.json` at **gen 125336** (copied to
/tmp/popact_champ_2p.json before the run; the live file is rewritten by the
climb every few minutes).

WHY THIS MEASUREMENT EXISTS
===========================
`population_curve_bot_vs_human_2026-08-28.txt` compared a human population
LEVEL against a bot population LEVEL and its human column turned out to be
~1.9x too steep; it is withdrawn, and the +5.67 r17 gap with it. The journals
were then re-derived as an ACTION count
(`human_pop_from_journals_2026-08-29.txt`: 8.089 population-increase actions
per player by r17, 2.98 by r6), but until now no bot binary emitted the
matching quantity:

  - `botcensus` classifies `Move::Pop` but counts per GAME with no round
    keying, and returns `None` for `Move::PopFree` -- it would undercount.
  - `openerprobe` counts `Move::Pop|PopFree` but truncates at round 3, and
    counts decision POINTS, not actions.
  - `behavcensus` was closest but recorded CA SPENT on pops
    (`CivilSpendCounts::record(kind, ca_spent)`), so a free pop contributed 0,
    and only over a fixed `(3..=9)` round window.

So `behavcensus` gained a counter that is the same measure as the journals:
**one increment per `Move::Pop`/`Move::PopFree`** (an action, never a CA
debit -- a free pop counts as 1), keyed by `round_before` (`state.round`
pre-move), over the WHOLE game.

THE DENOMINATOR
===============
Two populations, printed separately and never reconciled:

(1) PER-ROUND: n = player-rounds that reached that round, read off
    `production_by_round[round].2`, the turn-start sample this file already
    takes once per player-round. n FALLS as rounds go up because games end at
    different rounds. A cumulative over this falling-n population would be
    survivorship-biased and is NOT printed.
(2) COMPLETED-GAMES CUMULATIVE: the journals' figure is per PLAYER over games
    that COMPLETE, so BOTH sides of the bot cumulative come from the completed
    population -- numerator `pop_actions_by_round_completed` (folded only when
    `!cap_hit`), denominator `completed_player_games`. Summing the all-games
    numerator over a completed-only denominator would divide one population's
    actions by another population's count and inflate the cumulative by every
    capped game; that is the class of error that produced the withdrawn +5.67
    and it is structurally excluded here, not merely avoided in this run.

In THIS run the two are numerically equal at 400 (200 games x 2 seats, every
game completed, no MOVE_CAP hit), so the completed-only fix is a no-op on these
numbers. It is not a no-op in general.

THE COMPARISON
==============
Cumulative per player, same definition on both sides:

  by r6:  bot 3.110  vs human 2.98   -> bot +0.13 (parity, bot marginally ahead)
  by r17: bot 5.945  vs human 8.089  -> human +2.14

The r17 lead of ~+2.1 ACTIONS is consistent with the corrected +1..+3 mid-game
band and has nothing in common with the withdrawn +5.67 LEVEL gap.

Per player-round, against the `increases` row of
`human_midgame_actions_2026-08-28.txt` (632 2p games, n=1264 player-rounds per
round, flat):

WHAT IT SAYS
============
1. **The bot matches humans on population through round 9 and then flatlines.**
   Parity at r8-r9; the gap opens at r10-r11 and holds at ~2.5x from r12 on.
   The r12 peel is real and now rests on a trusted like-for-like instrument
   rather than the withdrawn curve.

2. **It is a SHAPE inversion, not a level shortfall.** Both sides front-load,
   but the bot front-loads HARDER -- 3.11 of its 5.95 lifetime actions land by
   r6 (52%), against the human's 2.98 of 8.09 (37%) -- and then never
   accelerates, sitting at ~0.2/round while the human climbs to 0.672. This is
   the same signature as the food curve running backwards: the bot's rate peaks
   at r3 and decays, the human's rises all game.

3. **On POPULATION COUNT specifically, the opener is clean.** That clears one
   claim only. The bot buys as many workers as a human by r6 and still cannot
   build a Farm 88.3% of the time for want of a free worker
   (`analysis/` no-free-workers finding), and its workers are placed within one
   round against a human idle rate of 43-73%. So the opener defect is worker
   SLACK, not worker COUNT -- a different problem from the one this measurement
   was built to test, and untouched by it.

4. **The obvious next decomposition is "couldn't" vs "wouldn't".** Over r10-r17
   the shortfall is ~2.1 actions. Two known instruments bound it from opposite
   sides: food is the binding reason at >=39.7% of pop-ILLEGAL points and rising
   (`food_gates_population_2026-08-28.txt`), and when Pop IS legal the bot takes
   it only 23.1% of the time, at its LOWEST in exactly this window (r6-10 ~16.8%,
   `pop_taken_when_legal_2026-08-28.txt`). Both effects point the same way in the
   same rounds; neither has been sized against the other per round.

REPRODUCTION
============
  cp experiments/rust_champion_2p.json /tmp/popact_champ_2p.json   # record its gen
  cd rust && cargo build --profile difftest --bin behavcensus
  ./target/difftest/behavcensus --games 200 --players 2 --threads 2 \
      --weights /tmp/popact_champ_2p.json
Section "### Pop-increase actions per round (bot)", printed after
"### Production curve". Deterministic: two runs at the same gen reproduce the
table byte for byte (verified).
