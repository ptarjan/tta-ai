Result
------

interaction), so per the job's rule I recorded that and bisected from the B
side. The B-side recursion reached single keys without isolating a carrier:
every tested revert — from n=81 down to n=1 — is either inert (r1 stays 0.689)

single key, and no contiguous half at any level, raises r1 toward 1.161.

Conclusion
----------
The round-1 take-vs-endturn devaluation (A 1.161 vs B 0.689) is a LARGE-SCALE
interaction across the 162 moved weights. It is not localized to any single key
nor to any contiguous block found by bisection. Both halves of S are

preclimb->postclimb weight drift as a whole reshapes the round-1 take
valuation, and no small subset of keys explains it.

Practical reading: to restore round-1 spending you would need to revert a large
fraction of the weight vector (both halves together = full B->A revert = r1
1.161 by definition). There is no small "fix" among the 162 moved keys. The
round-1 effect is a property of the postclimb weight distribution as a whole,
not a defect in any one feature weight.
