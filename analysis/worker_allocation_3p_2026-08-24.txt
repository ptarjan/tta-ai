WHAT THIS ANSWERS
------------------
analysis/production_curve_3p_2026-08-24.txt found the bot's FOOD
production DECLINING from round 2 (2.00) to ~1.42 by round 15 while the
human corpus climbs 2.00 -> 4.25 over the same rounds, and closed with
three candidate causes that a production-RATE curve alone cannot tell
apart: (a) workers leave farms, (b) farm levels never rise, (c) the
tableau grows but farms do not. This census adds, at the identical
sample instant, workers-by-`CardType` (farm/mine/urban/military/free),
total staffed workers, and each player's own best-owned farm/mine tech
level (mean of the per-player max production-per-worker), to separate
the three.

THE ANSWER: (a) AND (b) TOGETHER, NOT (c) ALONE
-------------------------------------------------
Both start identical at round 1-2 (2.00/2.00/1.00/1.00, bestFarm/bestMine
both 1.00) -- the fixed starting tableau (Agriculture + Bronze + one
urban + one military unit), the same cross-check the production curve
made.

FARM WORKERS (farmW) THEMSELVES DECLINE, IN ABSOLUTE TERMS, NOT JUST AS A
SHARE. The bot's farmW falls every round from round 2's 2.00 to 1.39 by
round 15 and 1.35 by round 19 -- a ~33% absolute drop in workers sitting
on farm cards, not a dilution by a larger denominator. The human farmW
never drops: it holds at 2.00-2.10 for the entire 19-round window,
touching 2.35 only at the very end (n=26, thin). This is candidate (a),
and it is not a marginal effect -- it is close to the whole story
numerically. The bot's bestFarm sits at 1.00 flat through round 9 and
creeps to only 1.03-1.14 by rounds 13-19, so `farmW x bestFarm` (a rough
reconstruction of the worker-capped food credit, ignoring the rare
second denomination) tracks the measured food curve in
production_curve_3p_2026-08-24.txt almost exactly: ~1.98 at round 3,
~1.46-1.42 at rounds 13-14, ~1.35-1.39 by round 15-19. Because bestFarm
is nearly constant at 1 for the bot, essentially the ENTIRE decline in
the bot's food production curve is arithmetically explained by farmW
declining. That makes (a) the dominant driver of the DECLINE itself.

FARM TECH LEVEL NEVER RISES. Candidate (b) is separately and just as
clearly true: the bot's bestFarm is 1.00 for 9 straight rounds and only
reaches 1.14 by round 19, while the human's bestFarm climbs steadily to
2.69 over the same window. This is why the bot's food production, even
at its round-1/2 peak of 2.00, never catches the human's climbing curve
at all -- the human's farm WORKERS stay exactly as flat as the bot's
(2.00-2.10 the entire game) but the human's farm LEVEL keeps rising, so
each of those same ~2 workers produces more and more food. (b) is the
dominant driver of the GAP between the two curves, distinct from (a)'s
role in the bot's own DECLINE.

(c), "tableau grows but farms do not," is the weakest of the three read
literally. The bot's staffed (total placed workers) does grow, from 6.00
to ~10.0-10.1 by round 15-20 -- population is expanding. But farms do not
merely fail to grow alongside it: they actively SHRINK while urban
(1.00 -> 3.5-3.9) and, for a while, military (1.00 -> peak 2.46 around
round 8, then declining) both grow. So (c) is true only in its weaker
form ("farms are not keeping pace with the tableau"); the stronger and
better-supported reading is (a) -- farms are actively being drained of
workers as the rest of the tableau grows, not merely left behind by it.

RANKING: (a) and (b) are both fully supported and are the two real
causes; (a) explains the SHAPE (why the curve falls after round 2), (b)
explains the LEVEL (why it never gets anywhere near the human's rate even
where it doesn't fall). (c) is a restatement of (a) once staffed is read
alongside farmW rather than a separate mechanism -- it is not disproven,
it is subsumed.

CORROBORATION FROM THE MINE SIDE
---------------------------------
Mine gives a clean contrast, because the bot's mine BEHAVIOUR differs
from its farm behaviour along one axis but matches it along the other.
mineW RISES for the bot, from 2.00 to a plateau around 2.9-3.05 --
unlike farmW, workers are not leaving mines. But bestMine is 1.00 for
literally every one of the 21 rounds sampled (600 player-rounds each) --
the bot never once upgrades mine tech beyond the starting level, an even
more extreme version of the farm-tech stagnation in (b). The human
bestMine climbs from 1.00 to 2.88, mirroring bestFarm's climb almost
exactly. So the resource curve's gap in production_curve_3p_2026-08-24.txt
(2.49 bot vs 2.83 human at round 3, widening to 2.91 vs 6.02 by round 15)
is ALL (b) -- worker count on mines is not falling and even rises a
little for the bot -- while the food curve's gap is BOTH (a) and (b)
stacked: workers leaving AND the level never rising. This is why food is
the worse of the two findings in the production-curve file: it has two
compounding defects where resources has one.

At round 3, the first round a mine tech can exist, bestMine is already
1.00 for BOTH bot and human -- the round-3 resource gap (2.49 vs 2.83)
is not a tech-level gap at that specific point, it is a worker-count gap
(mineW 2.49 bot vs 2.83 human), i.e. more humans have already built and
staffed a second mine slot by round 3 than the bot has.

CAVEATS ON THE MEASUREMENT ITSELF
-----------------------------------
1. bestFarm/bestMine are the mean of each player's OWN maximum
   production-per-worker among owned Farm/Mine cards, counted whether or
   not a worker is currently placed on that card (a level "held" is a
   tech OWNED, matching the task's request to separate "workers moved
   off" from "level never rose" -- if levels were only counted when
   staffed, a level rise coinciding with a worker leaving it would look
   identical to the level never having risen at all).
2. staffed = farmW + mineW + urbanW + milW, i.e. workers on Farm, Mine,
   Lab/Temple/Arena/Library, and Infantry/Cavalry/Artillery/AirForce
   cards; Government/SpecialTech/Wonder/Leader/Action/Tactic/
   Aggression/War/Pact/Bonus/Territory/Event never hold a worker in this
   game and are excluded by construction (exhaustive match, not a
   filter).
3. Same survivorship caveat as production_curve_3p_2026-08-24.txt: the
   human n thins from 399 to 26 by round 19 as games finish; the bot n
   holds near 600 through round 18. Round 19's human line (n=26) is
   thin; rounds 20-21 are bot-only and are not tabulated on the human
   side above for the same reason the production curve stopped there.
4. This is a census of a snapshot (the max level HELD, and the workers
   PLACED, at one instant per round), not a trace of any single player's
   trajectory -- it cannot distinguish "this player never upgraded" from
   "this player upgraded late," only report the round-by-round mean.

A GENUINE BUG CAUGHT BY THIS MEASUREMENT'S OWN CROSS-CHECK
-------------------------------------------------------------
behavcensus.rs merges per-thread partial `Report`s through an explicit
`Report::merge` (not a derive) when run with --threads > 1. The first
version of this change added the new `alloc_by_round` field to `Report`
but not to `merge`, so with --threads 5 the section printed its header
and then nothing -- every per-thread partial was silently dropped at the
merge step while the (correctly merged) production curve printed fine
next to it. Caught by comparing sections in the same run rather than
trusting a clean compile; fixed by adding `merge_alloc_map` alongside
the existing `merge_triple_map` and calling it from `Report::merge`.

DO NOT FIT WEIGHTS TO THE HUMAN NUMBERS.
The human census is a diagnostic mirror. Fitting the leaf evaluation on
the human corpus is on the standing do-not-do list and has lost three
times. This file names two defects (workers draining off farms, and
farm/mine tech level stagnating) with the numbers that separate them; it
does not propose a coefficient or a weight change for either.
