Round-1 Take-vs-EndTurn margin, split by decision index, seat, and their pair
==============================================================================

Background: openerprobe's Item 7 reports the round-1 Take-vs-EndTurn score
margin (best_take - end_turn) pooled over ALL round-1 civil decisions. At 2p
the section-1.9 seating grant gives seat 0 ONE civil action and seat 1 TWO,
so a player can face a FIRST take decision (hand empty) and then a SECOND
one (hand has 1 card). The postclimb margin distribution is strongly
bimodal (pooled TAILTIGHT 0.017); the preclimb one is smooth (0.258).

Hypothesis 1 (decision index): the two modes are the two decision
positions. Several weights (science_gap clearest: -1.199 preclimb ->
-5.815 postclimb) read structurally 0 at the first decision (empty hand)
and non-zero at the second (one card in hand).

Hypothesis 2 (seat, added mid-task by the coordinator): `rank_moves` scores
each candidate in its POST-APPLY trial state (eval.rs:548-554), and
`civil_action_gap` reads 1 on Take trials but 0 on EndTurn at one seat, and
0 everywhere at the other -- a seat-dependent constant offset on the Take
side, independent of decision index. Seat and decision index correlate
(seat 0's 1-CA grant structurally limits it), so both splits and their
product (seat, decision index) are needed to separate the two candidates.

Item 7's collection site (`record_decision`/`R1MarginStats` in
rust/src/bin/openerprobe.rs) had been added in an EARLIER session but left
in `git stash@{0}` ("WIP on master: 85eb086 ...") rather than committed --
its base blob matched HEAD exactly, so it applied cleanly with no conflicts.
One of its own tests
(`r1_margin_rows_account_for_every_round1_own_turn_exactly_once`) was
WRONG and failing (asserted `n + n_no_take == turns_round1`, i.e. one
margin row per TURN; the real invariant is one row per round-1 own-turn
DECISION POINT, and a turn can hold more than one when a seat's CA grant is
more than 1) -- fixed to check against an independently replayed decision
count instead of the turn count. This is very likely *why* that session's
work never got committed. All 26 unit tests pass after the fix, plus the 6
new ones added for this task (partition-of-samples checks for the index,
seat, and (seat,index) splits).

VERDICT
=======

Hypothesis 1 (decision index) is FALSIFIED for the postclimb champion, and
not by a close margin: splitting by decision index changes NOTHING, because
there is nothing to split. Every one of the 720 sampled postclimb round-1
Take-vs-EndTurn decisions is decision index 1 (n=720 both before and after
the split; index 2 has n=0). The "second decision" does happen mechanically
in some fraction of turns (n_no_take=428 decision points exist beyond the
sampled 720), but under postclimb weights it NEVER lands as a live
Take-vs-EndTurn tie -- it always falls into "Take illegal" once reached.
Compare with preclimb, where index 2 is real and sampled 160 times (seat 1
reaches a live second Take-vs-EndTurn choice in 44% of its games). So the
postclimb weights have themselves eliminated the second-decision population
this hypothesis needed; pooling can't be un-pooling something that isn't
there. TAILTIGHT by index is 0.017 = the pooled value, i.e. still fully
bimodal.

Hypothesis 2 (seat) is PARTIALLY CONFIRMED but does NOT fully dissolve the
bimodality either. Seat cleanly separates two very different distributions
(seat 0 sits almost entirely positive, mostly 3-5 with a long tail out to
41; seat 1 sits almost entirely negative, -12 to -10, with a smaller upper
tail to 25-27), which is the shape `civil_action_gap`'s seat-dependent
offset predicts. But NEITHER subgroup is unimodal by the TAILTIGHT
threshold that separates preclimb (0.258) from postclimb (0.017): seat 1 is
0.025 (better than pooled but still 10x below preclimb), and seat 0 is
0.005 -- WORSE than the pooled value. Seat 0 alone (n=360, single decision
index, single seat) still shows a sharp internal jump from its p25 (4.161)
to its p75 (40.713): a two-cluster structure inside seat 0 that neither
tested hypothesis explains. Something else is splitting seat 0's own games
into a low cluster (~3-5) and a high cluster (~40-41); this is a real,
reportable residual and a candidate for further bisection, but is outside
this task's scope.

Bottom line: decision index is not the mechanism (it's a non-factor for
postclimb -- the subgroup doesn't exist in the data), seat is a real but
incomplete factor (separates the pool into two differently-shaped
distributions, neither of which clears the unimodal bar on its own), and at
least one more undetermined factor drives an internal split within seat 0.
