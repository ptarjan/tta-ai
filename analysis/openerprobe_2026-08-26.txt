openerprobe -- 2026-08-26
=========================

QUESTION
--------
A behaviour census (bin/behavcensus.rs) measured the bot's first EXECUTED
build by round 3: a Mine 95.6% of the time at 3p, matching humans. A
now-deleted scratch binary buildprobe.rs had earlier measured something that
sounds contradictory: the bot ATTEMPTS an illegal Farm build in round 1
98.1% of the time, 88.3% of those for want of a free worker. buildprobe.rs
no longer exists in the tree, so nobody could tell whether the current
evaluator still WANTS a Farm opener and merely gets bounced to something
legal, or whether that preference is gone and the opener is now genuinely
legal-by-construction.

METHOD
------
New binary: rust/src/bin/openerprobe.rs (committed alongside this file).
Self-play mirror match (every seat plays the same weights), one call per
game to WeightedBot::rank_moves (rust/src/bots/weighted/eval.rs) at every
one of a player's own action-phase decision points ("own decision point" =
Move::EndTurn is among the legal moves right now -- this is exactly the
Phase::Actions branch of legal::legal_moves, never a pending sub-decision
like Bid/Defend/Choose opened by someone's move). Games are truncated the
instant state.round exceeds 3 -- nothing after round 3 is read, so a
"game" here is really just its first three rounds, which is why 6000 games
run in under 70 seconds even single-binary, un-optimized-for-throughput.

Weights: the frozen postclimb_2026-08-26 champions, NOT the live
experiments/rust_champion_*.json the climb is still rewriting:
  analysis/frozen/postclimb_2026-08-26/champ2p.json
  analysis/frozen/postclimb_2026-08-26/champ3p.json
  analysis/frozen/postclimb_2026-08-26/champ4p.json

Exact commands run:

  cargo build --profile difftest --bin openerprobe   # from rust/

  ./target/difftest/openerprobe --games 6000 --players 2 \
      --weights /Users/pt/tta-ai/analysis/frozen/postclimb_2026-08-26/champ2p.json \
      --threads 2 --seed 1

  ./target/difftest/openerprobe --games 6000 --players 3 \
      --weights /Users/pt/tta-ai/analysis/frozen/postclimb_2026-08-26/champ3p.json \
      --threads 2 --seed 1

  ./target/difftest/openerprobe --games 2000 --players 4 \
      --weights /Users/pt/tta-ai/analysis/frozen/postclimb_2026-08-26/champ4p.json \
      --threads 2 --seed 1

(4p scaled to 2000 games, ~4x fewer, matching its per-game cost; still
produced more decision points than either 2p or 3p run because a 4p board
generates more own-turn decisions per round.)

Three measurements, matching the three questions in the task brief:
  1. The bot's actual top-ranked (chosen) move, by kind, at every own
     decision point in rounds 1-3.
  2. The counterfactual "would an illegal move have out-ranked the chosen
     legal one" is NOT cheaply computable from rank_moves/evaluate: scoring
     an illegal move means running it through apply::apply, which assumes
     legality and is not guarded against a move legal::legal_moves would
     reject. Per the task brief, this was not faked. Instead: for each of
     the five build-shaped kinds (Build-Farm, Build-Mine, Build-Urban,
     Build-Military, WonderStep), the rank position (1 = actual top pick)
     of the best-scored LEGAL move of that kind, whenever one is legal.
  3. Specifically for Farm: how often a Build move targeting a Farm-type
     card is legal at all in rounds 1-3, and when not, why -- read off the
     same facts legal.rs's own build loop already branches on
     (PlayerState::workers_free, costs::build_cost_net,
     PlayerState::civil_actions), not re-derived independently.

VERDICT
-------
NULL RESULT: the opener is legal-by-construction as far as Farm is
concerned. The evaluator does not want a Farm build as its top pick, ever,
anywhere in this sample:

  "Build Farm" is the CHOSEN (top-ranked) move 0 times out of 85240 (2p),
  155698 (3p), and 75833 (4p) own decision points -- 316771 decision points
  total, zero Farm builds chosen. This is not "usually not chosen" rounded
  down; the raw numerator is exactly 0 in all three player counts.

  Stronger than that: even restricted to the decisions where a Farm build
  IS legal (23101 / 29127 / 14634 decisions respectively), its rank among
  every other legal move is NEVER 1 -- the minimum observed rank is 2 in
  all three player counts, and the median rank is 3-5. So this is not a
  case of "Farm nearly wins the argument, then legality removes it, then
  something else wins" -- some OTHER legal move (usually Mine or Military)
  out-scores Farm at literally every single one of these 89,862 legal-Farm
  decision points sampled.

This directly updates the old buildprobe.rs finding rather than confirming
or refuting it head-on, because that binary no longer exists to compare
against apples-to-apples: buildprobe.rs's "98.1% illegal Farm attempt" most
likely measured a rank-1 pick over a synthetic, NOT legality-filtered move
set (i.e. "if Farm build were unconditionally offered, would it win the
argument"), which nothing in the current engine reproduces and which this
probe was explicitly told not to fake. What this probe DOES establish,
cleanly, on today's champions: the bot's real decision-time preference
(scored only over what legal.rs actually offers) never ranks a Farm build
first, whether or not one is legal. There is no live "wants a dumb Farm
opener" habit hiding behind the legality filter to find.

The old 88.3%-of-illegal-Farms-for-want-of-a-free-worker claim is,
however, qualitatively still true in DIRECTION, if not magnitude: across
all three player counts "no free worker" is the single largest reason a
Farm build is illegal in rounds 1-3 (59.6% at 2p, 64.4% at 3p, 41.8% at
4p), consistent with every civilization starting all 6 workers already
placed (Agriculture holds 2, Bronze holds 2, Warriors 1 -- game.rs
START_TECHS) and needing a population increase before any new Farm/Mine/
unit can be staffed. "No such Farm card in play" is 0% in every sample --
Agriculture is always present from turn 1, so that failure mode never
fires; the closed reason set turned out to only need 3 live buckets, not
all 4 defined.

Secondary finding, matching the earlier behaviour census rather than
contradicting it: Mine's chosen-move share (11.9% at 3p, the single
largest named Build kind there) and its rank-when-legal (median 1 at 3p,
i.e. it usually IS the top legal pick when offered) both point the same
direction as behavcensus's "first executed build is a Mine 95.6% of the
time at 3p" -- the champion's stated preference and its executed behaviour
agree; there is no gap between "what it wants" and "what it does" for
Mine, the same conclusion this probe reaches for Farm (just at 0% instead
of 95.6%).

Nothing here contradicts the task brief's premises; it resolves the open
question the brief posed. The corpus/lint/climb-safety constraints in
CLAUDE.md were not implicated (no bots/ files touched, no corpus games
replayed).


FOLLOW-UP 2026-08-26: did end_turn_bias's per-table-size split change behaviour?
==================================================================================

QUESTION
--------
Over this climb, the trained `end_turn_bias` weight (bots/weighted/weights.rs)
moved in OPPOSITE directions by table size: 2p relaxed -9.60 -> -2.49 (turn-
ending penalized much less) while 4p moved the other way, -29.49 -> -37.34
(penalized much more). Does the 2p bot now actually choose to end its turn at
a markedly higher RATE than the 4p bot in rounds 1-3, or did the weight move
without changing behaviour?

METHOD
------
`Move::EndTurn` was pulled out of `DecisionKind::Other` into its own
`DecisionKind::EndTurn` bucket (rust/src/bin/openerprobe.rs) -- previously it
was silently folded in with every response/military-phase move `Other`
covers. Every other match arm is untouched; every other Item-1/2/3 count
below is byte-identical to the first run (spot-checked: `Other`'s new count
equals its old count minus exactly the new `EndTurn` count, at all three
player counts). Same weights, same games, same seeds as the first run:

  ./target/difftest/openerprobe --games 6000 --players 2 --weights .../champ2p.json --threads 2 --seed 1
  ./target/difftest/openerprobe --games 6000 --players 3 --weights .../champ3p.json --threads 2 --seed 1
  ./target/difftest/openerprobe --games 2000 --players 4 --weights .../champ4p.json --threads 2 --seed 1

A STRUCTURAL fact matters for reading the raw numerator: every player-turn
that completes inside the rounds-1-3 window ends with EXACTLY ONE decision
point whose top pick is `EndTurn` (a turn can only end by choosing it, and it
only ever ends once) -- so the raw `EndTurn` count is forced to equal
(players * 3 rounds * games) whenever no game/turn is cut short by the
round-4 truncation or MOVE_CAP, and is NOT itself evidence of a behaviour
change. Confirmed exactly: 36000 = 2*3*6000 (2p), 54000 = 3*3*6000 (3p),
24000 = 4*3*2000 (4p) -- all three land on the exact product, meaning every
sampled player-round completed inside the window with no truncation loss.

The informative quantity is therefore the SHARE of ALL own decision points
(not just the terminal one) at which EndTurn was the top pick -- equivalently
1 / (average number of own decisions taken per turn before ending). A higher
share means the bot is choosing to stop and end its turn sooner, on average,
relative to how many Take/Build/Develop/... decisions it works through first.

RAW RESULTS (Item 1, EndTurn row only -- see the full per-run Item 1 tables
above for the unchanged Take/Build/.../Other rows)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

  2p: EndTurn top pick  36000 / 85240 own decisions  (42.2%)
      average own decisions per turn: 85240 / 36000 = 2.37
  3p: EndTurn top pick  54000 / 155698 own decisions (34.7%)
      average own decisions per turn: 155698 / 54000 = 2.88
  4p: EndTurn top pick  24000 / 75833 own decisions  (31.6%)
      average own decisions per turn: 75833 / 24000  = 3.16

VERDICT
-------
The weight move shows up in behaviour, and in the DIRECTION the bias change
predicts: 2p's EndTurn share (42.2%) is the highest of the three and 4p's
(31.6%) is the lowest, monotonically, matching end_turn_bias moving toward
"cheap to end" at 2p (-9.60 -> -2.49) and toward "expensive to end" at 4p
(-29.49 -> -37.34). In concrete terms, the 2p bot now works through 2.37
other decisions per turn on average before ending it, against 3.16 for 4p --
roughly a third fewer actions worked through per turn at 2p than at 4p. This
is not "the weight moved without changing behaviour": the relaxed 2p penalty
correlates with a genuinely shorter average 2p turn in rounds 1-3, and the
tightened 4p penalty correlates with a genuinely longer one, in the same
self-play sample this whole file already used for the Farm question.

One caveat stated plainly, not smoothed over: this is a correlational read
across three different tables (2p/3p/4p), each with its own economy (fewer
rivals to react to, different card-row pressure, different production
curves), any of which could independently affect how many actions a turn
takes before ending -- `end_turn_bias` is not the only thing that differs
between these three runs. What this measurement rules out is the null
hypothesis stated in the question ("the weight moved without changing
observed behaviour") -- it does not, by itself, prove `end_turn_bias` is the
SOLE cause of the gap, only that the gap exists, is sizeable (2.37 vs 3.16
actions/turn), and points the same way the weight change points.

Develop is the OPPOSITE: overwhelmingly a LEGALITY wall, not a preference.
Legal at only 0/85240 (2p), 348/155698 (0.2%, 3p), 27/75833 (0.0%, 4p) of
decision points. The dominant reason, after round 1, is that the bot simply
holds no develop-eligible card in its civil hand at all: 63332/85240
(74.3%, 2p), 113112/155350 (72.8%, 3p), 55767/75806 (73.6%, 4p) of illegal
decisions -- civil-action shortage and science affordability are minor by
comparison (<=8.2% each at any count), and the science-pact-partners gate
never fires anywhere (0 everywhere). And on the rare occasions Develop IS
legal, the bot takes it almost every time -- rank is essentially always the
top pick (median 1, mean 1.00-1.15 across all three counts; chosen 333/348
= 95.7% of the time at 3p, and literally every one of the 27 legal
instances at 4p). The 2p "zero technologies developed in rounds 1-3"
finding is therefore not a ranking problem at all: it is a hand-composition
wall -- rounds 1-3's Take choices apparently never put a develop-eligible
card into `hand_civil` this early, so there is nothing to spend science on,
whatever the bot would otherwise prefer.

Net: fixing the Farm/food death spiral needs a PREFERENCE fix on Pop (rank
it above Build Mine/Military/EndTurn early, when it's legal and cheap), not
a legality change -- Pop is already offered most of the time it should be.
Fixing zero-Develop, if it is worth fixing at all this early, needs a
different lever entirely: either the Take ranking has to start prioritising
develop-eligible cards into hand in rounds 1-3, or the "zero Develops in
rounds 1-3" fact is simply not a bug -- humans may not develop that early
either, and this probe was not asked to check that baseline.
