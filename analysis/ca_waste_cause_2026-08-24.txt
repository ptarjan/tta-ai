============================================================
HYPOTHESIS TESTING
============================================================

H2 (move generation too narrow) -- RULED OUT.
In all 11 hits, `legal::legal_moves` (shared with `advisor`'s own board,
identical function the replayer and bots both call) offered real,
CA-costing TAKE/PLAY LEADER/wonder-TAKE candidates alongside EndTurn. The
move set was never empty or narrowed to just EndTurn.

H4 (affordability -- can't afford the CA-consuming move) -- RULED OUT as the
general explanation, and this is the most important negative result: every
alternative candidate in all 11 hits costs "[1 civil action(s)]" (one case
"[2 civil action(s)]") with NO food/resource component -- Take's cost in
this engine is paid in CA (plus whatever the row slot's own listed cost is,
already folded into "N civil action(s)"), not from stock. The player had
that many CA sitting unspent at the moment EndTurn was chosen. 0/11 sampled
hits were resource-blocked. This means essentially none of the measured 28%
looks "legitimate" by this test -- the waste is avoidable, not a food/CA
mismatch.

H1 (valuation: every legal move genuinely prices worse) -- CONFIRMED as the
PROXIMATE mechanism: WeightedBot really does score EndTurn higher, by a
real, computed margin (-0.6 to -8-ish across the 11 hits), using the exact
evaluator the champion self-play uses. But WHY every alternative prices
worse turns out to have a specific, structural cause (next section), not a
generic "cards are just overpriced-negative" story.

H3 (search/turn-continuation ends the turn early) -- CONFIRMED, in a
specific and previously-undocumented form: it is not a beam or loop
"stopping early" in the sense of a bug in the search LOOP -- WeightedBot's
1-ply search is a single argmax, not a loop that can stop partway. What
actually happens is a STRUCTURAL ASYMMETRY in what gets scored for each
candidate.

============================================================
THE ROOT CAUSE
============================================================
`WeightedBot::choose` (rust/src/bots/weighted/eval.rs:445-501) is a 1-ply
search: for each candidate move it clones a SHARED root state, applies ONLY
that one move, and scores the result:

    for &mv in moves {
        let mut trial = root.clone();
        apply::apply(&mut trial, mv);
        let mut val = evaluate(&trial, idx, w, Some(&ctx), None);
        if matches!(mv, Move::EndTurn) { val += end_bias; }
        ...
    }
                                                    (eval.rs:479-496)

`Move::EndTurn`'s `apply` arm is `crate::game::end_turn` (rust/src/
apply.rs:314), which calls `resume_end_turn` (rust/src/game.rs:923-946),
whose FIRST substantive call is `economy::end_of_turn(state, idx)`
(rust/src/economy.rs:935) -- the turn's production step, which converts
worker output into food/resource STOCK and is what resolves a player's
`food_gap`/`resource_gap` features. `economy::end_of_turn` has exactly ONE
call site in the whole engine: `resume_end_turn`, reached only through
`Move::EndTurn`.

The consequence: in the SAME 1-ply comparison, the EndTurn candidate's
trial state has ALREADY had this turn's production applied, while every
OTHER candidate's trial state (Take, PlayLeader, Build, ...) has NOT --
production has not happened yet in that trial, because the trial is exactly
one move deep and that move was not EndTurn. `food_gap`/`resource_gap` are
negatively-weighted features (champ2p.json: food_gap=-1.237,
resource_gap=-1.803), so a player sitting at food=0/res=0 (routine at this
point in the opener) is charged the FULL penalty in every non-EndTurn
trial, and credited close to zero penalty (having just produced) in the
EndTurn trial -- worth the observed "+3 resources, -1..-2 food/resource
gap" swing, several points on this vector's scale.

This is not a wrong fact about the position -- ending the turn NOW really
does produce sooner than ending it one action later. The bug is that the
1-ply evaluator has no way to score "take the card, THEN also end the turn
and get the SAME production" as a candidate: that is a 2-move sequence, and
this search only ever considers 1-move sequences from the current root. It
is comparing "produce now" against "hold this position forever" when the
true comparison a human makes is "produce now vs. produce one action later,
after also banking a free card" -- an option this searcher structurally
cannot represent. `hand_civil` (-0.470) and `take_cost_paid` (-0.346) being
negatively weighted on top of that (the previously-known "prefers to
overpay" pattern) is real but secondary here -- in most hits it is only a
fraction of a point; the multi-point production-timing swing is what
actually flips the decision, confirmed by it appearing in essentially every
one of the 11 EndTurn "why" lines and in NONE of the alternatives' "why"
lines (structurally impossible for it to, by the mechanism above).

============================================================
WHY THIS IS WORSE AT 2P THAN 4P -- RESOLVING THE ca_left CONTRADICTION
============================================================
The task's framing noted `ca_left` (rewards/penalises leftover CA) points
the "wrong way" for this to be worse at 2p: 2p's ca_left=-0.196 SHOULD
discourage hoarding, 4p's ca_left=+1.393 should REWARD it, yet 2p wastes
2.9x more. Measured weight comparison (champ2p.json vs champ4p.json)
resolves this: ca_left is not the operative term, and its sign was never
going to be -- it is a flat, position-independent nudge on the order of
0.2-1.4 points, completely swamped by the multi-point, position-dependent
production-timing swing described above. The swing's SIZE is set by
food_gap/resource_gap, and those differ sharply between the two frozen
champions:

  weight            2p (champ2p.json)   4p (champ4p.json)
  food_gap          -1.237              0.0   (OFF)
  resource_gap      -1.803              -1.175
  hand_civil        -0.470              -12.29
  take_cost_paid    -0.346              +0.218  (reward, not penalty)
  hand_potential    +9.93               -1.20

At 4p, `food_gap` is turned off entirely (weight 0) and `resource_gap` is
weaker, so half of the production-timing mechanism cannot fire and the
other half fires less hard. `take_cost_paid` is actually POSITIVE at 4p
(taking a card is rewarded, not fined), which works directly against the
2p pattern found here. This is consistent with, and gives a mechanistic
explanation for, 4p's measured 9.5% opening-CA-waste rate being milder than
2p's 28.0% -- not because 4p's evaluator reasons any better about turn
sequencing (the SAME 1-ply structural gap exists at 4p, unmeasured directly
here for budget reasons), but because the weight vector that would trigger
it is largely defused at 4p. The task's own note that 4p is far less
trained (gen 19761 vs 108239) is the most likely reason these vectors
differ this much, not a reasoned fix.

============================================================
WHAT FRACTION OF THE 28% IS UNAVOIDABLE (H4) VS GENUINELY DUMB
============================================================
Of the 11 reproduced hits (a real sample, not the full 84 -- exhaustively
enumerating all 84 was out of budget), 0 were resource/food-blocked: 11/11
had a fully legal, fully affordable (CA-only-cost) alternative to EndTurn
available and rejected it in favour of EndTurn. So on this sample, 0% looks
legitimately unavoidable and 100% looks like the production-timing artifact
above. This is a SAMPLE OF 11, not the full 84 -- stated as measured, not
extrapolated to an exact percentage of the full 28.0%.
