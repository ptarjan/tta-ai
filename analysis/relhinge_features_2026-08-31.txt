1. THE SEVEN KEYS
====================================================================

All seven are `WeightGroup::Rivals`, all authored default 0.0, all
`SignIntent::Free`. Formulas are in phi's own units -- see section 2.

  WeightKey                JSON name                   from screen column
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------
  CultureRateTrailFrac     culture_rate_trail_frac     rel_trail_culrate
  ScienceRateTrailFrac     science_rate_trail_frac     rel_trail_scirate
  CultureRateShare         culture_rate_share          rel_share_culrate
  CultureRateBehindNear    culture_rate_behind_near    rel_bkt_culrate_m1
  CultureRateAheadNear     culture_rate_ahead_near     rel_bkt_culrate_p1
  ScienceRateBehindNear    science_rate_behind_near    rel_bkt_scirate_m1
  ScienceRateAheadNear     science_rate_ahead_near     rel_bkt_scirate_p1

  *TrailFrac   max(0, (best rival's rate - mine) / best rival's rate)
  *Share       mine / (mine + best rival's), over the non-negative parts;
               exactly 0.5 when neither side produces at all
  *BehindNear  1.0 when I am behind by 1..far-1, else 0.0
  *AheadNear   1.0 when I am ahead  by 1..far-1, else 0.0
               far = 4 on culture, 3 on science -- one step of each
               quantity's own scale, copied from the screening rig

The *Near indicators are guarded by `rival_count > 0`. With no live rival the
rival rate is 0.0, which would read as a full-size LEAD and fire the ahead-side
indicator on a race that no longer exists. The existing deficit keys need no
such guard because zero-rival makes their `max(0, .)` vanish on its own; these
do, and the screening rig had the same guard via its `r.any` flag.

The FAR and LEVEL buckets are deliberately NOT keys. Five indicators over a
five-way partition sum to the constant 1 and would add a flat direction to the
weight space.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
1a. SIGN_INTENT -- Free, all seven, and why
--------------------------------------------------------------------

[[tta-sign-gate-snaps-to-zero]]: a sign_intent is not a hint, it is a hard
constraint enforced on every load and every mutation, and a wrong one destroys
the key's accumulated magnitude every time. The brief said prefer Free over
guessing. Free is not a shrug here; each has its own argument:

  *TrailFrac    `RivalCultureDeficit` / `RivalScienceDeficit` are already
                classified Free, with a stated reason (weights.rs: no
                RULES_SPEC chapter ties a trailing culture or science RATE to a
                rules-imposed outcome the way the combat chapter does for
                military strength). These two ARE those deficits divided by the
                leader's own rate. Dividing a quantity of unfixed sign by a
                positive quantity cannot fix a sign.

  *Share        the same race read as a bounded fraction, and it points the
                OPPOSITE way to the two above (higher share = ahead), so it
                could not share their gate even if they had one.

  *Near         stronger than "no citation": the sign is genuinely two-sided.
                An indicator's coefficient is measured against the OMITTED
                level and far buckets, not against zero, so even the
                behind-side pair has no a-priori direction -- "slightly behind"
                is worse than level and better than far behind, and one
                coefficient answers to both. RULES_SPEC imposes no penalty at
                any of these thresholds; they are cuts this project chose.

Section 5 measures a direction empirically and it is a large effect. It is
recorded there as a hint for whoever prices these keys, and deliberately NOT
promoted into a sign_intent: it is one magnitude, at one player count, against
one champion, and the gate is the one decision the climb cannot undo.

2. ONE DELIBERATE DEVIATION FROM THE SCREEN'S ARITHMETIC
====================================================================

The screening rig computed each rate from `effects::compute` on the trial
state. `features.rs` does not: its `CultureRate` is `effects::compute(...)
.culture + g(GainFeature::CultureRate)` (the deferred-credit adjustment) and
its `rival_culture_rate` is a max over rivals that also folds in
`dc.partner_gains` (an offered pact raises the partner's own rate).

The promoted keys are built off phi's OWN `CultureRate` / `RivalCultureRate` /
`ScienceRate` / `RivalScienceRate`, not re-derived. Reason: a phi coordinate
that disagreed with the `CultureRate`/`RivalCultureRate` pair sitting three
lines above it in the same function would be a second, quieter definition of
the same race. The cost is that the promoted columns are not bit-identical to
the screened ones; section 4 measures the difference and it is ~1%.

`rate_trail_fraction` is kept as its own function rather than shared with
`rivals::trailing_fraction`, which is the same expression on the WEIGHT side
(it scales card marginals through `feature_marginal`, keyed by the pre-existing
`CultureRateTrailing` / `ScienceRateTrailing`). Folding them together would
drag the deferred-credit and pact adjustments into the card marginal, which is
a behaviour change nobody asked for.

NAMING TRAP: `CultureRateTrailing` and `ScienceRateTrailing` ALREADY EXIST as
weight-side scales with no phi coordinate. The new phi keys are therefore
`*_trail_frac`, not `*_trailing`. Anyone reading the champion JSON now sees
four culture/science "trailing" names; two price card marginals, two are
standing-position coordinates, and they never meet.

3. THE COLUMN I DROPPED, AND WHY -- rel_scirate_lead
====================================================================

The brief listed `rel_scirate_lead` (R2 +0.000448) as the eighth promotion,
"the MIRROR of a hinge phi already has". It is NOT promoted. It is an exact
linear combination of three columns phi already emits.

phi emits, verbatim, in `features.rs`:

    ScienceRate          = my science rate                        (:688)
    RivalScienceRate     = best rival's science rate              (:1110)
    RivalScienceDeficit  = max(0, rival - mine)                   (:1128-1131)

and for any real g, `max(0,g) - max(0,-g) == g` identically. With
g = mine - rival:

    ScienceRateLead = max(0, mine - rival)
                    = g + max(0, -g)
                    = ScienceRate - RivalScienceRate + RivalScienceDeficit

exactly, at every state, with no approximation. The leaf eval is dot(w, phi)
([[tta-leaf-eval-is-a-dot-product]]), so a column in the span of three live
columns adds ZERO representational power at any weight -- the learner can
already express any weighting of it by moving those three. This is precisely
`StrengthRel`, which [[tta-relative-features-need-a-hinge]] already records as
redundant for the identical reason.

The families report scored it spanned 0.9984 rather than 1.0000 and read that
as "the mirror half is missing from phi, so the same construction scores 5x to
25x more". The 0.16% residual is not a missing half: it is the section-2
definitional gap -- the rig's `effects::compute` rates against phi's
deferred-credit- and pact-adjusted ones. Implement the column faithfully in
`features.rs` units, as this work did for the other seven, and the residual
goes to zero. The R2 gain of +0.000448 is measuring the deferred-credit
adjustment, not a lead hinge.

The same algebra retires `rel_culrate_lead` (+0.000320, spanned 0.9986), whose
three phi columns are `CultureRate` (:686), `RivalCultureRate` (:1109) and
`RivalCultureDeficit` (:1142-1145).

So the families report's section 6 needs one correction: the high-spanned
hinges it separated into "phi already carries this one" (~0 gain) versus "the
mirror half is missing" (5-25x more) are the SAME case. Both are spanned; the
second group's gain is an artefact of the rig's own arithmetic. Where phi
carries a deficit hinge AND both raw rates, it carries the lead too.

I ALSO AGREE WITH THE BRIEF'S OWN EXCLUSIONS (`rel_proj_*`, `rel_x_*_late`) and
did not include them.

The "scale" rule, stated once: each key's weight is set so that its
contribution to a TYPICAL candidate-set spread is a fixed fraction of what
`CultureRate` contributes, using featdisc's own mean_spread as the unit --
w_new = frac * |w_culture_rate| * mean_spread(CultureRate) / mean_spread(new).
At 5% that is w = -30.75, -9.54, +45.92, -15.41, +15.82, -8.66, +14.50 in the
table's key order. "Intuitive signs" = behind is bad, ahead and share are good.

WHAT B SAYS. Row B is not a statistical wash, it is BIT-IDENTITY: 50.0% +/-
0.0, and mean moves 301.5 and mean culture 184.1 match row A to every printed
digit. The seven keys at their authored 0.0 change nothing whatsoever. That is
the invariant [[tta-relative-features-need-a-hinge]] requires of a new key, and
it is the honest reading of "a wash is the expected outcome at defaults" -- the
arena cannot say more than this, because there is nothing stochastic left to
measure.

WHAT D DOES NOT SAY. Row D on its own reads "the new features cost 1.9pp".
That reading is wrong, and rows E and F are why.

  * E is the perfectly matched control: same seven keys, same seven magnitudes,
    only the signs reversed. 48.1% against 32.4% is a 15.7pp swing produced by
    nothing but direction. A coordinate that could not reach the argmax could
    not do that. This is a second, independent proof of reachability, and a
    much harsher one than section 4's.

  * F asks what a perturbation of this size costs ANYWHERE. Seven established
    live coordinates (free_workers, ca_left, hand_value, take_cost_paid,
    science_gap, wonder_stages_left, military_action_gap), moved by the same
    per-key contribution: 14.6%, a 35.4pp loss. The same-sized move on the
    seven new coordinates costs 1.9pp, nineteen times less.

    CAVEAT, stated because it cuts against my own conclusion: F is not a
    perfectly matched control. Those seven keys carry small fitted values
    (ca_left -0.034, hand_value +0.009), so an equal-contribution delta moves
    them by multiples of their current magnitude, whereas the new keys move
    from exactly zero. F is an upper bound on "what perturbing a converged
    champion costs", not a like-for-like. E is the airtight one.

SO THE HONEST SUMMARY IS: reachable (twice over), inert at defaults (exactly),
and not actively harmful -- at equal perturbation magnitude they cost 1.9pp
where established coordinates cost 35.4pp, and at 1% they sit slightly on the
positive side of the null. The 5% overshoot plus the 1% near-wash is the shape
of a coordinate whose weight has to be FITTED, which is the climb's job and was
explicitly out of scope here.

DIRECTIONAL HINT, NOT A GATE. E vs D is a large, clean signal that "behind on a
rate is bad, ahead is good" is the right direction for these shapes. It is
recorded here and deliberately NOT encoded as a sign_intent, for the reason in
section 1a: one magnitude, one player count, one champion, against a constraint
the climb cannot undo.

7a. THE REAL-CORPUS DOT-PRODUCT TEST DID NOT EXIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------

The brief and [[tta-leaf-eval-is-a-dot-product]] both say there is an existing
real-corpus identity test,
`linear_features_dotted_with_the_champion_reproduces_evaluate_on_real_replayed_bgo_positions`,
in `rust/src/bots/weighted/eval.rs`. THERE IS NOT ONE, and there never was:
`git log -S` over that path returns no commit that ever contained the string.
The memory is wrong on this point and should be corrected.

What eval.rs actually had were three identity tests, all on `game::new_game`
toy deals or hand-built fixtures, all at `Weights::default()`. That leaves
exactly the blind spot these seven keys sit in: a feature that is structurally
zero on turn one (nobody has a rate, nobody is ahead) contributes nothing
whatever weight it is given, and a coordinate whose authored default is 0.0
(all seven of these, every hinge landed 2026-08-27, and 40-odd others) is
dotted against zero -- so `linear_features` could read the wrong index for it
and the sum would not move.

Added, in eval.rs's trailing test block:

    linear_features_reproduces_evaluate_on_played_forward_positions_at_a_nondefault_vector

Real self-play driven to depth at 2p/3p/4p, sampling every 5th move past move
10, asserting dot(w, candidate_features) == rank_moves' own score for every
candidate. Every key gets a DISTINCT non-zero weight (0.25 + i * 0.03125) so a
transposed pair of indices cannot cancel. Two premise assertions stop it
passing vacuously: >5000 candidates checked, and the seven new coordinates
non-zero on >1000 of them. It passes. Max discrepancy stays under the existing
1e-6 tolerance.

It does not read `sources/`, so it runs in a fresh checkout without the private
corpus symlink -- which is probably why the corpus-based test the memory
remembers was never committed.
