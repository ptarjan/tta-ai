CAVEAT on the taken/played numbers: behavcensus only reports the full
"taken/played" card-fate breakdown split by EARLY (first wonder-stage build by
round 5) vs LATE (round 10+ or never) wonder tempo, per analysis/opener_census_
postclimb_2026-08-26.txt's own methodology. There is a "middle" group (first
wonder round 6-9) that is NOT covered by either bucket. Coverage of the full
population: 2p 51/120 (42.5%), 3p 119/180 (66.1%), 4p 211/240 (87.9%). The
taken/game and played% figures above are therefore averages over that partial
population, not the whole game population. behavcensus does not print a
whole-population "cards taken per game" or "% played" figure anywhere else in
its output.

--- What openerprobe reports (players=2/3/4, rounds 1-3 only, 60 games each) ---
openerprobe does NOT report food production, cards-taken-per-game, played-
fraction, or civil-action-waste at all. It reports something orthogonal: for
each decision point in rounds 1-3, the chosen move's kind, and for Farm/Pop/
Develop specifically, why the move was illegal when it was. None of this is
directly comparable to the four baseline figures. Two possibly-relevant
sub-findings that did come out of it (3p, rounds 1-3, 1841 own-turn decisions):
Farm build illegal 1487/1841 times, of which 44.0% "no free worker", 34.8%
"no resources", 21.2% "no civil action"; Pop illegal 1000/1841 times, of which
43.5% "no civil action available", 38.5% "food unaffordable". This corroborates
the 2026-08-26 census's finding that the bottleneck is production/workers, not
civil-action supply -- but it is not one of the four requested metrics.

=====================================================================
BASELINE COMPARISON
=====================================================================

1. "Food production DECLINES from round 2 onward; strong humans climb to ~4.95"
   -> DID NOT MOVE. Confirmed again today: food stays flat at 2.00 through
   round 2-3 then declines (2p, 3p) or flattens around 1.9 (4p) at every
   player count. Never approaches 4.95 at any round measured. Same shape as
   the 2026-08-26 postclimb finding.

2. "Bot takes ~17.5 cards/game and plays ~61% of them; humans take ~26.9 and
    play ~78%"
   -> PARTIALLY MOVED, but not cleanly measurable against the same
   denominator as the original baseline (see caveat above -- these are
   EARLY+LATE-only averages, not whole-population). Taken/game is UP at all
   three player counts (19.2-21.3 vs the baseline's 17.5), moving toward the
   human 26.9 but still well short. Played fraction is UP at all three player
   counts vs both the original 61% baseline AND yesterday's 57-58%
   postclimb-census figure (2p 67.4%, 3p 62.6%, 4p 82.2% -- 4p now exceeds the
   human 78% figure outright). Call this MOVED IN THE RIGHT DIRECTION since
   the 2026-08-26 check, with the caveat that the metric excludes ~12-58% of
   games (the "middle" wonder-tempo group) depending on player count.

3. "~20.3% of civil actions are wasted"
   -> WAS NOT MEASURABLE with these tools using the ORIGINAL denominator
   (behavcensus has no whole-game civil-action-waste stat; this was already
   true on 2026-08-26). Using the same substitute metric the 2026-08-26 census
   used (player-games leaving >=1 CA unused in rounds 1-3): 2p and 3p are
   essentially unchanged and near-zero (2.3%->3.3%, 1.4%->1.7%, both still far
   below 20.3%, i.e. still NOT the same denominator as the original claim and
   still not comparable to it). 4p MOVED substantially: 24.0% -> 6.7%, a real
   drop in opening-round CA waste at 4p specifically since yesterday.

VERDICT: The recent leaf-eval feature work has NOT moved the headline food-
production defect at all -- production is still flat-then-declining at every
player count, nowhere close to a human-like climb. It HAS moved card economy
in the right direction since yesterday's postclimb check: the bot takes more
cards and plays a higher fraction of what it takes at all three player counts,
with 4p now playing a higher fraction than the cited human baseline. The
civil-action-waste picture is mixed and mostly not measurable against the
original claim; the one real signal is a large drop in 4p opening CA waste.
Net read: the climb is fixing symptoms downstream of production (card
economy) while the production shortfall itself -- which the 2026-08-26 census
already identified as the root cause behind the card-play defect -- remains
untouched. Until food/resource production actually rises, "the opener" is not
fixed; it is playing its bad hand slightly better.
