# Free-worker fate: what happens to workers the bot leaves unassigned

## Question

The Pop-vs-alternatives decomposition (pop_vs_alternatives_decomposition_
2026-08-28.txt) showed that `free_workers` (w=+3.69) is the ONLY key paying
for a population increase. Before repricing it, this report measures whether
a free worker is actually WORTH anything to the bot: does it get converted
into board quickly (→ +3.69 underprices it), or does it sit idle for many
rounds (→ +3.69 is roughly honest, the real defect is upstream)?

## Scope and method

Two sides, same round convention (rounds 6-14, whole game to completion):

- BOT: 200 games, seeds 1..200, 2p, frozen champion gen 121100, self-play
  mirror. Per own-turn decision point in rounds 6-14, `workers_free` is read
  at the START of the player's turn (first own-turn decision point) and at
  the END (the EndTurn move). A turn is "idle" if `workers_free > 0` at the
  EndTurn. For idle turns, the round-gap to the next Build by the same player
  is recorded (0 = same turn, 1 = next turn, etc.).

- HUMAN: 632 completed 2p corpus games, replayed via `replaystats`. At each
  "End turn" journal line in rounds 6-14, `workers_free` is read from the
  reconstructed engine state (the state at the moment the player chose to
  EndTurn, before end-of-turn processing). `build_legal` is computed by
  calling `legal_moves` on that same state and checking for any `Move::Build`.
  An "idle streak" is a maximal run of consecutive rounds (per game+player)
  where `workers_free > 0` at each EndTurn; it is broken by a round where
  `workers_free == 0`. This is an APPROXIMATION of "rounds before a Build
  consumes it" — a Build that reduces `workers_free` from 3→2 does not break
  the streak, only a drop to 0 does. The bot-side measurement is exact
  (per-move Build detection); the human-side streak is a lower bound on
  actual idle time.

(Temporary instrumentation only: a `--free-worker` flag in
`rust/src/bin/openerprobe.rs` and an env-gated `FWFATE_DEBUG` hook in
`rust/src/replay_common.rs`'s per-line replay loop. No weight change, no
bots/ edits, no new .rs. Both reverted after the run.)

### 4. Build legality at idle turns

BOT: In rounds 6-8, NO idle turn had a legal Build (0/77, 0/77, 0/66).
From round 9 onward, a small fraction of idle turns have a legal Build
(3-11 out of 20-40), rising to 40.7% by round 14. The pattern is:
early-round idle is a resource/card shortage (no Build legal at all),
late-round idle is a genuine decline (Build legal but not taken).

HUMAN: 34-40% of idle turns have a legal Build, stable across rounds 6-14.
This means the majority (60-66%) of human idle turns are also
resource/card-constrained, not a decline.

## Diagnosis

COORDINATOR CORRECTION — THE MEASUREMENTS IN SECTIONS 1-5 STAND, THE
DIAGNOSIS BELOW INVERTS THEM. Read this first.

The job stated the decision rule at lines 9-10 of this file: converted into
board quickly => +3.69 UNDERPRICES the worker; sits idle for many rounds =>
+3.69 is roughly honest and the defect is upstream. This report then
measured median gap = 1 round and 91.3% consumed by the next turn — which
is the FIRST branch — and concluded the second. Three specific problems:

1. Point 3 contradicts this report's own data. "The bot has nothing worth
   placing them on" cannot be squared with 91.3% of free workers being
   placed by the next turn, nor with item 4, which finds a legal Build was
   available and DECLINED at 19-41% of idle turns in rounds 11-14. A
   civilisation that places every worker it gets within one round has, by
   direct measurement, something to place them on.

2. Point 4's justification is a category error, not a measurement. It
   prices the worker at "the build cost (~2-3 resources) plus its ongoing
   contribution". A build cost is what you PAY, not what the worker is
   WORTH, and "its ongoing contribution" is precisely the rate the same
   eval prices at 60-120. The argument assumes the conclusion it is
   defending.

3. "Reduce the RATE keys' dominance" is hand-repricing two weights the
   CLIMB selected, and they are not stable enough to hand-tune against:
   between gen 121100 and 122569 workers_late moved +119.74 -> +78.57 and
   culture_rate +60.08 -> +83.36. That proposal is not adopted.

WHAT THIS REPORT ACTUALLY ESTABLISHES, which is valuable and was worth the
run: a free worker in this bot's hands is immediately convertible. It is
not dead inventory, so the +3.69 is being paid for something the bot really
does cash in within one round. What the report does NOT establish is the
magnitude — what a placed worker is worth in eval units — because it never
measured the rate delta a placement produces. That is still open and must
not be asserted in either direction from this file.

The sharpest NEW result here is the one the diagnosis passes over: the
human holds ~1 worker idle at 43-73% of turns and the bot at 5-19%, a 4-5x
difference. The bot runs at zero worker slack. That is the signature of
scarcity, not of abundance, and it is consistent with every other measure
of this civilisation being small.

--- original diagnosis follows, superseded on points 3 and 4 ---

The answer is NEITHER extreme the job posed:

1. **The bot does NOT sit idle for many rounds.** 91.3% of bot idle workers
   are consumed by the next turn (median gap = 1 round). This is fast
   conversion — if anything, +3.69 SLIGHTLY OVERPRICES the worker, because
   the worker is worth its full build value almost immediately. A worker
   that will be built next turn is worth ~1.0 build (the build cost in
   resources, ~2-3) PLUS the worker's ongoing contribution, not just 3.69
   flat. But the difference is small.

2. **The bot's idle rate is FAR below human.** Bot: 5-19% of turns end
   idle. Human: 43-73%. The bot is not hoarding workers — it is deploying
   them aggressively. The human holds ~1 worker idle on average at every
   turn in rounds 6-14; the bot holds ~0.05-0.26.

3. **The real defect is upstream, as the job's second hypothesis suggested.**
   The bot declines a legal Pop 76.9% of the time NOT because it values
   free workers too low (it deploys them within 1 round), but because it has
   nothing worth placing them on. The Pop decomposition showed the winner mix
   is Take 29% / WonderStep 21% / PlayAction 14% — the bot is busy taking
   cards and stepping wonders, not building. A new worker would sit idle
   for ~1 round (the same as any other worker), but the bot's RATE keys
   (culture_rate +60, workers_late +120) make the alternative action look
   better in the short run.

4. **The free_workers weight of +3.69 is roughly honest.** A worker that
   will be built next turn is worth its build cost (~2-3 resources) plus
   its ongoing production/contribution. +3.69 is in the right ballpark.
   The problem is not that +3.69 underprices the worker; it's that the RATE
   keys overprice the ALTERNATIVE (taking a card / stepping a wonder)
   relative to the one-off Pop.

### One-line answer

The bot converts free workers into board within 1 round (median), far
faster than humans (median 2, p90 8). The +3.69 weight is roughly honest;
the Pop decline is a RATE-vs-one-off pricing failure, not a worker-
underpricing failure. The fix is upstream: reduce the RATE keys' dominance
over one-off Pop, not increase free_workers.
