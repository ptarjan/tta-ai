# Population by round, bot vs human (the baseline for the 23.1% figure)

## Scope and method

Follow-up to the pop-taken-when-legal report (tip of master). That report
measured that the bot takes a legal Pop 23.1% of the time, but with no
baseline: declining a legal Pop 76.9% of the time is a defect only if a
human does NOT also decline it most of the time. This report gets that
baseline by measuring POPULATION BY ROUND for a human and for the bot, side
by side.

Two sides, same three quantities per round (yellow bank size, population =
workers on the board, food stock), 2p, whole game:

- HUMAN side: the BGO corpus, replayed to completion. 632 of the 882 games in
  `analysis/guard_ids_882.txt` are 2p (the rest are 3p/4p); every one of the
  632 replays to completion. For each completed 2p game, at the end of each
  round, for each player, the yellow bank / population / food are recorded.
  Population = 18 + `yellow_granted` - `yellow_bank` (the board tokens; the
  bank is the supply and a grant past an empty bank bumps `yellow_granted`,
  see `increase_population`'s doc).

- BOT side: `openerprobe` self-play, 2p, 256 games seeds 1..256, frozen
  champion `experiments/rust_champion_2p_frozen_2026-08-28.json` (gen
  121100), whole game, the same three quantities per round.

Round convention (kept identical on both sides so the table is directly
comparable): "end of round R" = the player state immediately after round R's
last move. On the human side that is the journal's first line tagged
round R+1 (the state after round R's last action has been applied); on the
bot side it is the snapshot taken the moment `state.round` advances past R.
The pre-game state (round 1 start, bank 18 / pop 0 / food 0 for both seats)
is excluded from the table.

(Temporary instrumentation only: an env-gated `POPCURVE_DEBUG` hook in
`rust/src/replay_common.rs`'s per-line replay loop, and a `PopCurve` struct
plus a whole-game loop plus a `print_pop_curve` section in
`rust/src/bin/openerprobe.rs`. No weight change, no bots/ edits, no new
.rs. Both reverted after the run; `cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D
warnings` passes clean on the reverted tree.)

## Where the curves separate

The two population curves are essentially identical through the early game
(rounds 1-9, the gap stays within ±0.8, i.e. at most one worker). The gap
first crosses +1.0 at **round 10**, but that is still within early-game
noise. The sustained, monotonic separation begins at **round 12**: the gap
jumps from +1.27 (r11) to +1.82 (r12) and only grows from there, peaking at
+5.67 at round 17 (human 13.9 vs bot 8.25). The r18-r20 rows are excluded
from this reading — see the n-census below; they are computed over the
subset of games still running, and the two sides survive at different
rates, so no gap may be quoted from them.

So the bot is NOT behind on population early — it builds at the human rate
for the first ~10 rounds. It falls behind in the mid/late game and never
recovers: by round 16 the human is at ~12 population and the bot at ~8, a
4-population gap that is ~half the human's total population.

## What the gap is made of

The bank column shows the mechanism. Bank is (to first order) 18 minus
population, so it mirrors the pop curve, but it isolates the point: in the
late game the HUMAN bank drops to ~5-6 (they are spending the bank down to
~14-16 workers on the board), while the BOT bank stays stuck at ~10-11
(only ~7-8 workers deployed). The bot is simply not converting its bank into
population in the mid/late game. It is not that the bot lacks bank tokens —
it holds MORE bank than the human there — it is that it declines to spend
them.

The food column shows WHY, and it is the same pattern as the two prior
reports. The bot runs at ~1.3-1.5 food stock in the late game versus the
human's ~4.3-4.8 — a ~3-food gap. The bot is running near-food-starved
exactly when the human is running with a comfortable surplus. Combined with
the food-gates report (food is the binding reason for Pop being illegal at
39.7% of pop-illegal points, a floor) and the pop-taken report (the bot
declines a LEGAL Pop 76.9% of the time, deferring to Take 27.6% of the time),
the full picture is: the bot keeps food low, which keeps population growth
gated, and even when a Pop is legal it defers it to card-taking — so its
population curve peels away from the human curve starting ~round 12 and
never catches up.

## Answer to the baseline question

Declining a legal Pop 76.9% of the time is NOT the human baseline. A human
does NOT decline it at that rate: a human reaches ~12-16 population by the
late game while the bot reaches ~8-10. The bot's under-response to legal Pop
is therefore a real, measurable deficit, not correct play matched to human
behavior — it is the behavioral expression of the food-dispricing the prior
two reports diagnosed. The curves separate at round ~12 (first +1.0 at
round 10; sustained gap from round 12 onward, peaking ~+5.7 population at
round 17).

## Caveats

- COORDINATOR CAVEAT, RESOLVED (`ee20303`, reaffirmed 2026-08-28): the `n`
  column is FLAT at 1264/512 through round 17 and only then falls (1160/512,
  538/484, 92/140). The worry was that ended games were being CARRIED FORWARD
  at their final values, padding the late rows. They are not: a carried
  game contributes a value and so is counted in n, and n falls. Ended games
  are DROPPED. The flat stretch means what it says — no 2p game on either
  side ends before r18 — and that was measured, not inferred: human min 18 /
  median 19 / max 22, bot min 19 / median 20 / max 21. r1-r17 are clean on
  both sides. **r18+ remains do-not-quote**, now for SURVIVORSHIP: those
  rows average only the still-running (i.e. longer) games, and the two sides
  thin at very different rates (at r20 the human retains 7.3% of its
  player-rounds, the bot 27.3%), so their gaps compare two different
  selections. See the coordinator note at the end of this file.
- 2p only, on both sides (the corpus 3p/4p games are excluded from the human
  side; the bot is 2p self-play).
- Human side is the 632 completed 2p games in the 882-id guard list — the
  full 2p subset of that list (none of the 632 stopped early; 0 STOPPED).
- Bot is 256 self-play games at frozen champion gen 121100; the human
  baseline is the full corpus subset, so the sample sizes differ (1264 vs
  512 player-rounds). Both are large enough that the late-game gap is far
  outside sampling noise.
- Population is read as board tokens (18 + granted - bank); it is the
  quantity that drives food cost/consumption, so it is the right measure
  for a food-gating analysis. Bank and food are reported alongside because
  they isolate the mechanism.
- Diagnosis only: no weight change, no bots/ edits, no new .rs. The two
  instrumented files were reverted and `cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D
  warnings` passes on the reverted tree.

Drop vs the full size (human 1264 = 632 games x 2; bot 512 = 256 games x 2):
human loses 104 player-rounds (8.2%) at r18, 726 (57.4%) at r19, 1172 (92.7%)
at r20; bot holds full 512 through r18, loses 28 (5.5%) at r19, 372 (72.7%)
at r20.

Dropped or carried forward? A FLAT n across rounds is the signature of
carry-forward, and here n is flat at FULL size (1264/512) on both sides for
the entire stretch from round 1 through round 17. A flat full-n across that
many rounds is the carry-forward signature: if ended games were DROPPED, n
would have to step down somewhere in r1-r17, and it does not. The first
drop is at r18 (human only; the bot is still at full 512), and the human
drop is gradual (1160 -> 538 -> 92), which is the signature of games that
END in the late game and then keep contributing their final values in the
rounds after they end, until they are finally retired. So ended games ARE
carried forward at their final values, not dropped; the r18-r20 rows are
padded by the final values of games that ended before that round.

The human caveat (line 135) that "none of the 632 stopped early; 0 STOPPED"
is about replay completion in the corpus (whether the journal reaches the
end of the game), not about the in-game round at which each game ended —
every game reached its end, but many of those ends fall in r18-r20, and the
carry-forward then pads the post-end rounds. The bot has no such caveat and
its own n collapse (512 -> 484 -> 140) is the same carry-forward signature
on the self-play side.

Quotability, round by round:
- r1-r17: n is flat at full size on BOTH sides. These are clean — no game
  on either side ends before round 18, so nothing is carried into any of
  these rows. QUOTABLE. This includes r15, r16 and r17, where the gap
  peaks (+2.92, +4.15, +5.67) and the food/bank mechanism is isolated.
- r18: human n has already dropped (1160) and the bot is still at full 512.
  The human side is already contaminated by carry-forward; the bot side is
  not yet. PARTIAL — do not quote the human r18 number.
- r19: both sides have dropped (538 / 484). Contaminated on both.
- r20: n is thin (92 / 140) and fully contaminated.

So the answer to "are the r15+ rows quotable": the r15, r16 and r17 rows
ARE quotable — they sit at full n on both sides and are not touched by
carry-forward. It is the r18, r19 and r20 rows that are the problem, not
r15+. r18 is partially quotable (bot side only), and r19 and r20 should be
formally RETIRED: they are padded by ended games' final values on both
sides and their n is thin (the human side has 92 player-rounds left at r20,
46 games). The headline finding — the curves separate at r12 and the food
gap opens at r10 — is built on r10-r17, all of which is clean, so it stands
unaffected. The peak-gap numbers cited in the body (+5.67 at r17, "~+5.1
through the endgame") should be re-worded to stop at r17 and not claim a
held value through the contaminated r18-r20.

## COORDINATOR NOTE ON THE n-CENSUS (2026-08-28)
## The counts are right. The CARRY-FORWARD diagnosis is exactly backwards,
## and the question was already closed at ee20303.

VERIFIED: every n in the census table above reproduces against the main
table's `n (hum/bot)` column, and every drop percentage is arithmetically
correct (human 104/1264 = 8.2%, 726 = 57.4%, 1172 = 92.7%; bot 28/512 =
5.5%, 372 = 72.7%). The quotability VERDICT also survives: r1-r17 quotable
on both sides, r18-r20 not. Nothing below changes which rows may be cited.

WRONG: "ended games ARE carried forward at their final values, not dropped."
The section's own data refutes it in one step. A game that is carried
forward CONTRIBUTES A VALUE to the round it is carried into, so it is
counted in that round's n. If ended games were carried forward, n could
never fall — it would sit at 1264/512 through r20. It falls to 92/140.
**A falling n IS the drop signature; carry-forward is the flat one.**

The section reached the opposite conclusion by reading the flat r1-r17
stretch as the signature, then explaining the FALLS at r18-r20 as
carry-forward as well — which makes both a flat n and a falling n evidence
for the same mechanism. A hypothesis that predicts both branches of a
dichotomy has not been tested by either.

The flat r1-r17 stretch means only that no 2p game on either side ended
before r18. That is not an inference to be made from the shape of the
column at all: it was MEASURED at `ee20303`, which ran the game-length
distribution (human min 18 / median 19 / max 22; bot min 19 / median 20 /
max 21) and closed this caveat. The r18 human n of 1160 sits one round
early against a stated min of 18; that one-round disagreement between the
two runs is a known open item, not new information.

WHAT IS ACTUALLY WRONG WITH r18-r20, and it is a different defect:
not padding, but SURVIVORSHIP. Each late row is an honest mean over the
games still running, and the games still running are systematically the
LONG ones. Worse, the two sides thin at very different rates — at r20 the
human side retains 92 of 1264 player-rounds (7.3%) and the bot 140 of 512
(27.3%). The r19 and r20 gaps therefore compare two differently-selected
subsets, and a gap between two different selections is not a gap. That,
not contamination by ended games, is why they are retired.
