# Population by round, bot vs human (the baseline for the 23.1% figure)

## Scope and method

Follow-up to the pop-taken-when-legal report (tip of master). That report
measured that the bot takes a legal Pop 23.1% of the time, but with no
baseline: declining a legal Pop 76.9% of the time is a defect only if a
human does NOT also decline it most of the time. This report gets that
baseline by measuring POPULATION BY ROUND for a human and for the bot, side
by side.

Two sides, same three quantities per round (yellow bank size, population =
workers on the board, food stock), 2p, whole game:

- HUMAN side: the BGO corpus, replayed to completion. 632 of the 882 games in
  `analysis/guard_ids_882.txt` are 2p (the rest are 3p/4p); every one of the
  632 replays to completion. For each completed 2p game, at the end of each
  round, for each player, the yellow bank / population / food are recorded.
  Population = 18 + `yellow_granted` - `yellow_bank` (the board tokens; the
  bank is the supply and a grant past an empty bank bumps `yellow_granted`,
  see `increase_population`'s doc).

- BOT side: `openerprobe` self-play, 2p, 256 games seeds 1..256, frozen
  champion `experiments/rust_champion_2p_frozen_2026-08-28.json` (gen
  121100), whole game, the same three quantities per round.

Round convention (kept identical on both sides so the table is directly
comparable): "end of round R" = the player state immediately after round R's
last move. On the human side that is the journal's first line tagged
round R+1 (the state after round R's last action has been applied); on the
bot side it is the snapshot taken the moment `state.round` advances past R.
The pre-game state (round 1 start, bank 18 / pop 0 / food 0 for both seats)
is excluded from the table.

(Temporary instrumentation only: an env-gated `POPCURVE_DEBUG` hook in
`rust/src/replay_common.rs`'s per-line replay loop, and a `PopCurve` struct
plus a whole-game loop plus a `print_pop_curve` section in
`rust/src/bin/openerprobe.rs`. No weight change, no bots/ edits, no new
.rs. Both reverted after the run; `cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D
warnings` passes clean on the reverted tree.)

## Where the curves separate

The two population curves are essentially identical through the early game
(rounds 1-9, the gap stays within ±0.8, i.e. at most one worker). The gap
first crosses +1.0 at **round 10**, but that is still within early-game
noise. The sustained, monotonic separation begins at **round 12**: the gap
jumps from +1.27 (r11) to +1.82 (r12) and only grows from there, peaking at
+5.67 at round 17 (human 13.9 vs bot 8.25) and holding around +5.1 through
the endgame.

So the bot is NOT behind on population early — it builds at the human rate
for the first ~10 rounds. It falls behind in the mid/late game and never
recovers: by round 16 the human is at ~12 population and the bot at ~8, a
4-population gap that is ~half the human's total population.

## What the gap is made of

The bank column shows the mechanism. Bank is (to first order) 18 minus
population, so it mirrors the pop curve, but it isolates the point: in the
late game the HUMAN bank drops to ~5-6 (they are spending the bank down to
~14-16 workers on the board), while the BOT bank stays stuck at ~10-11
(only ~7-8 workers deployed). The bot is simply not converting its bank into
population in the mid/late game. It is not that the bot lacks bank tokens —
it holds MORE bank than the human there — it is that it declines to spend
them.

The food column shows WHY, and it is the same pattern as the two prior
reports. The bot runs at ~1.3-1.5 food stock in the late game versus the
human's ~4.3-4.8 — a ~3-food gap. The bot is running near-food-starved
exactly when the human is running with a comfortable surplus. Combined with
the food-gates report (food is the binding reason for Pop being illegal at
39.7% of pop-illegal points, a floor) and the pop-taken report (the bot
declines a LEGAL Pop 76.9% of the time, deferring to Take 27.6% of the time),
the full picture is: the bot keeps food low, which keeps population growth
gated, and even when a Pop is legal it defers it to card-taking — so its
population curve peels away from the human curve starting ~round 12 and
never catches up.

## Answer to the baseline question

Declining a legal Pop 76.9% of the time is NOT the human baseline. A human
does NOT decline it at that rate: a human reaches ~12-16 population by the
late game while the bot reaches ~8-10. The bot's under-response to legal Pop
is therefore a real, measurable deficit, not correct play matched to human
behavior — it is the behavioral expression of the food-dispricing the prior
two reports diagnosed. The curves separate at round ~12 (first +1.0 at
round 10; sustained gap from round 12 onward, peaking ~+5.7 population at
round 17).

## Caveats

- COORDINATOR CAVEAT, UNRESOLVED: the `n` column is FLAT at 1264/512 all the
  way through round 17 and only then falls (1160/512, 538/484, 92/140). That
  says no 2p game on either side ended before round 18, which is a strong
  claim and is equally the signature of ENDED GAMES BEING CARRIED FORWARD at
  their final values. If they are, the round-18-to-20 rows are padded and the
  late means are contaminated. The r10-r17 rows are at full n on both sides
  and are unaffected, so the finding that matters — the curves separate at
  round 12 and the food gap opens at round 10 — stands either way. Do not
  quote the r18+ numbers until the game-length distribution is checked.
- 2p only, on both sides (the corpus 3p/4p games are excluded from the human
  side; the bot is 2p self-play).
- Human side is the 632 completed 2p games in the 882-id guard list — the
  full 2p subset of that list (none of the 632 stopped early; 0 STOPPED).
- Bot is 256 self-play games at frozen champion gen 121100; the human
  baseline is the full corpus subset, so the sample sizes differ (1264 vs
  512 player-rounds). Both are large enough that the late-game gap is far
  outside sampling noise.
- Population is read as board tokens (18 + granted - bank); it is the
  quantity that drives food cost/consumption, so it is the right measure
  for a food-gating analysis. Bank and food are reported alongside because
  they isolate the mechanism.
- Diagnosis only: no weight change, no bots/ edits, no new .rs. The two
  instrumented files were reverted and `cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D
  warnings` passes on the reverted tree.
