## Verdict

The forced-food-build variant loses 90.7% of the time against the same weights with no forcing. Forcing a legal food build/upgrade at every r3–r9 own-turn decision is a net loss: it costs 94.9 culture on average, buys +4.1 food at end of r12, but costs −3.1 population. The food the forced side accumulates does not convert into culture; the opportunity cost of the civil actions spent on food builds outweighs the food gained.

The CI is entirely below 50% — this is not a wash. The forced side does not clear the null.

## Method

`openerprobe --arena-food`: two independent 2p replays per seed, both seats play the same weights.
- **Forced side:** seat 0 takes a legal r3–r9 food build/upgrade (`is_food_build_move`) at every own-turn decision; bot's own picks everywhere else.
- **Champion side:** no seat forces; both seats play the bot's own picks.
- Both sides track seat 0. Winner = higher final `culture` after `game_over`.
- End-of-r12 snapshot: `last_end_of_turn_food` after the tracked seat's last r12 end-of-turn credit/consumption. Population = `yellow_bank + workers_free + yellow_granted` (supply + board + out-of-bank grants).

## Caveats

- The forcing rule is the same lever used in the food-upstream job (`live_counterfactual`): take a legal food build/upgrade at every r3–r9 own-turn decision. This is a blunt instrument — it does not distinguish between "this food build is a good investment" and "this food build is a waste of a civil action." The result measures the value of *always* forcing, not the value of *sometimes* forcing.
- The −3.1 population gap is the mechanism: food builds spend civil actions that the champion side spends on population increases. Fewer workers means less production, less happy, more famine risk — a compounding disadvantage that the +4.1 food at r12 cannot offset.
