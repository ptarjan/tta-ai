METHODOLOGY
-----------
Frozen champion: experiments/rust_champion_2p_frozen_2026-08-28.json
    "gen" field = 121100

At each sampled decision point (rounds 8-14, exactly one legal Farm, the
chosen move is a military Build, mil_score - farm_score > 1e-9) I:
  * read the two scores from the bot's own rank_moves()  [the measured
    mil_score / farm_score the bot actually ranks on],
  * replicate evaluate() EXACTLY, term by term, on root / farm-trial /
    mil-trial (each trial = root with the Build applied),
  * report, for every WeightKey, the per-move delta
        delta(k) = [contrib(k, mil-trial) - contrib(k, root)]
                  - [contrib(k, farm-trial) - contrib(k, root)]
    so that  sum_k delta(k)  ==  mil_score - farm_score  (the HARD
    REQUIREMENT).

Sample: 200 games (seeds 1..200), 2p, both seats, rounds 8-14.
649 decision points matched the sampling gate.

Reconciliation (the HARD REQUIREMENT)
-------------------------------------
  per-term sum_k delta(k)  reproduces  mil_score - farm_score
  to < 1e-9 at EVERY sampled point.

  Points checked:  649 (seeds 1..200, both seats, rounds 8-14).
  Max |gap_sum| over all 649 points = 6.47e-13   (requirement < 1e-9)  PASS
  (gap_sum = per-term sum - measured(mil-farm), computed at full f64
  precision inside the binary and printed in the gapsum= field of each
  sample header).

  NOTE ON A BUG FIXED DURING THIS JOB: the first version of the dump
  hard-coded the else-arm of the StrengthRel block (the branch taken when
  combat_unreachable is FALSE). At rounds 8-14, combat_unreachable can be
  either TRUE or FALSE depending on whether any active player currently
  holds a military card in hand (eval.rs:285-292). When it is TRUE, the
  gated arm applies, which uses a different formula:
      gated:  total += early * early_full * v + late * late_full * v
              where early_full = w[StrengthRel.early()],
                    late_full  = w[StrengthRel] + w[StrengthRel.late()]
      else:   total += w[StrengthRel] * v
                 + early * w[StrengthRel.early()] * v
                 + late  * w[StrengthRel.late()]  * v
  The hard-coded else-arm produced gaps up to -8.99e-2 in 26 of 649
  samples — all in positions where nobody had drawn a military card yet
  (combat_unreachable = true). After branching on combat_unreachable per
  trial-state (eval.rs:285), all 649 samples reconcile to < 6.5e-13.
  The dump (rust/src/bin/openerprobe.rs, fn dump_farm_mil) was the ONLY
  code touched and has been REVERTED; no .rs is left modified.

THE TWO DOMINANT TERMS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. strength (w = +10.64, POSITIVE)
   The military Build adds one unit, raising the player's strength by 1.
   The Farm Build adds a farm (a worker-producing building), leaving
   strength unchanged. Delta = +1 in the mil-trial, 0 in the farm-trial.
   Contribution: +10.64 * 1 = +10.64 per Warriors build (the most common
   mil card, 88.6% of samples). Higher-level units (Cavalrymen +2,
   Riflemen +4, Cannon +6) give proportionally larger deltas, up to
   +63.84 for a Cannon.
   This term is present at 99.8% of samples (the 0.2% absence is when
   the mil card is the same level as the player's existing best unit and
   the strength feature does not change).

2. strength_rel (w = -0.081 base, but the phase-blended coefficient is
   what matters)
   strength_rel measures the relative strength gap vs the rival. The
   military Build narrows this gap (the player's strength rises), so the
   strength_rel feature DECREASES (becomes less negative or more positive).
   With the champion's phase-blended weights:
     - combat_unreachable = FALSE (else arm):
         effective coeff = base + (1-L)*early + L*late
                          = -0.081 + (1-L)*(-15.765) + L*(-0.187)
         At L=0.5:  -0.081 - 7.883 - 0.093 = -8.06 (NEGATIVE)
         A DECREASE in strength_rel (good for the player) against a
         NEGATIVE weight is a PENALTY. Delta: -8.06 * (-1) = +8.06.
     - combat_unreachable = TRUE (gated arm):
         effective coeff = (1-L)*early_full + L*late_full
         where early_full = w[strength_rel_early] = -15.765,
               late_full  = w[strength_rel] + w[strength_rel_late]
                           = -0.081 + (-0.187) = -0.268
         At L=0.5:  -7.883 - 0.134 = -8.02 (NEGATIVE, same sign)
   In both branches the effective coefficient is strongly NEGATIVE
   (dominated by strength_rel_early = -15.77), so the military Build's
   narrowing of the strength gap is penalised. Mean contribution: -9.77.

   The strength and strength_rel terms are partially offsetting:
   +15.74 (strength) - 9.77 (strength_rel) = +5.97 net.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE SECONDARY TERMS (collectively ~2.0 of the mean gap)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

3. worker_gap (w = -2.56, NEGATIVE; 33.4% of samples)
   The Farm Build adds a worker-producing building, which changes the
   worker_gap feature. The mil Build does not. The negative weight means
   a change in worker_gap that increases the gap is penalised. Mean
   contribution: +0.85 (military-favouring).

4. prod_workers (w = +0.84, POSITIVE; 99.8%)
   The Farm Build adds a prod worker; the mil Build does not. The mil-trial
   has 0 prod workers vs root, the farm-trial has +1. Delta = -1 * 0.84
   = -0.84 (military-penalising, but small).

5. unit_workers (w = -0.60, NEGATIVE; 99.8%)
   The mil Build adds a unit that requires a worker to operate. The
   unit_workers feature increases by 1 in the mil-trial. Against the
   negative weight, this is a penalty: -0.60. (The Farm Build does not
   add a unit, so no unit_workers change.)

6. civil_action_surplus / civil_action_gap (w = +1.35 / -0.96)
   The two builds have different CA costs. The mil Build (Warriors: 2 CA)
   vs the Farm Build (Agriculture: 1 CA) leaves different CA remainders.
   These terms partially offset each other. Mean: +0.57 + 0.55 = +1.12.

7. ca_left (w = -0.40, NEGATIVE; 99.8%)
   The mil Build spends more CA, leaving fewer CA. The negative weight
   penalises the lower ca_left in the mil-trial: -0.40.

8. row_playable_count (w = +0.19; 53%)
   The Farm Build can affect the row's playable card count (if the farm
   card was on the row). Mean: +0.30.

9. strength_deficit (w = -0.36, NEGATIVE; 51.5%)
   The mil Build narrows the strength deficit. Against the negative
   weight, this is a gain: +0.26.

10. food_rate (w = -0.20, NEGATIVE; 99.8%)
    The Farm Build adds food production, raising food_rate by 1. Against
    the NEGATIVE weight, this is a penalty for the Farm: +0.20 to the
    mil-farm margin. (Same mechanism as in the round-2 Farm-vs-Mine case,
    where food_rate's negative weight penalised the Farm build.)

THE RARE-TRIGGER TERMS

11. attack_target_weakness (w = +3.62, POSITIVE; 2.9% of samples)
    Fires when the player's strength change makes a rival a viable attack
    target. Mean when present: +6.23 (large, up to +18.08). This is the

    strength/strength_rel, but it only fires when the strength delta

12. tactic_gain / tactic_short (w = +0.44 / -0.003; 27% / 83%)

13. wonder_potential / wonder_promise (w = +1.66 / +0.55; 4% each)
    Eval-only, read on the post-move state. Fire when a wonder is in
    play at the seat. Small mean contribution but can reach +3.0.

The mean mil-farm gap grows from +3.16 at round 8 to +15.15 at round 14.
The horizon rate multiplier (hz) grows from 0.88 to 1.12 over the same
range, amplifying the RATE_KEYS (food_rate, resource_rate) contributions.

weights (strength_rel, workers, tech_levels, hand_value) toward their
late-game values.

The dominant driver of the round-dependence is the strength term: higher
rounds have more existing units, so the marginal value of one more unit
is larger (the strength feature is additive, but the game context makes
the relative gain more significant in the later rounds where the rival
also has more strength).

THE DIAGNOSIS (why Military 821 : Farm 0 in rounds 8-14)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. The margin is ALWAYS in Military's favour and NEVER below +0.80.
   Across all 649 sampled points the measured mil-farm margin ranged
   from +0.80 to +37.42 and was positive at 100% of them. Farm's best
   rank at these points is therefore never 1.

2. The margin is driven by TWO partially-offsetting strength terms:
   a) strength (w = +10.64): the mil Build adds a unit, raising
      absolute strength. This is the LARGEST single positive term
      (mean +15.74, up to +63.84 for high-level units).
   b) strength_rel (effective w ≈ -8 to -16): the mil Build narrows the
      relative strength gap, but the champion's strength_rel weights are
      strongly NEGATIVE (dominated by strength_rel_early = -15.77), so
      narrowing the gap is penalised. Mean contribution: -9.77.
   Net: +15.74 - 9.77 = +5.97 (75% of the mean +7.97 gap).

3. The secondary terms (worker_gap, prod_workers, unit_workers, CA terms,
   food_rate) add ~+2.0 collectively. The food_rate term (w = -0.20,
   NEGATIVE) is the same mechanism that prices the Farm out in the
   round-2 Farm-vs-Mine case: the Farm Build's +1 food production is
   penalised by the negative food_rate weight.

4. The gap grows with round (mean +3.16 at r8 to +15.15 at r14) because
   the strength term's contribution grows as the game progresses (more
   existing units make the marginal unit more valuable in context) and
   the horizon rate multiplier (hz) amplifies the RATE_KEYS.

5. The margin is NOT driven by card quality / level (no level-sensitive
   terms are in play at rounds 8-14 for these cards — Agriculture is
   level 0, and the military cards' level effects are captured entirely
   through the strength feature). It is driven ENTIRELY by the strength
   terms plus the action-economy and rate terms.

SCOPE / LIMITS
--------------
* Sampled rounds 8-14 only, 2p, seeds 1-200, both seats. 649 of the 821
  target points were captured (the remaining ~172 are in games where the
  sampling gate did not fire, e.g. multiple legal Farms or a non-military
  top choice).
* The per-term split replicates evaluate() EXACTLY (max residual 6.5e-13),
  so the term attribution is exact, not approximate.
* This is a diagnosis. No weight was changed and none is proposed. If the
  goal is to ever build a Farm mid-game, the levers this data points at
  are: (a) the strength weight (+10.64) which makes every military Build
  a +10.64 bump, (b) the food_rate sign (negative, -0.20) which penalises
  the Farm's food production, and (c) the strength_rel_early weight
  (-15.77) which penalises the relative-strength narrowing. But that is a
  tuning decision for the coordinator, out of scope here.

COORDINATOR NOTES (verified before commit)
=============================================================================

1. SIGN SLIP IN THE DERIVATION, table is right. Line ~142 derives the
   strength_rel contribution as "-8.06 * (-1) = +8.06", i.e. POSITIVE, then
   the conclusion at ~151 and the summary table both give -9.77, NEGATIVE.
   The table is correct and the derivation line is the error: if strength_rel
   contributed +9.77 alongside strength's +15.74 the pair would total +25.5,
   which cannot sit inside a mean margin of +7.97. The two terms partially
   CANCEL (+15.74 - 9.77 = +5.97). Whichever way the strength_rel feature
   moves on a military build, the eval takes ~10 points back off it.

2. THE DECISION-GRADE READING: food is NOT what loses the farm mid-game.
   food_rate contributes +0.20 of a mean +7.97 margin — 2.5% of it, and less
   than worker_gap, prod_workers, unit_workers or either civil-action term.
   The minimum margin over 649 samples is +0.80, so even zeroing food_rate
   entirely flips no decision here. A food-pricing fix alone cannot change
   mid-game farm behaviour; the farm loses to the warrior on STRENGTH
   (w = +10.64 per unit, +63.84 for a Cannon), not on food.

3. OPEN, NOT PROPOSED: two strength keys with large opposite-signed weights
   (strength +10.64 against an effective strength_rel of about -8) that
   cancel to a third of their size is the classic redundancy shape, and it
   is the same pair already flagged as the RivalStrength/StrengthDeficit
   question. Nothing here proposes a sign_intent or a reweight — a wrong
   gate pins a key at zero forever.

4. NOT ANSWERED HERE: whether +10.64 on strength is itself too high. That
   needs a human baseline for military strength by round, the way the
   population curve gave one for population. That is the next measurement.
