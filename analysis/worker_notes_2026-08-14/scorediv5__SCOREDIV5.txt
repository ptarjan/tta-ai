LEAD 1 -- Impact of Happiness / Homer leader-tuck (Group A, 4 games): RULED
OUT this session with a fresh, testable experiment
================================================================================
All 4 Group-A Happiness games share one pattern, confirmed by grepping every
game's own journal: the player with the divergent seat elected HOMER, then
almost immediately replaced him with a different leader ("X elects Homer"
... "X elects <other leader> Homer dies"):
  7522277 Purple: elects Homer (line45), Homer dies electing Genghis Khan (85)
  7522446 Purple: elects Homer (line33), Homer dies electing ... (later)
  7523253 Green:  elects Homer (line37), Homer dies electing Da Vinci (137)
  7522397 Orange: elects Homer (line78), Homer dies electing Da Vinci (80)

Homer's card text (bga_throughtheages_material.inc.php, verified against
this codebase's own card source): "When you replace Homer, you may slide
this card under ONE OF YOUR COMPLETED WONDERS to give it an extra 1 happy
face." `apply.rs::h_play_leader` (~line 878-883) tucks Homer under
`completed_wonders.as_slice()[0]` UNCONDITIONALLY -- i.e. whichever wonder
was completed FIRST. This is a real player CHOICE the BGO journal text never
records ("Homer dies", no wonder named) -- a prior session (scorefinal, see
`analysis/worker_notes_2026-08-14/scorefinal__SCOREFINAL.txt`) hand-traced
7522277 specifically and found the TRUE choice there was the wonder
completed SECOND (St. Peter's Basilica), not first (Library of Alexandria),
and called this a hidden-information limitation, out of scope.

THIS SESSION TESTED WHETHER "tuck under the MOST RECENTLY completed wonder"
is a safe, general fix (i.e. whether BGO's own UI defaults to the newest
wonder, making this a real deterministic rule rather than arbitrary choice):
  Edit: `rust/src/apply.rs` line ~882, `completed_wonders.as_slice()[0]` ->
  `wonders[wonders.len() - 1]`.

  REVERTED (`git checkout -- rust/src/apply.rs`) immediately after

journal's 12, the true sum must be 6 -- i.e. exactly Code of Laws's own

exhaustively grepped) that Purple ALSO discovered Architecture (Construction
icon, level 2) at line 243 -- but Architecture is CORRECTLY ABSENT from our
engine's own reconstructed tech list, because `apply.rs::develop_special`

RULES_SPEC.md line 86, RB p.12/CoL p.5) correctly removed it when Purple
later discovered Engineering (also Construction icon, higher level). This
formula and its icon-replacement machinery are independently proven correct
by this exact removal.

The mystery: Code of Laws is Law icon, and Purple never discovered any other
Law-icon SpecialTech (Justice System, Civil Service) -- exhaustively
confirmed absent from Purple's full discover list. §7.6's icon-replacement

same-icon-replacement rule described in RULES_SPEC -- no bug found there.
