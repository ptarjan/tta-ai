====================================================================
Worker: cardblame, 2026-08-14. Clone: /private/tmp/cardblame (deleted at
end per instructions), branched from c772970.

WHAT WAS BUILT
--------------
rust/src/bin/cardblame.rs, a new permanent analysis binary, plus two small
additive extensions to rust/src/replay_common.rs that it depends on:

  - GameResult::cards_in_play: Vec<&'static str> -- the SET of card ids in
    play (all players' tableaus, plus leaders and tactics in force),
    snapshotted from the replayer's own final GameState at the point a
    game's replay stopped OR completed. Computed unconditionally (helper
    `game_cards_in_play`), so it is available for both a clean completion
    and a mid-game Mismatch stop.

  - GameResult::corruption_checks: Vec<CorruptionCheck> -- one entry per
    journal "End turn" line: whether this reconstruction's own
    `economy::corruption(economy::blue_available(..))` (recomputed
    independently, NOT by letting economy::end_of_turn run and reading a
    mutated field back -- see Replayer::record_corruption_check's doc for
    why that is safe) agrees with whether BGO's own journal line for that
    turn carries a "CORRUPTION!" marker. Each entry also carries the
    ACTING PLAYER's own broader card set (tableau + government + leader +
    wonder + tactic + territories won at colonization) via the
    `player_cards_in_play` helper -- broader than `cards_in_play` on
    purpose, because the standing hypothesis under test is a TERRITORY
    card, which lives in PlayerState::colonies, not techs.

Neither field changes anything any existing binary reads or computes --
replaystats' own output is byte-identical before/after (see BAR below).

cardblame itself replays the same corpus the same way as replaystats
(tta::replay_common::replay_game, same sampling convention) and does THREE
enrichment cuts:

  1. Completion-failure enrichment: for every card, of the games it was in
     play for, what share failed vs the corpus base failure rate.
  2. Score-mismatch enrichment, same cut restricted to games that DID
     complete (a different failure mode -- no engine stop to blame, so a
     scoring-formula bug is the likelier culprit).
  3. Corruption cross-check enrichment, the owner's specific sub-question:
     run the same enrichment math over the (engine-charges vs
     journal-charges) agreement/disagreement population instead of over
     game completion.

STATISTICS DISCIPLINE, AS IMPLEMENTED
--------------------------------------
- enrichment = card's own rate / corpus base rate. 1.0 = no signal.
  Printed alongside the raw games/failures count and the rate itself, not
  instead of them.
- MIN_EXPOSURE = 20 games/checkpoints gates the ranked table. Cards below
  it are NOT dropped -- they print in a separate "low exposure" section,
  sorted by exposure, explicitly labelled as noise.
- Confound visibility: every ranked row in cuts 1/2 prints round_with
  (mean stop-round for games WITH the card) and round_without (mean
  stop-round for games WITHOUT it). This is the tool ACTUALLY catching the
  confound in practice, see finding #1 below -- it is not a hypothetical
  caveat, it is what separated real candidates from an artifact.
- What this does NOT control for (stated in the binary's own module doc):
  no round-matched control group. A big round_with/round_without gap
  means "treat this enrichment as partly a game-length proxy"; it cannot
  by itself distinguish that from a genuinely broken late-game card. The
  BGA-source diff is what actually discriminates the two, and that's what
  I did for the top candidates (see below).
- Cut 3's "games" column is CHECKPOINTS, not games (one per "End turn"
  line) -- documented in the printed header so a reader doesn't misread
  40790 as a games-sampled figure.

FINDING #1 (the tool doing its job): single-slot cards structurally cannot
be read from a "cards in play at the end" snapshot without being
confounded, and the round_with/without columns show it immediately.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every one of the top-10 completion-failure entries (enrichment exactly
3.12x = 1/base_rate, i.e. literally EVERY game with that card in play
failed) is a NAMED LEADER: Robespierre, Newton, Joan of Arc, Shakespeare,
Napoleon, Hammurabi, da Vinci, James Cook, Genghis Khan, Michelangelo (plus
8 more below the exposure cutoff, also all leaders, also all 100%).

This is NOT evidence any of these leaders are broken. `PlayerState::leader`
is a SINGLE slot -- a player who takes an early leader typically replaces
it with a better one later. In a COMPLETED (full-length) game, whichever
leader is present at the final snapshot is definitionally the LAST one
taken -- an early/mid leader can only ever show up in `cards_in_play` for a
game that never reached the point of being superseded, which for a
completed game essentially never happens (confirmed directly: 0 of 687
completed games have Napoleon, Newton, etc. in their final leader slot; the
completed-game leader list is dominated by Age III/IV leaders -- Churchill,
Einstein, Gandhi, Bill Gates, Chaplin, Sid Meier). The round_with column
makes this legible without needing to read code: every one of these
"broken" leaders has round_with 5-14 against round_without ~17.5-17.7 --
i.e. they are systematically present only in SHORT/stopped games, which is
exactly the single-slot-supersession signature, not a card-correctness
signature.

The exact same mechanism, weaker, explains the next tier down: Age I
TACTICS (Phalanx 1.88x/98 games, Medieval Army 1.79x/187, Legion 1.69x/164,
Heavy Cavalry 1.59x/57) -- `PlayerState::tactic` is also a single slot,
upgraded over the game. round_with for these (14.7-15.5) vs round_without
(~17.4-17.7) shows the same, smaller version of the same artifact.

TAKEAWAY FOR FUTURE READERS OF THIS TOOL: any row where round_with is
noticeably below round_without AND the card occupies a known single/
replaceable slot (leader, tactic, government, wonder-under-construction) is
this artifact, not a lead, UNLESS the BGA-source diff also turns up a
transcription error (checked below -- it did not, for any of these).

SUB-QUESTION: does a CARD explain the corruption/journal disagreement?
------------------------------------------------------------------------
357/40790 "End turn" checkpoints disagree on whether corruption was
charged (0.9% base rate). Full population (agreements AND disagreements)
was dumped and cut the same way, keyed on the ACTING PLAYER's own broader
card set (tableau + government + leader + wonder + tactic + territories),
since the standing hypothesis is specifically a TERRITORY card
(colonization prize, lives in PlayerState::colonies, not techs).

THE HYPOTHESIS: cards that permanently adjust blue-token capacity by -1
(the territory printed "+3 yellow tokens & -1 blue token"). Found it:
"Vast Territory (I)" and "Vast Territory (II)" both carry blue_tokens: -1
in card_table.rs, confirmed against BGA source exactly
(bonus_per "+3 yellow tokens & -1 blue token" / "+4 yellow tokens & -1 blue
token", tokendelta blue=>-1 -- both ages MATCH card_table.rs exactly, no
transcription error there either).

RESULT OF THE CONTROL-GROUP TEST: the hypothesis does NOT hold up as THE
explanation.
  - Vast Territory (II) (-1 blue): 3.14x, 838 checkpoints, 23 disagreements
  - Vast Territory (I)  (-1 blue): 1.89x, 2787 checkpoints, 46 disagreements
  - Historic Territory (II) (NO blue effect at all, +2 happy/+11 culture
    only): 2.75x, 664 checkpoints, 16 disagreements -- nearly as enriched
    as Vast Territory (II), MORE enriched than Vast Territory (I), despite
    touching zero blue tokens.
  - Historic Territory (I) (also no blue effect): only 1.10x -- the SAME
    card design one age tier down reads as noise, while its own age-II
    sibling reads at 2.75x. That inconsistency within one card FAMILY that
    has no blue-token mechanic at all is itself evidence the ranking here
    is dominated by something else (age/round position, general economy
    complexity) rather than a specific token mechanic.
  - Developed Territory (+2 blue, the OPPOSITE direction from the
    hypothesis) is INCONSISTENT too: age I reads 1.57x, age II reads
    0.45x (UNDER base rate).
  - The single highest-enrichment cards in this whole cut have nothing to
    do with blue tokens at all: Bill Gates (6.02x, a leader whose special
    is a per-turn resource/culture formula unrelated to corruption),
    Transcontinental Railroad (4.98x), Shock Troops (4.71x, a military
    tactic), First Space Flight (2.97x), Internet (2.78x), Computers
    (2.67x) -- all rank above or comparable to Vast Territory.

Pooled for completeness: the two -1-blue territories combined = 3625
checkpoints / 69 disagreements = 1.90% (enrichment ~2.17x); the other four
territory variants (0 or +2 blue) combined = 6998 checkpoints / 83
disagreements = 1.19% (enrichment ~1.36x). There IS a small directional
gap in the pooled numbers, but it is far smaller than, and dominated by,
unrelated top-ranked cards, and it does not survive the same-family
control comparison above (Historic Territory (II), zero blue effect,
matches or beats Vast Territory's own enrichment).

CONCLUSION: no single card explains the corruption/journal disagreement
population. The -1-blue-token territory hypothesis is NOT dead outright
(there's a small pooled directional signal), but it is clearly NOT the
dominant or sole cause -- the ranking is dominated by cards spanning every
mechanism (leaders, tactics, urban buildings, territories in both
directions), which is the signature of a shared, general confound (most
likely: later-round/more-complex-economy turns are simply more likely to
disagree, for reasons unrelated to any one card) rather than a specific
card bug. NOT fixing anything here per the task's own instruction --
reporting the control-group result as requested.

METHODOLOGY NOTES / WHAT THIS DOES NOT COVER
----------------------------------------------
- cards_in_play (cuts 1/2) is a SNAPSHOT at the stop/end point, not
  "ever in play this game" -- a card built and later antiquated away
  before the game ended will NOT show up, even though it may have caused
  the very failure being counted. This under-counts exposure for any card
  whose bug manifests, then the card leaves play before the game stops.
  Not fixed this pass; flagged for whoever extends this tool next (an
  "ever seen" variant would need a per-line rather than end-of-game hook).
- The single-slot artifact (finding #1) applies to leader/tactic/
  government/wonder-under-construction cards in cuts 1 and 2 alike. Read
  round_with/round_without before trusting a high-enrichment SLOT card.
- Cut 3's checkpoint-level "games" column deliberately does NOT get a
  round-reached confound column (a per-checkpoint binary doesn't have a
  single meaningful "round reached" the way a per-game outcome does) --
  documented directly in the tool's own printed header, not just here.
