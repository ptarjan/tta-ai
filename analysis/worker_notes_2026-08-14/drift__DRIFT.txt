DriftDivergence>` and a `maybe_record_drift(quantity, lineno, actor,

Threaded into:
  - the pre-existing, always-on culture oracle (`record_culture_check`)
  - the pre-existing, always-on discard-phase hand-size oracle
    (`check_discard_phase_oracle` -- quantity tag "hand_military_excess")
  - the pre-existing but previously PRINT-ONLY science/resources debug
    checks at the "End turn" checkpoint (upgraded to structured recording;

    check already had but science did not -- see "false start" below)
`GameResult::drift_first` carries it out of the replayer. `replaystats.rs`
collects one `DriftRecord` (id, quantity, lineno, round, age, delta,

FALSE START, CAUGHT BEFORE REPORTING (worth recording, since a fixer will
hit the same trap): the pre-existing `REPLAY_DEBUG` science check at the
"End turn" checkpoint never had the `discard_blocked` guard the neighbouring
`resources` check has (both read the SAME class of quantity at the SAME
point, one is guarded, one is not -- an inconsistency that was silently
invisible while both were merely eprintln'd). Naively promoting it to
structured recording made SCIENCE look like the dominant divergence in
990/1011 games, always at Age I round ~4, delta -1 or -2 -- which is exactly
what a checkpoint fired BEFORE a still-open `DiscardMilitary` pending
resolves looks like (production hasn't run yet, so our own science reads
short by whatever this turn's production would have added; the journal's
own "(now M)" already reflects the POST-production total on the same line).
Hand-traced the very first example (game 7523818, line 84 = "Orange End
turn ... 2 science (now 6)"; line 85, the very next line, is "Orange
discards 1 card" -- confirming the discard was still pending when the
checkpoint fired) and confirmed it structurally: game 7523818's own
Discard-Phase announcement precedes its EndTurn line ("Discard Phase 1
military card must be discarded" at line 83, before the EndTurn at 84).
Added the same `discard_blocked` guard to the science check (matching
`resources`'s pre-existing one) before trusting ANY of these numbers. This
is the single most important caution for whoever re-runs this: an
"always-on except for one inconsistently-applied guard" checkpoint will
silently manufacture a 98%-prevalence false signal. All numbers below are
POST-fix.

===========================================================================
1. HOW MANY GAMES DIVERGE AT ALL
===========================================================================
803/1011 games (79.4%) diverge on at least one asserted quantity (culture,
science, resources, or discard-phase military-hand excess) at some point.
558 of those 803 (69.5%) still reach `completed` anyway -- divergence is
common enough, and recoverable enough, that it is NOT itself a strong
predictor of a stopped replay (see section 4).

hand_military_excess dominates by a wide margin (77.7% of all first

EXCLUSION LIST (quantities the task named that are NOT included above, and
why): food, happiness, worker/population counts, civil-action counts,
military-action counts, and military strength were checked against the
corpus's own journal text (`grep`-swept every `*.tsv`) and EXCLUDED as not
independently comparable:
  - food/happiness/population/civil-actions: the journal states these only
    as event-triggered DELTAS ("<Color> loses N population", "<Color>
    scores N culture per happy face", "N civil actions on his next turn",
    etc.), never as a cross-validated running TOTAL the way science,
    resources, and culture get a "(now M)" clause on every single "End
    turn" line (verified: `grep -oE "[a-zA-Z]+ \(now [0-9-]+\)"` across the
    whole corpus returns ONLY "science (now N)", "resources (now N)",
    "culture (now N)" -- 46,990 each, nothing else). Comparing a delta
    against our own state would just be re-deriving our own number, not
    checking it against an independent truth.
  - military strength: DOES appear as an absolute ("<Color> strength: N")
    on combat-resolution lines, which in principle IS comparable -- but
    only during active wars, an order of magnitude fewer checkpoints than
    the always-present per-turn "(now M)" triad, and hooking it needed
    locating the specific combat-strength computation in combat.rs, which
    the time budget did not allow. Flagged here as the one deferred,
    plausibly-easy addition for a future pass, not silently dropped.
  - hand size, specifically: the pre-existing "discard-phase hand-size
    oracle" this pass reuses does NOT compare raw hand size directly; it
    compares the derived EXCESS (`max(0, hand_len - limit)`) against the
    journal's own cross-validated "Discard Phase N ... must be discarded"
    announcement (itself cross-checked against the sum of that round's
    "<Color> discards N card(s)" resolutions -- see
    `prescan_discard_phase_oracle`). Reported here as "hand_military_excess"
    rather than a literal "hand size" to be precise about what is actually
    being asserted and compared.

===========================================================================
4. CORRELATION WITH LATER COMPLETION (against /tmp/rd_ids.txt, 644 ids)
===========================================================================
rate of completing AMONG games that diverge:  558/803 = 69.5%
rate of completing OVERALL (all 1011 sampled): 644/1011 = 63.7%

Plainly: divergence is barely correlated with failing to complete -- if
anything, diverging games complete SLIGHTLY more often than the corpus
average (69.5% vs 63.7%), the opposite of what "drift causes the eventual
stop" would predict. Read this as: divergence is so common (79.4% of the
whole corpus) that the diverging set is nearly the whole corpus, so its
completion rate can't help but track close to the overall rate. This is
itself informative -- it means hand_military_excess drift, in particular,
is NOT the direct proximate cause of most `StuckPending`/`IllegalMove`
stops (the stop-reason histogram's own top bucket, "StuckPending: decider #
!= expected actor #", 96 games, is a DIFFERENT, disjoint symptom from this
one). Whatever downstream damage a 1-2-card hand miscount does, it is
subtle enough to coexist with a game finishing cleanly two-thirds of the
time.

At this checkpoint: journal_excess = 2 (BGO's own cross-validated "Discard
Phase 2 military cards must be discarded" / sum of that round's "Purple
discards 1 card" x2 resolutions, one BEFORE this EndTurn line at journal
line 446, one AFTER it at line 448 -- BGO logs a discard-blocked EndTurn's
second discard resolution AFTER printing the turn's own scores line, same
established pattern `PendingCultureCheck`'s own doc documents for culture).
This binary computes reconstructed_excess = 3, from hand_military_len = 6,
limit = 3.

Ruled OUT a limit-computation bug before concluding it's a count bug:
  - `effects.rs:1919` -- Theocracy (Purple's government since round 8,
    "Purple revolutions Change government to Theocracy") is table-defined
    as `("Theocracy", 4, 3, 3)` i.e. 3 military actions -- confirmed
    correct, not a stale-government read.
  - `card_table.rs` -- grepped every card's `military_hand_limit` field
    corpus-wide; the ONLY nonzero one in the whole card table is Library of
    Alexandria (`military_hand_limit: 1`), which Purple never built this
    game (Purple's wonders were Great Wall, Universitas Carolina, Ocean
    Liner). So `limit = 3 + 0 = 3` is very likely CORRECT, and the journal's
    own implied hand_len (limit + journal_excess = 3 + 2 = 5) is the true
    number -- our reconstruction's hand_len = 6 has one phantom card.

Narrowed but NOT pinned with certainty (ran out of budget before a final
single-line answer): walked every Purple action between round 13's own
discard-phase checkpoint (which DID match -- journal_excess and
reconstructed_excess agreed there) and round 14's line-447 divergence:
draws 3 (round-13-end), "Purple plays event ... Emigration" (PrepareEvent,
-1), "Purple elects Isaac Newton" (PlayLeader, does not touch

Cavalrymen in hand" (+1), "Purple discards 1 card" (line 446, -1). Net

exactly `limit`=3 up to 3+3=6 -- EXACTLY this binary's own reconstructed
value, with no arithmetic slip in between. That means every action in this
window nets out consistently with our own bookkeeping; the phantom card was
ALREADY present, uncounted as excess, AT OR BEFORE round 13's own
checkpoint -- round 13 "agreeing" was a coincidence of the hand happening to
land exactly at the limit that round, not proof the count was actually
right. The strongest remaining suspect going further back is Purple's round
10 colonization ("Purple colonizes a Wealthy Territory Sacrificed Units:; 1

parsed by `parse_sacrifice_clauses` (rust/src/replay_common.rs:4986-5013)
into `SacrificeClause::Bonus` entries via `colonization_bonus_card` -- the
only multi-bonus-card-consuming mechanism in Purple's whole game history
before round 13, and worth checking FIRST by whoever picks this up: does
sacrificing TWO DIFFERENT-VALUE Colonization bonus cards ("+1" and "+2") in

`apply.rs`'s own colonize handler, or does the queue/consumption logic
