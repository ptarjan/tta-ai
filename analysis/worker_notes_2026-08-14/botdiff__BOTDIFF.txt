RESULTS:
  old: 22/30 games reached COMPLETE, 8 STOPPED early on an illegal-move
       replay wall. 7666 decision points total. 1847/7666 = 24.09% top-1
       agreement with the human.
  new: 24/30 games COMPLETE, 6 STOPPED. 7729 decision points total (+63).
       1876/7729 = 24.27% top-1 agreement.

Two games flipped from STOPPED to COMPLETE outright:
  7523818: 235 -> 290 decision points (STOPPED -> COMPLETE), 57/235 -> 80/290
  7523809: 278 -> 286 decision points (STOPPED -> COMPLETE), 78/278 -> 79/286
Today's rules fixes let the replayer walk further into (or all the way
through) these two games' real move sequences than it could yesterday --
this is a real, positive replay-correctness gain, visible even in a small
sample; it means the bot is now asked to judge MORE real human positions
than before, not just the same ones differently.

Three already-complete games' totals also shifted (decision COUNT
unchanged, agreed count changed):
  7522612: 53/266 -> 55/266   7522645: 50/261 -> 51/261   7522623: 65/286 -> 67/286

Per-decision diff on those three (diffed row-for-row on game 7522612, the
clearest case): from round 3 onward, EVERY `political_action` decision
gained exactly +1 to its `legal_count` column (e.g. 2->3, 3->4, 5->6, 2->4->5,
3->4->5) with the human's own rank in the list usually UNCHANGED (the new
option just slots in below the human's move) -- i.e. today's fixes added
one previously-missing legal political-action option, consistently, from
round 3 on, in this game. That is a real legality change, not a scoring
change. Separately, starting mid-game (round 13, game 7522612) the
`bot_top_move`/scores diverge more substantially at MATCHING linenos
(different top-ranked move, different score) -- once one decision's legal
list differs, the whole downstream ranking trajectory can drift even where
the human's real recorded move (which the replay always forces regardless
of the bot's opinion) stays the anchor.

VERDICT (1b): the bot's own move RANKINGS did change under today's fixes --
not cosmetically. Aggregate top-1 human-agreement moved only marginally
(+0.19pp on this sample), which per this project's own established finding
(agreement is anti-correlated with strength, never a target) says nothing
about whether the bot got BETTER -- only that its opinions measurably moved,
and that more of the human corpus is now reachable at all for this kind of
diagnostic (2 fewer STOPPED-early replays out of 30, extrapolate cautiously
to the full 692).
