THE ASSIGNED LEAD ("DeclareWar's card resolution has no age-sibling
correction") -- CHECKED AND RULED OUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Verified directly against `rust/src/card_table.rs`: every War card
("War over Territory", "War over Technology", "War over Culture") has its
OWN unique `name`/`base_name` -- there is exactly ONE `Card` table entry per
War card, at a single fixed age (II, II, III respectively). Unlike
Aggression families (Raid/Enslave/Plunder/...), which share one `base_name`
across 2-3 age-tiered entries, `corpus::build_card_index`'s bare-name
lookup for a War card has nothing to disambiguate -- it resolves to the
correct, unique `CardId` on the first try, every time. `resolve_aggression_
age`-style age-sibling correction would be a no-op if bolted onto
`ActionClass::DeclareWar`/`ProposePact`; there is no bug to fix there. This
was the task's own explicit hypothesis to check first, and it does not
survive contact with the card table. I did not add anything to
`ActionClass::DeclareWar`.

ROOT CAUSE, ACTUALLY FOUND (confirmed by hand-tracing both games with
REPLAY_DEBUG_ALL=1 against a pristine, unpatched 628c0dd clone at
/private/tmp/base0815, and against `discard_solver.rs`'s own documented
design)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Both games share the same shape: a Raid play whose card the fix now
CORRECTLY resolves to the actually-played age-sibling (matching the
journal's own "uses N military action" clause, RULES_SPEC.md 5.5's Raid
I/II/III MA-cost table) -- immediately followed, one to three rounds later,
by a `DeclareWar` play, landing on a `discard_phase_oracle` checkpoint
(BGO's own literal "Discard Phase N cards must be discarded" text, ground
truth, not a derived guess) that this binary's own reconstruction now
computes as 0 excess when the true game shows 2.

7522290 (Purple, round 13): before the fix, Purple's simulated hand held
THREE ambiguous "Aggression: Raid" family cards simultaneously -- two
"Aggression: Raid (I)" and one "Aggression: Raid (II)" -- all three
independently, genuinely drawn by this binary's own SIMULATED (seeded-RNG)
deck-draw order, well before the Raid was ever played (confirmed identical,
byte-for-byte, between the pristine 628c0dd trace and the patched trace up
to the play itself -- the deck/RNG stream is untouched by this fix, only
WHICH already-drawn sibling gets treated as "the played card" differs).
  - OLD code (628c0dd, always-oldest-sibling guess): the Raid play
    consumes a stray "Aggression: Raid (I)", leaving 1x RaidI + 1x RaidII
    in hand. At the Age II->III transition (RULES_SPEC.md 12.2, "cards of
    ages OLDER than the age that just ended become antiquated"), only the
    1 remaining RaidI (Age I) antiquates; RaidII (Age II) correctly
    survives. Net: hand ends up 1 card "too high" relative to the fix's own
    accounting, which happens to be enough slack to trigger a real (if
    undercounted, 1-not-2) discard choice -- enough to avoid getting stuck.
  - NEW code (this fix, cost-matched to the journal's own "2 military
    action" text): the Raid play correctly consumes the actually-played
    "Aggression: Raid (II)", leaving BOTH "Aggression: Raid (I)" copies
    untouched. Both are genuinely Age I, so BOTH legitimately antiquate at
    the same Age II->III transition -- a RULES_SPEC-correct application of
    12.2, not a bug in `game::antiquate` itself (read and confirmed
    correct: `cutoff = ended as u8`, keeps `age >= cutoff`, i.e. keeps the
    just-ended age's own cards and only culls strictly older ones -- exact
    match for "War over Technology" (Age II) correctly surviving the same
    transition in both traces). The extra antiquated card is exactly the
    1-card gap between this binary's computed excess (0) and the journal's
    stated excess (2) at the very next discard checkpoint.
Full arithmetic (round-12 hand 6, +3 drawn, -2 antiquated, -1 DeclareWar
consumption = 6, matching what the trace shows) is in the working notes;
solving backwards from the journal's OWN stated excess (2) implies the
TRUE antiquation loss at that transition was ~0, i.e. real Purple most
likely never simultaneously held two live copies of "Aggression: Raid (I)"
at that point at all -- an artifact of this binary's necessarily-guessed
deck shuffle order, not a rule violation.

7522053 (Orange, round 14 Raid II -> round 16 DeclareWar) shows the SAME
surface shape (a discard-phase-oracle checkpoint the fix now under-counts
by 2) but does NOT go through antiquation at all -- Orange's whole span
from the Raid play to the stop is already inside Age III (no age
transition occurs). The round-15/16 hand arithmetic (checkpoint hand 3,
+3 drawn, -1 DeclareWar consumption = 5, matches the trace exactly) fully
explains the computed 5 with no unaccounted step -- meaning THIS game's
2-card shortfall against the journal's true excess must have already been
present in the hand size at or before the round-15 checkpoint, i.e. earlier
than the Raid play itself. This confirms the underlying issue is NOT one
single discrete mechanism (antiquation) but the general class of problem:
this binary's simulated, seeded-RNG deck-draw order is an approximation
that mostly agrees with the true game (measured: 25/26, 96.2%, of
discard-phase-oracle checkpoints match exactly across just these two
games' journals) but is not guaranteed to, and the Raid fix's correction of
WHICH sibling gets consumed changes which of several indistinguishable
(by journal text) simulated cards survives to interact with a LATER,
independently-correct rule (antiquation here; something upstream and not
further traced for 7522053) in a way that happens to erase the very
excess a real discard-phase checkpoint needed to see.

WHY THIS ISN'T FURTHER ROOT-CAUSABLE WITHIN THIS SESSION'S SCOPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every card sitting in a simulated hand that was NEVER named by the journal
(never played, never revealed) is, by this binary's own design
(`discard_solver.rs`'s module doc: "no card sitting in hand at a discard
decision ever carries a journal-verified identity of its own") a guess --
a real card drawn from a correctly-sized, correctly-aged deck pool, at a
RANDOM position this binary's seeded shuffle assigned, which cannot be
checked against the true game's own (unknown, unrecorded) shuffle except at
the sparse points the journal happens to name a card explicitly. `game::
antiquate` (game.rs ~595) and the Raid fix (`resolve_aggression_age`,
replay_common.rs ~3699) are each independently correct against their own
inputs; the 2-card gap comes from those correct inputs occasionally
disagreeing with the true game's actual (unknowable from the journal
alone) card identities. Fixing this in general would mean improving deck-
shuffle/hand-composition fidelity itself -- a materially larger, riskier
undertaking than a 90-tool-call regression chase, with no guarantee of not
trading these 2 games for others (as the Raid-exclusion experiment above
already demonstrates happens easily: 7522864 appeared the moment Raid's
identity resolution changed at all).
