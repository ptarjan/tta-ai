PRODCAP -- who actually loses production when the blue supply runs short
measured 2026-08-15 on live master d4130c1, full 1011-game replaystats sweep

WHY
    Paul, 2026-08-15: "But you can choose food or iron."  The blue tokens are ONE
    shared supply.  When it cannot cover a round, the player chooses which
    production to forgo -- and that choice spans the food/resource boundary, not
    just which cards within a kind.

    economy::end_of_turn hardcodes the order:
        3c  food bonus (gain_food)      -> food production (credit_production)
        3d  food consumption (pay_food, which RETURNS tokens to the supply)
        3e  resource bonus              -> resource production
    Every one of those four draws on the same blue_available(p) pool.  Food is
    therefore credited first unconditionally, so food can never be the thing that
    gets dropped.  That is not a player choice, it is an arbitrary tie-break.

METHOD
    Temporary env-gated probe (PRODCAP=1), reverted immediately after measuring:
      * economy.rs, both production blocks: eprintln when `got < full`, i.e. when
        the cap actually cut this kind's production.
      * bin/replaystats.rs: eprintln "PRODCAP_GAME <id>" per game, to attribute
        stderr events to a game.  replaystats is SERIAL (no rayon/par_iter), and
        Rust's stderr is unbuffered, so the interleaving is trustworthy.
    Sweep was a no-op on the numbers: still 748 completed / 721 exact.

RESULT -- the bias is entirely one-sided
    farm production starved ....  0 events
    mine production starved .... 23 events, in 22 distinct games

    So the food-first order never costs the player food.  It ONLY ever costs iron.

WHAT IS NOT YET DECIDED
    A cross-kind chooser needs a RULE, and it must be a single behaviour, not a
    policy knob (see the standing "no policy knobs, no hardcoded formulas"
    constraint).  "Match whatever the journal says" is NOT available: that is
    coercing the replayer to manufacture a completion, which is banned.
    Question is out to Paul.  Do not build a chooser before that answer lands.

DO NOT
    Do not "fix" this by weakening the blue_total - blue_used cap.  The supply is
    LIMITED and unplaceable production is LOST (Paul, 2026-08-14); the cap is
    correct.  The defect, if any, is purely WHICH production the cap eats.
    Do not re-derive production_pairs' within-kind order -- descending printed
    value is already the value-maximising choice and is settled.

FOLLOW-UP 2026-08-15: the one STRICT-DOMINANCE rule fires ZERO times
    Paul, asked what he actually does when the supply is short: "It depends. If you
    are out of yellow people to birth then only take what you can eat. If you have
    too much production and need more people then take food. If you don't care for
    more people then take only enough to eat then iron. Maybe take less this turn
    and more next turn."

    So the cross-kind split is a VALUE decision, not a rules fact -- it belongs to
    the evaluator, not to a hardcoded order.  Exactly ONE clause is strict
    dominance and needs no judgement: with an EMPTY population reserve, food beyond
    consumption can never be spent, so taking it instead of iron is dominated.

    Re-measured all 23 events with yellow_bank/food/need attached:
        yellow_bank == 0 ....  0 of 23 events
        minimum observed ....  2  (7522822, food=17 need=4)
    The dominance rule would never fire on this corpus.  DO NOT BUILD IT.

    What the numbers DO show is food hoarding while mines starve, e.g.
        7521459 food=27 need=2 | 7522786 food=26 need=3 | 7522402 food=23 need=2
        7522822 food=17 need=4 | 7522796 food=16 need=3 | 7523223 food=14 need=2
    but "that food is surplus" is a judgement about future turns -- Paul explicitly
    said "maybe take less this turn and more next turn" -- so it is a heuristic, not
    a dominance bound, and must NOT be hardcoded.

    CONCLUSION: the real fix is to make short-supply production an actual decision
    point the evaluator prices, alongside the existing move choices.  That is a
    design change, not a patch.  Until then the food-first order stands as a known,
    measured, one-sided approximation costing at most 23 events in 1011 games.
