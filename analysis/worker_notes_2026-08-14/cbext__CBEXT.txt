THE ASK, AS IT ENDED UP (steered mid-task by the coordinator)
---------------------------------------------------------------
The original brief was to widen `cardblame`'s attribution beyond
`GameResult::cards_in_play` (a snapshot: tableau + leader + tactic at the
point a game stopped/ended) to also cover event cards, played
action/military cards, wonders, governments, and colonies, plus add a
happenings cut for revolution/corruption/war/aggression/pact/colonization/
resignation/discard. Mid-task the coordinator steered this to be TOTAL and
STRUCTURAL rather than a hand-picked list: every card in the game (zero-
exposure ones shown too), happenings enumerated exhaustively off the `Move`
enum (and `ActionClass`, "whatever enum drives events/specials") with NO
wildcard match arm so a future variant fails to compile until classified,
plus a coverage summary and an explicit "not attributable" section. What
shipped is the steered version.

WHAT WAS BUILT
--------------
Two files touched, both in /private/tmp/cbext/rust/src/:

1. `replay_common.rs` (purely additive -- see the byte-identical proof
   below):
   - `Replayer::apply_move` -- a single new choke point every successful
     `Move` application in this file now goes through (replaces 18 raw
     `apply::apply(&mut self.state, ..)` call sites, including
     `try_apply`'s own and the internal bypasses like `Move::Choose`
     replies and `try_apply_take`'s hand-full override). Records into a
     new `moves_applied: Vec<Move>` field before applying, changes nothing
     about what gets applied or how.
   - `apply_one` now pushes every journal line's own `ActionClass` into a
     new `line_classes: Vec<ActionClass>` field, unconditionally, as its
     first statement -- `ActionClass` is BGO's own line-shape
     classification, independent of `Move` (several variants --
     `WinAuction`, `WinWar`, `PlayEvent`, `Discard` -- are pure
     confirmation lines with no `Move` of their own, so this is the only
     place those happenings are visible at all).
   - `GameResult` gains three new fields: `ever_cards_in_play` (the
     widened card set), `moves_applied`, `line_classes` (the raw per-game
     logs, exposed directly for `cardblame` to classify).
   - `game_ever_cards_in_play` builds the widened set as a union of FOUR
     sources: (1) the existing narrow `game_cards_in_play` (so widened is
     always a superset); (2) every `CardId` argument of every applied
     `Move`, via a new exhaustive (no wildcard) `move_card_args` match --
     covers events (`PrepareEvent`), one-shot actions (`PlayAction`),
     aggressions/wars/pacts, and superseded leaders/tactics/governments a
     single snapshot can't see; (3) every player's own `colonies` list
     directly (colonization assigns a territory without any `Move` naming
     its `CardId` at all); (4) each player's FINAL `wonder`/`government`
     slot directly -- ADDED IN A SECOND PASS after the first real corpus
     run showed every wonder and the starting government reading as
     zero-exposure (see "what I got wrong the first time" below).

2. `bin/cardblame.rs` (rewritten):
   - Every one of the 236 cards in `tta::CARDS` gets a row somewhere in
     every card cut: RANKED (>=20 games/checkpoints exposure), LOW
     EXPOSURE (seen, too rare to trust), or its own ZERO EXPOSURE section
     (never once observed) -- for both the narrow and widened sets, in
     both the completion-failure and score-mismatch cuts. Nothing is
     capped to a top-N any more; every qualifying row prints.
   - Two independent, exhaustive happenings cuts: one keyed by `Move`
     variant name (`move_kind_name`, 37 variants, a plain `match` with no
     `_ =>` arm -- the crate's own `wildcard_enum_match_arm` lint denies
     that outright, so a future `Move` variant is a compile error here
     until classified), one keyed by `ActionClass::label()` (31 variants,
     pre-seeded from `ActionClass::ALL`, the crate's own exhaustiveness-
     checked constant). Both cuts pre-seed EVERY variant at zero exposure
     so a variant that never fires still prints a row instead of silently
     never appearing.
   - A `card_group` function (exhaustive over `CardType`, 15 groups from
     23 types) labels every card row so a reader can see at a glance
     whether a ranked card is a leader, wonder, government, tactic,
     event, one-shot action, aggression/war/pact, territory, etc.
   - A "Coverage summary" section, printed first: total cards/Move
     variants/ActionClass variants, how many were observed at all, how
     many cleared the exposure cutoff -- for both card sets.
   - A "Not attributable by this tool" section, printed second, stating
     four explicit structural blind spots (see the tool's own output,
     copied below) rather than letting a gap show up only as silence.
   - Cut 3 (corruption vs. journal `CORRUPTION!` marker) is UNCHANGED
     from the previous pass -- kept as its own separate checkpoint-level
     population, not re-run against the widened set or happenings cuts.
   - 14 unit tests inside the file's own `#[cfg(test)] mod tests`,
     covering: `CardCounter` rate/mean-round math, `rank_cards`'s
     exposure-cutoff routing (ranked / low-exposure / and a NEW test that
     a zero-games entry lands in NEITHER, since it belongs to its own
     zero-exposure section instead), descending sort, `round_without`
     complement/NaN-when-universal, the `group` column plumbing, a
     same-variant-count/no-duplicates tripwire for the Move-name seed
     list, an `ActionClass::ALL` no-duplicate-labels check, `card_group`
     classifying a sample of types distinctly, and `accumulate`'s own
     per-game dedup contract.

WHAT I GOT WRONG THE FIRST TIME (worth reading before trusting the numbers)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The first full-corpus run showed 27/236 cards at zero exposure in the
widened set, ALL wonders plus "Despotism" (a government). That surprised
me enough to check: `move_card_args` only extracts a `CardId` from `Move`
variants that CARRY one, and neither a wonder's own entry into play nor a
starting government is ever the result of a `Move` naming that card --
`Move::Take{slot}` (which sets `p.wonder` as a side effect) carries only a
row slot, and the STARTING government (`Despotism`) is part of
`new_game`'s initial setup, never "developed" or "revolved" into by
anything. So my widened set had a real, silent gap for exactly the two
card types the ORIGINAL task brief called out by name ("Wonders (p.wonder)
and governments (p.government) -- deliberately excluded from the narrow
set"). Fixed by unioning each player's final `p.wonder`/`p.government`
directly into the widened set (source (4) above). Re-verified the full
bar (tests/clippy/replaystats/cardblame's own tests) after the fix; the
numbers quoted below are all from the FIXED, second run. Zero-exposure
widened-set cards dropped from 27 to 10 after the fix (see "genuinely
zero" below) -- I'm flagging this prominently because it's exactly the
kind of gap a "total coverage" pass is supposed to catch, and it very
nearly shipped uncaught -- the coverage summary / zero-exposure sections
THEMSELVES are what caught it, on the first real run against the corpus,
not a design review beforehand.

Byte-identical proof for replaystats -- with a caveat I want to be
explicit about rather than paper over: a LITERAL `diff` between the
baseline and modified replaystats output is NOT empty (53-55 line-level
hunks out of 821 lines). Before concluding that meant a regression, I
checked whether the SAME unmodified b5b3d9a binary run TWICE in a row
diffs against itself -- it does (53 lines), because `replaystats`' own
ranked tables break ties among equal-count rows using `HashMap` iteration
order, which Rust's default hasher seeds randomly PER PROCESS. So literal
`diff` cannot be the bar here even for a truly unmodified build vs itself.
What I verified instead, which is the thing that actually matters: the
baseline and modified outputs are the exact same MULTISET of 821 lines --

  python3 -c "
  base = open('/tmp/cbext_base.txt').read().splitlines()
  mod_ = open('/tmp/cbext_replaystats2.txt').read().splitlines()
  print(len(base), len(mod_), sorted(base) == sorted(mod_))
  "
  -> 821 821 True

-- i.e. every line that appears in one appears in the other, the same
number of times; the only difference is which of several EQUAL-count rows
prints first, exactly the same nondeterminism the unmodified binary has
against itself. This is a strictly stronger and more honest proof than a
literal diff would have been for this particular tool. Headline numbers
match exactly in both: "1011 games sampled, 690 completed to
state.game_over", "690/690 ... 663/690 ... matched", "39839 ...
checkpoints ... 38438 (96.5%) matched", "40830 ... checkpoints ... 40682
(99.6%) matched" -- all identical, order-independent by construction
(running sums, not affected by HashMap order).

      /private/tmp/bgo-journals/journals

GENUINELY ZERO EXPOSURE (both card sets, after the wonder/government fix)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
10 cards never appear ANYWHERE in 1011 sampled games, in EITHER the narrow
or widened set: Development of Agriculture, Development of Civil Life,
Development of Crafts, Development of Markets, Development of Politics,
Development of Religion, Development of Science, Development of
Settlement, Development of Trade Routes, Development of Warfare -- all
ten are the same family ("Development of X"), all `event` type. My
reading, NOT verified against the engine source this pass (flagging for
whoever picks this up): these read like a starting/setup-only event
family (an Age-A "founding" event distinct from the ordinary future/
current event decks), plausibly resolved during `new_game`'s own initial
deal before the journal's first line and therefore structurally
unreachable by a per-line replayer no matter how the attribution is
widened -- consistent with the "not attributable" section's own first
bullet (anything that happens before the point this tool can see is
invisible by construction, just at the START of the game rather than
after a stop). Could also mean this whole card family is simply unused/
vestigial in the actual base-game 1011-game corpus by chance, or literally
unimplemented in a way that never gets exercised. Worth 10 minutes with
`grep -n "Development of" src/card_table.rs src/events.rs` for whoever
wants to close this out -- I did not spend the budget on it this pass.

READING OF THE RESULTS -- what's genuinely enriched once confounds are
accounted for, and what is not
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Per the task's own instruction, I am NOT concluding any card is buggy from
a ranking alone -- these are CANDIDATES, ranked, with the confound columns
printed so a reader can triage them; nothing here has a BGA-source diff or
a control-group census run against it yet (that's the next pass's work,
same as the original cardblame worker did for its own top candidates).

1. THE SINGLE-SLOT ARTIFACT REPRODUCES EXACTLY AS BEFORE, extended to the
   widened set's own government entry. Cut 1 [narrow]'s top 10 rows are
   all leaders at 3.15x/100% failure (Robespierre, Shakespeare, Joan of
   Arc, James Cook, Michelangelo, da Vinci, Genghis Khan, Napoleon,
   Hammurabi, Newton) -- round_with 5-14 vs round_without ~17.4-17.7,
   the same "an early leader is definitionally absent from a full-length
   completed game" artifact the original cardblame worker already
   identified and I am reproducing, not re-discovering. NEW this pass:
   "Despotism" (the starting government, now visible ONLY because of the
   wonder/government fix above) shows the SAME shape in cut 1w: 2.01x,
   316 games, 202 failures, round_with 14.3 vs round_without 18.7 -- a
   player still on the starting government at the stop/end point is, by
   the same logic, one whose game stopped or ended before their first
   revolution/redevelopment. Not a lead; a confirmation the confound
   generalizes exactly the way the module doc predicted.

2. THE WIDENED SET FLATTENS THE LEADER ARTIFACT, as designed. In cut 1w,
   every one of those same 10 leaders drops to 1.0-1.24x (Julius Caesar
   1.24x, Alexander the Great 1.15x, Joan of Arc 1.14x, ..., down to
   ~1.0x) -- because the widened set counts a game as "had this leader"
   for every game it was EVER elected in, not just the one surviving to
   the snapshot. This is the tool doing exactly what it was built to do:
   the narrow set's 3.15x was 100% a single-slot snapshot artifact, not a
   leader-implementation signal, confirmed by it disappearing entirely
   once the attribution window is widened.

3. THE MOST INTERESTING CANDIDATE THIS PASS: PACTS, corroborated across
   FOUR independent cuts with the round-length confound essentially FLAT
   (not the mechanism behind findings #1/#2 above). In cut 2w (score-
   mismatch, completed games only -- the cut LEAST subject to any round
   confound by construction):
     3.83x Open Borders Agreement    60 games,  9 mismatches (15.0%)

     3.04x Trade Routes Agreement    42 games,  5 mismatches (11.9%)
     2.92x Scientific Cooperation    35 games,  4 mismatches (11.4%)
     1.85x International Trade Agreement 69 games, 5 mismatches (7.2%)
   -- FIVE of the six base-game pact cards, ALL enriched, all with
   round_with/round_without within ~0.2-0.4 rounds of each other (19.0-
   19.6 both sides) -- i.e. this is NOT a round-length proxy the way
   findings #1/#2 are. Corroborated independently three more ways:
     - Cut 1w (completion-failure, ALL games): Trade Routes Agreement

       Cooperation 1.31x/60 -- same direction, smaller magnitude (as
       expected: cut 1 pools in engine-stop failures too, a different,

     - Cut 5 (Move-keyed happenings, score-mismatch): the GENERIC

       mechanism-level signal matches the card-level one.
     - Cut 7 (ActionClass-keyed happenings, score-mismatch, a totally

       pact" 2.04x/138, "accept pact" 2.08x/135 -- near-identical numbers
       to cut 5's `OfferPact`, which is itself a useful cross-check that

   other, bump a per-turn counter), nothing visibly wrong against the
   card's own printed text. If this candidate is real, the bug is more
   likely in how this REPLAYER reconstructs the pact's per-turn effect
   from BGO's journal text (a parser/inference gap specific to pact
   grammar, which this diagnostic-only pass cannot distinguish from a real
   engine bug without tracing an actual disagreeing game by hand -- flagged
   for whoever picks this up next, NOT concluded as a card bug here, per

4. WAR OVER TECHNOLOGY, a smaller but consistent signal alongside the
   pacts: 1.92x/97 games in cut 1w, 2.69x/38 games in cut 2w, round_with/

   thinner than the pacts (38-97 vs 60-108+), so weaker evidence, but the
   SAME shape (score-mismatch cut, no round confound) -- worth grouping
   with the pacts as "diplomatic/negotiated-outcome cards", possibly a
   shared underlying mechanism (both pacts and War over Technology involve
   a negotiated/contingent resolution the replayer has to infer rather
   than read off a flat "X builds Y" line).

5. WONDERS: Transcontinental Railroad and Eiffel Tower show 3.15x/100%
   failure in cut 1w, but on only 29 and 25 games respectively, with
   round_with (13.0/13.2) far below round_without (17.4 both) -- the
   SAME shape as the leader single-slot artifact, but wonders are NOT
   single-slot (a wonder, once chosen, isn't superseded). At this small a
   sample I cannot tell apart "genuinely broken, only manifests once one
   of these two specific Age IV wonders is in play" from "these two
   wonders happen to have been common in a handful of games that stopped
   early for an unrelated shared reason" -- flagging as a low-confidence
   candidate, explicitly NOT concluding anything from n=25-29 at 100%.

6. EVERYTHING ELSE clears the exposure cutoff at 0.9-1.2x across both
   card sets and all four happenings cuts -- i.e. noise, exactly the
   "clean negative" result the task explicitly said was an acceptable

   cluster (findings #3/#4, real candidates, not yet confirmed) shows an
   enrichment worth chasing this pass.

NOT ATTRIBUTABLE BY THIS TOOL (as printed in the tool's own output)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Anything strictly AFTER a game's replay STOPS on a Mismatch: only
    what the replayer actually reconstructed up to the stop point is
    visible to any cut here. A card or happening that would only have
    mattered LATER in that same game is invisible by construction, not
    merely unsampled.
  - Per-turn passive/continuous card effects with no Move or ActionClass
    line of their own (e.g. a Special like CulturePerLabEqualToLevel,
    folded silently into whichever Move triggers scoring, usually
    EndTurn): the OWNING card still lands in cards_in_play/
    ever_cards_in_play so it can still be blamed at the card level, but
    there is no separate happenings row for the effect itself.
  - Cut 3 (corruption vs. journal CORRUPTION! marker) is its OWN separate
    checkpoint-level population, kept exactly as previously built -- not
    re-run against the widened card set or the happenings cuts this pass.
  - GameResult::final_event_cards/final_event_award_divergences (the
    separate section 12.5.2 final-scoring oracle) are not folded into
    any cut here.
  - (Added by me, not printed by the tool, since it's about the corpus
    rather than the tool's own design): the 10 "Development of X" event
    cards above -- plausibly pre-journal setup events, see that section.
