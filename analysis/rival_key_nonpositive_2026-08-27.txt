NONPOSITIVE CITATION SEARCH: RIVAL KEY FAMILY
==============================================
2026-08-27. Eight keys sit in ONE |-joined Free arm in sign_intent
(weights.rs:1861-1875, the "Raw RIVAL board facts" arm): RivalCulture,
RivalMeanCulture, RivalCultureRate, RivalScienceRate, RivalStrength,
RivalWonders, RivalFreeCa, RivalHandCivil. The question: is there a RULES_SPEC
citation that a rival having MORE of this is never GOOD for me? That is the
NonPositive test. Verdict per key: citable / not-citable / needs-care.

The emit sites (features.rs:1107-1114) and the quantity each holds:
- RivalCulture: max over non-resigned rivals of q.culture (features.rs:629)
- RivalMeanCulture: mean over non-resigned rivals of q.culture
  (features.rs:639-640)
- RivalCultureRate: max over non-resigned rivals of s.culture (projected
  culture production), adjusted for pending pact gains (features.rs:654,673;
  rivals.rs:658)
- RivalScienceRate: max over non-resigned rivals of s.science (projected
  science production), same pact adjustment (features.rs:655,674; rivals.rs:659)
- RivalStrength: max over non-resigned rivals of s.strength (projected
  strength), same pact adjustment (features.rs:656,675; rivals.rs via
  effects::compute)
- RivalFreeCa: max over non-resigned rivals of q.civil_actions (features.rs:632)
- RivalHandCivil: max over non-resigned rivals of q.hand_size_civil()
  (features.rs:636)
- RivalWonders: max over non-resigned rivals of q.completed_wonders.len()
  (features.rs:637)

All eight are MAX-over-rivals (except RivalMeanCulture, which is the MEAN).
All are rival-board quantities: they read no own-board state. The NonPositive
test asks: does the rulebook cite a mechanic where a rival having MORE of this
is NEVER good for me?


THE ONE CITATION THAT COULD BOUND ALL EIGHT
--------------------------------------------
RULES_SPEC 5.3 [CoL p.7]: "Statistic comparisons ('strongest/weakest' =
strength rating [FAQ p.11])..." and the card data confirms ~16 events that
target "the strongest player" / "the weakest player" / "the player with the
most culture" / "the player with the least culture" (card_table.rs:3247-3741:
Raiders, Foray, Reign of Terror, Uncertain Borders, Border Conflict, Crusades,
Crime Wave, Independence Declaration, Cold War, Refugees, International
Agreement, Politics of Strength, plus the "player with most/least culture"
events in data/cards_military_actions.json:1671,1983,2027).

The official UB guide (sources/ubg_subsequent-rounds.txt:72-73) states this
explicitly: "Many events help the strongest players, punish the weakest
players, or both." And the critical line: "having the most culture points is
sometimes a disadvantage."

This is the citation. But it does NOT make a rival's high statistic
NonPositive for me. Here is why, and why it is a needs-care citation rather
than a citable one:

The events target ME, not my rival. "The strongest player gains 4 culture.
The weakest player loses 4 culture" (Politics of Strength, card_table.rs
~line 3741) means that if I am the strongest, I gain 4 culture and the weakest
loses 4. It does NOT mean that my rival being strong is bad for me. In fact,
for that specific event, a rival being strong (and me not) means the rival
gains 4 and I (as the weakest) lose 4 — which IS bad for me, but the mechanism
is "I am the weakest", not "my rival is the strongest". The NonPositive test
is about the RIVAL's quantity, not my relative position.

The events cut BOTH ways. "The strongest player gains 3 food/resources"
(Crusades, card_table.rs ~line 3380) means a strong rival GAINS resources,
which is bad for me (they are stronger). But "The strongest player takes a
yellow token from the weakest player's yellow bank" (card_table.rs ~line
3762) means a strong rival STEALS from the weakest — if I am the weakest, a
strong rival is bad; if I am the strongest, a strong rival is irrelevant (they
take from someone else). The direction depends on my own position relative to
the rival, which a single max-over-rivals coordinate does not capture.

The UB guide's own tip confirms this is a STRATEGIC read, not a rules fact:
"It is not a bad idea to end your turn with higher strength than the other
players. This may lead to a cold war even in a game with no direct attacks."
Being strong is a STRATEGY, not a liability. The events that target the
strongest are as likely to REWARD the strongest as to punish them.


PER-KEY VERDICTS
-----------------

1. RivalCulture
   Quantity: max rival culture points (a stock, not a rate).
   NonPositive test: Is a rival having MORE culture never good for me?
   Verdict: NOT-CITABLE.
   Reason: Culture is the victory condition (RULES_SPEC 12.5: "Most culture
   points wins"). A rival having more culture means they are ahead in the race
   — bad for me. But "never good" is too strong: a rival with a large culture
   stock is a TARGET for culture-stealing aggressions (Armed Intervention,
   RULES_SPEC 5.5: "take up to 7 culture") and for the "player with most
   culture" events (card_table.rs:1671: "The player with the most culture
   decreases population if he or she is the weakest"). A rival with a huge
   culture stock is a rich target — exploiting them is GOOD for me. The
   direction depends on whether I can attack them, which is a strategic read,
   not a rules fact. No citation makes a rival's high culture "never good".

2. RivalMeanCulture
   Quantity: mean rival culture points.
   Verdict: NOT-CITABLE.
   Reason: Same as RivalCulture, but the mean dilutes the "rich target"
   signal. A high mean means all rivals are moderately ahead, which is
   uniformly bad (I am behind everyone). But "never good" still fails: a high
   mean culture across rivals means the game is culture-active, which paces
   age advancement (RULES_SPEC 12.1: ages end when the civil deck runs out,
   not when culture hits a threshold — so culture does NOT directly pace ages,
   but a high-culture game means more events have resolved, which can be good
   or bad depending on which events). No citation.

3. RivalCultureRate
   Quantity: max rival projected culture production (a rate, not a stock).
   Verdict: NOT-CITABLE.
   Reason: A high rival culture rate means they are producing culture fast —
   they will catch up or pull ahead. Bad for me in the race. But "never good"
   fails: a rival with a high culture rate is a future rich target (same
   logic as RivalCulture, one turn earlier). Also, the rate is a PROJECTION
   (effects::compute), not a committed stock — it can change if the rival
   loses a culture-producing card to antiquation or an aggression. The
   NonPositive test requires "never good under any reading", and the
   projection's mutability means a high rate today is not a guaranteed
   liability tomorrow. No citation.

4. RivalScienceRate
   Quantity: max rival projected science production.
   Verdict: NOT-CITABLE.
   Reason: Science does NOT directly affect the victory condition (culture
   wins the game, RULES_SPEC 12.5). A rival's high science rate means they
   will develop technologies faster, which gives them better production,
   better units, better wonders — all indirectly bad for me. But "never good"
   fails: a rival burning science on a technology I do not want is irrelevant
   to me; a rival with a high science rate is a target for Spy aggressions
   (RULES_SPEC 5.5: "take up to 5 science") and for War over Technology
   (RULES_SPEC 5.8: "science points equal to advantage"). A scientifically
   strong rival is a rich science target. No citation makes a rival's science
   rate "never good".

5. RivalStrength
   Quantity: max rival projected strength.
   Verdict: NEEDS-CARE.
   Reason: This is the one key where the RULES_SPEC 5.3 citation is MOST
   relevant. Strength is the combat statistic: aggressions are illegal if the
   rival's strength >= mine (RULES_SPEC 5.4 step 2), and wars are resolved by
   strength comparison (RULES_SPEC 5.7). A rival with high strength means I
   cannot aggression them (illegal) and may lose a war against them. That is
   a rules-imposed constraint on MY options, not just a strategic race.
   However, "never good" still fails: a strong rival is a target for me to
   become stronger than (I can upgrade units, build armies, form a pact that
   grants strength — RULES_SPEC 5.9). Also, the UB guide's own tip
   (ubg_subsequent-rounds.txt:72) says being strong is a STRATEGY, not a
   liability: "It is not a bad idea to end your turn with higher strength
   than the other players." A strong rival is a challenge, not an automatic
   bad. The citation supports "a strong rival constrains my military options"
   (which is the existing FreeWorkerWall/StrengthDeficit shape), but it does
   NOT support "a rival's high strength is never good for me". Verdict:
   needs-care — the citation is relevant but does not pass the strict
   NonPositive test.

6. RivalWonders
   Quantity: max rival completed wonders count.
   Verdict: NOT-CITABLE.
   Reason: Completed wonders give their holder permanent effects (RULES_SPEC
   9.2). A rival with many completed wonders has many permanent bonuses —
   bad for me in the long run. But "never good" fails: a rival with many
   completed wonders has spent a lot of civil actions and resources on them,
   which means they had less available for other things (opportunity cost).
   Also, wonders are not directly attackable (no aggression targets "a
   rival's wonder" except Infiltrate, RULES_SPEC 5.5: "remove rival's leader
   or unfinished wonder from game" — note: UNFINISHED, not completed). A
   rival's completed wonders are a sunk cost for them; they are not a target
   I can exploit. No citation makes a rival's wonder count "never good".

7. RivalFreeCa
   Quantity: max rival civil action total (from their government).
   Verdict: NOT-CITABLE.
   Reason: A rival's CA total is a function of their government (RULES_SPEC
   8.1: Despotism = 4 CA; higher governments have more or fewer). A rival
   with more CAs can take more cards, build more, research more — bad for me
   in the race. But "never good" fails: a rival with a high CA total from a
   high-level government has already invested science in a revolution or
   peaceful change (RULES_SPEC 8.2-8.3), which means they spent science they
   could have used elsewhere. Also, the CA total is a GOVERNMENT property, not
   a stock that changes turn-to-turn — it is fixed for the duration of that
   government. A rival with 5 CAs (Democracy) is not a "threat" in the same
   way a rival with 3 free CAs this turn is; the total is an allowance, not a
   current availability. No citation.

8. RivalHandCivil
   Quantity: max rival civil hand size.
   Verdict: NOT-CITABLE.
   Reason: A rival with a large civil hand has many options — they can play a
   leader, develop a technology, take a card — bad for me in the race. But
   "never good" fails: a large hand means the rival has NOT played those cards,
   which means they are waiting for a better moment (or they are stuck with
   cards they cannot use). Also, at age end, antiquated cards are discarded
   from hands (RULES_SPEC 12.2 step 1), so a large hand of old-age cards is a
   liability for the rival, not a threat. A rival with a full hand of Age II
   cards at the end of Age II is about to discard them all — that is GOOD for
   me (they lose options). No citation makes a rival's hand size "never good".

HEADLINE
---------
NO key passes the strict NonPositive test. The RULES_SPEC 5.3 citation
("strongest/weakest" targeting) is relevant to RivalStrength but does not make
a rival's high strength "never good" — it makes it a CONSTRAINT on my options
(a different shape, already priced by StrengthDeficit's NonPositive gate) and
a STRATEGY the rival is pursuing (UB guide: "It is not a bad idea to end your
turn with higher strength"). The other seven keys have no citation at all that
a rival having more of the quantity is "never good" for me. The existing Free
classification (weights.rs:1861-1875) is correct: these are strategic race
coordinates, not rules-imposed penalties.

The one key that deserves a second look is RivalStrength, but the second look
should ask a DIFFERENT question: not "is a rival's high strength never good
for me?" (No — it is a strategy they are pursuing, and I can match it) but
"does a rival's high strength CONSTRAIN my options in a way that is already
priced elsewhere?" (Yes — StrengthDeficit's NonPositive gate prices "I am
weaker than the max rival" as a rules-imposed penalty, RULES_SPEC 5.4/5.7.
RivalStrength is the same quantity measured from the rival's side. The two
are not redundant: StrengthDeficit is max(0, rival - me), which is 0 when I
lead; RivalStrength is the raw rival value, which is high even when I lead.
But the NonPositive test is not the right test for RivalStrength — the right
test is whether it is REDUNDANT with StrengthDeficit, which is a different
question for a different job.)

No sign_intent change proposed. No weights.rs edit. No citation looked up
beyond the ones already in RULES_SPEC and the sources/ files. The web
cross-check (fandom, BGG, UB official) returned the same conclusion: the UB
guide's own tip (ubg_subsequent-rounds.txt:72-73) is the closest thing to a
citation, and it says being strong is a STRATEGY, not a liability.
