THE METHOD'S BLIND SPOT -- worth more than either verdict
---------------------------------------------------------
The sweep enumerated negative INTEGER LITERALS IN CARD DATA. Two card TEXTS
describe losses that are implemented in event code, not as negative data fields,
and so are invisible to that method:

    cards_military_actions.json:1655  "The weakest player (2p) / the two
      weakest players (3-4p) lose up to 2 food and/or resources."
    cards_military_actions.json:1681  "The player with the most culture
      decreases population if he or she is the weakest ..."

So "no negative literal in the data" does NOT mean "no mechanism can reduce this
quantity". Food stock and population CAN be reduced, by events.

It changes no verdict, and the reason is the one that actually matters for a
sign_intent: NonNegative constrains the WEIGHT, i.e. it asserts that a LARGER
value is never worse. A subtractive event is not that. Losing 2 food from a
larger stock still leaves a player strictly better off than losing 2 from a
smaller one -- the event is a subtraction, not a penalty scaled to holdings.
A counterexample has to be a mechanism where MORE IS WORSE, and neither of these
is one. Do not re-raise them as if they were.


A CORRECTION TO HOW THE EXPERIMENT MUST BE ARGUED
--------------------------------------------------
FoodRate is NOT gross food production. features.rs:713:

    f.set(WeightKey::FoodRate, f64::from(s.food) + g(GainFeature::FoodRate)
                               - consumption);

It is a NET SURPLUS, production minus consumption(proj_yellow), exactly as
ResourceRate is production minus corruption(proj_blue). The queued writeup was
framed as "more food PRODUCTION is never a downside"; that argues about a
quantity the feature does not hold.

The rules argument survives the restatement and is in fact stronger. Consumption
is already deducted, so the feature IS the famine margin, and RULES_SPEC 6.6-3d
charges 4 culture per missing food -- a larger margin is strictly less exposed.
6.6-3b makes food the corruption shortfall buffer on top of that. No rule
charges a player for a larger surplus, and the worker's opportunity cost is
carried by the other production terms, not by this one. The feature's VALUE may
legitimately go negative in a famine; that is not in tension with a NonNegative
weight, which is a claim about direction, not about range.

Restate the writeup in terms of net food surplus before landing it.
