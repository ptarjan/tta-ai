3. SIGN GATE / GROUPING (weights.rs:1421 and weights.rs:1480+/1854)
================================================================================
Two DIFFERENT mechanisms sit near "line 1421" and must not be conflated:

(a) weights.rs:1421-1423, inside `WeightKey::group()` (a WeightGroup
    classification used only for reporting/bucketing -- WeightGroup::name()
    at line 2640 gives it the label "row", and WeightKey::of_group() at
    2657 filters WeightKey::ALL by it). This grouping does NOT constrain
    values, bounds, or signs. It is a label, nothing more:
        RowUrgency | RowBargainForgone | RivalTakeShare | RowLastCopy
        | RivalDesire => { WeightGroup::Row }

(b) The actual sign gate lives in a SEPARATE function, `sign_intent`
    (weights.rs:1480, `pub const fn sign_intent(self) -> SignIntent`).
    At weights.rs:1854, in that function:
        RowUrgency | RowBargainForgone | RowLastCopy | RivalDesire
        | RivalTakeShare => Free,
    "Free" means NO sign is imposed -- the surrounding comment
    (weights.rs:1846-1853) states explicitly: "Row-reading and rival-row
    preference coordinates -- how much to weigh a rival wanting a card is
    a strategic read, not a rules fact ... Still Free -- fixing the units
    did not establish a sign any rule cites." Contrast with e.g. WarImmune
    | AttackCostDoubled => NonNegative(...), which DOES gate a sign.

So: no sign_intent gate applies to any of the three keys, and the
line-1421 grouping is unrelated to bounds -- it is a reporting label only.

================================================================================
4. VERDICT PER KEY
================================================================================

row_bargain_forgone: bound ~103-109 (measured; flat-capped at 60 during the
  actual climb, since CLAMP_CEILING was raised only after this collapse).
  Old value 2.180842, new value 0.123251 -- both are COMFORTABLY INTERIOR,
  using at most ~3.6% of even the tighter 60.0 rail. The bound was never
  close to binding in either direction. VERDICT: not bound-caused. The
  climb chose to collapse this key toward zero on its own, well inside a
  range that would have permitted it to stay at 2.18 or grow further.

row_last_copy: bound ~78-97 (measured; flat-capped at 60 during the actual
  climb, same timing note as above). Old value -0.244516, new value
  0.005789 -- both trivially interior (well under 1% of the bound).
  VERDICT: not bound-caused. Same conclusion: the climb had enormous room
  and chose near-zero anyway.

row_urgency: this is the one key where the bound is genuinely TIGHT --
  5.468 / 3.491 / 1.698 at 2p/3p/4p, one to two orders of magnitude
  smaller than the other two keys' bounds. But even here, the observed
  values are far interior: old -0.582632 uses ~10.7% of the 2p bound
  (5.468), and new -0.163023 uses ~3.0% of it. To reach its OWN bound at
  4p (the tightest of the three, 1.698) row_urgency would need to be
  roughly 10x its pre-climb magnitude, not shrink. VERDICT: not
  bound-caused either, though this key's headroom is much smaller than
  the other two's -- worth watching if a future climb pushes it further,
  since it is the one arm where the rail is within an order of magnitude
  of plausible values, unlike the other two which have effectively no
  rail at these magnitudes.

BOTTOM LINE: for all three keys, the collapse toward zero was NOT forced
by the search bounds. Every final (and every prior) value sits comfortably
inside its permitted [-bound, +bound] interval -- by a factor of roughly
30x to 400x of headroom depending on the key. The climb was fully ALLOWED
to keep row_bargain_forgone, row_last_copy, and row_urgency large, and
chose not to. Whatever is driving these three toward zero is a property of
the self-play objective/gradient, not a clamp artifact.
