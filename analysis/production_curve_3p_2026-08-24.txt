THE ANSWER: THE BOT UNDER-BUILDS, AND ITS FOOD CURVE RUNS BACKWARDS
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Both curves start identical at 2.00/2.00 for rounds 1-2, which is the fixed
starting tableau before any farm or mine tech can be in play -- the two
measurements agree exactly where they are forced to, which is the check that
they are measuring the same thing.

RESOURCES diverge at round 3, the first round a mine tech can exist: 2.49 bot
against 2.83 human, ~12% low. The gap only widens -- 30% low by round 10
(2.90 vs 4.17), and by round 15 the bot produces less than half the human
rate (2.91 vs 6.02).

FOOD is the worse finding, because the two curves differ in DIRECTION and not
only in size. The human food curve climbs monotonically from 2.00 to 4.95
through round 18. The bot's food curve DECLINES from round 2 onward, bottoms
around 1.40-1.50 for the entire midgame, and never recovers. By round 14-15
the bot is producing roughly a third of the human food rate.

A declining food curve cannot be explained by the human side at all. It says
the bot's own farms are producing less as its game goes on -- workers moving
off farms faster than farm levels rise -- while its population, and therefore
its consumption, keeps growing.

THIS SETTLES THE CARD-ROT CAUSE
-------------------------------
The two candidate causes of "had a civil action, could afford nothing" (65.6%
of a wasted card's blocked decision points) were under-built production versus
committed blue tokens. It is under-built production.

The committed-token reading is doubly dead. production_this_turn worker-caps
against blue_available (blue_total minus tokens already sitting on the board
as stock), so a player holding a large stock has its measured production
capped DOWNWARD by exactly the mechanism that reading proposes. The humans,
who are the richer side, therefore have the more binding cap, and they still
measure far higher. If the cap is biasing this table at all it is biasing it
in the bot's favour, and the bot still loses on every round after 2.

CORROBORATION FROM THE n COLUMN
--------------------------------
The bot's n holds at 600 through round 18 while the human n falls from 399 to
203 -- human games END sooner. That is the same fact as
analysis/card_fate_human_2026-08-24.txt's take rate (humans take 26.92 civil
cards per player-game at 3p, the bot 17.49): humans consume the card row
faster, so the deck runs out earlier. A bot whose games run LONGER while
producing LESS per round is not being outpaced on tempo alone.

CAVEATS ON THE MEASUREMENT ITSELF
---------------------------------
1. production_this_turn takes ONE blue_available snapshot and caps both food
   and resources against it. end_of_turn re-reads blue_available at four
   sites (3b corruption spends bank tokens, 3c credits food, 3d consumption
   frees tokens back, 3e credits resources), so the real food-step and
   resource-step caps can differ. A non-mutating query cannot replay that
   drift. The new test in economy.rs proves exact agreement with what
   end_of_turn actually credits in the regime where the bank never binds.
2. The human side is survivorship-filtered after round 8 as games finish;
   the bot side is not until round 19. This does not touch the early rounds,
   where the resource gap already opens.
3. This is a RATE, not a stock: effects::Stats' uncapped rating is a
   different quantity and is not what is plotted here.
