Structure: the CAS drop for a take of cost c is
  min(c, max(0, CaLeft - HandCivil))
i.e. the take costs one unit of CAS per CA spent, but only while the surplus
is still positive. The zero-tax cells are exactly the cells with
CaLeft <= HandCivil (the hand is at least as full as the action pool) OR
CaLeft == 0 (nothing to spend). In the c=1 table the zero region is the
upper-right triangle (H >= CA); in c=2 and c=3 it shrinks to the upper-right
plus the CA=0 row.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHEN THE TAX BITES -- CONFIRMED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The tax bites if and only if CaLeft > HandCivil AND CaLeft >= c (the take is
affordable). In words: the hand is STRICTLY emptier than the action pool.
Your reading is confirmed, with the affordability caveat. The intuition is
exact: the state in which the tax bites is precisely the state in which the
hand is emptiest relative to the CA pool -- i.e. the bot most needs to
refill -- and that is the state in which the CAS feature charges it a
positive price for the very act of refilling (the take drops CaLeft by c and
HandCivil by +1, both of which reduce the surplus). The tax is a tax on
refilling when the hand is light, which is backwards if the hand being light
is what the surplus was meant to reward.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. CIVILACTIONGAP -- THE OTHER HINGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CAG = max(0, HandCivil - CaLeft) = (ca_need - ca_have).max(0), ca_need =
HandCivil, ca_have = CaLeft (features.rs:1199-1202, the line immediately
above the CAS line).

Live weights:
  2p gen 120861:  civil_action_gap = -1.285290
  3p gen 67460:   civil_action_gap = -0.005153
  4p gen 21894:   civil_action_gap = -0.013682

When CAG fires: it is > 0 exactly when HandCivil > CaLeft -- the hand is
FULLER than the action pool. A Take does HandCivil +1, CaLeft - c, so CAG
rises by 1 + (CAS_drop) in general; concretely the CAG rise is
  min(1 + c, max(0, HandCivil + c + 1 - CaLeft) - max(0, HandCivil - CaLeft))
and in the cells where CAS already fires (CaLeft > HandCivil) a take of cost
c that exhausts the surplus (CaLeft - c <= HandCivil) pushes CAG up by
(HandCivil + 1) - (CaLeft - c) = (HandCivil + c + 1 - CaLeft). Example:
CA=2, H=0, c=3: CAS 2->0 (drop 2), CAG 0->2 (rise 2), so the take pays
2*2.098429 (CAS) + 2*(-1.285290) (CAG) = 4.196858 - 2.570580 = +1.626278
net on the two hinge keys. The CAG rise partially offsets the CAS fall in
the cells where the take crosses the hinge, but it never fully cancels it:
|w[CAG]| = 1.285290 < |w[CAS]| = 2.098429 per unit, so the CAS side always
dominates the net.

Symmetry: the pair is NOT symmetric. At 2p the CAS side (surplus, w +2.098)
is 1.63x the CAG side (gap, w -1.285) in magnitude. The hinge prices
"hand lighter than pool" (surplus) more heavily than "hand fuller than pool"
(gap). At 3p and 4p both weights are ~0 (3p CAG -0.005, 4p CAG -0.014; 3p CAS
+0.009, 4p CAS 0.000), so the whole hinge is effectively unpriced at those
counts -- the asymmetry is a 2p-only phenomenon.
