1. WHAT THE EVALUATION ACTUALLY SEES TODAY
==========================================

The headline in the brief -- "roughly one scalar per card TYPE" -- is no
longer the whole truth of this code, and the design below is only useful if
that is stated accurately first. Three conditioning mechanisms are already
landed. Two of the owner's three complaints are therefore partially
addressed, and the remaining gaps are narrower and more specific than
"features are by category".

1a. Genuinely per-card-TYPE scalars (the complaint, where it still holds)

  BestFarm, BestMine, BestLab, BestTemple, BestTheater, BestLibrary,
  BestArena, BestUnit -- features.rs:668-676, each the highest level of that
  one type in the tableau, one f64 apiece, unconditioned on anything.

  The "board credit" family, one weight per card CATEGORY, dispatched by
  card kind in cards.rs:1944-2030: TechBoardCredit (1947), GovBoardCredit
  (1956), ActionBoardCredit (1961), WonderBoardCredit (1991),
  TacticBoardCredit (2004), AggressionBoardCredit (2007), WarBoardCredit
  (2012), PactBoardCredit (2017), EventBoardCredit (2022), with
  CardRateCredit (2029) and CardBoardCredit (2030) as the generic fallback.
  Also UnitTechCredit, BonusCardCredit, TerritoryCredit, BuildFreshCredit,
  RestrictedResourceCredit, FreeActionCredit.

  IMPORTANT: these credits are multipliers on a board-AWARE price, not flat
  per-type values. TechBoardCredit scales tech_value(id, state, idx, ...);
  WonderBoardCredit scales board_yields::board_yields(id, b), the swap DIFF.
  So a level-2 Farm replacing a level-1 Farm is already priced by the yield
  DELTA, not by a constant "farm is worth X". The per-type scalar is the
  GAIN, not the VALUE.

1b. Absolute stock / count features (unconditioned levels)

  FoodStock, ResourceStock, Science, Culture, BlueFree, YellowBank,
  FreeWorkers, Workers, ProdWorkers, UrbanWorkers, UnitWorkers, CivilActions,
  MilitaryActions, CaLeft, MaLeft, TechLevels, NumTechs, SpecialTechs,
  Wonders, Colonies, Pacts, HandCivil, HandMilitary, Strength -- all set as
  plain counts in features.rs. Rates likewise: FoodRate, ResourceRate,
  ScienceRate, CultureRate (features.rs deliberately reports the BOARD and
  leaves the horizon discount to evaluate/feature_marginal, features.rs:353).
  CorruptionHeadroom / ConsumptionHeadroom (features.rs:555-556) are token-
  band headroom off blue/yellow, not happiness.

1c. Already state-relative -- this is the part the brief did not assume

  (i) Gap/surplus pairs, features.rs:812-855. Real key names, from the
  WeightKey enum and the name table in weights.rs:
    food_gap / food_surplus, resource_gap / resource_surplus,
    science_gap / science_surplus, culture_gap / culture_surplus,
    happy_surplus, civil_action_gap / civil_action_surplus,
    military_action_gap / military_action_surplus,
    worker_gap / worker_surplus.
  Always a hinged PAIR, never one signed difference. The thresholds come
  from features::marginal_needs (features.rs:297ff), which is four rulebook
  quantities: food = economy::pop_food_cost for ONE population increase;
  resource = the cheapest unstaffed tableau slot's PRINTED resource cost;
  science = the cheapest developable card IN MY CIVIL HAND; worker = the
  count of unstaffed slots. culture_gap is different again and is already
  field-relative: its threshold is the strongest rival's culture, not a
  rulebook number (features.rs:826-834).

  (ii) The STANDING hinge, rivals.rs feature_marginal (~965-980). For
  STANDING_KEYS the per-unit marginal is w[key] + w[key.trailing()] *
  trailing_fraction(key, ...), i.e. a unit is worth more to the player behind
  the field. Live keys: culture_rate_trailing, science_rate_trailing.

  (iii) The NEED hinge, rivals.rs (~993-1002). For NEED_KEYS the marginal is
  additionally + w[key.needed()] * need_fraction, where need_fraction is
  (threshold - have)/threshold in [0,1] off the SAME marginal_needs. Keys:
  food_stock_needed, resource_stock_needed, science_needed,
  free_workers_needed. NEED_KEYS is exactly four axes -- FoodStock,
  ResourceStock, Science, FreeWorkers (the match in need_fraction,
  rivals.rs ~940-946).

  This last one matters: feature_marginal is the SINGLE definition of what
  one unit is worth, and every card-pricing site in cards.rs calls it
  (cards.rs:724, 730, 770, 1289), so "card pricing sees the gap" is ALREADY
  TRUE for food, resources, science and free workers. The owner's approved
  direction is partly shipped. What follows must not re-propose it.

  (iv) Quotients deliberately outside the linear span: take_cost_share =
  ca_spent_taking / costs::ca_total (features.rs:617-627, with the explicit
  argument that this is the only way the vector can distinguish 3 actions out
  of 4 from 3 out of 7). This is the precedent every ratio proposed below
  leans on.

  (v) Cliffs, not slopes, already used where a rule is discontinuous:
  has_unit, has_colony, war_immune, attack_cost_doubled (features.rs:637-663).
  Precedent for proposal 3 below.

  (vi) Row and rival terms priced through w in eval.rs's non-linear block,
  not as linear features: row_urgency, row_bargain_forgone (eval.rs:372-383,
  via row::row_pressure), row_last_copy (eval.rs:385-388), rival_desire,
  rival_take_share, rival_hand_potential, hand_potential, wonder_potential
  (eval.rs:336-343), wonder_promise (eval.rs:344-349).

  (vii) Wonder terms, features.rs:696-712: wonder_progress, wonder_remaining,
  wonder_stages_left, wonder_turns_to_finish, wonder_overrun,
  wonder_age_overrun, wonder_stages_per_action, plus wonder_pool_rival_claimed
  (a public count of THIS age's wonders taken by rivals). cards::
  wonder_potential already applies horizon::WonderOutlook::earned_share, a
  computed "how much of it is paid for times how much of the game you would
  own it for" discount, explicitly to kill the build-now bias
  (cards.rs:2206-2228).

  UNVERIFIED: I read the call sites of row::row_pressure and row::
  row_last_copy (eval.rs:372-388) and row.rs's doc fragments, but not
  row_pressure's body. Any claim about exactly which quantity
  row_bargain_forgone measures is not mine to make; proposal 6 below overlaps
  that function's territory and its implementer must diff against it first.


2. DECISIONS THE CURRENT FEATURE SET CANNOT EXPRESS
===================================================

2a. "I am low on science now, so take yellow cards for temporary science."
The science threshold is the cheapest developable card IN MY HAND
(marginal_needs, features.rs:297ff). With an empty hand, or a hand of only
non-levelled cards, needs.science == 0, so science_gap == 0 AND
need_fraction(Science) == 0. Situation: age A, I hold no civil cards, my
science is 1/turn, and the row shows Philosophy at 3 science plus an action
card granting +2 science immediately. The action card is priced at flat
w[science] * horizon at exactly the moment the player is most starved,
because "need" is measured against a hand that is empty. The threshold never
looks at the row, which is where the next thing I will want to pay for
actually sits.

2b. "Should I build this very last stage of the wonder, since it gives me
the bonus?" wonder_stages_left is linear and earned_share is continuous, so
the value of a wonder is smeared evenly across its stages. Situation:
Pyramids, 2 of 3 stages paid, 3 resources outstanding, and I could instead
build a level-2 Farm. Every stage buys the same fraction of the wonder's
value under earned_share, so the last stage -- the only one that flips the
printed effect ON, permanently -- carries no premium over the first. Nothing
in the vector is discontinuous at completion except the Wonders count itself,
which is a single scalar shared with wonders finished ten rounds ago.

2c. "Maybe I need more economy right now instead of building that wonder."
wonder_remaining (resources still owed) and resource_rate (resources per
turn) are two independent linear coordinates. Their RATIO is not in the span
of the pair, so the vector cannot distinguish 11 resources outstanding at
6/turn (two turns, fine) from 11 outstanding at 2/turn (six turns, a
disaster). resource_gap does not help: its threshold is the single cheapest
unstaffed slot, a one-step quantity, not the total outstanding obligation.

2d. "Nothing in the row is worth a civil action to THIS position." This is
what the human data force. Round 1 is 100% takes, zero builds, zero
population actions across 2525 player-games. Seat 1 faces a completely fresh
row, pays the 1-CA band on 100% of its takes, and still leaves a median 3 of
its 4 civil actions unused; 99.9% of seat-1 player-games waste at least one.
The civil hand cap is reached by 0.6% of player-games, so legality is not the
restraint and the measurement file says so explicitly. The bot's side of this
is: ca_left is a flat positive scalar priced identically whether the row in
front of me is full of cards I can use or full of cards I cannot pay to play;
civil_action_gap = max(0, hand_civil - ca_left) compares hand size to THIS
turn's remaining actions, not to whether I can afford to do anything with a
card once I hold it. Situation: round 1, Despotism, 4 CA, 1 science and 1
resource of income. A second and third hand card cannot be developed or built
for several turns and may antiquate first. Nothing in the vector prices the
SECOND card differently from the first except hand_perishable, which is a
clock, not a capacity check.

2e. A note on the owner's opportunity-cost sentence. "I weight each decision
against the opportunity cost for every possible action" is, structurally,
what the 1-ply search in eval.rs already does: it evaluates the resulting
state of every legal move, including end-turn (end_turn_bias, weights.rs:798,
default -3.0), and picks the max. Cross-action opportunity cost does not need
a feature and should not get one. What is missing is narrower: the value of
an action KEPT is a constant, so the search has nothing to trade against.
Proposals 4 and 6 supply the two terms that make banking an action a
position-dependent judgement instead of a constant.


3. PROPOSED FEATURES
====================

Six, not a catalogue. No proposal is a new per-type scalar and none is a
retune; that is explicitly rejected. Every one is a quantity the ENGINE
computes from the rulebook and public state, priced by a weight the hill
climb fits -- no proposal contains a value, a coefficient, or a threshold
that a human would pick. Where a proposal is a ratio, the precedent is
take_cost_share (features.rs:617-627).

--- 3.1  science_need_row  (a second science gap, threshold from the ROW)

QUANTITY: max(0, T - my science), where T is the smallest printed science
cost among the cards in the current row that are of a levelled (developable)
type and that I could legally take. 0.0 when the row holds no such card.
Paired with a surplus half only if the learner needs it; the gap half is the
live one. Deliberately a SEPARATE coordinate from science_gap rather than a
change to marginal_needs -- editing that threshold would move every champion
on disk and both readers of it at once.

ENGINE: the card row is public. Printed science costs are static card data
(cards.rs / data/cards_civil.json). My own science is my own stock. One pass
over a bounded row (<= 13 slots) comparing u8s.

DECISION CHANGED: 2a. It is nonzero exactly when the player is starved and
the hand-based threshold has gone blind, so a science-yielding action card or
a Library take gets its need premium at the moment it is actually needed.

EXPECTED SIGN: negative, the same family as food_gap -- a shortfall is a bad
state, so the move that shrinks it scores higher. If the implementer instead
feeds it through the need hinge as a science_row_needed coefficient on
feature_marginal, the sign flips positive there (a hinge is an ADDITION to a
unit's worth, rivals.rs:907-911); pick one, not both, or the axis is
double-counted.

LEGALITY: PASSES. Row contents are public. Uses no hand identity of any
rival and no deck order.

--- 3.2  resource_commitment_turns  (economy versus wonder)

QUANTITY: (wonder_remaining + sum of printed resource costs of my unstaffed
tableau slots) / max(resource_rate, 1). How many turns of my ENTIRE resource
production are already spoken for. A ratio, deliberately outside the linear
span of the two coordinates it is built from.

ENGINE: wonder_remaining is already computed (features.rs:697). The unstaffed
slot costs come from the same tableau walk sweep_tableau already performs and
must use the PRINTED cost, exactly as unbuilt_min_resource_cost does and for
the reason stated there -- costs::build_cost_for recomputes a full
effects::state_stats per call and must not be called per slot in this loop
(features.rs:163-179). resource_rate is already in Stats.

DECISION CHANGED: 2c, the owner's own sentence. Taking a wonder or
developing a new building raises it; building a Mine lowers it. It makes
"start another wonder" bad specifically when production is thin, and
harmless when production is fat, which no pair of linear coordinates can say.

EXPECTED SIGN: negative. Obligations outrunning production is a bad state.

LEGALITY: PASSES. Entirely my own board, all of it face up anyway.

--- 3.3  wonder_one_stage_short  (the completion cliff)

QUANTITY: 1.0 if a wonder is in progress with exactly one stage left,
else 0.0. An indicator, not a slope.

ENGINE: stages_left is already computed (features.rs:699). This is a
comparison against 1.

DECISION CHANGED: 2b. Evaluation is of the post-move state, so the move that
completes the wonder moves the vector from (wonder_one_stage_short = 1,
Wonders = n) to (wonder_one_stage_short = 0, Wonders = n+1). If the learner
finds a NEGATIVE weight -- sitting one stage short is a bad place to be, the
value is banked and not yet earned -- then the completing build gains
w[wonders] + |w[wonder_one_stage_short]| while every earlier stage gains only
its earned_share slice. That is a cliff at completion, expressed inside a
linear model. The repo already uses exactly this idiom for rule
discontinuities: has_unit, has_colony, war_immune (features.rs:637-663).

EXPECTED SIGN: negative, per the reasoning above. If the learner instead
finds it positive, that is informative rather than wrong -- it would mean the
league values being POISED to finish, and the completion cliff should then be
sought as a convex transform (1/(1+stages_left)) instead. Let the climb
decide; do not pick the shape by hand.

LEGALITY: PASSES. My own wonder in progress is face up.

--- 3.4  hand_over_capacity  (why round 1 is one take and stop)

QUANTITY: max(0, hand_civil - K), where K is the number of cards currently in
my civil hand that I could actually pay for right now: a levelled card whose
printed science cost <= my science, or an already-developed card whose
printed resource cost <= my resources. Counts cards, not actions. 0.0 for a
player who can afford to play everything they hold.

ENGINE: my own hand and my own stocks. Printed costs, static card data --
NOT costs::build_cost_for / costs::tech_cost, for the state_stats reason
above; a linear feature's need signal does not require discount-exact
precision, which is the argument features.rs:163-179 already makes for
unbuilt_min_resource_cost. The civil hand is bounded (median 1 card in the
human corpus, cap 4 under Despotism), so this is a handful of u8 compares.

DECISION CHANGED: 2d, and it is the marginal-value-of-the-next-unit shape the
brief asks for. In round 1 the first take is free of this penalty; the second
and third are not, because with 1 science and 1-2 resources of income K stays
at 0 or 1. It gives the evaluation a reason to prefer end-turn over a
second take that is a reason -- I cannot pay for what I already hold -- rather
than a budget or a legality bound, which the human measurement explicitly
rules out as the cause.

EXPECTED SIGN: negative. Cards I cannot pay to play are dead weight that also
antiquates (hand_perishable already prices the clock; this prices the
capacity, and the two are different questions).

LEGALITY: PASSES. My own hand.

--- 3.5  happy_margin_after_next_pop

QUANTITY: my happiness margin recomputed as if one population increase had
already happened -- i.e. the margin at (workers + 1) under the same
happiness sources -- minus nothing else; report the post-increase margin as
its own coordinate alongside the existing happy_margin. Equivalently and more
usefully as a hinge: max(0, -(margin after the next pop)), the discontent the
NEXT worker would create, 0.0 when the next worker is free of consequence.

ENGINE: economy.rs already owns the happiness and population band tables,
and effects::compute already produces the current margin; this asks the same
tables one worker forward. My own board only.

DECISION CHANGED: the population action. happy_margin, discontent and
happy_surplus all describe the board as it stands, so the bot learns whether
being unhappy is bad but never that THIS increase is the one that tips it.
Round 1 has zero population actions in 2525 human player-games and rounds
2-5 plateau at a median of exactly 1 -- a plateau is what a per-unit
consequence looks like, not what a flat per-worker weight produces.

EXPECTED SIGN: negative on the discontent-hinge form.

LEGALITY: PASSES. My own board.

UNVERIFIED: I did not open economy.rs's happiness tables. I confirmed only
that ConsumptionHeadroom / CorruptionHeadroom are yellow/blue TOKEN band
headroom (features.rs:548-556), not happiness, so this is not a duplicate of
them. Whether effects::Stats already exposes a one-worker-forward margin
cheaply is for the implementer to check before writing a second computation
of a rule -- a duplicated rules formula is exactly what MarginalNeed exists
to prevent (features.rs:265-277).

--- 3.6  row_playable_count  (is this row worth an action to ME)

QUANTITY: the number of cards in the current row that I could both legally
take AND afford to play in the near term -- concretely, count a row card if
its printed science cost <= my science + my science_rate (levelled types), or
its printed resource cost <= my resources + my resource_rate (already-
developed types / wonder first stage). A count, not a valuation.

ENGINE: the row is public; printed costs are static data; my own stocks and
rates are mine. Bounded loop over <= 13 slots.

DECISION CHANGED: 2d, from the other side. Together with the existing
ca_left this is what lets the evaluation say "hold the action, this row has
nothing I can use" -- today ca_left is a constant regardless of what the row
holds, so banking an action can never be position-dependent. It is also the
term that makes seat 1's measured behaviour representable: a fresh row full
of cheap cards I cannot yet pay to develop is a row worth one take, not four.

EXPECTED SIGN: positive. A row I can use makes spending actions good; the
learner should trade it against end_turn_bias and ca_left.

LEGALITY: PASSES. Row contents are public. Note this uses no deck ORDER and
does not ask what will refill the row -- an earlier draft of this proposal
scaled ca_left by how much of the row would turn over before my next turn,
and it is DROPPED: the refill COUNT is public but the value of a banked
action then depends on the identity of unseen cards, and the honest version
of that quantity cannot be built without deck order. Dropped at the gate
rather than weakened.

--- Explicitly NOT proposed

  - Any new per-card-TYPE scalar, and any retune of the existing Best* or
    *_board_credit family. Rejected by the brief and it is the right call:
    the credits are multipliers on board-aware prices, so a retune moves
    every card of a category together, which is the defect, not the fix.
  - A cross-action "opportunity cost" feature. The 1-ply search already
    compares every legal move including end-turn (section 2e). A feature for
    it would double-count the search.
  - A rival-hand-contents feature. Rival CIVIL hand identity is public
    (docs/RULES_SPEC.md 2.6) and rival_hand_potential already uses it
    (eval.rs:369-371); I found no gap there worth a new coordinate.


4. LEGALITY GATE -- SUMMARY
===========================

Private, per the brief: a rival's MILITARY hand identity, and the unshuffled
deck ORDER. Public: card row, played cards, all boards, discards and
discard-phase counts, and a rival's CIVIL hand identity.

  3.1 science_need_row            PASSES -- public row + static card data
  3.2 resource_commitment_turns   PASSES -- my own board only
  3.3 wonder_one_stage_short      PASSES -- my own board only
  3.4 hand_over_capacity          PASSES -- my own hand only
  3.5 happy_margin_after_next_pop PASSES -- my own board only
  3.6 row_playable_count          PASSES -- public row + my own stocks

None reads a rival's military hand. None reads deck order. One candidate
(scaling a banked civil action by the row refill ahead of my next turn) was
DROPPED at this gate because its value depends on the identity of unseen
cards; it is recorded in 3.6 so it is not re-proposed.


5. COST IN SEARCH-HOT CODE
==========================

features::features runs once per candidate move of the 1-ply search, and
feature_marginal runs once per priced unit inside card pricing. The rules
that apply: no fresh Vec allocation per call, no process-global mutable
state, no lazy cache.

CHEAP -- fold into work already being done, no allocation:
  3.2 resource_commitment_turns. The unstaffed-slot cost SUM is one extra
      accumulator in the sweep_tableau loop that already visits every
      tableau entry and already computes unbuilt_min_resource_cost. Adding a
      field to TableauSweep costs nothing; a second walk would be the wrong
      way to do it. One division at the end. NOTE: TableauSweep is shared
      with bots::neural::encode (features.rs:148-158), so a new field must
      not change what that encoder reads.
  3.3 wonder_one_stage_short. One integer comparison against a value
      features.rs:699 already has in hand.
  3.4 hand_over_capacity. One pass over the civil hand, which
      marginal_needs (features.rs:297ff) and hand_value already walk -- fold
      the counter into that existing pass rather than adding a third. Hand is
      bounded at the government's hand limit; printed-cost lookups are static
      and involve no allocation.

MODERATE -- new bounded work, must be written allocation-free:
  3.1 science_need_row and 3.6 row_playable_count. Both walk the card row
      (<= 13 slots) reading static printed costs. Each is a scalar
      accumulator over a slice -- no Vec, no collect, no sort. They should
      share ONE pass over the row producing both numbers, not two passes,
      and that pass belongs where the row is already being read. If both
      weights are 0.0 the pass must be skipped entirely, matching the gating
      idiom eval.rs uses for every non-linear block (eval.rs:336-390) and
      that features.rs already uses for event_scoring_margin.

NOT CHEAP -- needs a decision before implementation:
  3.5 happy_margin_after_next_pop. A naive implementation calls into
      economy/effects a second time to recompute stats one worker forward.
      effects::compute is already the expensive half of features() and
      rivals::need_fraction is already noted in-source as re-sweeping the
      tableau (rivals.rs, the NEED hinge comment). Doing this per candidate
      move by recomputing full Stats is the wrong shape. It is only
      acceptable if the one-worker-forward margin can be derived from the
      Stats object features() already holds by adding one worker to the
      band lookup -- a table read, not a recomputation. If it cannot, drop
      3.5 rather than pay for it; it is the least load-bearing of the six.

  Also flagged: any proposal routed through the NEED hinge instead of
  becoming a linear coordinate inherits need_fraction's cost, which calls
  effects::state_stats AND sweep_tableau AND marginal_needs per call
  (rivals.rs ~933-948). That is already gated behind "the hinge weight is
  nonzero" for a reason. A new hinge axis must keep that gate.


6. WHAT THIS NOTE DOES NOT SETTLE
=================================

The human evidence establishes WHAT humans do in round 1 (one take, stop,
bank the rest) and rules out the hand cap and legality as the cause. It does
not establish that the bot currently gets round 1 wrong, because no bot run
was permitted for this audit and none was made. Proposals 3.4 and 3.6 are
the two that would let the evaluation represent the human behaviour; whether
the learner then FINDS that behaviour is a league question, to be settled on
master against real runs, not asserted here. Nothing above should be
implemented as a hardcoded round-1 opening rule: every quantity here is an
engine fact and every value attached to it must stay fitted.
