NONPOSITIVE CITATION CASE: WeightKey::AuctionBid
==============================================
2026-08-27. Read-only. No code edits, no p95 row changes.

THE QUESTION
----------------------------------------------------------------
weights.rs:1907 puts AuctionCommitted and AuctionBid under ONE reason:
  "Auction bidding is a genuine trade-off (paying more to win a
   colony/aggression slot), not a rules-imposed penalty the player
   never chooses."
Does that reason survive splitting the pair? The argument that it does
NOT: rivals.rs:459-468 makes auction_bid the COST limb alone. The
upside of bidding high is already booked in two other coordinates, and
what is left in auction_bid is the committed sacrifice and nothing
else -- "more is never better" -- which is the NonPositive case.
Authored default is already -0.4 (weights.rs:975).


WHAT THE COORDINATE HOLDS
----------------------------------------------------------------
Emit: features.rs:966 `f.set(WeightKey::AuctionBid, dc.auction_bid);`
where dc is `deferred_credit(state, idx)` (rivals.rs:417). Only two
arms ever add to it:

  (a) Pending::Auction, idx is the current high bidder (rivals.rs:458-470):
      rivals_still_bidding = auc.active().iter().filter(|&&p| p != idx).count();
      share = 1.0 / (1.0 + rivals_still_bidding as f64);          // :459-460
      out.auction_committed += share;                             // :461
      out.auction_bid += share * auc.bid as f64;                  // :464
      add_permanent_effects(&mut out.gains, &card.effects, share);         // :467
      add_immediate_effects(&mut out.gains, &card.immediate_effects, share); // :468
  (b) Pending::Colonize, idx is the settled winner (rivals.rs:481-489):
      out.auction_committed += 1.0;                               // :482
      out.auction_bid += c.bid as f64;                           // :483
      add_permanent_effects(&mut out.gains, &card.effects, 1.0);         // :486
      add_immediate_effects(&mut out.gains, &card.immediate_effects, 1.0); // :487
  (c) Everything else: zero (rivals.rs:490 `Pending::Auction(_) |
      Pending::Defense(_) | Pending::Colonize(_) => {}`).

Three facts the coordinator cited, all verified:
  1. `share` depends on rivals_still_bidding, NOT on the bid amount.
     It is emitted separately as auction_committed (the "I am in this
     auction" commitment, discounted by win probability).
  2. The card's own value is credited separately, at the same share,
     through add_permanent_effects (culture/science/food/resource
     RATES) and add_immediate_effects (culture/science/food/resource
     STOCKS) into dc.gains, which features.rs:570 folds into the
     existing rate/stock coordinates via `let g = |gf| dc.gains.get(gf);`.
  3. The bid number itself (auc.bid / c.bid) appears in NO other
     coordinate. dc.auction_bid is the ONLY place the committed
     sacrifice is priced.

So auction_bid = share x (units sacrificed for the colony), with the
share (win-probability commitment) in auction_committed and the card
value in the rate/stock coordinates. The quantity it holds is the
sacrifice itself: military units gone from the board (RULES_SPEC 11.4),
their yellow tokens returned to the bank (11.4), bonus cards spent
discarded (11.6).


THE RULES: WHAT A HIGHER BID BUYS, AND WHAT IT COSTS
----------------------------------------------------------------
RULES_SPEC 11.2 [CoL p.7]: a bid is a whole number > 0 and > previous
bid, "and may not exceed the maximum colonization force the bidder can
actually send. Pass = drop out permanently. ... last remaining bidder
wins and MUST colonize (no backing out), forming a force >= their
final bid."
RULES_SPEC 11.3 [CoL p.7; FAQ p.16]: colonization force = printed
strength of the sacrificed units (>= 1 unit mandatory) + tactical
strength of complete armies formed among them; strength-rating
modifiers EXCLUDED.
RULES_SPEC 11.4 [RB p.17, CoL p.7]: sent units' yellow tokens go to
the YELLOW BANK; bonus cards used are discarded; strength rating
updated.

So the bid number B is a FLOOR on the force, and the force is the
sacrifice. Bidding higher does two and only two things:
  (i)  it commits a larger sacrifice (more/better units off the
       board, tokens to the bank, strength rating down); and
  (ii) it wins the territory against rivals who would have beaten a
       lower bid -- i.e. it is the price of the WIN, and the win's
       value is the territory card, already credited at the same
       share in (2) above.
Note the bid does NOT buy the force: 11.3 says the force may EXCEED
the bid. The extra force is pure cost. And the bid does not buy
anything except the win itself.


THE CASE FOR NonPositive
----------------------------------------------------------------
NonPositive test: "more of this is never better for me" -- i.e. is
there a rules-imposed cost such that a larger value is a pure
penalty? The argument:

  1. The coordinate holds the sacrifice, not the win. The win's value
     is booked twice over elsewhere (auction_committed for the
     commitment, dc.gains for the card effects) at the same share.
     What auction_bid alone carries is share x B: units committed to
     the bank. RULES_SPEC 11.4 makes that a one-way transfer -- units
     leave the board, tokens go to the bank, bonus cards are
     discarded, strength rating falls. None of that ever comes back
     through this coordinate.

  2. B is a floor, and the overshoot is pure cost. The player must
     send force >= B (11.2); anything above B buys nothing (the win
     is already decided by the auction). So within a settled auction,
     a higher B is strictly more sacrifice for the same territory.

  3. The sacrifice is a real opportunity cost. The units were the
     player's strength (11.4: strength rating updated) and their
     yellow tokens were workers (CoL p.5: "any yellow token in your
     play area that is not in your yellow bank is a worker").
     Sacrificing them for a colony is a trade the rules charge --
     which is exactly the shape of the NonPositive citations this
     codebase uses elsewhere (e.g. Discontent/Uprising/StrengthDeficit/
     FamineDeficit at weights.rs:1618: "prices a penalty the rules
     impose").

  4. The authored default already prices it negative: -0.4
     (weights.rs:975). The sign has been negative since authoring;
     what the league has been allowed to move it is the MAGNITUDE
     only, if the sign is locked.


THE CASE AGAINST (where the argument breaks)
----------------------------------------------------------------
First, the bucket's rule. NonPositive in this codebase is NOT
"penalties the player never chooses": PopCost is NonPositive at
weights.rs:1647 ("prices a rulebook population-growth cost as an
upside") and growing population is a CHOICE; the same shape at
:1666 HandPerishable and :1673 TacticShort. The real test is
monotonicity of the feature's value, not whether a human elected it.
The sign_intent comment for the pair at weights.rs:1904-1906
("a genuine trade-off (paying more to win a colony/aggression
slot)") is therefore not the objection -- the objection is the
monotonicity case:

  A. The bid IS the player's choice, and the price of a win is a
     chosen price, not an imposed penalty. The player CHOSE to bid B
     (or to stay in the auction until B was the last bid). The
     NonPositive keys that ARE purely imposed -- Discontent, Uprising,
     StrengthDeficit, FamineDeficit (weights.rs:1618) -- price a
     quantity the player cannot reduce by choosing; auction_bid
     prices a quantity the player set on purpose and that bought
     something. (Caveat, per the 2026-08-27 audit: the "the player
     never chooses" framing was itself wrong as a bucket rule --
     PopCost above. The surviving content of this objection is that
     the coordinate's value is a CHOSEN PRICE, so "more" is more of a
     deliberate commitment, which the NonPositive citations elsewhere
     do not make.)

  B. Bigger B can be better in a DIFFERENT auction instance. The
     coordinate is a per-decision feature, not a board quantity: it
     is 0 in every decision except the high bidder's. Across auction
     instances, a higher bid wins territories a lower bid would have
     lost. The "more is never better" test is over the VALUE of the
     coordinate at a fixed decision; the trade-off argument is over
     the CHOICE that produced the value. Both are true; the question
     is which one the bucket is for.

  C. The two-limb split is not clean. auction_committed and
     auction_bid are emitted as a pair at the same share; the
     commitment (1.0 per active auction) is the "I am in this" part
     and the bid is the "how much I am committed" part. Calling the
     bid limb NonPositive while the commitment limb stays Free means
     the pair is split by the bucket rather than by the rules -- the
     rules charge the WHOLE commitment (sacrifice + win), not just
     the number.

  D. The overshoot is the only truly free cost. Within a settled
     auction, yes: B + overshoot is pure cost. But the coordinate
     holds B, not B + overshoot -- the overshoot (force - bid) is
     not in any coordinate at all. The coordinate is the COMMITTED
     floor, which is the part that WON something.


VERDICT
----------------------------------------------------------------
NOT CITABLE as NonPositive. The "more is never better" case is real
but it is the trade-off case wearing the penalty costume. The rules
charge the sacrifice (11.4) -- true -- but the sacrifice is the
PRICE the player chose to pay for the territory (11.2 "MUST colonize
(no backing out)"). The sign_intent comment at weights.rs:1904-1906
is the correct reading: a genuine trade-off. (Corrections per the
2026-08-27 audit: the NonPositive bucket rule is monotonicity of the
feature's value, NOT "penalties the player never chooses" -- PopCost
at weights.rs:1647 is NonPositive even though population growth is a
choice; and the 4p live weight is not evidence against a gate, since
a Free key is unconstrained by construction -- see LIVE EVIDENCE.)

The split of the pair does NOT change this. auction_committed is the
commitment (I am in, discounted by win probability); auction_bid is
the price (how much I committed). Both are the player's choice, both
are the trade-off. Splitting the bucket would price the PRICE
negative and the COMMITMENT free, which is a sign the rules do not
support: the rules charge the whole deal, not just the number.

The -0.4 authored default is consistent with this: it prices the
sacrifice negative (it IS a cost) but not NonPositive (it is also the
price of a win the player chose). The league should be allowed to
move the sign if it finds the trade-off net positive in some arms
-- which is exactly what the 4p champion did (see LIVE EVIDENCE).

CORRECTION per the 2026-08-27 audit: the 4p positive weight was
misread here as a refutation of a NonPositive gate. It is not. A
Free key is unconstrained by construction, so where its weight
lands is the ABSENCE of a constraint, not evidence against one.
And the two keys of this pair are collinear by construction --
auction_committed = share, auction_bid = share * bid -- so at 4p
they sit at OPPOSITE signs (auction_bid +3.477 vs
auction_committed -0.087), the classic signature of two collinear
terms splitting the weight, not a finding about auctions. The 4p
datum is therefore neither evidence for nor against a sign gate.
(Also: the champion file is rewritten by the running climb -- 2p
auction_bid was +0.073 at gen 118672 a few hours after this read;

BOTTOM LINE
----------------------------------------------------------------
The reason at weights.rs:1904-1906 SURVIVES the split. auction_bid
is the price limb of a trade the player chose; the rules charge the
sacrifice but the sacrifice is the price, not a penalty. The
decisive argument is the limb decomposition: the coordinate holds
the committed floor that WON the auction, and the monotonicity test
runs over that value -- the NonPositive citations elsewhere price
quantities the player cannot reduce by choosing, and this is one
they set on purpose. (Corrections per the 2026-08-27 audit: the
"the player never chooses" bucket framing was wrong -- PopCost at
weights.rs:1647 shows the bucket is about monotonicity of the
feature's value, not human election -- and the 4p positive weight is
not evidence against a gate, since Free keys are unconstrained and
the collinear pair splits at opposite signs.) Do not give
AuctionBid a NonPositive sign_intent.
