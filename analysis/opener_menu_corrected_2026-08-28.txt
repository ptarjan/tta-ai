--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE CODE, VERBATIM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
rust/src/bots/weighted/eval.rs, `Weighted::choose`, lines 493-499:

    if !matches!(mv, Move::EndTurn) {
        apply::apply(&mut trial, mv);
    }
    let mut val = evaluate(&trial, idx, w, Some(&ctx), None);
    if matches!(mv, Move::EndTurn) {
        val += end_bias;
    }

Every candidate is scored at the SAME point in time, mid-turn, before this
turn's production. `Move::EndTurn` is the only move whose apply arm reaches
`economy::end_of_turn` (apply.rs -> game::end_turn -> resume_end_turn), so
applying it here would score it against a board that already holds this
turn's food and resources while every rival candidate is still charged the
full negative food_gap/resource_gap (block comment, eval.rs:482-492).
EndTurn is scored on the UNMOVED root, and end_turn_bias is what prices
ending a turn.

Consequences for the delta table (delta = candidate score minus evaluate(root)):
  * EndTurn:   delta = end_turn_bias            = -4.619540
  * Take(c):   delta = sum over keys of w * (feature_after_take - feature_root)
The old file's +1.98 EndTurn delta (production +2/+2, MA reset, science
scoring) is spurious: none of it is in EndTurn's score.

4. THE QUESTION: DOES THE BOT FORGO A TAKE IN ROUND 1?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Headline: seat 0, CA=1.

  Take s=0, h=0:  +1.372073 vs EndTurn -4.619540  -> Take wins by 5.99
  Take s=0, h=1:  +2.569369                        -> Take wins by 7.19
  Take s=1, h=0:  -1.060256 vs -4.619540           -> Take wins by 3.56
  Take s=2, h=0:  -3.492585                        -> Take wins by 1.13
  Take s=3, h=0:  -5.924914                        -> EndTurn wins by 1.31
  Take s=3, h=1:  -4.727618                        -> EndTurn wins by 0.11
  Take s=3, h=2:  -3.530322                        -> Take wins by 1.09

The bot forgoes a take in round 1 ONLY when the best affordable row card is a
3-science tech whose hand_potential increment is low. Every 1- or 2-science
tech, and every Action/Leader/Wonder (s=0), is preferred over EndTurn by a
wide margin. So at seat 0 the round-1 menu says TAKE (except in the thin
s=3/h~0 corner), and the known civil-action waste is NOT explained by seat 0
round 1 at all.

Seat 1 (CA=2) -- the highest-CA seat, the explicit second case:

  Take c=2, s=0, h=0:  -1.227211 vs -4.619540  -> Take wins by 3.39
  Take c=1, s=0, h=0:  -2.634841               -> Take wins by 1.98
  Take c=1, s=1, h=0:  -5.067170               -> EndTurn wins by 0.45
  Take c=2, s=1, h=0:  -3.659540               -> Take wins by 0.96

The conclusion CHANGES for seat 1: with two CA the CAS hit doubles
(-4.196858 vs -2.098429), EndTurn becomes the top-ranked move in several
slices (any take with s=1 and c=1 and h=0; all s>=2 techs), and even the
best take only wins by ~3.4. Seat 1 is where a round-1 forgo is plausible
from this menu alone; seat 0 essentially always takes.

But note the seat-1 caveat: the take here is a 1- or 2-CA spend of a pool
that resets at end of turn anyway. The CAS feature prices that spend as if
the surplus were permanent, which is why the seat-1 menu leans so hard
toward EndTurn. That is a feature-design observation, not a defect claim
(no sign_intent proposed, per standing rules).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. ROW-DEPENDENT TERMS AND BOUNDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
R = RowPlayableCount baseline (count of row slots that are take-legal AND
playable at have+rate = 1). R is identical in root and in every Take trial
(the row does not change during a turn; take_gate's have is recomputed from
p.civil_actions but R counts legal slots, and a slot that is playable in the
root is still playable in the trial -- the taken slot's card is in the hand,
not the row). So R * 0.244164 cancels from every delta. Likewise
science_need_row (row + science + rate all unchanged) cancels.
The only row/card-dependent term left is hand_potential (+1.197296 per unit
of increment h), which is card-specific and >= 0. The per-seat tables above
are therefore exact modulo h; the bounds use h = 0 (worst case for the
Take) and the natural card-specific values.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. NEGATIVE RESULT, STATED PLAINLY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Under the CORRECT comparison (EndTurn scored on the unmoved root plus
end_turn_bias, per eval.rs:493-499), the corrected 2p round-1 ranking says
the bot SHOULD take in round 1:

  * Seat 0 (CA=1): every take with s <= 2 beats EndTurn by at least 1.13;
    s = 0 beats it by 5.99+. The forgo happens only for a 3-science tech
    with a low hand_potential increment -- a thin corner.
  * Seat 1 (CA=2): the menu is genuinely mixed; EndTurn can be top-ranked
    (s=1/c=1/h=0 slice, all s>=2 techs), but the best take still wins by
    3.39.

Therefore the known civil-action waste (rounds where the champion leaves CA
on the table) must be happening in LATER ROUNDS, or in seat-1 round 1 in the
specific slices above, not in the seat-0 round 1 the old file analysed. The
old file's +1.98 EndTurn "windfall" was an artefact of applying end_of_turn
to EndTurn's own trial; the real EndTurn delta is exactly end_turn_bias
(-4.619540), and it LOSES to most takes in round 1.

Mechanism 2 from the previous file survives: CivilActionSurplus really does
drop by 2 under a take (CaLeft -1, HandCivil +1, features.rs:1199-1202
`CivilActionSurplus = max(0, CaLeft - HandCivil)`), at -2.098429 per unit
= -4.196858 at seat 1 -- and it is the reason seat 1's menu leans toward
EndTurn. But it is not strong enough at seat 0 to produce a forgo.
