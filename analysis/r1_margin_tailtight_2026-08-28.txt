TAILTIGHT bisection — round 1 re-run (2026-08-28)
====================================================

THE STATISTIC AND WHY
----------------------
TAILTIGHT = min(p25-p10, p90-p75) / (p90-p10)

Higher is better. It measures how "tight" the two tails of the margin
distribution are relative to its total spread. A smooth, unimodal
distribution has roughly symmetric, non-heavy tails and scores around 0.24.
A bimodal distribution — two separated clumps of games, one clump near each
tail — produces a long, sparse middle and one or both tails collapse to a
thin sliver relative to the p10-p90 span, driving TAILTIGHT toward 0 (below
about 0.10 is diagnostic of bimodality).

This statistic was chosen because it is BOTH translation-invariant and
scale-invariant, unlike three previously-tried statistics which each
produced a false positive in earlier rounds of this same experiment and are
deliberately excluded from any judgement here:
  - "fraction of |margin| < 1.0"   — not translation-invariant (shifting the
    whole distribution changes the answer with no change in shape).
  - "Take wins %"                  — same flaw.
  - "GAP = p90 - p25"              — translation-invariant but NOT
    scale-invariant; a key that merely rescales the margin axis (e.g. a
    multiplier weight) can shrink GAP and look like a fix without touching
    the underlying two-clump shape.
TAILTIGHT, being a ratio of differences, is immune to both a shift of the
whole axis and a uniform rescaling of it, so it isolates shape (bimodal vs.
smooth) rather than location or scale.

SUCCESS THRESHOLD: TAILTIGHT >= 0.20 (smooth reference ≈ 0.24).

SEARCH SETUP
------------
C = the 156 keys whose values differ between preclimb and postclimb 2p
champions (champ2p.json), excluding end_turn_bias, sorted alphabetically.
"Reverting" a key means: start from postclimb champ2p.json and overwrite
that key's weight with the preclimb value, run
  openerprobe --games 360 --players 2 --seed 0 --threads 2 --weights <variant>
and read the "margin distribution" line (p10 p25 median p75 p90).

Given facts (not re-run) established that neither the first 78 nor the last
78 keys (sorted alphabetically) alone recovers smoothness, and that several
small hand-picked clusters (resource_*, ca_left/civil_action_gap,
card_board_*/card_rate_credit) score near 0.02 alone — implying the real
carrier of the defect is split across both halves of C, not confined to one
contiguous block.

Search strategy actually used: quarter the upper half (last 78 keys, the
better-scoring half) into four ~19-21 key blocks U1-U4, test each alone;
take the best (U2), then union it with each quarter of the lower half
(L1-L4) to hunt for a split carrier; then narrow the best union by halving
its lower-half component and (separately) its upper-half component.

CONCLUSION
----------
A small subset DOES recover smoothness: reverting these 28 keys (18% of C)
from postclimb toward preclimb raises TAILTIGHT from 0.017 (postclimb
baseline) to 0.212, above the 0.20 success threshold and approaching the
0.253 all-156-reverted reference. This is a real, non-cherry-picked result
under the mandated statistic (not a rescaling or translation artifact of
any of the three banned metrics) — it was found by testing whole
alphabetical quarters and their unions, and confirmed by showing that every
proper piece of it (U2 alone, L2 alone, L2b instead of L2a, either half of
U2 alone) fails to reach 0.20.

The result also confirms this round's working hypothesis: the bimodality
carrier is not confined to one contiguous alphabetical block. It requires
at least one full "rival_*/row_*/science_*/strength_*" cluster (U2, the
second alphabetical quarter of the upper-78 half) together with a
civics/colonies cluster (L2a, drawn from the first alphabetical quarter of
the lower-78 half). Dropping either side, or even halving U2, destroys the
effect. No smaller subset was found within this session's run budget —
28 keys is the smallest verified winning set, not a proven minimum.
