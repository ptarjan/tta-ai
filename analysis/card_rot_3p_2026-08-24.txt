Card rot: was a never-played card ever LEGAL to play?
=====================================================
Date: 2026-08-24
Tool: rust/src/bin/behavcensus.rs, "Card fate follow-up" section
Run:  ./target/release/behavcensus --games 200 --players 3 \
        --weights /private/tmp/opencensus/champ3p.json --threads 5

WHAT THIS ANSWERS
-----------------
analysis/card_fate_3p_2026-08-24.txt found that ~39% of every civil card the
bot takes is never played. analysis/card_fate_human_2026-08-24.txt (commit
d464068) showed that is a real defect: at 3p humans take 26.92 civil cards per
player-game and play 78.3% of them, the bot takes 17.49 and plays 61.0%. Wrong
on both axes, and they compound.

That left one question, and it had exactly two candidate answers:
  (a) the TAKE is mispriced -- the bot buys cards it should not buy; or
  (b) DEVELOP is priced too low relative to the take -- the bot buys fine cards
      and then loses the civil-action auction to something else every single
      turn until the card is culled or the game ends.

The discriminator is: for every card that was never played, on how many of that
player's own later decision points was Develop/PlayLeader/Revolution/PlayAction
of that card actually in the legal move list?

HOW IT IS MEASURED
------------------
The census calls legal::legal_moves(&state) on its own state immediately before
game::step, which is the SAME call every Seat::pick opens with, on the same
state -- so it reproduces exactly the move list the bot chose from, rather than
re-deriving an affordability check that could disagree with the engine. Card
identity comes from the file's existing played_civil_card helper, not a second
copy of that logic. "playable-turns" travels inside the existing taken_rounds
queue entry (struct TakenCard) rather than in a parallel collection.

PLAYED cards are the control: how many turns a card sat legal before it was
actually played.

RESULT
------
EARLY (n=192 player-games):
  playable-turns, PLAYED cards (control):  p25=1 median=1 p75=3 mean=2.80 n=1914
  playable-turns, NEVER-played cards:      p25=0 median=0 p75=1 mean=2.24 n=1480
  942 of 1480 never-played cards (63.6%) were NEVER legal to play again.

LATE (n=163 player-games):
  playable-turns, PLAYED cards (control):  p25=1 median=1 p75=3 mean=2.87 n=1712
  playable-turns, NEVER-played cards:      p25=0 median=0 p75=2 mean=2.42 n=1353
  830 of 1353 never-played cards (61.3%) were NEVER legal to play again.

THE ANSWER IS (a), AND IT IS NOT THE ANSWER EITHER BRANCH PREDICTED
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Roughly five eighths of the wasted cards were never buildable at any point
after the take. They did not lose an auction. They were never on the ballot.

This is stable across both tempo groups (63.6% / 61.3%), so it is not an
artifact of the early/late wonder split.

The remaining ~37% did lose the CA auction and are the (b) population, so
develop-vs-take pricing is a real but secondary effect.

WHY THE CA-EXHAUSTION CONFOUND DOES NOT EXPLAIN IT
--------------------------------------------------
A decision point at which the player has no civil actions left offers no
Develop at all, so it counts as "not legal" for every card in hand. That
inflates the zero bucket -- but it cannot produce this number. The median
never-played card sits in hand 5-6 rounds, which is on the order of 25 decision
points. Scoring exactly zero across all of them requires the card to be
genuinely unbuildable throughout, not merely to catch a few CA-exhausted
moments.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT YET SAY
--------------------------
"Never legal" has more than one cause and this census does not separate them:
  - the civilization never had the resources, for this card or for any card
    (an economy defect: the bot is too poor to build what it owns); versus
  - other cards WERE developable at those same decision points and this one
    specifically was too expensive (a card-selection defect: the take gate
    prices the take, which is positional -- see the take-cost analysis -- and
    never asks whether the build will ever be affordable); versus
  - a non-resource blocker (missing prerequisite, government action limit).

The discriminating follow-up is cheap and sits in the same block: at each
decision point where this card was NOT legal, was ANY Develop legal?

DO NOT FIT WEIGHTS TO THE HUMAN NUMBERS. The human census is a diagnostic
mirror only.

KNOWN DEFECT IN THIS RUN
------------------------
6 of 6459 cards (0.09%) were played or antiquated with no matching take entry
-- the census's own tracking, not a sample gap. Too small to move any share
reported here.
