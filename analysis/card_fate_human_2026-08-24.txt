Card fate, human corpus vs bot: the bot takes fewer cards AND wastes more of
them. The 39% never-played figure IS a defect.
=====================================================================
Measured 2026-08-24 with `humanopenings` over the 1011-game BGO corpus at
/private/tmp/bgo-journals/journals. The bot side is
analysis/card_fate_3p_2026-08-24.txt (200 games of 3p self-play against the
frozen champ3p). This file is the mirror that decides whether that 39% means
anything.

Definitions are deliberately IDENTICAL to the bot side
------------------------------------------------------
PLAYED = the card left hand_civil via Develop / PlayLeader / Revolution /
PlayAction. `played_civil_card` and `hand_multiset_diff` were copied from
bin/behavcensus.rs rather than reimplemented, precisely so the two percentages
are the same statistic. Dwell is per CARD IDENTITY through a FIFO of
take-rounds. Only games with GameResult::completed are counted; skipped
player-games are COUNTED and printed, never silently dropped.

This is a DIAGNOSTIC MIRROR. A divergence from human play says where to look.
It is not, and must never become, a target to fit weights against.

2p: taken 31.30, played 77.3%.  4p: taken 27.41, played 78.0%.  The human

both take MORE cards and play a HIGHER fraction of them than losers. The
direction is consistent and it is the opposite of the bot's behaviour on both
axes.

Two findings
------------
1. THE BOT TAKES ~35% FEWER CIVIL CARDS THAN HUMANS AT THE SAME TABLE SIZE
   (17.5 vs 26.9 per player-game). This is not a game-length artifact: TTA's
   length is set by deck exhaustion, and the row is replenished at a fixed rate
   per round per player count, so a 3p game runs about the same number of
   rounds whoever is playing. The bot is leaving cards on the row to slide off
   the end.

2. AND IT PLAYS A MUCH SMALLER FRACTION OF WHAT IT DOES TAKE (61% vs 78%).
   These compound. Per player-game the bot converts ~10.7 taken cards into
   played cards; a human converts ~21. The bot is not merely taking less, it is
   taking WORSE - its marginal take is one it will not use.

So 39% never-played is a real defect, not "how the game goes". The prior file
left this question open on purpose and the mirror has now closed it.

What it does NOT tell us
------------------------
Which way the causation runs. Taking fewer cards and playing a smaller share of
them are both consistent with (a) the take being priced too high, so only
already-marginal cards clear the bar, or (b) the DEVELOP side being priced too
low relative to the take, so taken cards lose the CA auction every turn
afterwards and rot in hand. The bot's never-played dwell of 5 rounds against
the human's 7 is a hint but not a decision: the bot's games end with fewer
cards in hand, so its censoring is tighter. Distinguishing (a) from (b) is the
next measurement, and it is a take-side/play-side pricing question, which is
exactly where analysis/wonder_progress_ab_3p_2026-08-24.txt pointed after the
wonder subspace closed.

Two known defects in this census's own tracking - read before quoting finer
numbers
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
ANTIQUATED READS 0 AT EVERY TABLE SIZE. That is certainly false; hands are
culled at every age transition. The bot side detects antiquation by a pre/post
hand_civil multiset diff across the one move that advances state.age_civil, and
in the replayer that transition evidently does not happen INSIDE a recorded
decision - the same shape as the war-resolution timing already documented
(resolve_war_outcome fires inside start_turn, not at the line that names it).
Cards culled that way stay in the tracking map and are counted in "still in
hand at game end".

  This does NOT affect the played / never-played split above, which is the
  headline: PLAYED is detected by an explicit removal site, and everything else
  is the complement. It DOES mean the human never-played bucket cannot be split
  into destroyed-by-antiquation vs stranded-in-hand, and that the human "cards
  in hand at game end = 6" is inflated by the culled cards. Do not compare that
  row against the bot's 3.5-3.9 - it is not the same quantity.

MISMATCHES: 468 of 9044 cards at 3p (5.2%), 2529 of 39569 at 2p (6.4%), 763 of
15130 at 4p (5.0%) were played with no matching take recorded. Some path puts a
civil card into hand without a Move::Take. The bot side's counterpart is 3 of
6177 (0.05%), so this is corpus-replay-specific, not shared instrumentation
rot. Mismatched cards are counted in NEITHER the played nor the never-played
bucket (taken = played + still-in-hand exactly), so they do not skew the shares;
they mean the true human take count is somewhat higher than 26.92, which would
only widen finding 1.
