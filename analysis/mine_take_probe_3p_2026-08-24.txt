Mine-take probe, 3 players -- WHY does a non-starting Mine card die?
=====================================================================
Date: 2026-08-24
Tool: rust/src/bin/takeprobe.rs (new, read-only diagnostic binary; does not
      touch legal.rs/costs.rs/eval.rs/bots logic or data/*.json).
Run:  cd rust && ./target/release/takeprobe --games 200 --players 3 \
        --weights /private/tmp/opencensus/champ3p.json --threads 5
      (release build; `cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D warnings` and
      `cargo test` both green before the build -- see the gate output this
      task required.)

WHAT THIS FOLLOWS UP
---------------------
analysis/tech_acquisition_3p_2026-08-24.txt measured, over 600 self-play
player-games under the champ3p.json champion, a non-starting Mine card
(Iron/Coal/Oil) SEEN in the card row 3.00/player-game but TAKEN (via
behavcensus.rs's pre-move `taken_card` variable) an exact 0.000 at every age
tier. That census explicitly did not trace WHY (its own caveat 6). This
probe does: at every decision point where a civil take is structurally
possible (`state.phase == Phase::Actions`, `state.pending.is_empty()`), for
every non-starting Mine card sitting in `state.card_row`, it independently
checks four pipeline stages -- SEEN / LEGAL (in `legal::legal_moves`) /
SCORED (survives into the candidate set `WeightedBot` evaluates) / and, if
scored, its value, the winner's value, and its rank -- via
`bots::weighted::eval::candidate_features`/`dot`, never touching
`behavcensus.rs`'s `taken_card` mechanism at all.

MID-TASK RETARGET (control run, reported by the coordinator, not this
probe): the coordinator ran the SAME `tech_acquisition` census on the
already-built `behavcensus` with a DIFFERENT weight vector
(`analysis/frozen/human_weights.json`), 200 games/3p:
  Mine: seen 2.98, taken 0.60, built 1.38, staffed 1.34
  Mine taken by tier: A=0.000 I=0.000 II=0.272 III=0.328
This proves the Take-a-Mine code path is mechanically alive in general: it
is legal-generated, survives to the candidate set, and gets CHOSEN under at
least one weight vector. `legal.rs::action_moves` takes only `state`/`p`, no
weights at all, so stages 1-2 (SEEN, LEGAL) are weight-independent by
construction and this control is strong evidence stages 1-3 are not where
champ3p's zero comes from either. This probe's own run below confirms that
directly, against champ3p.json itself, rather than resting on the control
alone.

STAGE VERDICT
-------------
* Stage 1 (SEEN): 24,412 decision-point x card occurrences across 200
  games -- the row is full of mine cards constantly, as the original census
  already found.
* Stage 2 (LEGAL): 12,299 of 24,412 (50.4%) -- roughly half the time a take
  is structurally legal (`costs::can_take_gated` passes: hand not full,
  enough spare CA, card not a duplicate, etc.). NOT a "never legal" problem.
* Stage 3 (SCORED): 12,299 of 12,299 legal occurrences (100.0%) survive into
  the candidate set `WeightedBot` actually evaluates. This is not
  incidental: `WeightedBot::choose`/`rank_moves`/`candidate_features` all
  start from `bots::filter_resign` (rust/src/bots/mod.rs), which removes
  ONLY `Move::Resign`, never a `Take` -- there is NO shortlist/narrowing
  stage between move generation and evaluation for this bot kind (unlike a
  beam/shortlist search). Stage 2 and stage 3 are therefore structurally
  identical whenever stage 2 holds, confirmed empirically (100.000% every
  time), not merely assumed from reading the code.
* Stage 4 (SCORED but ranked): this is where it dies. Across all 12,299
  scored occurrences, a Mine take's best-ever rank was 3rd (Oil) or 5th
  (Iron/Coal) -- IT NEVER ONCE RANKED 1ST OR 2ND, let alone won. Mean rank
  17.87 (this probe's decision points typically offer several dozen legal
  moves). Mean score gap to the winner ~190 evaluator-score units, and it is
  NOT small relative to the scale of the evaluator's own outputs in this
  position class.

THE BORING EXPLANATION, CHECKED AND RULED OUT
-----------------------------------------------
Is the original census's exact 0.000 an artifact of `behavcensus.rs`'s
pre-move `taken_card` variable rather than a real "never chosen"? NO. This
probe never reads or computes anything shaped like `taken_card` -- it
independently re-derives the bot's own argmax over
`candidate_features`/`dot` (proven, by `eval.rs`'s own doc comment and
tests, to reproduce `evaluate`'s exact score) at every decision point, and
finds the SAME thing the census found: across 12,299 independent
observations, the argmax is a Mine take in exactly 0 of them (best rank
ever achieved is 3rd). Two independent measurement mechanisms agree. This
is a real, reproducible "scored and always outranked" phenomenon, not an
instrumentation artifact of the earlier census.

THE FILE:LINE THIS ACCUSES
----------------------------
The row/take-specific pricing machinery -- `WeightKey::RowUrgency`,
`RowBargainForgone`, `RowLastCopy`, `RivalDesire`, `RivalTakeShare` -- is
NOT in the top 15. The dominant terms are instead the GENERAL board-rate/
stock/strength/worker/culture/science keys, which respond to what is
already BUILT AND STAFFED, not to what merely sits in hand. `TakeCostPaid`
(what the take itself costs) is a minor contributor (#14 of 15), so the
gap is not primarily "the take is priced too expensive" -- it is "the
alternatives pay off THIS ply and the take does not."

A freshly-taken Mine card's entire future value has to be captured by
`HandPotential` (the #1 contributor), computed at
rust/src/bots/weighted/cards.rs:2095 (`hand_potential`) ->
rust/src/bots/weighted/cards.rs:2055 (`hand_total`) ->
rust/src/bots/weighted/cards.rs:1852 (`card_potential`) ->
rust/src/bots/weighted/cards.rs:1863 (`card_potential_core`), whose
levelled-tech branch at rust/src/bots/weighted/cards.rs:1879-1880
(`else if tb != 0.0 && board_yields::is_levelled_type(kind) { return tb *
tech_value(id, st, ix, w, 1.0, late); }`) dispatches to `tech_value` at
rust/src/bots/weighted/cards.rs:1257, which prices the tech's FULL
staffed/developed board value in one shot (`priced_marginal` against the
current board, scaled only by a flat `late` lateness multiplier), competing
against candidates whose rate/stock/strength/worker gain is REAL and
immediate in the SAME 1-ply trial.

This project's own code already names and partially fixes exactly this
failure mode for a DIFFERENT card class: `wonder_potential`
(rust/src/bots/weighted/cards.rs:2148-2150) and `wonder_promise`
(rust/src/bots/weighted/cards.rs:2152 onward) carry an explicit doc comment
calling this "build-now bias" (citing `docs/AGREEMENT.md`: "at the exact
points a human advanced a wonder, the bot's own top pick was a plain build
59% of the time") and cite the fix -- `horizon::WonderOutlook::earned_share`
/ `promise_share` -- a computed discount for "how much of the remaining
game you would actually own this for," replacing the old implicit "you have
it already, forever" assumption. `tech_value` (cards.rs:1257), which prices
every ordinary levelled tech including Mine, has no analogous
per-ply-reachability discount visible in its body: it prices the full
staffed value directly, off the SAME flat `late` lateness term wonder
pricing used to use before `earned_share`/`promise_share` were added.

VERDICT: DEFECT OR GENUINE PREFERENCE?
-----------------------------------------
Not dead code, and not an instrumentation artifact (both checked and ruled
out above). The failure is real and lives entirely in stage 4 (scored,
always outranked) under champ3p.json -- confirmed directly against the
actual champion vector, not just inferred from the human-weights control.

Whether it is a defect: this probe did not run an ablation (forbidden --
touching weights is out of scope), so it cannot PROVE `tech_value`'s missing
reachability discount is causally sufficient to explain the whole gap. What
it CAN say: the ranked WeightKey list is consistent with exactly the
"build-now bias" class this codebase already diagnosed and partially fixed
for wonders, the row/take-specific pricing keys are NOT implicated (ruling
out a mispriced take-cost or row-desirability term), and the shape --
scored, never once ranking above 3rd across 12,299 occurrences, beaten by a
BROAD mix of same-ply-payoff moves rather than one dominant rival -- is the
signature of a structural 1-ply-horizon pricing gap, not a narrow
preference against mines specifically (Farm, in the original census, shows
the same taken-but-then-mostly-not-built shape at a smaller scale; this
probe was scoped to Mine only, per the task).

CAVEATS
-------
1. `mean_gap`/rank/best_rank are measured outcomes in the evaluator's own
   score units, reported because the task explicitly asked for them --
   not a proposed weight or coefficient for any `WeightKey`.
2. The WeightKey contribution ranking is a per-decision-point LINEAR
   decomposition (`candidate_features`/`dot`). Eleven `WeightKey`s
   (`HandPotential` among them -- see `eval.rs`'s own doc comment on
   `linear_features`) are bilinear in the full weight vector rather than
   linear in a single coordinate; `candidate_features` freezes them at the
   SAME champion vector being evaluated, matching what `bin/agreefit.rs`
   already relies on to reproduce `evaluate`'s score exactly -- so the
   ranking is faithful to what actually happened in this run, not an
   approximation error.
3. This is descriptive, not a proof of root cause: the `tech_value`/
   `wonder_potential` contrast is the most plausible mechanism the ranked
   keys point to, cited by file:line, but no code was changed and no
   coefficient is proposed to test it.
4. `L-rate` (50.4%) means roughly half of all "mine visible at a
   take-possible decision point" occurrences are not even legal to take
   (hand full, insufficient spare CA, etc.) -- a real, separate, and
   unsurprising gate; this file's stage-4 finding is about the OTHER half,
   where a take was legal and scored.

DO NOT FIT WEIGHTS TO THE HUMAN NUMBERS.
This file names a defect (or, at minimum, a striking and reproducible
weights-side pricing phenomenon consistent with a known, partially-fixed
bug class in this codebase) in why the champion's own evaluator never picks
a legal, scored Mine take. It proposes no weight value, coefficient, or fix
for it.
