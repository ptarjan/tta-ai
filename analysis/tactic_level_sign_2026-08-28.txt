TacticLevel sign analysis (2026-08-28)
======================================

QUESTION
--------
WeightKey::TacticLevel is priced +23.99 at 3p and -14.88 at 4p (see §3 for the
current live values; the job's -36.15 was a slightly earlier 4p snapshot).
Opposite signs on a live, frequently-firing key. Is that justified by the
rules, or a modelling defect?

1. WHAT THE FEATURE MEASURES (features.rs:935)
----------------------------------------------
    f.set(WeightKey::TacticLevel,
          if p.tactic.is_none() { 0.0 } else { f64::from(p.tactic.level()) });

`p.tactic: CardId` (state.rs:425) is the player's CURRENT tactic -- the card
their tactics standard sits on. `CardId::level()` (cards.rs:47) returns the
AGE as a number: A=0, I=1, II=2, III=3, IV=4.

So the feature value is:
    0  -- no tactic in play (standard in the common area on nothing)
    1  -- an Age-I tactic in play
    2  -- an Age-II tactic in play
    3  -- an Age-III tactic in play
(4 is unreachable in practice: no Age-IV tactics exist in the 236-card table,
and Age-IV tactics are never the current one at Age-IV end anyway.)

It is a SMALL-STEP feature: 0/1/2/3, integer, four distinct values. It says
"you have a tactic in play, and it is of this age." It says nothing about the
tactic's strength, about which unit types it depicts, about how many armies
you can form with it, or about whether it is up to date. Those are exactly
what the sibling channels in §2 price.

p95 candidate spread (weights.rs:2320, from featspread self-play):
    [2.0, 3.0, 2.0]  for (2p, 3p, 4p)
It fires often enough to be a real decision channel, not a dead coordinate.

2. SIBLING KEYS THAT PRICE THE SAME FAMILY
------------------------------------------
Four other keys sit on the tactic fact. What each one separately prices:

(a) TacticGain (cards.rs:2423 `tactic_terms`; eval.rs:682)
    gain = max(0, best_army_strength - current_army_strength) where the best
    is over (a) tactics in hand, (b) state.available_tactics (copyable for
    2 MA), (c) the one already in play. w-independent (no freeze).
    Prices: the army-strength UPGRADE you can still get by switching to a
    better tactic. Zero when the best tactic is already in play. It is a
    "how much further can the tactic improve my strength" channel.

(b) TacticShort (same `tactic_terms`; eval.rs:682; weights.rs:1687 NonPositive
    gate -- "prices a marginal-need shortfall")
    The number of unit workers still owed before the best reachable tactic
    forms one more army. Prices: the missing-units gradient that TacticGain
    lacks (a step function, e.g. flat at zero for the first two of a
    three-cavalry tactic). Negative weight = the bot is penalizing the
    shortfall, i.e. being pushed toward closing it.

(c) TacticBoardCredit (cards.rs:2004 inside card_choice; registry.rs:346)
    Multiplier on `tactic_value(id, ...)` (cards.rs:1428): the board-aware
    price of THIS SPECIFIC tactic card, given the player's current unit mix,
    cost-to-reach net, gated by its own credit. Prices: "is this tactic card
    on the row worth taking right now, for MY units."

(d) TacticReachCredit (cards.rs:1478 inside `tactic_value`)
    Trust knob: scales (potential_strength * strength_weight - cost). A
    "how much to trust this projection" multiplier, not a fitted constant of
    its own (the function's own doc comment says so).

None of the four prices "I currently have an age-N tactic in play." TacticLevel
is the ONLY channel carrying that fact. It is not a duplicate of any of them:
  * TacticGain/TacticShort are about future reachable strength, not current
    in-play age.
  * TacticBoardCredit/TacticReachCredit are about pricing a tactic card in
    hand or on the row, not about the current tactic.
So the sign question is not a duplicate-channel artifact.

4. RULES ARGUMENT (RULES_SPEC 5.x / 10.x on tactics and military)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Tactic age matters to play value for three rules reasons, all real:

(a) Antiquation (RULES_SPEC 5.x end-of-age): at each age end, the current
    tactic does NOT disappear (exclusive tactics remain in play even when
    antiquated), but a tactic of the current or prior age stays relevant
    while an older one is "outdated" for army purposes. RULES_SPEC 10.4:
    an Age II/III tactic prints two values (normal / outdated); an army is
    outdated if ANY of its units is 2+ levels below the tactic's printed
    age. So a tactic's age directly controls whether your armies pay the
    lesser outdated value. A bot that knows "my current tactic is age 2 in
    Age 3" knows its armies are at the outdated rate. TacticLevel carries
    exactly that.

(b) Replacement horizon: tactics are cheap to switch (play 1 MA, copy 2 MA,
    max one per Action Phase), so the in-play tactic is a short-lived
    decision. A positive price on "having a current-age tactic in play"
    rewards keeping your tactic fresh; a negative price would penalize it,
    which would only make sense if the bot were being pushed to AVOID having
    a tactic in play -- but that is never a rule-advantage: having a tactic
    in play is strictly better than having none (armies form only from the
    current tactic). So the RULES-sign expectation is: TacticLevel should be
    >= 0, and the sign should track "currentness": a tactic of the current
    age is good, an older one less so.

(c) Strength interaction: tactical strength is additive on top of unit
    strength (RULES_SPEC 10.3). The bigger the army, the more the tactic's
    age-outdatedness costs. This is a board-scaled effect that TacticLevel
    (a flat age number) cannot capture on its own -- which is why the
    Strength/TacticGain/TacticShort channels exist. TacticLevel is the
    crude "is my tactic fresh" bit; the rest is the sibling family's job.

So the rules permit a POSITIVE TacticLevel (fresh tactic = good). They do not
permit a meaningfully NEGATIVE one: there is no rules situation in which
having an older tactic in play is worth more than having a current one. The
4p value of -14.88 is therefore NOT justified by the rules as a genuine
3p-vs-4p strategic difference.

5. VERDICT
----------
TacticLevel is the ONLY channel for the fact "current tactic's age is N."
It is not a duplicate of TacticGain/TacticShort (future-reachable strength)
or TacticBoardCredit/TacticReachCredit (pricing a tactic card). So the sign
disagreement is not a duplicate-channel artifact.

The opposite signs are a MODELLING DEFECT, not a real 3p-vs-4p difference:
  * 3p's +23.99 has a clear rules reading (fresh tactic in play is good) and
    is on the more-converged arm. It is the defensible sign.
  * 4p's -14.88 has no rules reading that makes a current tactic a downside.
    4p is the least-converged arm (gen 21874), and the negative sign is most
    plausibly an under-convergence artifact: at 4p the tactic family is
    more crowded (more rivals copying, more common-area tactics to copy),
    so the fitter has more collinear coordinates to trade off against
    (TacticGain 1.4995, TacticReachCredit 0.3804 are both live at 4p and
    absent-or-small at 3p), and it has not yet settled TacticLevel onto the
    rules-consistent positive sign. The fitter can lean on TacticLevel being
    negative to offset a positive TacticGain while it converges, and the
    negative has not been pushed back to the rules-consistent side yet.

Recommendation: do NOT treat the 4p negative as a real strategic signal.
Treat TacticLevel's rules-consistent sign as positive (>= 0). If a fix is
wanted, a NonNegative gate on TacticLevel (weights.rs already has the
gating mechanism used for TacticGain NonNegative at 1595 and TacticShort
NonPositive at 1687) would force the 4p arm to converge to the rules-consistent
side instead of spending its negative magnitude collinearly offsetting
TacticGain. That is a one-line gate addition, not an engine change. Until
4p converges further (gen 21874 is far behind 2p's 120584), the 4p value
should be read as "not yet converged," not "tactics are bad at 4p."

COORDINATOR CORRECTION (2026-08-28) -- the NonNegative gate is already
REFUTED IN CODE, by the very rule the survey cites for it
=============================================================================

RECOMMENDATION REJECTED. `TacticLevel` is not an ungated key awaiting a sign
decision. It sits inside the deliberately-Free block at weights.rs:1751, and
the block's comment names it with a CITED COUNTEREXAMPLE:

    weights.rs:1740-1741
        // `TacticLevel` by RULES_SPEC 10.4, where a
        // higher-age tactic makes units 2+ levels below it outdated.

That is the same citation the survey offers as its rules argument FOR a
positive sign, read the other way round -- and the other way round is the
correct one. RULES_SPEC 10.4: "An army is outdated if ANY of its units is 2+
levels below the tactics card's printed age (CARD age, not current age)."
The comparison is against the TACTIC CARD, so raising your tactic's age is
exactly what turns your existing units outdated. Playing an Age III tactic
over an Age I one, with an Age I infantry still on the board, converts a
normal army into a lesser one. A higher `TacticLevel` is a real and rules-
grounded downside in that position, so the feature cannot be floored at >= 0.

The survey's own §2 is what makes this decisive rather than merely arguable:
it establishes that TacticLevel is the ONLY channel for "current tactic's age
is N" -- TacticGain prices the strength upgrade, TacticShort the missing
units, TacticBoardCredit and TacticReachCredit the card's board price. So the
outdating cost has nowhere else to be priced. Gating the one coordinate that
can express it to >= 0 would make the outdating penalty inexpressible, not
merely mispriced.

    STANDING RULE, now cited a second time: keys in the weights.rs
    cited-counterexample block are CLOSED and are not to be re-proposed. Read
    the block comment before proposing any sign_intent. It currently covers
    GovLevel/TechLevels, Colonies/HasColony, Leader, PactBlocksAttack,
    TacticLevel, and names StrengthLead as the deliberate near-miss.

WHAT SURVIVES, AND IT IS THE USEFUL HALF. Everything before the verdict
stands and is worth keeping:

  - the feature is 0/1/2/3, the AGE of the tactic in play, and nothing else;
  - the four siblings each price a DIFFERENT fact, so the family is not
    redundant and nothing here is a duplicate channel;
  - none of the four sits on a clamp wall on any arm, so the magnitudes are
    fitted rather than rail artifacts;
  - the self-correction of the 4p value (-14.8844 live, against the -36.15 in
    the job brief, 4p gen 21874) is exactly right and is the champion-file-is-
    live effect. The brief's number was the stale one.

The residual question is therefore NOT the sign. It is why 4p prices a real
cost at -14.88 while 3p prices what should be the same cost at +23.99. Both
arms face the same rule. The survey's collinearity story -- TacticLevel
absorbing magnitude against TacticGain (4p 1.4995 vs 3p 0.7071) while 4p is
far from converged -- is the plausible one, and it predicts the gap narrows as
4p converges. That is checkable for free at the next champion read, and it is
the right thing to do instead of landing a gate.

Not landing anything either way while the clamp-ceiling A/B is live, for the
same reason the FoodRate gate is being held: a sign change inside the A/B
window contaminates it.
