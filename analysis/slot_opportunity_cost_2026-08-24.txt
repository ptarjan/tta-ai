1. PARSER COVERAGE / GRAMMAR NOTES
====================================================================

Journal line grammar (tab-separated: date, player_colour, age, round, text;
CRLF line endings):

  "<Colour> takes <Card> in hand <Colour> uses <N> civil action"
  "<Colour> takes <Card> in hand <Colour> uses <N> military action"
    -> the row-take event. This is the moment a card (leader, wonder, or
       ordinary civil/military card) leaves the row and forecloses it to
       everyone else. N is the civil/military-action cost paid, which is
       the direct row-position/slot-cost proxy called for in the task
       (observed values: 1, 2, or 3 civil actions for leaders/wonders in
       this corpus -- never higher).

  "<Colour> elects <LeaderName>"
  "<Colour> elects <LeaderName2> <LeaderName1> dies; <Colour> gets 1 civil action"
    -> the leader-activation event. Self-contained: presence of the
       "<OldLeader> dies;" clause means this is a REPLACEMENT of an
       already-elected leader; its absence means it fills an EMPTY leader
       slot. Matching required a name-aware splitter (regex alone is
       ambiguous for two-word-vs-two-word leader names like
       "Bill Gates"+"Isaac Newton"); built one against the 24 known leader
       names (with 3 spelling aliases: "Charles Chaplin"->"Charlie Chaplin",
       "Maximillien Robespierre"->"Maximilien Robespierre",
       "Leonardo Da Vinci" case variant).

  "<Colour> builds <N> stage(s) of <Wonder> <Colour> spends <N> resources; ; Wonder completed"
    -> wonder completion. Linked back to the take event for the same
       wonder name by the same player (unambiguous: only the current
       holder can build/complete/return a given named card, and each
       card name is unique in a game).

  "<Colour> puts <Card> back in the row <Colour> gets <N> civil action"
    -> a take that was reversed: the card returns to the row, available
       again. Tracked as disposition "returned" (leader never elected /
       wonder never completed by this player).

  "WINNER IS <NAME> AS <COLOUR> (<PTS> PTS); 2nd is <name> as <Colour> (...); ..."
    -> end-of-game outcome line, giving colour->placement directly. Found
       in 956 of 1011 games (94.6%). The remaining 55 games (5.4%) ended
       without this line (resignations/abandons visible only as
       "concedes defeat" with no final tally) -- outcome is UNKNOWN for
       leader/wonder takes in those games and they are excluded from all
       win/loss tables below, but still counted in take/pass totals.
       Note: colour in the WINNER clause is upper-case ("AS ORANGE"),
       colour in "2nd/3rd is ... as Colour" is title-case -- had to
       normalize both to match the colour field used elsewhere in the log.

  Player count per game taken from index.tsv "players" column (692 x 2p,
  133 x 3p, 186 x 4p games; sums to 1011) -- used to stratify every table
  below, because it turned out to be a major confound (see section 2).

2. LEADER TAKES -- CONDITIONAL TABLE
====================================================================

IMPORTANT CONFOUND FOUND AND CORRECTED: baseline win rate is mechanically
~50% in 2-player games, ~33% in 3-player, ~25% in 4-player games. The
"how many leaders of this age has anyone already taken" ordinal also
scales with player count (more players draw from the same 6-card pool
faster), so an unstratified ordinal-vs-win-rate table showed a fake
"declining value" trend that was pure player-count confound. All tables
below are stratified by player count to remove it.

-- SLOT STATE (empty vs. replacement) x outcome, by player count --
(counts are "won of total", elected leader-takes with known outcome)

  2 players: empty slot        1256 of 2690 won  (46.7%)
             replacement slot  1010 of 1777 won  (56.8%)
  3 players: empty slot         230 of  753 won  (30.5%)
             replacement slot   165 of  428 won  (38.6%)
  4 players: empty slot         367 of 1510 won  (24.3%)
             replacement slot   208 of  713 won  (29.2%)

  This is the strongest, most consistent pattern found: taking a leader
  as a REPLACEMENT (you already held one) beats an EMPTY-slot take by
  roughly +10 points of win rate at 2p, +8 at 3p, +5 at 4p -- same
  direction in all three strata, all well above the 30-observation floor
  (n from 428 to 2690). This is exactly the kind of slot-state
  conditionality the hypothesis predicts, though it is also consistent
  with a simpler "players who are still alive/competitive later in the
  game get to replacement events at all" survivorship story -- the data
  cannot separate those two causes.

  Wonder age II:  2901 of 4044 possible slots never taken (71.7%)
  Wonder age III: 2276 of 4044 possible slots never taken (56.3%)

These are large majorities: most named leaders and wonders in this
corpus are never claimed by anyone in a given game. This is consistent
with (not proof of) the low depletion-ordinals seen in sections 2-3 --
most age-pools only ever get 1-2 cards pulled out of 4-6 before the game
moves on. It also means the "last one in the pool" scenario the
hypothesis describes is rare almost by construction: with 60-70% of an
age's cards going untouched, running the pool down to its last member is
the exception, not the rule.

I could NOT identify, per individual pass event, the row position or
"how many turns before it disappeared" for cards that were passed on --
see section 6.

====================================================================
5. VERDICT
====================================================================

MIXED, not flat, but the conditional structure that shows up is not the
one the hypothesis leads with (row-scarcity / "last one left"):

  - Leaders: value looks CONDITIONAL on SLOT STATE (replacement beats
    empty-slot by 5-10 win-rate points, consistent across all three
    player counts, well powered) but FLAT on scarcity-ordinal, row cost,
    and carryover.

  - Wonders: value looks CONDITIONAL on SCARCITY-ORDINAL (first wonder
    of an age's pool beats later ones by several win-rate points at 3p
    and 4p) but the slot-state (already-building) effect is only
    suggestive (thin n) and carryover is weak/borderline.

  - The "last card in the pool" extreme (ordinal 6/6 for leaders,
    4/4 for wonders) is real but rare: leaders NEVER reach full
    depletion in 1011 games; wonders reach it 162 times, of which only
    162 total (not broken out by player count above n=30 threshold in
    most strata) -- too thin to say anything about win rate specifically
    at full depletion.

So: one scalar per card type is a reasonable first-order approximation
for ROW COST and CARRYOVER (both flat), but is NOT capturing whatever is
driving the leader slot-state effect or the wonder ordinal effect -- two
different, real, non-overlapping conditional signals, one per card type.
Neither signal is the specific "last one visible in the row" scarcity
story from the hypothesis; that scenario is too rare in real games to
test directly with this corpus.

====================================================================
6. WHAT THE JOURNALS COULD NOT TELL YOU
====================================================================

  - TRUE ROW VISIBILITY / SCARCITY (item 2 of the brief) is not
    recoverable. The journal never logs the contents of the card row at
    a point in time -- only "X takes CARD" events. There is no "reveal"
    or "row refill" line anywhere in the corpus (checked). The
    depletion-ordinal used above (nth distinct card of that type/age
    ever taken by ANYONE in the game) is a real but coarser proxy: it
    tells you how much of the total age-pool has been claimed
    game-wide, not how many were visible in the row at this specific
    moment, nor whether this was "the last one visible" as opposed to
    "the last one anyone ever bothered to take." These are genuinely
    different quantities and I did not have a way to compute the first
    from this log format.

  - The "how far into the age" progress measure (item 3) could only be
    approximated by the carryover flag (card's own age vs. the game's
    current age tag at the moment of the take) -- this catches leftovers
    that survived into a later age, but says nothing about position
    within the origin age itself (e.g. "3rd of 20 rounds into Age I" vs.
    "18th of 20"). The round number is present in the log but is a
    game-wide round counter, not reset per age, so it doesn't map
    cleanly onto "age progress" without also knowing when each age
    started, which the log does mark (age column changes) -- this was
    not computed due to time budget, and is the most promising
    follow-up if this were extended.

  - Game-level "final_age" in index.tsv turned out to be uniformly
    "Age IV" for all 1011 rows -- it is not a usable per-game
    granularity signal for "did this game actually cycle its Age III
    card pool," because it reflects the terminal scoring tag, not actual
    card-draw depth. The "passes" restriction in section 4 that was
    meant to filter out structurally-unreachable ages therefore did not
    filter anything; the pass counts there should be read as slightly
    overstated for games that ended (conceded/short) before truly
    exhausting an age's row, and I have no way to separate that from
    genuine in-row passes with this data.

  - 55 of 1011 games (5.4%) have no parseable end-of-game WINNER line
    (early resignations/abandons) -- their leader/wonder takes are
    counted in totals but excluded from every win/loss table.
