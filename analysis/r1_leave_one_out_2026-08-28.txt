Findings
--------
1. NONE of the 8 single-key reverts moves round 1. r1 CA/turn stays 0.689 on every arm,
   identical to baseline B (and to the previous end_turn_bias counterfactual's 0.694 up to
   run noise — actually exactly 0.689 here). Take share stays 18.0% (911-932 of ~5000
   decisions, the spread is noise). The round-1 half of the collapse is NOT carried by any
   one of these keys.

2. Three keys are provably INACTIVE at 2p rounds 1-3: card_board_bonus,
   rival_hand_potential, and hand_swap_extra give byte-identical Item 1-6 output to
   baseline B on every line (same decision counts, same all metrics). has_colony is
   likewise inert in this window (its 60.0 -> 12.3 move is the biggest abs mover in the
   whole diff, but it has zero measurable effect on rounds 1-3 — its feature is evidently
   not firing in the opener). So has_colony, despite being the largest weight move in the
   diff, is NOT a candidate for the collapse.

3. The four ACTIVE keys all move rounds 2-3 slightly (row_last_copy and hand_potential
   DOWN, hand_military UP, take_cost_share negligible) but never round 1.

Conclusion
----------
The round-1 take-vs-endturn devaluation is a joint effect: no single key among these 8
explains it. The likely culprits are the keys that moved in the same direction but are
smaller/more diffuse (e.g. the cluster of sign flips in the hand/board features, or
interactions between take_cost_share and hand_potential) — or the round-1 effect is
mediated by a key not in this candidate set. The collapse's round-1 half (0.689 vs
1.161) is real (A vs B differ cleanly) and is not attributable to any one of the 8
plausible take-valuation keys.

Caveats
-------
- 360 games/arm; r1 CA/turn noise at this sample size is on the order of ±0.02-0.03,
  which is why the "identical to 0.689" readings on 7 of 8 arms are meaningful (they are
  literally identical, not merely close).
- Take% is reported all-rounds because openerprobe Item 1 does not split by round; the
  round-1-specific take share would require a separate instrument. The all-rounds Take%
  is nonetheless informative: it barely moves (18.0% on every arm) while r1 CA/turn also
  does not move, consistent with the round-1 effect being small in absolute CA terms
  (0.689 vs 1.161 = 0.47 CA/turn x 720 turns = ~340 CA total over the whole run).
- This is a necessary-but-not-sufficient analysis: "none of these 8 single keys" does not
  rule out that a combination of them (or a key outside this set) carries the round-1
  effect. A full leave-ONE-IN (start from preclimb, add one postclimb key at a time)
  would be the complementary experiment.
