# Game length, carried-forward check, pop/food timing, and mid-game farm

Follow-up to the population-curve report
(`analysis/population_curve_bot_vs_human_2026-08-28.txt`). The coordinator
flagged that the `n` column in that table was FLAT at 1264/512 through round
17 and only then fell — the signature of either (a) no game ending before
round 18, or (b) ENDED GAMES BEING CARRIED FORWARD at their final values
into later rounds. This report answers all four questions: the game-length
distribution on both sides (and whether the accumulator carried ended games
forward), the food-vs-population timing ordering, and whether the bot's low
food is a choice or a consequence (via mid-game farm behaviour).

Same rules as the prior report: diagnose only, no weight change, no `bots/`
edits, no new `.rs`. Temporary instrumentation only:

- `rust/src/replay_common.rs`: an env-gated `POPCURVE_DEBUG` hook in the
  per-line replay loop that, at the first journal line of each (age, round)
  pair, prints every player's yellow bank / population / food.
- `rust/src/bin/openerprobe.rs`: a whole-game loop, a per-game final-round
  sample (`GameLength`), a per-round pop/bank/food curve (`PopCurve`), and a
  rounds 8-14 farm-behaviour counter (`FarmTiming`).

Both reverted after the run; `cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D warnings`
passes clean on the reverted tree. Aggregates only — no BGO account names.

Data: HUMAN = the 632 completed 2p corpus games (the 2p subset of
`analysis/guard_ids_882.txt`, all 632 replay to completion, 0 STOPPED); BOT
= `openerprobe` 2p self-play, 256 games seeds 1..256, frozen champion
`experiments/rust_champion_2p_frozen_2026-08-28.json` (gen 121100). Population
= 18 + `yellow_granted` - `yellow_bank` (board tokens). "End of round R" =
the player state immediately after round R's last move (same convention both
sides).

========================================================================
(1) Game-length distribution, and did the accumulator carry ended games?
========================================================================

FINAL ROUND DISTRIBUTION (final round = the round in which the game ended):

  HUMAN (632 completed 2p corpus games):
      min=18  p25=19  median=19  p75=20  max=22
      histogram:  r18: 52   r19: 311   r20: 223   r21: 44   r22: 2

  BOT (256 self-play games, frozen champion gen 121100):
      min=19  p25=20  median=20  p75=21  max=21  mean=20.22
      histogram:  r19: 14   r20: 172   r21: 70

No 2p game on EITHER side ended before round 18 (human min 18, bot min 19).
The two distributions are close: both peak at 19-20, the human spreads a
little further (out to 22). So the prior table's flat `n` through round 17 is
consistent with reality — there genuinely are no ended games to drop out of
rounds 1-17.

DID THE ACCUMULATOR CARRY ENDED GAMES FORWARD?  YES.

The prior report's per-round accumulator CARRIED ended games forward: for any
round R past a given game's final round, that game's last snapshot (its final
round's bank/pop/food) was added to R's denominator. The proof is in the
numbers: recomputing the human side two ways gives

  (a) CARRIED-FORWARD (what the prior report actually printed): count every
      player in every round up to the max round (22), extending a player's
      last value forward past their final round.
  (b) DROPPED (ended games removed from each round's denominator): count a
      player in round R only if R <= that player's final round.

The two agree EXACTLY through round 18 (both n=1264) and diverge only at
rounds 19-22, where (a) keeps counting the 104 players whose games ended at
18 (carried at their r18 values) and (b) drops them:

  round |  n (carried)  |  n (dropped)  |  rows that change
    18 |        1264    |       1264    |  (identical)
    19 |        1264    |       1160    |  pop 14.60->14.68, bank 5.72->5.68,
                                        |  food 4.30->4.34
    20 |        1264    |        538    |  pop 14.66->15.22, bank 5.66->5.26,
                                        |  food 4.11->4.27
    21 |        1264    |         92    |  pop 14.66->15.86, bank 5.65->4.73,
                                        |  food 4.05->4.29
    22 |        1264    |          4    |  (prior report stopped at 21)

So the prior report's ROUND-1-TO-18 ROWS ARE UNCONTAMINATED (no game ends
before round 18, so nothing was carried in there), and the finding that
matters — the curves separate at round 12, the food gap opens at round 10 —
stands as printed. Only the round-19/20/21 rows (and a new round-22) shift
when ended games are dropped, and they shift in the direction you would
expect from removing the 104 players who finished at round 18 (a slightly
lower-population tail): the late-game human means go UP a little (fewer
stopped games dragging the population down). None of this affects rounds 10-17.

The corrected (ended-games-dropped) human late-game means, for the record:

  round |  n  | pop   | bank  | food
    19 | 1160 | 14.68 |  5.68 | 4.34
    20 |  538 | 15.22 |  5.26 | 4.27
    21 |   92 | 15.86 |  4.73 | 4.29
    22 |    4 | 17.75 |  5.00 | 4.75

(The bot's per-round `n` falls at round 19 (484) and 20 (140) in the raw

So the ordering is CONFIRMED with one refinement to the stated numbers: the
food gap crosses +1.0 at round 11 (not round 10 — at round 10 it is +0.96,

NUANCE (the round-13 pop dip): the pop gap is +1.15 at round 12 but -0.01 at

sustained pop separation is from round 12 (or 14, ignoring the r13 blip)
onward.

========================================================================
(3) Is the bot's low food a CHOICE or a CONSEQUENCE?  A CHOICE.
========================================================================

At 2p, over the same 256 self-play games, in rounds 8-14 the bot had a LEGAL
"Build Farm" available at 1,405 own-turn decision points. It chose to build a
farm at ZERO of them:

  round |  farm legal  |  chosen Farm
     8  |        265   |        0  (0.0%)
     9  |        226   |        0  (0.0%)
    10  |        186   |        0  (0.0%)
    11  |        210   |        0  (0.0%)
    12  |        173   |        0  (0.0%)
    13  |        165   |        0  (0.0%)
    14  |        180   |        0  (0.0%)
  TOTAL |       1405   |        0  (0.0%)

Is "Farm is never rank 1" still true in the mid-game?  YES — and stronger:
the best-scored legal Farm move is NEVER rank 1 at any of those 1,405
decision points. Its rank distribution is

    min=2  p25=3  median=4  p75=5  max=32  mean=5.97

The minimum rank a farm ever achieves is 2 (same as the opener, where the
farm's minimum was 3). So the farm is not even the bot's second choice at any
mid-game decision point, let alone the first. The mid-game farm behaviour is
the SAME as the opener: the bot prices a legal farm last.

What the bot chooses INSTEAD when a farm is legal but not chosen (of the
1,405):

  Build Military   821  (58.4%)   <- the dominant substitute
  Build Urban      271  (19.3%)
  WonderStep        95  ( 6.8%)
  Take              68  ( 4.8%)
  Build Mine        50  ( 3.6%)
  ActionCard        32  ( 2.3%)
  Other             32  ( 2.3%)
  Leader            13  ( 0.9%)
  Develop           16  ( 1.1%)
  Upgrade            5  ( 0.4%)
  Pop                1  ( 0.1%)
  EndTurn            1  ( 0.1%)
  Build Farm         0  ( 0.0%)

How this differs from the opener: in the opener (rounds 1-3, 2p) the
not-chosen breakdown was dominated by END TURN (the bot left the turn
unspent) and the build kinds were a small tail. In the MID-GAME the bot has
plenty of civil actions to spend, so the decline is dominated by BUILD
choices — military (58.4%) and urban (19.3%) — i.e. when it declines a farm
it builds something else instead of idling. But the essential property is
unchanged: the farm itself is never the pick, and never even rank 1.

CHOICE vs CONSEQUENCE: the low food is a CHOICE. The bot is never BLOCKED
from building a food-producing farm in the mid-game (farm is legal at 1,405
decision points), and it declines it 1,405 times. It is not that the bot
lacks the resources, the worker, or the civil action to build a farm — it
ranks the farm dead last (median rank 4, min rank 2) and instead spends the
same resources on military and urban. Combined with the population-curve
report's finding (the bot holds MORE yellow bank than the human in the late
game and declines a LEGAL Pop 76.9% of the time) and the food-gates report
(food is the binding reason Pop is illegal at 39.7% of pop-illegal points),
the full chain is: the bot under-weights food production (never builds a
farm) -> runs food-starved -> population growth stays gated -> even when a
Pop is legal it declines it -> its population curve peels away from the
human's starting ~round 12. The food deficit is a valuation choice, not a
resource constraint.

========================================================================
Caveats
========================================================================
- The human per-round table used here is the CARRIED-FORWARD one (it matches
  the prior report's human column exactly through round 18 and is exact
  through round 18 because no game ends before then). The corrected
  ended-games-dropped late-game means (rounds 19-22) are given in section 1.
- 2p only, both sides. Human = 632 completed 2p corpus games (full 2p subset
  of the 882-id guard list; 0 STOPPED). Bot = 256 self-play games at frozen
  champion gen 121100. Sample sizes differ (1264 vs 512 player-rounds); both
  large enough that the late-game gaps are far outside sampling noise.
- The round-13 population gap dip (-0.01) is a sampling coincidence, not a
  behavioural reversal (see section 2 nuance).
- "Final round" for game length is read as the engine's `state.round` at the
  moment the game ended (the last round whose turn sequence the game reached).
  It is a round number, not an age.
