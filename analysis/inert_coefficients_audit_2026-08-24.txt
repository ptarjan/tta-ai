SCOPE AND RE-FRAMING (read this first)

This started as "which weights are zero or absent" and was corrected mid-run
by the coordinator: a zero weight is NOT the target. The target is a
coefficient that CANNOT AFFECT PLAY, whatever its magnitude. The sharpest
proof that these are different questions is workers_late: it carries 60.0 in
both the 3p and 4p champions -- tied for one of the three largest magnitudes
in the entire 160-key vector -- and two independent play-tests (real
win-rate A/B, 240 games each, both seat arrangements) moving it to 30 and to
120 both came back statistically indistinguishable from leaving it at 60.
Sources: analysis/ceiling_playtest_3p_2026-08-24.txt lines 122-131 (30, both
arrangements p=0.696/0.603) and analysis/ceiling_up_sweep_3p_2026-08-24.txt
lines 105-119 (120, both arrangements p=0.573/0.406). A big number is not
evidence of usefulness. This report ranks on USELESSNESS, in this order:

  1. CANDIDATE-INVARIANT -- the feature never varies across the candidate
     set at a real decision (zero_frac = 1.0 in the 3p decisiveness table).
     Unreachable at ANY weight, by construction.
  2. NULL-IN-PLAY -- the feature does vary, but capable_frac sits at or
     below the 0.0924 tie floor: it has never once been able to be the
     deciding factor at a real, non-tied decision.
  3. REDUNDANT -- flagged where cheaply visible (no correlation matrix
     computed; see SUSPECTED-REDUNDANT section). Two keys carrying the same
     information can never be separately identified by the climb, and one
     is prunable.

Whether a key is zero/absent from the champion files is reported as a
SEPARATE COLUMN, never as the ranking axis -- it answers "what was never
trained," a different question from "what can never matter," and belongs to
a sibling audit.

All decisiveness numbers (zero_frac, fire_rate, capable_frac) are read
verbatim from analysis/feature_decisiveness_3p_2026-08-24.txt (85,404
decisions, 220 games, seed 12345, 3p only) -- nothing here was re-measured.
capable_frac's 0.0924 floor is a TIE ARTIFACT (9.24% of decisions are exact
ties where every key trivially "beats" a zero margin): read every
capable_frac as RANK, never as an absolute rate. See CAVEATS.

================================================================================
CAVEATS
================================================================================

1. TIE-FLOOR ARTIFACT. capable_frac's 0.0924 floor comes from the 9.24% of
   decisions in the source table that are exact ties (margin == 0), where
   every key trivially "beats" that margin, including keys that never fire
   at all. Every capable_frac number in this report should be read as
   RANK/comparison against 0.0924, never as an absolute decisive rate.
   "LIVE" items sitting within a few thousandths of 0.0924 (has_colony
   0.0926, colonies 0.0942, best_theater 0.0951, attack_cost_doubled
   0.0931, and several others in the 0.092-0.095 band -- see the full
   table) are functionally indistinguishable from NULL-IN-PLAY at this
   sample size; the bucket boundary is exact but the underlying signal is
   noisy right at the floor. Treat the top-12 ranked list, not the strict
   bucket label, as the actionable signal for these borderline keys.

2. STRUCTURAL vs STATISTICAL is a real distinction this report does not
   collapse. workers_late is the clearest case: statistically LIVE
   (capable_frac 0.42) but empirically null under real play (item 1 in the
   ranked list). The reverse direction (statistically CANDIDATE-INVARIANT
   but the RULE governing it is obviously correct, e.g. the *_trailing
   standing-hinge keys, which are candidate-invariant BY DESIGN since they
   read player position, not the candidate move) does not mean those keys
   are bugs -- it means the mechanism they participate in (a standing
   multiplier on a DIFFERENT candidate-varying key, e.g. CultureRate x
   CultureRateTrailing) is where the real signal has to live, and the
   trailing coefficient itself genuinely cannot move a decision alone. That
   is exactly why science_rate_trailing (item 3) is worth flagging even
   though nothing here suggests the mechanism is broken.

3. NEVER-TRAINED (as originally defined -- absent from a champion file) is
   VACUOUS for the three files actually available: all 160 WeightKey names
   appear, with a value, in champ2p.json, champ3p.json AND champ4p.json,
   confirmed by direct Python set comparison (zero missing, zero extra in
   either direction, for all three files). This does not mean every key has
   been meaningfully trained -- see CANDIDATE-INVARIANT and NULL-IN-PLAY
   above for that -- only that the champion serialization format writes the
   full vector rather than a sparse diff, so "presence in the file" cannot
   distinguish a trained key from a key that has sat at its compiled
   default since it was added.

4. This report reused the EXISTING 3p 85,404-decision table
   (feature_decisiveness_3p_2026-08-24.txt) rather than re-measuring.
   zero_frac/fire_rate/capable_frac are 3p-only; the champ2p/champ4p
   columns are weight VALUES only, with no decisiveness measurement behind
   them at this player count. A key ranked LIVE or NULL-IN-PLAY here is a
   3p verdict; its 2p/4p decisiveness is unmeasured and could differ.

5. SUSPECTED-REDUNDANT flags are exactly that -- suspected, on cheap
   evidence (same WeightGroup, overlapping zero_frac/capable_frac,
   overlapping semantics from the source doc comments), not a computed
   correlation. Per instruction, no correlation matrix was built; treat
   those three flags as leads for a follow-up, not settled findings.

6. This is diagnosis only. No weight was changed, no game was played by
