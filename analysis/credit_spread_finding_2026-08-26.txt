WHAT THE LINEARITY TEST FOUND
-----------------------------
The test probes each credit key k at c=1.0 and c=2.0 (the probe goes in
the FROZEN vector, spec section 3) and requires S_d(2.0) ~= 2*S_d(1.0)
within 1%. Instead, for essentially every key and every player count:

    S_d(1.0)  ~=  S_d(2.0)  ~=  T_players  (the TOTAL spread)

i.e. S_d is INDEPENDENT of the probe magnitude c, and it equals the
max-min of the whole candidate set under the unperturbed freeze -- NOT a
per-key quantity. Concretely, 2p (10153 decisions): 14 of 20 keys have
S_d(1.0) EXACTLY 214.092659 = T_p95, and S_d(2.0) is within <1% of the
same value; the other 6 keys run a little higher (card_board_leader
509, card_board_credit 362) but still not 2x. 3p and 4p show the same
pattern at their own T (427.8 / 416.5).

WHY THIS HAPPENS
----------------
The 20 credit keys have no coordinate in the linear feature vector;
their only readers are the card pricers, which resolve credit from the
FROZEN vector. The pricers read a SHARED credit pool (card rates,
board credits, etc.) and the total S_d is dominated by the largest
credit terms in the pool. Setting ONE key's frozen weight to 1.0 or to
2.0 perturbs that shared pool only in the c=1.0->c=2.0 delta of a single
term -- and the max-min is set by the OTHER terms, so it does not move.
Doubling one key therefore changes nothing: S_d(c) is flat in c and
pinned at the total spread. The credit pricers are not a set of 20
independent levers; they are one shared mechanism whose total the probe
can nudge but not scale per key.

CONSEQUENCE FOR THE BOUND
-------------------------
bound(k, players) = CLAMP_T * T / p95_slope assumes p95_slope is a
per-key quantity (the spread that key alone can produce). That
assumption is false here: p95_slope ~= T, so "bound" ~= CLAMP_T * T / T
~= 1.0 for most keys (see the per-key table) -- a number that carries no
per-key information. Emitting it would be exactly the "do not emit a
slope that cannot be defended" case the spec guards against. The RUST
TABLE in the measurement file therefore emits 0.000000 for all 20
credit rows (GATED), so clamp_bound's own <= 0.0 fallback keeps them at
CLAMP_BLIND -- the same mechanism featspread's zero rows rely on.

WHAT IS STILL MEANINGFUL (the other half of the spec)
------------------------------------------------------
The multcheck FLIP RATES (spec section 6) ARE per-key-defensible, because
they are the counterfactual argmax-flip instrument, not a scale:
  flip_rate_zero (all 20 credits -> 0.0): 2p 0.463, 3p 0.181, 4p 0.448
  flip_rate_abs  (credits -> |w|)      : 2p 0.083, 3p 0.0028, 4p 0.038
These are the INFLUENCE measure. The LEVER (S_d) is ~T and not per-key;
the INFLUENCE (flips) is real and player-count dependent. The
touched column (fraction of decisions where the c=1.0 probe changed any
candidate score) ranges 0.0 (card_rate_credit: the pricer never reads
it at c=1.0) to ~1.0 (action_board_credit, free_action_credit) -- which
is the genuinely per-key signal in this run.

GATES / DECISIONS FOR YOU
-------------------------
1. No per-key credit bound is defensible; the 20 rows stay at
   CLAMP_BLIND. I recommend NOT pasting any non-zero credit spread into
   p95_candidate_spread from this run.
2. If you want a per-key credit scale at all, the instrument must be
   changed: S_d as max-min of the whole candidate set cannot separate
   20 keys that share one credit pool. A per-PRICER-TERM delta (re-run
   each pricer with the key at c and at 0, diff that pricer's
   contribution, not the total score) is the only formulation that could
   be linear in c. That is a new spec, not this one.
3. The flip rates above stand as the influence result for the credit
   half, and are commensurable with multcheck's existing tables.
