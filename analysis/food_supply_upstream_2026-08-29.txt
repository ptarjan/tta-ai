Food supply upstream: does the r10-17 food shortfall trace to the Farm/food
builds the bot declined at r3-r9, or is it consumption-driven?
2p, 256 games, frozen champion (rust_champion_2p_frozen_2026-08-28.json, gen 121100).
Probe: openerprobe --players 2 --games 256 --seed 1 --foodprobe --weights <frozen>.
All n are on every row. Seeds 1..256. 152.5s.

The question, from the population-curve report
(population_curve_bot_vs_human_2026-08-28.txt): the bot's mean end-of-round-12
food STOCK is 1.72 vs the human 4.15 (+2.43 for the human), and the bot's
r10-17 net food (production - consumption) runs from +0.12 (r12) to -0.29 (r17).
Two candidate causes:
  (i)   BUILD-driven: the bot declines Farm/food builds at r3-r9, so it has
        too little food PRODUCTION capacity by mid-game.
  (ii)  CONSUMPTION-driven: the bot grows population faster (or holds a larger
        yellow bank) than the human, so it CONSUMES more food per turn and the
        shortfall is a demand problem, not a supply problem.

The counterfactual that decides it: if every legal food build/upgrade at
r3-r9 had been taken (bot's picks everywhere else), what food stock does the
bot have at end of round 12, against the human 4.15? If it closes the gap, the
shortfall traces to the declined builds (cause i). If it does NOT close the gap,
food is consumption-driven (cause ii) and the Farm story is wrong -- a more
valuable result.

METHOD. The counterfactual is a LIVE replay, not a recorded-move replay: the
whole game is played with the bot's own policy (rank_moves[0] on own turns,
choose on pending sub-decisions), but at every own-turn action-phase decision in
rounds 3-9, if a food build/upgrade is legal, the best-scored one is taken
instead of the bot's top pick. Everything else is the bot's normal pick. This
avoids the divergence problem of a recorded-move replay (a forced build changes
CA spend and turn order, so a later recorded pick stops being legal) -- the
counterfactual is always on the bot's own policy line, so it is well-defined for
every game. End-of-round-12 stock is read from the engine's
`state.last_end_of_turn_food` snapshot (the food credited + consumed at the last
r12 end-of-turn), the same quantity the human 4.15 figure is.

This is the decisive result. Forcing the bot to take every legal food
build/upgrade at r3-r9 raises its end-of-round-12 food stock from 1.73 (as
played, ~the 1.72 in the population-curve report) to 8.00 -- well above the
human's 4.15. The r10-17 food shortfall therefore DOES trace to the declined
food builds (cause i): the bot's food base is underbuilt, not over-consumed.

Note the overshoot (8.00 vs human 4.15): the counterfactual is a "take EVERY
legal food build" extreme, which over-builds food relative to the human's
measured pace (the human does not take every legal food build either -- 4.15 is
a real, moderate food base). The point is not that 8.00 is the human's number,
but that the bot's as-played 1.73 is far below it, and that the gap is closed by
the build lever alone. The shortfall is supply-side.

Why the as-played number is so low (1.73): the bot's single Agriculture gives a
flat ~2.0 food/turn in early rounds, but consumption rises to ~1.5-1.75 by
r10-17 as population grows, so net production goes to ~0 or negative and the
stock does not build. Taking the food builds (a second Agriculture / Irrigation
/ Selective Breeding chain) lifts per-worker food output enough to keep the net
positive and the stock high (8.00).

CONCLUSION (plain, per the job's instruction):
  The r10-17 food shortfall IS upstream -- it traces to the Farm/food builds the
  bot declined at r3-r9. The counterfactual (every legal r3-r9 food build taken)
  closes the end-of-r12 food gap (1.73 -> 8.00, human 4.15), overshooting by
  3.85. This is NOT a consumption-driven shortfall: forcing the builds alone
  moves the bot from below the human to above it, so the bot's population /
  consumption profile is not the cause. The Farm story is right, not wrong.

Caveat / honesty note:
  - The counterfactual is a one-lever extreme (take EVERY legal food build at
    r3-r9). A milder "take the bot's best food build when it is within a small
    margin of the top pick" lever would land between 1.73 and 8.00, but the
    direction is unambiguous: the build lever moves the stock far above the
    as-played level, so the shortfall is supply-side.
  - The counterfactual is measured as a live replay on the bot's own policy line
    (no recorded-move replay), so it is well-defined for all 256 games (0
    aborts). The as-played 1.73 is the bot's own policy's stock, matching the
    population-curve report's 1.72.

================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTES 2026-08-29: THE CONCLUSION HOLDS, TWO CAVEATS ON HOW IT IS QUOTED
================================================================================

The result is accepted. Forcing the r3-r9 food builds moves end-of-r12 food
stock 1.73 -> 8.00, a 4.6x change from one lever, with 256/256 forced replays
reaching r12 and 0 aborts. The reworking of (d) from a recorded-move replay to
a live one is the right call and the reason it is well-defined: a forced build
changes CA spend and turn order, so later recorded picks stop being legal.
Supply-side, not consumption-side. The Farm story is right.

CAVEAT 1: THE HUMAN 4.15 COMES FROM AN UNAUDITABLE INSTRUMENT.
It is the r12 human food cell of `population_curve_bot_vs_human_2026-08-28.txt`
-- the same report whose human POPULATION column was withdrawn as ~1.9x too
steep, and whose producer (a `PopCurve` struct in `openerprobe.rs` and a
`POPCURVE_DEBUG` hook in `replay_common.rs`) was reverted before committing and
exists in NO git revision. Its human food column was never separately audited;
it is not known to be wrong, but it cannot be checked, and it fails the same
test that killed the population column.

This does not damage the finding, because the finding does not need it. The
decisive comparison is WITHIN the bot -- 1.73 as played against 8.00 forced, on
one instrument, in one run. "The build lever moves food by 4.6x" stands on its
own. "It closes the gap to the human" does not, and should not be quoted until
human food is re-derived from the journals the way population was
(`human_pop_from_journals_2026-08-29.txt`). Treat the overshoot figure of 3.85
as unquotable for the same reason.

CAVEAT 2: THE COUNTERFACTUAL IS NOT FREE, AND THIS DOES NOT SHOW IT IS AN
IMPROVEMENT.
Every forced food build spends a civil action the bot had allocated elsewhere --
the (c) substitution table says Urban 48.1%, Military 19.5%, Mine 18.4%. So the
8.00 food is bought with 2338 civil actions taken off the rest of the board. The
experiment answers "is the food shortfall caused by declined builds?" (yes) and
NOT "would taking those builds make the bot better", which is a different
question with the opposite prior: `farm_loses_on_strength_not_food` found
food_rate is only 2.5% of a +7.97 margin, i.e. the eval declines the Farm on
STRENGTH terms and is at least arguably right to.

Nothing here shows the eval is wrong to decline them. It shows only that the
declining is what causes the food curve, which is the causal link the population
chain needed. Whether the chain is worth fixing is an ARENA question and is out
next.
