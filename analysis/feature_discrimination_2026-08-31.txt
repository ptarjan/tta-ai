WITHIN-DECISION DISCRIMINATION SCREEN -- 2026-08-31
Can a candidate feature column separate the LEGAL MOVES at a decision point,
or does it only separate decision points from each other? Measured for the 91
above-floor columns of analysis/feature_screen_families_2026-08-31.txt, plus
controls, at 2 players against the same frozen champion.

NO RECOMMENDATION IS MADE HERE. This file reports measurements only.


====================================================================
0. WHY THE R2 SCREEN CANNOT ANSWER THIS
====================================================================

The leaf eval is dot(w, phi(candidate)). A column that takes the SAME VALUE on
every candidate at a decision point adds the same constant to every candidate's
score and cannot change the argmax AT ANY WEIGHT. It is unreachable, not merely
underpriced ([[tta-most-keys-cannot-decide]]).

The R2 screen measures something else: how much a column improves a prediction
of the final margin from the state the champion actually reached. That is
variation ACROSS DECISION POINTS. The two can come apart completely, and
[[tta-corpus-fits-lose-three-ways]] records the failure mode -- an outcome model
prices the scoreboard, one-ply successors barely differ on the scoreboard, so a
column can explain outcome variance beautifully and still separate nothing.

rel_proj_final_culture_gap, rank 1 in the R2 table at +0.008046, is by
construction current culture gap + rate gap * rounds_left: SHAPED LIKE THE
LABEL. The R2 screen cannot tell that apart from a column that earns its number.
This one can. (Section 5 reports what it says about that column, which is not
what was expected.)

with Move::Resign dropped unless resigning is the only legal move, mirroring
bots::filter_resign (pub(crate), so not callable from a binary). That is the set
rank_moves ranks.


====================================================================
2. THE STATISTIC, AND WHY IT IS NORMALISED THAT WAY
====================================================================

At each decision point, for each column: spread = max - min over the candidate
set. Reduced over decisions:

  spread   = mean spread / SD of the column's value AT THE CHOSEN MOVE
  const    = fraction of decisions where the spread is zero
  dmed/dmean/d90 = median / mean / p90 count of distinct values per decision
  rawspr   = the mean spread itself, in the column's own units

WHY DIVIDE BY THE SD AT THE CHOSEN MOVE, in one line: the chosen-move value over
decisions is EXACTLY the population phidump writes one row of, so the ratio
reads as "within-decision variation in units of the across-decision variation
the R2 screen was fit on" -- which is the comparison this file exists to make,
and it makes columns of wildly different scale comparable (rawspr does not:
rel_x_culrate_gap_round's 9.54 and hand_gov_count's 0.18 are not on one scale).

Two values count as one below 1e-9 * (1 + max|v|), so f64 accumulation noise in
linear_features cannot report a structurally inert column as live.

THE STATISTIC IS A PROXY, NOT A FLIP TEST. What actually flips an argmax is
|w_k| * spread against the winning margin, and a candidate column has no weight
yet. A spread of zero is a PROOF of inertness at any weight; a nonzero spread is
a necessary condition, not a sufficient one. The sufficient test is a
counterfactual argmax flip ([[tta-spread-scan-is-blind-to-multipliers]]) and it
needs a weight to run.

READ const AGAINST ~0.4, NOT AGAINST 0. The most discriminating live phi column
in the whole vector is constant across candidates on 21% of decisions
(HandMilPotential); CultureRate is constant on 50%; the median nonzero phi
column on 81%. At most decisions most quantities simply cannot move.

====================================================================
5. rel_proj_final_culture_gap -- THE PREDICTION WAS WRONG
====================================================================

THE PREDICTION: high R2, LOW spread -- a column shaped like the label, pricing
the scoreboard, unable to separate any two legal moves.

THE MEASUREMENT:

  column                       r2gain    r2rk   spread    const   dmean
  rel_proj_final_culture_gap  +0.008046     1   0.1779   0.3776    1.86
  CultureRate (yardstick)     +0.003732     -   0.1624   0.4971    1.65

It is MORE discriminating than the positive control, not less, on both axes:
a higher spread ratio, and the LOWEST constant fraction of all 91 candidate
columns -- 37.8% against CultureRate's 49.7%. It moves across the candidate set
at nearly two in three decisions. It ranks 44th of 91 by spread, mid-table, not
at the bottom.

WHY, and the mechanism is measurable in this same run. The column is
culture_gap + rate_gap * rounds_left. Its two halves behave completely
differently across candidates:

  Culture (the stock, phi)                   spread 0.0077   const 0.8703
  rel_x_culture_gap_late (stock x lateness)  spread 0.0119   const 0.8700
  CultureRate (the rate, phi)                spread 0.1624   const 0.4971
  rel_x_culrate_gap_late (rate x lateness)   spread 0.1727   const 0.4895

The STOCK half is exactly the inert, scoreboard-shaped thing the hypothesis
described -- rank 83 of 91 by spread, flagged "low spread" in the table despite
a respectable +0.000915 R2 gain. The RATE half is not, because a one-ply move
changes CultureRate on half of all decisions and the horizon multiplier then
amplifies that difference by rounds_left rather than diluting it. The projection
is constant across candidates only where BOTH halves are (0.3776, close to the
0.4326 the two constant fractions would give if independent).

So the label-shaped column earns its discrimination from the investment half of
its own construction, not from the scoreboard half. The failure mode
[[tta-corpus-fits-lose-three-ways]] describes is real and is visible in this
table -- it is what Culture, CivilActions, rel_x_culture_gap_late and the whole
GRAN one-hot tail are doing -- but rel_proj_final_culture_gap is not an instance
of it.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY. Nonzero spread is a necessary condition, not a
sufficient one (section 2). This says the column is REACHABLE -- a weight on it
could change moves. It says nothing about whether those changed moves are
better, and nothing about the fact that phi already reconstructs 90.16% of it
(the families report's spanned column), which is a separate reason a gain here
might not survive an arena.

6. THE TWO SCREENS ARE VERY NEARLY UNCORRELATED
====================================================================

Over the 91 columns, R2 gain against spread ratio:

  pearson  -0.045      spearman  +0.149

That is the whole justification for running this. Knowing a column's R2 gain
tells you essentially nothing about whether it can move a decision, in either
direction, and neither does the families report's "spanned" column
(pearson +0.016 against spread ratio). The two screens have to be read together,
column by column; neither can be inferred from the other.

The OTHER off-diagonal quadrant is populated too, and heavily: the five most
discriminating columns in the table (hand_specialtech_count 1.8039,
hand_resource_cost_total 1.5291, gran_hand_distinct_types 0.9319,
hand_science_cost_total 0.7739, hand_science_cost_max 0.7719) carry R2 gains of
+0.000012 to +0.000128, i.e. 2x to 21x the noise floor, ranks 52-88 of 91. They
move across candidates more than any live phi column except a handful, and they
predict the outcome barely at all.

7. SEVEN COLUMNS THE R2 SCREEN CANNOT SEE AT ALL
====================================================================

Section 3 of the families report lists twelve granularity columns as CONSTANT
ZERO in all 903171 dump rows and therefore unscreenable: the champion never
begins six of the sixteen wonders, so both gran_wcomp_* and gran_wbuild_* are
identically zero for all six.

That is true of the CHOSEN move. It is not true of the candidate set.

  column                                  const   mean spread  max  rep const
  gran_wbuild_internet                   0.9755      0.02447   1.0    0.9752
  gran_wbuild_hollywood                  0.9770      0.02299   1.0    0.9769
  gran_wbuild_fast_food_chains           0.9771      0.02293   1.0    0.9763

Starting each of those wonders is a LEGAL MOVE at roughly 2.3% of decisions.
The champion never takes it, so the column is zero at every chosen move, its
across-decision SD is exactly 0, and no R2 gain can exist for it -- the spread
ratio is formally infinite and is printed as such in the TSV. The matching
gran_wcomp_* columns are genuinely inert here (spread exactly 0), because
completing a wonder you never started is not on any candidate list.

SIX OF THE SEVEN ARE STRUCTURAL, ONE IS SAMPLING. Pyramids, Transcontinental
Railroad, Ocean Liners, First Space Flight, Fast Food Chains and Internet are
the families report's own never-begun six, zero in all 903171 rows of the
3000-game dump; for those, the R2 screen's blindness is caused by the policy's
coverage and more games do not fix it. gran_wbuild_hollywood is different: the
champion did begin Hollywood in ONE of the 3000 games (the same game behind that
report's gran_wcomp_hollywood -0.002490 one-game artifact) and in none of these
400, so its infinity here is this run's sample, not a structural fact.

All seven are excluded from the section 4 table because the families report has
no R2 rank for them to be compared against.

8. STABILITY
====================================================================

An independent replication: 200 games, seed block 5001, no game shared with the
main run, 58114 decisions, 691487 successor states.

  max |delta const_frac| over all 359 columns   0.01585  (rel_proj_final_deficit)
  mean |delta const_frac|                       0.00182
  pearson r of log spread ratio, n=292          0.9963
  relative difference in spread ratio           median 3.2%, p90 16.7%
  zero-spread set                               58 columns vs 59

The zero-spread sets differ by exactly ONE column of 359: gran_gov_communism,
constant on 99.994% of decisions in the main run (mean spread 6.1e-5) and on
100% in the replication. Every other zero is a zero in both. So a zero here is
structural, not thin sampling -- the same conclusion
[[tta-zero-spread-row-may-be-thin-sampling]] reached for featspread by
quadrupling its sample, reached here by resampling instead.

Rare columns are the exception that proves it: a column that fires on 0.006% of
decisions is one game away from reading zero, which is why the CANNOT-DECIDE
flag is set at 99.9% and not at 100%.


====================================================================
9. LIMITS
====================================================================

1. SPREAD IS NECESSARY, NOT SUFFICIENT. Zero spread proves a column cannot
   decide at any weight. Nonzero spread proves only that it could. The
   sufficient test is |w| * spread against the winning margin, which needs a
   weight the candidate columns do not have.

2. THIS INSTRUMENT IS BLIND TO MULTIPLIERS, exactly as featspread is. A column
   applied inside another feature's computation has no linear slot to
   difference and reads zero however much it changes play. All 185 candidate
   columns are linear slots by construction, so it does not bite them -- but all
   20 credit keys are among the 40 never-populated zero phi rows here, and none
   of them is prunable on this evidence.

3. 9.24% of decisions have an exact winning margin of zero (the tie floor,
   [[tta-most-keys-cannot-decide]]). This file does not use margins at all, so
   the floor does not inflate any number here -- but it does mean a small
   nonzero spread is worth less than it looks.

4. ONE POINT IN WEIGHT SPACE. phi depends on w; the candidate set depends on the
   policy; the policy is gen 133131. A different champion visits different
   decisions and the whole table moves ([[tta-spread-table-ages-with-the-
   champion]]).

5. 2p ONLY. There are no pacts at 2p, and the rival maximum is a maximum over
   one opponent. Every relative column would have to be re-measured at 3p/4p,
   and control C shows the player count is not a detail: four keys that are
   candidate-invariant at 3p are live here.

6. The champion never begins six of sixteen wonders, so the GRAN family is
   measured against narrow coverage in both screens (section 7).
