Wonder-start tempo: humans vs the champion, and whether starting late loses
===========================================================================
Measured 2026-08-24 with the instrumentation added in 410e6d7.

Why this exists: after the EndTurn scoring fix (02086c6) the share of 2p
player-games with no wonder-stage progress by round 4 rose 66.0% -> 85.3%,
while wonders started (2.99 -> 3.11) and completed (2.57 -> 2.64) BOTH rose.
Starting later while finishing more may be correct play the bot was
previously prevented from finding, so no "fix" was attempted until the two
measurements below existed.

Commands
--------
  cargo build --release --bin humanopenings --bin behavcensus
  ./target/release/humanopenings ../sources/bgo/index.tsv \
      /private/tmp/bgo-journals/journals            # new numbers on stderr
  ./target/release/behavcensus --games 200 --players {2,3,4} \
      --weights /private/tmp/opencensus/champ{2,3,4}p.json --threads 2

Champion weights were the FROZEN snapshots in /private/tmp/opencensus/, not
experiments/rust_champion_*.json, which the running climb rewrites on
promotion.

The bot's ordering by table size is INVERTED relative to humans: it starts
latest at the SMALLEST table, where wonders are most affordable. 4p sits on
top of the human distribution (23.2% vs 23.0%, median 4 vs 4); 2p is 4.7x
outside it (84.5% vs 18.1%); 3p is median 7 against a human median of 4.

(C) Does starting late lose? Win rate by first-wonder-stage round
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Self-play, so the null is 1/players. One-sided binomial P(X<=k).

  2p  rounds 3-8   183/370 = 49.5%  null 50.0%  p=0.4380   at par
      rounds 9+      5/24  = 20.8%  null 50.0%  p=0.0033   LOSES
  3p  rounds 3-7   116/295 = 39.3%  null 33.3%  p=0.9868   above par
      rounds 10+    34/138 = 24.6%  null 33.3%  p=0.0171   LOSES
      never built    3/16  = 18.8%  null 33.3%  p=0.1659   n.s.
  4p  rounds 3-5   187/727 = 25.7%  null 25.0%  p=0.6907   at par
      rounds 6+     10/61  = 16.4%  null 25.0%  p=0.0757   n.s.

Verdicts
--------
1. 2p median-6 starts are NOT a defect on this evidence. Rounds 3-8 are at
   par in self-play, and among HUMAN 2p games winners start marginally LATER
   than losers (mean 3.86 vs 3.69) -- the one table size where early starts
   do not predict winning. Divergence from humans alone is a diagnostic
   mirror, not a defect; see the Cultural Heritage precedent. CLOSED.

2. The extreme tail IS a defect, at every table size. 2p round 9+ wins 20.8%
   against a 50% null (p=0.0033) and 3p round 10+ wins 24.6% against 33.3%
   (p=0.0171). Together that is 162 player-games the champion routinely
   throws away. This is the live opening lead.

3. 3p is the worst of the three: median 7 against a human median of 4, a
   bimodal split (16% start at round 3, then a tail out to round 19), and
   the only player count where 2.7% never build a stage at all. 3p also has
   the largest share of its population inside the losing tail.

Caveat on (C): a self-play crosstab compares a bot against ITSELF, so it can
only find within-population variation. It cannot tell you the population
median is wrong -- which is exactly why the human corpus in (A) was needed
and why (1) rests on the human winner/loser split rather than on (C) alone.
