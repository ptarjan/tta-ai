Tech acquisition pipeline, bot vs human corpus, 3 players
===========================================================
Date: 2026-08-24
Tool: rust/src/bin/behavcensus.rs and rust/src/bin/humanopenings.rs, both
      extended with a "### Tech acquisition" section that reuses the SAME
      hooks the pre-existing "Production curve" / "Worker allocation curve"
      sections already tap -- no new sample instant was added:
        * SEEN reads `state.card_row` at the identical turn-start instant
          `prev_actor`'s worker-allocation block already samples (bot) /
          the identical `d.state` turn-start instant `humanopenings`'s own
          alloc block already samples (human).
        * BUILT/STAFFED are read off the exact same `p.techs.iter()` walk
          that block already runs to compute `farm_workers`/`mine_workers`
          -- BUILT = card present in `techs` at that sample; STAFFED =
          present with `workers > 0`.
        * TAKEN is read off `taken_card`, the pre-move `Move::Take` value
          both files already compute for their pre-existing "openings"
          section, at every decision point (not gated to turn-start, since
          a Take can happen anywhere in a turn).
      `Move::Upgrade` is not a BUILT event: `apply::do_upgrade` requires
      BOTH the `from` and `to` card already present in `techs` (it panics
      "hi not in tableau" otherwise) -- it only ever moves a worker between
      two ALREADY-BUILT cards of the same family, never a hand -> tableau
      transition, so it cannot be how a higher-tier tech first appears.

WHAT THIS ANSWERS
------------------
analysis/worker_allocation_3p_2026-08-24.txt measured the bot's best-owned
MINE tech level flat at the starting Bronze in every one of 21 sampled
rounds across 200 self-play games at 3p -- it never upgrades a mine, ever
-- while its best FARM level barely moves either. A follow-up recon pass
established this is NOT evaluation blindness: the leaf evaluation carries a
`BestMine` feature and the live 3p champion prices it positively and
substantially. The bot WANTS a better mine and never gets one, so the
blockage is upstream of the build decision. This census instruments the
four-stage pipeline a tech has to survive to reach a worker -- appears in
the card row (SEEN) -> taken into hand (TAKEN) -> tech cost paid, enters
the tableau (BUILT) -> a worker placed on it (STAFFED) -- per card TYPE,
bot vs human, to find where a higher-tier Mine actually dies.

THE ANSWER: A HIGHER-TIER MINE DIES AT TAKEN, NOT AT BUILT OR STAFFED
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The bot's Mine row settles the question cleanly, and every number needed to
settle it is already nonzero except the one that is the finding:

  * SEEN = 3.00 per player-game. All three non-starting mine cards (Iron,
    Coal, Oil) are visible in the card row at a turn-start sample in
    essentially every single player-game. The higher-tier mine is not
    hidden from the bot -- it sees it constantly.
  * TAKEN = 0.000 at EVERY tier (I/II/III), summed and broken down. Across
    600 player-games and up to ~21 rounds each, the bot's own `Move::Take`
    never once put a non-starting Mine card into `hand_civil`. This is an
    exact zero, not a rounding artifact of the 2-decimal aggregate row --
    the 3-decimal tier breakdown shows `0.000` at every one of the three
    tiers.
  * BUILT/STAFFED at tiers I/II/III are consequently also exactly 0.000 --
    there is nothing to build or staff because nothing was ever taken.

So the pipeline does not fail midway through -- it fails at the very first
transition after the card is visible. The bot's own take-side evaluation
(not its build-side evaluation, which the recon pass already cleared) never
selects a Mine card over its `Move::Take` competitors, at any tier, in any
of 600 self-play player-games. This is fully consistent with, and gives the
mechanism for, worker_allocation_3p_2026-08-24.txt's "bestMine is 1.00 for
literally every one of the 21 rounds sampled" -- a card that is never taken
can never be built, and a card that is never built can never be staffed.

The human side shows this is not a structural inevitability of the game:
humans take a higher-tier mine constantly (1.18 taken per player-game,
spread across all three tiers: I=0.539, II=0.421, III=0.223) and build most
of what they take (2.01 built total; I 0.521/0.539 = 97% of takes convert
to a build, II 0.373/0.421 = 89%, III 0.115/0.223 = 52%). The human BUILT
column is not a free-standing fact about mine cost being different for
humans -- it is downstream of TAKEN being nonzero in the first place, the
same causal order the bot's own zero row already establishes.

A SECOND, DISTINCT DEFECT ON THE FARM SIDE -- TAKEN-BUT-NOT-BUILT
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Farm is not the same failure shape as Mine, and this census's four-stage
split is what separates them. The bot's Farm TAKEN is nonzero (0.24 per
player-game, almost entirely tier III: 0.195 of the 0.237 total taken-above-
starting), so the take-side gate is not fully closed for Farm the way it is
for Mine. But BUILT lags far behind TAKEN at that same tier: 0.018 built
against 0.195 taken at tier III, a ~91% falloff between taking a
Mechanized Agriculture and ever developing it. Library (0.36 taken -> 0.10
built) and Arena (0.10 taken -> 0.02 built) show the same taken-but-not-
built shape. This is a build-side defect, not a take-side one, and it is a
different mechanism from Mine's: Mine never clears the TAKEN gate at all;
Farm/Library/Arena clear TAKEN and then mostly fail at BUILT. Both are real
defects; they are not the same defect and do not have the same fix.

CAVEATS ON THE MEASUREMENT ITSELF
------------------------------------
1. Every count in the main table is DISTINCT CardIds reaching that stage in
   one player-game (a `HashSet<CardId>` per stage per player-game, folded
   into the report once at game end) -- a card seen in the row on ten
   different turns, or re-examined after leaving and re-entering the row,
   contributes at most 1 to SEEN. This matches the task's own "distinct
   cards ... present in the card row" phrasing for SEEN and was extended to
   the other three stages for consistency, since none of these card types
   can legally have more than one physical copy of the same name in play
   at once in the base game's civil deck.
2. SEEN and BUILT/STAFFED are turn-start SNAPSHOTS (the same sampling
   instant the pre-existing Worker allocation curve section already uses),
   not an exhaustive per-decision-point scan. A card that entered and left
   the row, or was built and destroyed, entirely within a single one of a
   player's own turns (between two of that player's own turn-start
   samples) would not be counted at that stage. TAKEN is the one
   event-exact signal (read off `Move::Take` at every decision point, not
   just turn-start). This is the same "small, named approximation," not
   invented for this census, that the Production/Worker-allocation curves
   already accept and document.
3. The A=1.000 BUILT row on both sides is the two starting production
   techs (Bronze mine, Agriculture farm), present in `techs` from turn 1
   and never actually taken via `Move::Take` -- this is why TAKEN at tier A
   is 0.000 on both sides. It is not a measurement gap; starting techs are
   printed on the player board, not civil-row cards (RULES_SPEC.md /
   `data/cards_civil.json`'s own note on Agriculture).
4. SEEN is scoped to the ACTING player's own turn-start decision points on
   both the bot and human side (not "every player who could see the row"),
   matching the per-player-game shape every other column in this table
   already has. The card row is a shared, public resource, so a player who
   acts less often (a game that ends early) sees fewer of their own
   turn-start samples of it -- this is the same survivorship shape the
   Production/Worker-allocation curves already carry and did not need a
   new caveat to explain.
5. The human n (399) thins across the game exactly as it does in
   production_curve_3p_2026-08-24.txt and worker_allocation_3p_2026-08-24.txt
   (game-length survivorship); this census reports one number per
   player-game over the WHOLE game, not a per-round curve, so that thinning
   does not bias any single number here the way it would a late-round point
   on a curve -- but it does mean the human sample averages over shorter
   games than the bot's on average, which is a separate, already-documented
   fact (production_curve_3p_2026-08-24.txt's "n column" section) about why
   human games end sooner, not a new confound this file introduces.
6. This is a census of WHAT HAPPENED, at one player-game granularity, not a
   trace of WHY the bot's `Move::Take` search rejects a Mine card 600-for-
   600 times. The `TakenCard`/`legal_moves`-based "playable but never taken"
   machinery `behavcensus.rs`'s pre-existing Card fate section already has
   is scoped to cards already IN hand, not to why a still-in-the-row card
   loses the Take auction -- that is the natural next question this census
   opens, not one it answers.

VERDICT
-------
A higher-tier Mine dies at TAKEN. It is not a "never in the row" problem
(SEEN = 3.00/game, effectively every non-starting mine card is visible
across a game) and it is not a "taken but never built" or "built but never
staffed" problem the way Farm/Library/Arena partly are (once a card clears
TAKEN, the bot mostly does build and staff it: e.g. Farm's own I/II tiers
convert TAKEN to BUILT above 90%). For Mine specifically, TAKEN itself is
an exact 0.000 at every one of the three non-starting tiers across 600
player-games -- the bot's own move-selection at the Take step never once
chooses a better mine over its alternatives. The data cleanly decides this
question for Mine; it is less clean-cut for Farm, where the defect is a mix
of a nonzero-but-small TAKEN and a much larger falloff at BUILT.

DO NOT FIT WEIGHTS TO THE HUMAN NUMBERS.
The human census is a diagnostic mirror; this file names a defect in
where the pipeline breaks for a higher-tier Mine (and a separate one for
Farm/Library/Arena) and proposes no coefficient, weight, or fix for either.
