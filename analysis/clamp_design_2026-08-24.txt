--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE SCHEME IN ONE SENTENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Replace the single `const CLAMP: f64 = 60.0` in `bin/climb.rs` with a
per-`WeightKey` bound, `WeightKey::clamp_bound(self) -> f64`
(`rust/src/bots/weighted/weights.rs`, next to the existing `sign_intent`/
`group` methods it is structurally identical to -- one big, deliberately
non-wildcard `match` over all 162 variants), so the MAXIMUM POSSIBLE
CONTRIBUTION a coordinate can add to `evaluate`'s total
(`|weight| * that key's typical candidate-set spread`) is comparable across
keys, instead of every coordinate sharing one magnitude ceiling regardless
of its feature's natural scale.

For an ordinary coordinate (a key `linear_features` actually writes into
`out[]`):

    bound(k) = CLAMP_SCALE / max(spread(k), SPREAD_FLOOR)

  - `spread(k)` = the LARGER of the 2p/3p/4p MEAN candidate-set spreads in
    the audit's per-key table (not per-player-count -- see §4a for why).
  - `SPREAD_FLOOR = 0.02`, exactly the audit's own RETIRE threshold ("mean
    spread < 0.02 in every player count" = "cannot move an argmax"), so a
    structurally-zero-spread coordinate's bound saturates at one shared,
    generous, deliberately-loose constant (`CLAMP_FLOOR = 500.0`) instead of
    dividing by zero.
  - `CLAMP_SCALE = 70.0`, chosen to satisfy the two required invariants
    (§3): `culture_rate` needs `C > 59.16`, `food_stock` needs `C < 75.79`;
    70 sits in the middle of that window with margin both ways.

Multipliers (§2) cannot use this formula at all -- their own measured
spread is 0.0 BY CONSTRUCTION, not by having no effect -- and get a
separate constant, `CLAMP_MULTIPLIER = 10.0` (derivation and a real
correction discovered while testing this design are in §2).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE MULTIPLIER PROBLEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

31 of the 56 RETIRE keys (`analysis/feature_audit_2026-08-24.txt`'s
appended appendix, "MULTIPLIER (31)") are never written into the linear
feature vector at all. `linear_features` leaves their `out[]` slot at 0.0,
so their measured spread is exactly 0.0 by construction of the audit's
instrument -- it says nothing about whether they influence a decision.
Mechanically they are pricing MULTIPLIERS: read via `w.get(k)` inside
`cards.rs`/`row.rs` helpers (`card_potential_core`, `sum_yields`,
`tech_value`, `gov_value`, `action_value`, `tactic_value`, `row_pressure`,
...), and the SCALED RESULT lands in a DIFFERENT coordinate than `k`'s
own -- one of four documented "family landing coordinates": `HandPotential`,
`RivalHandPotential`, `RowUrgency`, `RowBargainForgone`. "weight × typical
spread" is meaningless for a key whose own spread column is a permanent
artifact of the measurement, not a fact about the key.

FIRST ATTEMPT (wrong, caught by testing, kept here as the record): bound
every multiplier by "it must not let the landing coordinate it feeds
exceed THAT coordinate's own contribution cap" -- take the MINIMUM of the
four family coordinates' own formula bounds and share it across all 31.
`HandPotential`'s own bound is 0.416 (its measured spread, up to 168.3 at
3p, is the single largest number anywhere in the audit's whole table --
`card_potential` sums whole-hand printed yields, an unnormalised scale, not
a per-card fraction), so that was the shared value.

THIS IS A UNIT ERROR. A multiplier is a dimensionless SCALE on an
already-priced quantity, not a linear coefficient commensurate with a
landing coordinate's weight. Proof, found by actually running the patch's
own tests rather than by inspection: `weight_key_table!`'s AUTHORED
DEFAULTS put 9 of these 31 keys at exactly `1.0` (`action_board_credit`,
`bonus_card_credit`, `card_rate_credit`, `gov_board_credit`,
`rate_horizon`, `restricted_resource_credit`, `tech_board_credit`,
`territory_credit`, `unit_tech_credit`) -- "no discount/no bonus yet," the
engine's own neutral starting point, authored long before this task and
independent of anything the league has measured. A bound of 0.416 clamps
those DEFAULTS on the very first load, before any training runs at all.
`runaway_weights_is_empty_for_a_vector_nowhere_near_the_clamp` (an existing
test, unmodified in intent) caught this immediately: `Weights::defaults()`
tripped the runaway guard under the first-attempt bound.

FIX: `CLAMP_MULTIPLIER = 10.0` -- 10x the largest authored default (1.0),
comfortably clear of every multiplier's unmeasured starting point, while
still 6x tighter than the old flat 60. This is NOT derived from the same
spread arithmetic as every ordinary coordinate -- it is a judgment call,
and it is the least rigorously justified constant in this design. A
proper fix would trace each multiplier's actual reachable magnitude
through `card_potential`/`row_pressure` (which printed-yield ranges each
credit can multiply, card by card) rather than pick one shared constant.
That tracing is future work, explicitly out of scope for this pass (see
"WHAT I COULD NOT DECIDE" at the end).

`HandPotential` itself IS one of the four family coordinates, so it keeps
its own formula-derived bound (0.416) as an ORDINARY coordinate, entirely
separately from `CLAMP_MULTIPLIER` -- the two constants are coincidentally
close in the first cut but are conceptually unrelated after the fix, and
the code no longer ties them together (see the patch: `HandPotential` has
its own `match` arm with a literal, not `CLAMP_MULTIPLIER`).

`FreeCivilAction` (the one DEAD key -- audit confirms it is read nowhere
outside plumbing/tests) is folded into the multiplier bucket too: any
finite constant is safe since nothing ever consults it, and reusing
`CLAMP_MULTIPLIER` avoids inventing a bespoke value for one inert key.

GENUINELY-ZERO-SPREAD COORDINATES (the other kind of RETIRE key, 21 of the
56 -- "COORDINATE" in the audit appendix, e.g. `attack_cost_doubled`,
`colonies`, `war_immune`): these ARE written into `out[]` by `features.rs`
(a real `f.set(...)` call each), they are just root-frozen or otherwise
structurally unable to vary across a candidate set. Their spread is
genuinely, not artifactually, ~0, so the ordinary formula naturally hits
`SPREAD_FLOOR` and they land on `CLAMP_FLOOR = 500.0` -- a large, boring,
deliberately loose constant, because correctness here never depends on the
exact number (nothing they do can move an argmax at their measured
spread), only on staying finite so the climb's mutation arithmetic never
sees an unbounded coordinate. Also on `CLAMP_FLOOR`: `resource_discount`/
`restricted_resources` (UNCLEAR in the audit -- the enum variant is a real
dispatch tag but the audit's exhaustive grep confirms the WEIGHT VALUE
itself is never read via `w.get` anywhere), and `LeaderReplacement`/
`WonderPoolRivalClaimed` (two `WeightKey` variants added to the enum AFTER
this audit ran, so no measured spread exists for them at all yet).

`wonder_promise` (UNCLEAR in the audit) is neither a multiplier nor a
zero-spread coordinate: it has no `out[]` slot of its own (spread 0.0 by
the same construction as a multiplier) but reads straight into
`evaluate`'s `total`, not into another coordinate. `sign_intent` already
pairs it with `WonderPotential` ("prices the in-progress wonder's
completion value"), so it inherits that sibling's formula bound (13.6949)
rather than either extreme.

3. THE TWO REQUIRED SANITY CHECKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

culture_rate: max(2p,3p,4p) mean spread = max(0.798, 0.7998, 0.986) = 0.986
  bound = 70 / 0.986 = 70.97  ->  70.97 > 60. PASS, headroom over the old
  clamp (confirmed by `clamp_bound_satisfies_the_culture_rate_and_
  food_stock_invariants`, a passing test in the patch).

food_stock: max(2p,3p,4p) mean spread = max(1.017, 0.6765, 1.263) = 1.263
  bound = 70 / 1.263 = 55.41  ->  55.41 < 60. PASS, cannot reach 60
  (same test).

Note the 3p champion's food_stock is CURRENTLY sitting at exactly 60 --
the audit's own CHANGE-list finding ("level standing in for a need," the
pinned-at-the-old-rail drift signature). This new bound makes that
specific value illegal, which is intentional: see §5.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE FOUR REQUIRED QUESTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4a. Per-player-count bounds, or one shared table?

ONE SHARED TABLE, keyed by the MAX of the 2p/3p/4p mean spreads.

A per-player-count table was the first instinct (`bin/climb.rs` already has
exactly this shape for `lead_scale(players: u8)`, so the plumbing precedent
exists) and looked attractive from `hand_potential`'s numbers: its spread
swings 39x across counts (4.33 / 168.3 / 13.99), so a shared max-based
bound crushes the tolerable range in 2p and 4p to whatever 3p's outlier
demands. But `food_stock` argues the opposite direction, and it is the
decisive case: its OWN 3p spread (0.68) is small precisely BECAUSE the
weight is already pinned at the old rail there (the audit's "level
standing in for a need" drift signature) -- a per-count bound keyed to
each count's own spread would read that low spread as room to grow the 3p
bound LOOSER (to ~103, per-count arithmetic), in exactly the player count
already showing the pathology this whole design exists to stop. The
shared-max table is the conservative, safe choice: it can never let ANY
player count's contribution exceed `CLAMP_SCALE`, at the cost of being
tighter than strictly necessary for player counts where a given key's real
spread is smaller than its worst-case count. It also needs no new
plumbing: `clamp`/`clamp_bound`/`parse_weights` stay `WeightKey`-indexed
only, with no player-count parameter threaded through any of them.

4b. Interaction with `sign_intent`.

`dominance_repair` (`eval.rs`) already runs `sign_intent`'s repairs
(pinning a `NonNegative`/`NonPositive` violation to 0.0, or raising a
`DOMINATES` pair) on every load and on every accepted mutation
(`bin/climb.rs:336`). `clamp_repair` (this design's load-time counterpart,
§4c) is ORDER-DEPENDENT against it and is applied SECOND, after
`dominance_repair`, for the same reason `dominance_repair` itself orders
`ORDERINGS LAST` internally: a `DOMINATES` repair can raise one
coordinate's value onto another's, so running the magnitude check first
could pass a since-overwritten value and skip clamping it.

No live conflict exists between a `sign_intent` gate and a `clamp_bound`:
sign classification restricts the SIGN (or pins to exactly 0.0), magnitude
restricts the ABSOLUTE VALUE -- an interval like `[-500, 0]` (a
`NonPositive` key at `CLAMP_FLOOR`) is non-empty for every key in this
table, since every `clamp_bound` is strictly positive. A conflict could
only arise from a key `sign_intent` pins to a FIXED nonzero value outside
its own bound; no such key exists in this codebase today (0.0 is the only
fixed pin `sign_intent`'s repairs ever write).

4c. Every place `CLAMP` is enforced (grep, `rust/src`, before this patch):
  - `bin/climb.rs:98`         `const CLAMP: f64 = 60.0;` (the definition)
  - `bin/climb.rs:201-202`    `fn clamp(x: f64) -> f64` (the enforcement)
  - `bin/climb.rs:218`        `runaway_weights`'s pinned-coordinate threshold
  - `bin/climb.rs:1276`       the RUNAWAY log line's printed value
  - `bin/climb.rs:1566`       `no_weight_escapes_the_clamp` test assertion
  - `bin/climb.rs:2513-2517`  `runaway_weights` test fixtures/assertions
  - `bin/climb.rs:381, 419`   the two `mutate_raw` call sites (`rescale`,
                              scatter/group/kick) -- these are the only
                              places a weight's value is actually WRITTEN
                              through `clamp()`.

CLAMP WAS **NOT** ENFORCED AT LOAD TIME. `eval::parse_weights`/
`load_weights` call `dominance_repair` (sign only) and never touched
`CLAMP` at all. A champion JSON could already contain -- and, per §5, the
live files ALREADY DO contain -- a weight beyond the old bound, and
nothing at load time would catch it; only the next climb mutation that
happened to touch that exact key would re-clamp it, and a key the climb
rarely mutates (or one outside `FROZEN`'s complement that a hand-edit
touched) could sit out of bounds indefinitely. A fix that only edits
`bin/climb.rs`'s `clamp()` function reproduces this exact gap for the new
per-key bounds. This patch closes it: `WeightKey::clamp_bound` moved out
of `bin/climb.rs` into the library (`weights.rs`, alongside `sign_intent`),
and a new `eval::clamp_repair` (mirroring `dominance_repair`'s shape and
call site exactly) is wired into `parse_weights`, so every champion load
-- not just every climb mutation -- enforces the same bound.

4d. On load, silently clamped or an error?

SILENTLY CLAMPED (via `clamp_repair`, matching `dominance_repair`'s
existing "repair on load, log the violation list, never reject" precedent
-- same `Violation` struct, same shape, called right alongside it in
`parse_weights`). This is a direct consequence of §5: the three LIVE
champion files already contain at least one out-of-bounds coordinate each
under this design's own bounds. An `Err` here would make `load_weights`
fail on every champion this repo currently has the moment this patch
lands, with no migration path -- clearly wrong for a first landing. Silent
repair matches the repo's own established convention for exactly this
class of problem (a loaded file that predates or otherwise violates a
constraint the evaluator now enforces) and gives the same audit trail
`dominance_repair` already provides: `clamp_repair` returns
`(Weights, Vec<Violation>)`, so a caller that wants to log loudly (a
future `bin/climb.rs` load path, or a startup check) can, without the
"unloadable file" failure mode. `Errors are Result<_, String>` (this
task's own style rule) still holds for genuine parse failures -- bad JSON,
an unknown key name, a non-finite number -- `clamp_repair` does not touch
or weaken any of those; it only extends the existing REPAIR path, not the
error path.

5. CHAMPION WEIGHTS OUT OF BOUNDS UNDER THIS PROPOSAL (the important part)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Checked all three live files, /Users/pt/tta-ai/experiments/rust_champion_
{2,3,4}p.json, against the champion values already quoted verbatim in
analysis/feature_audit_2026-08-24.txt's per-key table (not re-read from
the live files, which the climb rewrites every few minutes -- same
champion snapshot the audit itself used). 9 weight instances, across 7
keys, are out of bounds:

  key                     players   champion value      proposed bound
  ----------------------- --------- ------------------- ---------------
  food_stock              3p        60.0000              55.41
  resource_stock          3p        60.0000              32.6491
  blue_free               3p        60.0000              25.3696
  tech_board_credit       3p       -27.0717               10.0
  science_rate_trailing   2p        20.4793               10.0
  my_event_threat         2p        22.7845                8.729
  hand_potential          2p         9.2874                0.416
  hand_potential          3p         0.4344   (barely)      0.416
  hand_potential          4p        -0.9908                0.416

Three shapes, worth separating:

  (a) food_stock / resource_stock / blue_free (3p, all exactly 60.0000):
      these are literally PINNED AT THE OLD CLAMP right now. `food_stock`
      and `resource_stock` are two of the audit's own 5 CHANGE-list keys
      (the "level standing in for a need" drift signature -- a raw stock
      LEVEL doing the job an already-built Gap/Surplus hinge should be
      doing instead). This design's bound making them illegal is not a
      side effect, it is the mechanism working as intended: a raw level
      pinned at a magnitude bound has always been the audit's own evidence
      that the wrong feature is being priced, and the new tighter,
      key-specific bound simply catches it sooner than the old blanket 60
      did. `blue_free` (a KEEP key, not on the CHANGE list) pinning at the
      exact same old rail in the exact same 3p file is new evidence this
      audit didn't have -- worth a human's attention as a possible sixth
      CHANGE candidate, not something this design can adjudicate.

  (b) tech_board_credit (3p, -27.07) / science_rate_trailing (2p, 20.48):
      both multipliers, both dramatically past even the generous,
      default-driven `CLAMP_MULTIPLIER = 10.0` -- these are not marginal;
      whatever the climb has done with them looks like genuine runaway,
      the exact failure `runaway_weights`/the pool veto exist to catch,
      and neither is close enough to the boundary to be a rounding
      artifact of this design's own choices the way (a) partly is.

  (c) my_event_threat (2p, 22.78) / hand_potential (all three counts):
      both real KEEP coordinates, both among the audit's "identity-aware,
      freeze-priced gate" family (Limitations item 1) -- their measured
      spread is unusually large because `card_potential`/`event`-pricing
      helpers sum whole, unnormalised printed effects rather than a single
      bounded feature. This is the most important finding in this whole
      section: `hand_potential` is a REAL, actively-priced, nonzero-in-
      every-champion KEEP key (not a multiplier, not a drift artifact),
      and its own formula-derived bound (0.416, driven by its own 3p
      spread of 168.3 -- the single largest number in the entire audit
      table) is smaller than ALL THREE of its live champion values. Under
      this design as specified, landing it would force `hand_potential`
      down by roughly 22x in 2p and 2x in 4p. Whether that is a correct
      correction (the champion overpriced an unnormalised sum) or a sign
      the FORMULA mishandles "identity-aware, freeze-priced" coordinates
      as a class (both `hand_potential` and `my_event_threat` are members
      of that class, and both are now flagged) is exactly the kind of
      question this design surfaces rather than answers -- see below.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT I COULD NOT DECIDE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- The multiplier bound (`CLAMP_MULTIPLIER = 10.0`) is a judgment call, not
  a measurement -- §2 explains why the formula-derived approach is a unit
  error and what a properly-traced fix would need (per-multiplier reach
  through `card_potential`/`row_pressure`'s actual printed-yield ranges),
  but that tracing is real work this pass did not do.

- §5(c): whether `hand_potential`'s and `my_event_threat`'s formula bounds
  are right, or whether the "identity-aware, freeze-priced gate" class
  (10 keys total per the audit's Limitations item 1: `HandPotential`,
  `WonderPotential`, `HandMilPotential`, `RivalHandPotential`,
  `RowUrgency`, `RowBargainForgone`, `RowLastCopy`, `MyEventThreat`,
  `TacticGain`, `TacticShort`) needs a DIFFERENT organizing principle than
  ordinary coordinates -- their spread is measured through a helper that
  sums unnormalised printed effects, not a single bounded feature, which
  may make "spread" itself a different KIND of quantity for this class
  than for the other 90-odd KEEP keys. This design applies one formula
  uniformly and reports where that produces a champion-invalidating
  answer; it does not re-derive the formula per class.

- `blue_free` pinning at exactly 60 in the 3p champion (§5a) looks like
  the same drift signature the audit's own CHANGE list already caught for
  `food_stock`/`resource_stock`, but it is not itself on that list -- a
  human should decide whether it belongs there before or alongside
  landing this clamp.
