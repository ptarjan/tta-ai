STEER RECEIVED MID-TASK (governs the priority order below)

Paul's position, verbatim in substance: civil-action waste rate must NOT
become something the evaluator is scored on -- efficient use should EMERGE
from correctly valuing the moves actions are spent on, not from a bolted-on
"spend your actions" term. His hypothesis: `civil_actions`/
`civil_action_surplus`'s negative weights ARE exactly that hack -- a direct
penalty on unspent actions with no information about WHAT to spend on --
and a prior experiment zeroing them reportedly made the bot bank 3 unspent
CA on turn 1 instead of taking a card, suggesting the penalty props up an
otherwise too-low valuation.

Revised priority (this file follows it): (1) the best-available-move score
histogram on "chose to stop" turns, testing whether those scores are <= 0;
(2) the move family of the top forgone alternative, looking for one family
clustering the negative scores; (3) a zeroed-weights rerun, comparing waste
rate and take-cost split; (4) human baseline demoted to a yardstick, skip
if it needs a full replay; (5) no finding here is to be framed as "add a
punish-waste weight."

=============================================================================
METHOD

New binary: `rust/src/bin/wastecensus.rs`. 200 games, 2p, seeds 1..200 (same
convention as `actioncensus.rs`: `--seed 1 --games 200`, seed = 1+g).

For every player-turn, the decision point is captured IMMEDIATELY BEFORE the
`Move::EndTurn` that ends it (reusing `actioncensus.rs`'s own established fix
for the `economy::end_of_turn`-overwrites-`civil_actions` trap -- `pre_ca` is
read off `state.players[pre_current].civil_actions` before `game::step` is
called on the EndTurn move, not after). If `pre_ca > 0`:

  1. `moves = legal::legal_moves(&state)` -- the engine's own generator, at
     the exact state the bot faced. Every non-`EndTurn` move it returns is
     ALREADY known-affordable (legality itself gates on the real
     civil/military/resource budget -- there is no separate "cost <=
     remaining CA" filter layered on top by this file).
  2. `WeightedBot::rank_moves(&state, moves)` -- NOT a re-implementation of
     the bot's valuation. This is the exact function `WeightedBot::choose`
     (what the champion actually calls to pick `EndTurn` in the first
     place) is built on top of: same clone-per-candidate, same
     `apply::apply`, same `evaluate`, same `end_turn_bias` treatment
     (`rank_moves`'s own doc comment: `ranked[0].0 == choose(...)` always).
     Reusing it means the scores reported here are PROVABLY what the bot
     itself computed to make the choice, not an approximation of it.
  3. Per candidate, whether it actually spends >=1 civil action is decided
     empirically -- clone the state, `apply::apply` the move, compare
     `civil_actions` before/after on the acting player -- rather than by a
     hand-maintained "which `Move` variants cost CA" table (unit
     `Build`/`Upgrade`/`Destroy` cost a MILITARY action instead, and
     `moves.rs`'s own doc history records a prior bug from exactly this
     kind of hand classification).
  4. Bucket: **"nothing legal"** if no legal move besides EndTurn spends a
     civil action; **"chose to stop"** otherwise, recording the best-scoring
     (highest-`evaluate()`) civil-action-costing alternative's score, its
     move family, and EndTurn's own score on the same ranked list.

5 unit tests (`cargo test --profile difftest --bin wastecensus`, all
passing): the exhaustive `move_family` match pins `Take`->`Take` and
`EndTurn`->`Other`; `spends_civil_action` is pinned both ways (true for a
round-1 Take, `None` for `EndTurn` itself because applying it changes whose
turn it is -- documented as the reason `analyze_stop` never calls it on
`EndTurn`); a full 2p game closes with at least one `EndTurn`; a turn with
0 unspent CA is correctly skipped.

The zeroed-weights vector (`champion_2p_zeroed.json`, NOT committed to the
weights file's own history) is a plain Python-edited COPY of
`champion_2p_now.json` with `civil_actions` and `civil_action_surplus` both
set to 0.0 -- the live champion file was never touched.

WHY THE RAW-SCORE TEST UNDERSELLS THE FINDING, AND WHAT THE GAP SHOWS
INSTEAD: `evaluate()` returns an ABSOLUTE valuation of the whole resulting
board (culture, resources, tech, wonders, everything, weighted and summed),
not a marginal "was this specific move good" delta -- by round 10+ that
absolute number is naturally in the hundreds-to-thousands range regardless
of which of two very similar candidate boards (one with an extra card
taken, one without) you're looking at. So "is the best alternative's raw
score <= 0" is really asking "is the whole game going badly", which is a
different, coarser question than "is this specific move worth making" --
and the honest answer to THAT coarser question is NO, 96.3% of the time the
game is going fine (positive absolute score) even on turns where the bot
stops early. Read literally, this REFUTES the strongest form of the
hypothesis ("the evaluator thinks doing nothing is better than everything
it could buy, in absolute terms") for all but a small tail of turns.

The GAP is the quantity that actually decided the choice, and it tells the
sharper, truer story: on the median chose-to-stop turn, the best purchasable
move's resulting board scores about 9.3 points BELOW leaving the board
alone -- consistently negative (only ~0.1% of the mass is positive, since
the bot always picks the higher-scoring option and EndTurn won every one of
these turns by definition), but SMALL relative to the ~900-1500 typical
scale of the boards themselves (under 1%). This is not "the evaluator
thinks the game is lost" (it doesn't, per the raw-score result above) -- it
is "the evaluator prices the marginal cost of spending this civil action
(the resource/food/CA opportunity cost the move consumes, priced by
`consumption_headroom -5.43`, `corruption_headroom 0.66`, `food_gap -0.40`,
and similar per-unit-cost terms in the vector) as very slightly larger than
the marginal benefit of what it buys" on the close calls, and the
`end_turn_bias` weight (-0.252 in this champion, applied as a small
ADDITIVE PENALTY to EndTurn's own score, not a bonus -- confirmed by
reading `WeightedBot::rank_moves`'s source directly) is not enough to flip
that near-tie back toward spending. This matches the mechanism Paul's
hypothesis names (undervaluation of ordinary moves, not insufficient
spend-pressure) far better than the raw-score framing does -- it just shows
up as a small, close-call margin rather than a blowout.

Take dominates the forgone-alternatives list two-thirds of the time -- when
the bot stops with CA left and a real alternative existed, that alternative
is overwhelmingly "take one more card from the row", not some exotic
family. Every family except `Build` has a strongly positive mean score
(consistent with the raw-score result above: these are not bad boards, they
score well, just marginally less well than doing nothing). `Build`'s
negative mean (n=24, small) is the one family where the forgone alternative
genuinely looked bad in absolute terms -- too small a sample (0.5% of
chose-to-stop turns) to generalize from, flagged rather than over-read.

WHERE THE WASTE CONCENTRATES: monotonically increasing with age/round, not
concentrated at the start or a single phase --
  Age I 22.3% of wasted turns / 18.8% of unspent CA
  Age II 33.0% / 32.9%
  Age III 35.7% / 39.2% (the peak, both by turn-count and by CA-volume)
  Age IV 7.5% / 8.4% (fewer turns overall in age IV, not less wasteful)
Round buckets tell the same story: waste share climbs steadily from 17.6%
of wasted turns in rounds 1-5 to 29.3% in rounds 16-20 -- there is no early-
game-specific or late-game-specific spike, waste grows roughly in step with
how long the game has run, consistent with "more legal alternatives exist
as the tableau grows, and the marginal-value miscalibration applies to all
of them about equally" rather than a phase-specific bug.

Player-turns with CA unspent  62.5% (5111/8172)             63.5% (5125/8070)

Takes per player-game         13.68                          14.50

VERDICT: at the baseline this task can actually measure (45%, not the
inherited-but-unreproducible 20.3%), removing the penalty entirely moves
the overall waste rate by 0.4 percentage points -- noise, not a cliff, and
in the WRONG direction if the penalty were the thing suppressing waste (it
went up slightly, not down to near-zero). There is a real but small
early-game effect in the predicted direction (round-1-5 share of wasted CA
rises from 14.6% to 16.1%), consistent with SOME turn-1-ish banking effect
existing, just far smaller in this data than "banks 3 actions on turn 1"
implies as a general pattern across 200 games -- that anecdote may describe
a different champion/weights snapshot, a more extreme zeroing, or a specific
game this sample didn't happen to reproduce; it was not independently
reproduced here. Take-cost band 3 share actually FALLS slightly (40.2% ->
37.4%) when the penalty is removed, which is the opposite of "forced to
spend, it spends on whatever's most expensive" -- if anything, at this
baseline, the penalty's presence is very weakly associated with MORE
expensive-band spending, not less.

Read together with (1): this is consistent evidence for the same
conclusion -- the ~45% waste rate is not substantially propped up or
explained by these two weights either way. Whatever the load-bearing cause
is, it is not "the penalty is currently the only thing making the bot
spend" (zeroing it barely changes anything), and the score-histogram GAP
analysis above already points at the more likely mechanism: ordinary moves
(mostly Take, per the family breakdown) are priced with a small, systematic
NEGATIVE marginal margin relative to doing nothing, close enough that a
purely additive `end_turn_bias`/penalty term of the size in this vector
cannot reliably flip it, in either direction. This is squarely a
valuation-of-ordinary-moves finding, not a spend-pressure-strength finding
-- consistent with Paul's framing that the fix, if there is one, belongs in
correctly pricing what the civil action buys, not in the size of a
stop-penalty.

HUMAN BASELINE -- demoted per steer, not computed to full fidelity

Per the mid-task steer, this was explicitly deprioritized and budget was
NOT spent chasing a true per-turn CA-pool reconstruction. What IS available
cheaply, already established by the PRIOR worker's `corpuscensus.rs` (not
re-derived here): human 2p BGO players take **37.26 cards/player-game**,
against the bot's rate in this session's own sample of **13.68-14.50
takes/player-game** (roughly 2.6-2.7x fewer). This is a RATE, not a waste
fraction -- the denominator (a human's true civil-action pool per turn,
which depends on government and a handful of CA-granting techs/wonders --
Code of Laws +1, Justice System +1, Civil Service +2, Pyramids +1, Kremlin
+1, established this session by reading `card_table.rs` and
`effects::state_stats`) is not established anywhere in this repo, and BGO's
journal never prints a player's remaining CA pool directly.

Full reconstruction was judged not worth the (now-demoted) budget for two
independent reasons, both established this session: (a) `docs/REPLAY.md`'s
full journal->engine-state replay is 0/24 sample games complete (dominant
blocker: genuinely unrecoverable hidden information, forced military
discards, 58% of stops) -- a per-turn CA-pool binary built on top of that
replay would inherit its incompleteness, not bypass it; (b) `replay_common
.rs` DOES already carry a working CA-total-vs-spend cross-check
(`ca_take_spend_this_turn`/`costs::ca_total` compared live per line, gated
behind `TTA_REPLAY_DEBUG`) that a future worker could extend into a genuine
per-turn "unspent CA" census WITHOUT re-deriving government/card-effect
tracking from scratch -- flagged here as the concrete next step, not
attempted, because it still inherits the same 0/24-completion ceiling as
(a) for any game that runs long enough to hit a discard, which by
`REPLAY.md`'s own estimate is most real games eventually.

Bottom line: the human waste FRACTION is not established by this task. The
human take RATE (37.26/player-game) is roughly 2.6-2.7x the bot's own rate
in this exact sample, a large gap in the same direction the previous
worker's take-cost/dwell-time investigation already characterized -- this
new data neither strengthens nor undermines that finding, it just was not
the priority this session and is reported honestly as skipped.

=============================================================================
FRAMING CHECK (per steer point 5)

Nothing above is a recommendation to add or size a "punish waste" weight.
The two candidate mispriced quantities this data points at, in order of
how directly the evidence supports them:
  1. The marginal value of an ordinary Take/WonderStep/Population/Destroy
     move, net of its resource/food/CA opportunity cost, is priced with a
     small but consistent negative margin relative to doing nothing on
     exactly the turns where the bot stops (median gap -9.3, see the score
     histogram above) -- this is a pricing-of-ordinary-moves finding, which
     is where Paul's framing says to look.
  2. `Build`'s forgone-alternative mean score is negative (n=24, 0.5% of
     chose-to-stop turns) -- too small to generalize from in this sample,
     named rather than acted on.
Zeroing `civil_actions`/`civil_action_surplus` (the literal "punish waste"
terms) changed the waste rate by 0.4 points either way -- confirming they
are not the mechanism to adjust, consistent with not treating waste-rate
itself as a target.
