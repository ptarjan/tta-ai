THEIR CHANGE: gates StrengthRel's blend on `state.round <= 3`:
    round <= 3:  eff(L) = (1-L)*E + L*(B + La)      (B folds into the LATE
                                                       endpoint only; E is
                                                       the WHOLE early coef)
    round  > 3:  eff(L) = B + (1-L)*E + L*La         (old formula, untouched)
  where B = w[StrengthRel], E = w[StrengthRelEarly], La = w[StrengthRelLate].

IDENTIFIABILITY CONSEQUENCE (checked, not assumed): under the OLD uniform
formula A(L) = B + (1-L)E + L*La, the direction (B,E,La) += t*(1,-1,-1) is
in the kernel for every L -- exactly T1-B's degeneracy. Under the NEW
round-gated formula, apply that same shift: in the round<=3 branch,
eff_new(L) = (1-L)*(E-t) + L*(B+La) = eff_old(L) - (1-L)*t, which is NONZERO
for any round<=3 state with L < 1 (round<=3 is by definition early -- L is
essentially never exactly 1 there). So the old kernel direction is NOT in
the kernel of the new formula: moving along it changes evaluate()'s output
on every round<=3 state. The round<=3 branch identifies E and (B+La)
directly (already a non-redundant basis); the round>3 branch identifies
(B+E) and (B+La) as before. Combining both regimes: B = (B+E) - E, then
La = (B+La) - B. All three raw numbers become separately identifiable once
both regimes are visited (self-play trivially visits round<=3 -- every game
starts there) -- earlymil's fix does not just avoid the old degeneracy, it
STRUCTURALLY REMOVES it for this one triple.

I reported this finding to the coordinator (asked to check specifically
whether the round<=3 gate is a magic constant or a computed rulebook fact):
confirmed it IS a literal magic constant (`state.round <= 3`, repeated in
three call sites, arrived at empirically -- their own A/B: ungated
continuous blend measured 27.7% vs champion, round<=3 measured 44.1%), not
derived from any rulebook fact. Per this project's own rule ("the ENGINE
computes rulebook facts, the LEARNER learns worth"), this is a defect in
THEIR fix, not mine to touch -- flagged to the coordinator with a suggested
computed-fact replacement (gate on whether any rival has nonzero
strength/any staffed military unit exists yet, rather than a fixed round
count), NOT implemented (out of scope, explicitly told not to fix their
code).

CONCLUSION / RECOMMENDATION FOR THE MERGE: collapsing StrengthRel/
StrengthRelEarly/StrengthRelLate to a 2-parameter {start,end} basis, as
T1-B's original (pre-earlymil) analysis prescribed, would be WRONG once
earlymil's fix lands -- it would delete a distinction (E alone, separate
from B) their round-gated formula depends on and cannot be reconstructed
from {start,end} alone. Once earlymil's round-gate is in place, StrengthRel's
triple should NOT be collapsed the way this pass collapses the other three

(1) THE BLEND ALGEBRA -- confirmed against the actual code
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
rust/src/bots/weighted/eval.rs::evaluate(), two loops:

  for k in WeightKey::ALL:              total += w[k] * v * scale      (*)
  for k in PHASE_KEYS:                  total += w[k.early()] * (1-L) * v*scale
                                         total += w[k.late()]  *  L    * v*scale

Since PHASE_KEYS = [Workers, StrengthRel, TechLevels, HandValue] is a SUBSET
of WeightKey::ALL, the base term from (*) is ALSO added for every phase key.
So the total coefficient actually applied to a phase feature k, at lateness
L = horizon::lateness(state), is exactly:

    A(L) = w[k] + (1-L)*w[k.early()] + L*w[k.late()]

matching BASISCUT.txt's formula exactly (the `hz`/RATE_KEYS `scale` factor
multiplies v uniformly and does not change this argument -- PHASE_KEYS
members are never in RATE_KEYS, confirmed by reading horizon.rs, so scale is
always 1.0 for these four features in practice).

A(L) is an affine function of L -- exactly 2 degrees of freedom (its value
at L=0 and L=1). Three raw numbers (w[k], w[k.early()], w[k.late()])
represent that 2-parameter object. The map (base,early,late) -> (A(0),A(1))
is 3-to-2; its kernel is the line (base,early,late) += t*(1,-1,-1) for any
t. Moving all three weights along that direction changes NOTHING evaluate()
ever computes, at any lateness, on any board, for k in {Workers, TechLevels,
HandValue} (StrengthRel excluded, see Section 0).

`rivals.rs::feature_marginal` (the generic per-feature marginal used by the
card pricer) implements the identical formula for the same four keys --
confirmed by reading it, and updated in lock-step (Section 3).

`eval.rs::linear_features` (the per-key feature vector `sum_k w[k]*out[k] ==
evaluate()`'s own total, used by `bin/agreefit.rs`) implements the same
formula via `out[k.early()]=v*(1-L)`, `out[k.late()]=v*L`, with `out[k]=v`
set by the generic loop -- also updated in lock-step (Section 3).

(3) THE CHOSEN 2-PARAMETER FORM, AND WHY
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Chose (start, end) = (A(0), A(1)) -- the value at the early extreme (L=0)
and the late extreme (L=1) -- evaluated as `start*(1-L) + end*L`, exactly
BASISCUT's own recommendation. Justification against the ACTUAL ramp read
from the code (not assumed):

  * A(L) is genuinely affine in L (confirmed in Section 1), so (A(0),A(1))
    is a COMPLETE, non-redundant description of the curve -- no information
    is lost, and no third coordinate could ever be independently pinned
    down by any training corpus, real or synthetic (this is the entire
    point: the old third coordinate was NOT under-trained, it was provably
    unobservable).
  * Both coordinates are directly interpretable on their own terms: `start`
    is literally "what this coefficient is worth in the literal opening"
    and `end` is literally "what it's worth at the literal endgame" -- a
    league or a human reading a champion file no longer has to mentally
    recombine three numbers (and get the sign of the interaction wrong, as
    the champion evidence shows happened) to answer "what does this bot
    actually think a worker is worth on turn 1".
  * A (flat, delta) basis (start = base, end = base+delta) was considered
    and rejected: it is equally non-redundant in principle, but reuses
    "base" with a DIFFERENT meaning than the old base (old base was
    "unconditional, every lateness"; new base-as-flat would silently become
    "value at L=0 only"), inviting exactly the kind of misreading a
    minimal-diff-looking rename produces. (start,end) makes the semantic
    change explicit at both ends instead of hiding it in one of them.
  * Implementation reuses the EXISTING two WeightKey identifiers per triple
    (the base key becomes `start`, the `_late` key becomes `end`) rather
    than introducing new names -- only the `_early` key is retired. This
    keeps every file's `_late` JSON field name meaningful across the change
    (still literally "the late-phase coefficient") and minimises the
    surface touched (`WeightKey::early()`'s match arm, not a whole new pair
    of identifiers).


(4) THE CONVERSION FORMULA -- exact, mechanical, applied at load
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
    new_start = old_base + old_early     (stored under the SAME base key,
                                           e.g. `workers`)
    new_end   = old_base + old_late      (stored under the SAME `_late` key,
                                           e.g. `workers_late`)

Implemented in rust/src/bots/weighted/eval.rs::parse_weights (called by
load_weights, called by every real loader in the crate):

  * `LEGACY_PHASE_EARLY_FOLD: &[(&str, WeightKey)]` names the three retired
    legacy field names ("workers_early", "tech_levels_early",
    "hand_value_early") and which live key each folds into.
  * The main field loop captures each legacy name's value into
    `legacy_early: [Option<f64>; 3]` (NOT a bare f64 defaulting to 0.0 --
    presence vs. absence is load-bearing, see below) instead of erroring
    "unknown weight".
  * After the loop, for each triple: IF the legacy `_early` field was
    PRESENT in the source JSON, `old_base = w.get(target)` is captured (the
    value the normal field-resolution loop already set from the file's own
    `"workers"`/etc. field, or the default if absent), then
    `w.set(target, old_base + early_v)` and
    `w.set(target.late(), old_base + w.get(target.late()))`.
  * IF the legacy field was ABSENT, no fold happens at all -- the file is
    treated as already being in the new {start,end} shape. This is the
    idempotency guard that makes the conversion safe to apply
    UNCONDITIONALLY on every load, including a file already written by the
    new code: a genuinely new-format file (no `workers_early` key at all,
    since the variant no longer exists to write one) round-trips completely
    unchanged.

    THIS GUARD IS NOT COSMETIC -- it was a real bug caught by
    `a_champion_round_trips_through_text` on the first implementation
    attempt: an early version folded UNCONDITIONALLY (defaulting the
    missing legacy value to 0.0 rather than skipping the fold entirely),
    which corrupted a fresh `Weights::defaults()` round-trip
    (`workers_late` came back as `3.0` instead of `0.8` -- the file had no
    `workers_early` field at all, so the fold should have been a no-op, but
    the old code still added `old_base` into `workers_late` a second,
    spurious time). Fixed by switching `legacy_early` from `[f64; 3]` to
    `[Option<f64>; 3]` and gating the fold on `Some`.

  * `dominance_repair` runs after the fold (unchanged position in
    `parse_weights`), so the new {start,end} values are still subject to
    every existing sign gate.

Migration note (accepted, documented, does not affect any file on disk): a
hand-crafted legacy file that carries `workers_early` while OMITTING the
base `workers` field itself would fold against the NEW default (2.2) rather
than the OLD one (1.4), since `Weights::defaults()` is what backfills a
missing base key. `save_weights`/`weights_json` have always written every
live key, so no real champion/gauntlet/scratch file has this shape -- this
is a known, accepted limitation for a shape that cannot occur in practice,
not a silent bug in the 5 files this pass had to prove.


(5) `sign_intent` / dominance_repair CONSEQUENCE: HandValue's gate simplifies
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
HandValue's feature (`features.rs`'s `hand_value`, sum of `level()+1` over
the civil hand) is always >= 0. Before this pass, the constraint "the
EFFECTIVE coefficient must never go negative at any lateness" needed a
composite mechanism (`eval::NET_NONNEG_PHASE`, containing `WeightKey::
HandValue`) because the three raw numbers made "the base alone" and "the
net" different things.

After the collapse: `start*(1-L) + end*L` is a CONVEX combination of `start`
and `end` (weights `1-L` and `L`, both in `[0,1]`), so the net is `>= 0` at
EVERY lateness in `[0,1]` if and only if BOTH endpoints are individually
`>= 0` -- exactly what a plain `SignIntent::NonNegative` gate on each
already checks. `HandValue` and `HandValueLate` moved from `Free` (guarded
by the composite mechanism) to `NonNegative` directly; `NET_NONNEG_PHASE`
is now `&[]` (kept as a live, empty mechanism rather than deleted, in case a
future key needs a genuine composite constraint the per-key gates cannot
express). Strictly simpler, not weaker -- the old composite constraint set
and the new pair of per-key gates are IDENTICAL once the redundant degree of
freedom is gone, and this was exactly BASISCUT's own prediction (Section 6:
"HandValue's NET_NONNEG_PHASE gate becomes two much simpler constraints").

`WeightKey::sign_intent` stays exhaustive, no wildcard `_ =>` arm added --
the compiler forced every match site touching the removed variants
(`WorkersEarly`/`TechLevelsEarly`/`HandValueEarly`) to be fixed by hand:
`sign_intent`, `group`, `early()`/`late()`, the two registry.rs exemption
tables, and every literal test reference. `cargo build` caught every one of
these; none were found by manual inspection alone.

7c. ROOT CAUSE OF THE OBSERVED DIVERGENCE (investigated, not assumed)

  Confirmed via deterministic bisection (games=2,4,...,20, both pre-fix and
  post-fix binaries, same seed): the 2p file's before/after outputs are
  IDENTICAL through games=10 and diverge starting at games=12 -- a single,
  fixed point, reproduced identically across repeated runs (proving this is
  a genuine, deterministic effect of the code change, not measurement
  noise/flakiness).

  Quantified the mechanism directly: for the collapsed triples, the OLD
  formula computes THREE separate floating-point products added to the
  evaluate() accumulator at different points (`base*v*scale`,
  `early*(1-L)*v*scale`, `late*L*v*scale`); the NEW formula computes TWO
  products from PRE-COMBINED numbers (`start*(1-L)*v*scale`,
  `end*L*v*scale`, where `start=base+early` and `end=base+late` were summed
  ONCE at load time). These are algebraically identical in real-number
  arithmetic (proven in 7a) but NOT bit-identical in IEEE754 floating point,
  because floating-point multiplication does not distribute over addition
  exactly: `(a+b)*c` and `a*c + b*c` can differ by a rounding-error-scale
  amount. A Monte Carlo sweep (200,000 samples) over realistic feature
  values, using rust_champion_2p.json's OWN triple numbers, found a worst-
  case absolute gap of 4.5e-13 on evaluate()-scale totals around 1600 (a
  RELATIVE error of ~3e-16 -- exactly machine epsilon for f64, the
  theoretical floor for this kind of restructuring, not evidence of a
  computational mistake).

  This gap can only flip a decision when two DIFFERENT candidate moves'
  TRUE evaluate() scores happen to be closer than ~1e-13 apart -- an
  extremely rare coincidence in a game with continuous-valued weights
  dotted against integer/rational board facts. Measured rate: 0 flips in
  the first 5 deals (10 games) of the 2p file, then (apparently) 1 flip
  somewhere in deals 6-10, cascading into a different but STILL FULLY
  DETERMINISTIC subsequent trajectory for that one deal/seat (1-ply search
  is chaotic under a changed early decision, so one flipped tie can shift
  mean-moves/culture by a few units in that single game, which is exactly
  the scale of difference observed: ~1 move and ~0.2-0.4 culture points
  averaged over 10-20 games). The gauntlet file's divergence WASHED OUT by
  n=200 (exact re-match), consistent with an isolated, non-recurring flip
  rather than a systematic bias.

  THIS IS NOT SPECIFIC TO THE (start,end) BASIS CHOSEN IN SECTION 3 -- it is
  an unavoidable consequence of representing "base+early" and "base+late"
  as two independently-stored numbers (any lossless 2-parameter basis for
  this family requires exactly this kind of pre-combination, since the
  eliminated third degree of freedom has to be folded into ONE of the two
  survivors via floating-point addition, at some point, for any legacy file
  conversion). There is no algebraic trick that avoids it while still
  achieving the actual point of this pass (removing the redundant stored
  parameter) -- confirmed by working through the alternative of keeping
  `base` un-folded and instead trying to recompute a phase-dependent
  "delta" fresh at every call: that requires knowing `early`/`late`
  SEPARATELY, i.e. not eliminating them at all.

  CONCLUSION: this pass achieves REAL-NUMBER-EXACT algebraic equivalence
  (proven in 7a to 1e-12, i.e. far tighter than the ~1e-13 IEEE754 floor
  itself) and BYTE-IDENTICAL empirical play for 3 of 5 required files and
  the overwhelming majority of positions in the other 2 (the two files
  with the largest, most pathological drift values -- the exact evidence
  this whole fix exists to correct). It does NOT achieve literal IEEE754
  bit-for-bit identical evaluate() output on files whose drifted phase
  weights are large enough for the ~1e-13 rounding floor to occasionally
  cross a genuine near-tie between two candidate moves. This is flagged
  here explicitly, per the task's own instruction to report rather than
  silently declare success, and per my own judgement that shipping the
  fix (with this documented, bounded, provably-inherent caveat) is the
  right call: the alternative -- NOT collapsing the triples -- leaves the
  proven, unconditional, EXACT redundancy (BASISCUT Section 2) in place
  indefinitely, which is a strictly worse and unbounded defect (an entire
