takeworth: per-WeightKey decomposition of chosen Take moves
=============================================================

Instrument: rust/src/bin/takeworth.rs (new analysis binary, 3 unit tests,
all passing). For every turn where WeightedBot::choose actually returned
Move::Take{slot}, it recomputes the exact 1-ply comparison choose() made
(same rival_context, same end_turn_bias asymmetry) and additionally walks
eval::evaluate()'s own body TWICE (pre-take root state, post-take trial
state), replacing `total +=` with `out.push((label, contribution))` for
every term evaluate() has (the flat WeightKey::ALL loop, the 4 PHASE_KEYS
blended pairs, and all 10 non-linear "priced through w" special terms:
hand_potential, wonder_potential, wonder_promise, hand_mil_potential,
tactic_gain/short, rival_hand_potential, row_urgency/row_bargain_forgone,
row_last_copy, my_event_threat). The per-key delta (after-before) for a
label is exactly the slice of evaluate(after)-evaluate(before) that label
owns, by construction.

VERIFICATION: pinned by a unit test (per_key_deltas_sum_to_the_raw_score_
delta) and confirmed on the real run: max |sum(per-key deltas) - raw_delta|
over all 7031 real Take samples = 0.000000000. The decomposition is exact,
not approximate.

Weights: frozen 2p champion, copied to
/private/tmp/cardworth/frozen_champion_2p.json (md5-identical to
/private/tmp/ipadgame/champion_2p_game.json). Never touched
/Users/pt/tta-ai or its experiments/rust_champion_*.json.

Sample: 200 self-play 2p games, seed 1..200, both seats playing the frozen
champion. 400 player-games, 7031 Take decisions. Runtime: 4.1s total
(6 threads) -- the whole binary, build+test+run, took under a minute.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------
row_last_copy attribution check (is it really "what the card gives"?)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
row_last_copy sums cards::card_potential(name) * P(no further copy) over
the ENTIRE VISIBLE ROW, not just the taken slot (row.rs's own doc comment).
A large before/after swing could come from (a) the taken card's own term
vanishing, (b) the slot's RANDOM REFILL card entering, or (c) every OTHER
row card's P(no further copy) shifting because the deck lost a copy.
Measured directly (taken_card_last_copy_term: the taken card's own before-
state term, isolated):

  ALL takes:  full delta mean=374.13, taken-card's-own-term slice=366.98
              (98.1% of the full delta)
  cost=1:     94.2% attributable to the taken card's own term
  cost=2:     98.5%
  cost=3:     98.4%
  other:      60.5% (wonder-surcharge takes; noisier, smaller n)

So row_last_copy's dominance is REAL and mostly attributable to the
specific card taken (its own last-copy scarcity), not a refill/deck-noise
artifact -- clean result, not a confound, for cost 1/2/3 (the 99.3% of
takes at those three costs). The "other" bucket (surcharge takes, n=243)
is noisier and only ~60% attributable; not enough signal there to say more.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------
3d. card_board_* family (card_board_leader, wonder, credit, government,
    action, bonus)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Direct per-key contribution: 0.0000 on EVERY sampled take, at every cost,
despite the weight being present/nonzero (card_board_leader = -1.0158 in
this frozen champion) and the term being "gated in" (present_on = 100% of
takes). Reconciled with the source: features::features() never WRITES the
CardBoardLeader/Wonder/Government/Action/Bonus coordinates at all (per
features.rs's own doc comment) -- those WeightKeys are read ONLY inside
cards.rs's card_potential()/board_credit_key() machinery as a per-card-TYPE
multiplier used when PRICING a leader/wonder/government/action/bonus card
(registry.rs literally labels them "cards.rs board-credit multiplier").
That pricing is what feeds row_last_copy, hand_potential, and
wonder_potential -- all of which DO move on a take.

CANNOT ATTRIBUTE: this decomposition cannot cleanly separate how much of
the measured +374 mean row_last_copy delta is attributable to the
card_board_leader=-1.0158 multiplier suppressing leader valuations
specifically, versus the rest of card_potential's pricing, without a
further counterfactual re-run (e.g. zeroing card_board_leader and
re-measuring) -- which is a "what if" experiment, out of scope for a
measurement-only task. What IS certain: the direct WeightKey::ALL-loop
contribution of every card_board_* key is exactly 0.0 on a Take, because
Take never touches board state (a taken card sits in HAND until a later
Build/PlayLeader/WonderStep move); their real influence, whatever it is,
is entirely indirect, folded into the row_last_copy/hand_potential numbers
already reported above.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. VERDICT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
NO -- on a typical chosen Take, ca_left is NOT the dominant term. It is
~30x SMALLER than row_last_copy (mean +12.32 vs +374.13 across all takes;
ratio ca_left/sum(all other keys) = 0.033, i.e. ca_left is about 3.3% the
size of everything else combined). This holds at every take cost (1/2/3):
the ratio stays in the 0.027-0.056 range throughout, so the cost-3
preference is not "ca_left's proportional-to-price push outvoting a flat
card value" -- row_last_copy itself grows in lockstep with cost and stays
30-40x larger than ca_left at every tier.

The working hypothesis as stated ("taking a card is intrinsically valued
far too low, and ca_left compensates by brute force") is NOT supported by
this decomposition of ACTUALLY CHOSEN takes: the dominant positive term is
row_last_copy (a card-scarcity/last-copy-avoidance value, 94-98.5%
attributable to the specific card taken, not a refill artifact), and once
row_last_copy is set aside, the remaining "traditional" what-the-card-gives
channels (hand_value, hand_potential, hand_civil, civil_action_surplus,
science_surplus, wonder_*) sum to roughly ZERO net (~-2.3), essentially
matching the earlier finding that ordinary moves are priced barely
negative/near-zero at the margin. It is row_last_copy, not ca_left, that is
currently doing the work of making Take moves score positive at all.

This does not contradict the earlier established fact that zeroing ca_left
flips waste from 20.3%->49.4% and loses head-to-head: that experiment
measured the EndTurn-vs-everything decision across ALL move families and
ALL turns, including many where row_last_copy is small or zero (row not
scarce yet). This file only decomposes turns where a Take was ALREADY the
chosen move -- it says nothing about how often ca_left, rather than
row_last_copy, is the deciding factor on the marginal turns wastecensus.rs
found. Not measured here; would need a further slice (row_last_copy near
zero vs not) to speak to that, which was out of this run's scope.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------
What could not be cleanly attributed
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. row_last_copy on the "other cost" bucket (surcharge takes, n=243, 3.5%
   of the sample): only 60.5% of its delta traces to the taken card's own
   term vs 94-98.5% elsewhere -- smaller n and wonder-surcharge interaction
   make this noisier; not investigated further.
2. card_board_* family's real (indirect) influence on row_last_copy/
   hand_potential's magnitude -- direct contribution is exactly 0.0 and
   provably so (features() never writes those coordinates), but their
   folded-in effect inside cards::card_potential's pricing was not
   separately isolated (would require a counterfactual re-run, out of
   scope for measurement-only).
