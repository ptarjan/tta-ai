WHAT WAS ADDED
--------------
A new boolean WeightKey, HasColony, mirroring WeightKey::HasUnit's shape
exactly: 0.0 with zero colonies, 1.0 with one or more. It is a cliff, not a
slope, priced separately from the existing linear Colonies count. Left
Free (no sign_intent declared) per instructions -- the direction is
genuinely unknown and must be climbed, and a sign declaration here would
repeat the mistake just reverted in commit 52c3615 ("Revert 'Classify
Colonies as NonNegative...'"), which is HEAD~1 in this tree. Colonies
itself (computation, default, classification) was NOT touched.

Citation for the cliff: RULES_SPEC.md line 123 (section 5.4) -- Annex
prints "one opponent who owns at least one colony" as its target clause,
and rust/src/legal.rs's aggression_target_qualifies (line 170) gates it on
q.colonies.is_empty(). That is the one fact about holding a colony that was
priced nowhere before this change; every other colony benefit already
flows into other priced coordinates (production/culture/science/strength)
or EventScoringMargin.

EXHAUSTIVE-MATCH DISCIPLINE -- VERIFIED, NO LATENT DEFECT
-----------------------------------------------------------
Adding the WeightKey::HasColony variant forced compiler errors (before I
fixed them) at exactly two match sites: `WeightKey::group()` and
`WeightKey::sign_intent()`, both in weights.rs, both `match self { ... }`
with no wildcard arm -- exactly as expected. After fixing both, the crate
builds clean.

I searched separately for any `_ =>` arm anywhere in weights.rs/features.rs
that might have silently swallowed the new variant without erroring:
  - weights.rs has three `_ => panic!(...)` arms (WeightKey::early/late/
    trailing) -- these are intentional runtime guards for keys with no
    early/late/trailing partner, not silent defaults; HasColony (like
    Colonies and HasUnit) simply has no such partner and was never meant to
    appear there.
  - features.rs has two `_ => {}` arms, both on `match kind` where
    `kind: CardType` -- unrelated to WeightKey entirely.
No latent wildcard defect found; the exhaustive-match discipline holds.
