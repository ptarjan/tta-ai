METHOD
=============================================================================
Plain logistic regression of `win` (0/1) on the 156 standardised feature
columns (mean/std fit on TRAIN only, applied to every split), full-batch
Adam (no external optimizer crate -- Cargo.toml's [dependencies] stays
empty), L2-regularised. This is a MARGINAL state-value regression (one row =
one state, no candidate-move grouping), per the task's explicit
instruction ("Logistic regression of `win` on the 156 feature columns") --
NOT the conditional-logit "which move did the human pick" model
`human_policy::train` already implements for a different project. See "WHY
IT LOST" for why this distinction plausibly matters.

SPLIT BY GAME, NEVER BY ROW (MEASURED game-level split sizes):
  FNV-1a hash of game_id, %10: {0,1}->test (~20%), {2,3}->val (~20%),
  {4..9}->train (~60%). Deterministic, same construction as
  human_policy::is_held_out, widened from 2 buckets to 3.

  Split   2p games   3p games   4p games   total games
  train      112         11          2         125
  val         49          6          2          57
  test        32          6          2          40
  TOTAL      193         23          6         222

  Row counts: train=35,606  val=16,415  test=11,896  (train+val=52,021)

L2 chosen by GRID SEARCH on validation log-loss (NOT picked by hand):
  candidates {0, 1e-4, 1e-3, 1e-2, 3e-2, 1e-1, 3e-1, 1, 3, 10}, 400 epochs

SECOND FIT: SIGN-CONSTRAINED (MEASURED)
=============================================================================
Same data, same chosen L2, same optimizer, but a per-coordinate PROJECTED
gradient step: after every Adam update, a coordinate whose WeightKey::
sign_intent is NonNegative/NonPositive is clipped back to 0.0 if it crossed
that boundary -- a true projected-gradient fit of the CONSTRAINED optimum,
not a post-hoc clamp of the unconstrained one. Confirmed to produce zero
sign violations (see above) and to be a genuinely different vector from the
unconstrained fit (not merely the same vector with 7 coordinates zeroed --
e.g. best_theater/leader appear in the constrained fit's own top-20 list but
not the unconstrained one's).

    left them at their zero-initialized value. This neither confirms nor
    refutes the ~188-point-per-miss finding; it is a DATA COVERAGE gap in
    this corpus (leafdump snapshots the state a decision is made FROM, not
    the mid-auction candidate-trial states a search evaluates), not a
    measurement this fit can make either way.
  - `colonies` (the acting player's OWN colony count, `p.colonies.len()`,
    confirmed by reading features.rs:557) fires in 38.7% of rows (24,752/
    63,917) -- plenty of real signal. The CURRENT 2p champion prices it at
    -2.09 (i.e. treats owning more colonies as a substantial PENALTY). This
    fit, trained directly on real human win outcomes, prices the identical
    coordinate at a small POSITIVE +0.065/+0.061. That is a SIGN FLIP on a
    well-observed, non-gated (`Free`-classified, so never auto-repaired)
    coordinate between the champion and what real outcomes support -- this
    reads like a genuine TUNING defect in the champion's `colonies`
    coefficient specifically, not a scaling artifact, though it was not
    investigated further here (out of this task's scope).
  - Overall vector SCALE differs enormously: L2 norm of the champion's 158
    stored weights is 182.4 (mean |w|=5.20, max |w|=60.0); this fit's L2
    norm is 1.30 unconstrained / 1.25 constrained (mean |w| ~0.032, max |w|
    ~1.1) -- roughly 140x smaller. Per the task's own framing, an overall
    POSITIVE scalar multiple of the whole vector cannot change which
    candidate move a single 1-ply argmax picks (see "Standardise-then-
    denormalise" in leaffit.rs's own doc comment for the algebra), so this
    alone does not explain a 3% match-play result by itself -- but see "WHY
    IT LOST" below for why it is flagged as a live hypothesis anyway.

WHY IT LOST -- MEASURED vs HYPOTHESISED
=============================================================================
MEASURED facts, not in dispute:
  - The fit predicts win/loss from a single state well above base rate on
    held-out games (2p: 66.7-67.4% accuracy vs 52.8% base rate; logloss
    0.59 vs 0.69) -- a real, replicated statistical signal.
  - The SAME fitted vector, used as a 1-ply move-ranking evaluator in actual
    play, loses to the current champion at ~3% (n=1000, tight CI).
  - The fitted vector's overall scale (L2 norm ~1.3) is ~140x smaller than
    the champion's (L2 norm ~182), and its largest single coefficient
    (pact_blocks_attack, ~1.1) is smaller than the champion's mean |weight|
    (~5.2).
  - This task's earlier documented lesson (a search-imitation student that
    copied a WEAKER teacher's judgement lost match play at 29.9%) is a
    DIFFERENT project and a different mechanism -- this fit is not imitating
    the champion's search at all, it is fit directly on human outcomes,
    which are a genuinely stronger signal for position value in principle.
    That the result is even WORSE here (3% vs 29.9%) is measured, not
    assumed to follow from the same cause.

HYPOTHESIS (plausible, NOT verified within this task's budget -- flagged for
whoever picks this up next, not asserted as fact):
  - Marginal single-state P(win) regression optimises a DIFFERENT objective
    than 1-ply move discrimination needs. It rewards a coefficient direction
    for HOW WELL a raw feature correlates with eventual victory averaged
    over the whole corpus (dominated by broad, slow-moving game-progress
    signals -- culture/science stocks, round number proxies), not for how
    much that feature should move the SCORE GAP between two candidate next-
    states reachable by one move THIS turn. `human_policy::train`'s existing
    conditional-logit ("softmax over the legal-move list", fit on human
    MOVE CHOICES rather than eventual win/loss) is the model shape this
    project already built for exactly the move-discrimination objective;
    this task was explicitly instructed to fit `win` by plain logistic
    regression instead, which is a materially different target. Whether
    THAT difference (rather than the L2 shrinkage, rather than the 7 sign
    violations already ruled out by the constrained-fit comparison above)
    is the dominant cause of the match-play loss was not isolated here --
    would need, e.g., a conditional-logit fit on this same corpus's implied
    "which state resulted from the move actually taken" for direct
    comparison, which is out of scope for this task's budget.
  - The 140x scale gap is flagged as a live, UNRESOLVED hypothesis, not
    dismissed: an overall positive scalar multiple is provably free for a
    SINGLE 1-ply argmax (see the round-trip section), but this codebase's
    search is not necessarily a single flat 1-ply argmax end to end
    (`bots::plan`/`bots::human::choose_with_search` reference a "root
    shortlist" and deeper lookahead) -- whether any deeper-search mechanism
    combines `evaluate()` scores in a way that is NOT scale-invariant (e.g.
    across plies, or against some other quantity) was not checked here.
    A quick grep for absolute-threshold/temperature logic in
    bots/weighted/eval.rs and bots/plan.rs found none obviously, but this
    was not an exhaustive audit.

WHAT WAS NOT DONE (explicitly out of scope / not attempted)

  corpus was attempted for direct comparison -- flagged above as the most
  promising next step given the hypothesis about the objective mismatch.
- No attempt was made to rescale the fitted vector up to the champion's L2

  changed sign recently, or whether some OTHER coordinate is compensating
  for it in the champion's own vector).

  are reported per the task's own instruction to state small-sample caveats,
  not treated as reliable standalone findings.
