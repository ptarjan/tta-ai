FOODCHOICE -- does the LIVE ENGINE, not just the replayer, misplay the
"strongest/weakest civilization gains/loses N food and/or resources" event?
=============================================================================
ANSWER: NO, this is NOT purely a replay-side gap. The LIVE ENGINE plays this
rule wrong during ordinary self-play too. `events::food_or_resources`
(rust/src/events.rs:216) is a fixed deterministic formula ("resources

1. WHAT THE RULES ACTUALLY SAY (MEASURED -- every claim below is quoted)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

RULES_SPEC.md line 119, §5.3 "Evaluating events" [RB p.16, CoL p.7]:
  "Multiple-player decisions resolve clockwise from the revealing player."
This is the formal rules-spec anchor for ORDER: when an event affects
several players' own individual resolutions, each affected player resolves
their own piece of it in turn, clockwise from whoever revealed the card --
i.e. an ORDER of individual decisions, not a single shared formula applied
identically to everyone.

RULES_SPEC.md does not carry a line specifically glossing the "and/or"
gain/lose wording as a per-player CONTENT choice (it is silent on that
point) -- so the CONTENT half of the finding rests on the primary evidence
already gathered by a prior pass (docs/REPLAY.md, ~line 5005-5157), which I
verified by reading rather than re-deriving from a fresh log:
  Game 7522886 (BGO human journal), Raiders event: "Each of the two weakest
  civilizations lose a total of 2 resources and/or food.; Green and Orange
  each lose 2 resources and/or food; Green choses first" -- then the SAME
  journal logs each player's OWN, DIFFERENT resolution: "Green spends 1
  food; Green spends 1 resource" (a 1/1 split) vs "Orange spends 2 food"
  (all-food, zero resources) for an IDENTICAL total-2 loss. The Foray gain
  two lines earlier shows the identical shape on the gain side: "Purple
  produces 3 resources" vs "Grey produces 3 food" for an identical
  gain-3 block. Cross-checked against a THIRD, independent number (round
  7's own running resource total, matching Orange's real round-7 spend
  lines) in docs/REPLAY.md -- not just the two conflicting clause texts.
  The literal journal word is "choses" (chooses).

Cross-reference, BGA's own card text (sources/bga_throughtheages_material.
inc.php, the only BGA implementation artifact present in sources/ -- it is
card-text/material data, not executable game logic; no BGA server-side
event-resolution code is present in this tree to cross-check against
directly):
  line 990, Foray: "Each of the two strongest civilizations gains a total
  of 3 food and/or resources."
  line 1561, Raiders: "The 2 weakest civilizations each lose a total of 2
  resources/food"
Neither BGA card-text string says "(aggressor choice)" or names any actor
-- contrast with Plunder's OWN BGA text at the same file, lines 1511-1512:
"Loses a total of up to 3 resources and/or food (aggressor choice) ...
Gain that many resources and food", which explicitly names the choice-maker
(the attacker). Foray/Raiders' silence on WHO chooses is exactly why BGO's
own journal text ("<Color> choses first") is the load-bearing evidence, not
the card text alone -- the card text only establishes the TOTAL, and the
journal is what proves the split is player-directed. This is consistent
with, not contradicted by, RULES_SPEC's §5.3 ordering rule above.

CONCLUSION on the rule itself: the choice has TWO parts, both genuine:
  - ORDER: who resolves first is fixed by rule (clockwise from the
    revealing player, RULES_SPEC §5.3, confirmed by "Green choses first"
    naming a specific seat first).
  - CONTENT: each affected player independently picks their OWN
    food/resources split summing to the block's stated total (MEASURED
    from the journal's differing real splits on identical totals, prior
    pass's finding, verified by me by reading, not re-derived).
This is the SAME shape as Plunder's already-correctly-implemented
`ChoiceKind::PlunderSplit` (interact::offer_plunder_split), except
self-directed (each targeted player chooses for themselves) rather than
attacker-directed (one player chooses for a victim).

`events::food_or_resources`, rust/src/events.rs:216-226:

    pub(crate) fn food_or_resources(p: &mut PlayerState, amount: i32, sign: i32) {
        let amount = amount.max(0) as u16;
        if sign > 0 {
            let got = economy::gain_resources(p, amount);
            economy::gain_food(p, amount - got);
        } else {
            let take = p.resources.min(amount);
            p.resources -= take;
            p.food = p.food.saturating_sub(amount - take);
        }
    }

4. ADVANTAGED, DISADVANTAGED, OR NEUTRAL? (mostly HYPOTHESISED --
   flagged honestly where this cannot be settled without more work)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

HYPOTHESISED, reasoned from the code, not measured by playing it out:

- Within a single self-play game, the SAME fixed formula applies
  identically to whichever seat is currently strongest/weakest -- it does
  not hardcode a specific SEAT's advantage. Since self-play typically pits
  the same bot/weight-vector against itself (or symmetric opponents,
  selfplay.rs's own doc comment: seats rotate specifically to cancel
  seating bias), there is no reason to expect this bug to systematically
  favor "seat 0" over "seat 1" etc. across a training run. In that narrow
  sense it looks NEUTRAL between self-play participants.
- But it is very unlikely to be neutral in a DEEPER sense: `pending_moves`
  (section 2) shows the engine's other choice kinds (PlunderSplit etc.) are
  genuinely decided by the bot's own weighted evaluation, not a hardcoded
  default -- so wherever a REAL Pending::Choice exists, the trained weights
  already encode a learned, situational policy for it. `food_or_resources`
  gives the bot NO such opportunity: whatever `resources_first` computes is
  forced, regardless of whether that split is good or bad for THAT player's
  actual board state (e.g. a player who is food-starved and resource-rich
  would rationally choose to protect food, but the "loss" formula always
  drains resources first -- correct only for that direction; a player who
  desperately needs resources for an imminent Build, as the concrete
  example on game 7522886 shows a REAL human doing (Orange chose "spends 2
  food", the opposite of the engine's guess, precisely to protect
  resources for an upcoming build), is not reachable at all).
- Net effect on the TRAINED BOT specifically: the bot has never had the
  opportunity to learn this decision (it is not in its action space at
  all, unlike PlunderSplit), so its weights encode zero preference or
  skill here. Whether the engine's fixed default happens to be good or bad
  strategy on average is a separate, unmeasured question. If it is
  suboptimal on average, self-play games are systematically slightly worse
  than "true" Through the Ages for BOTH sides equally (symmetric handicap,
  doesn't change relative win rates between two self-play bots of equal
  skill) -- but it DOES mean any single-player metric derived from
  self-play (win probability against a hypothetical outside opponent who
  DOES get to choose optimally) is contaminated, because a real opponent
  would extract value here that the bot's training never modeled and the
  bot itself never learned to extract when it's on the beneficial side of
  a future real matchup.
- HONEST LIMIT: whether the fixed "resources first" default is closer to
  optimal play or clearly worse cannot be determined from this pass's
  work -- it would need either (a) an actual `Pending::Choice`
  implementation played out against the current weighted evaluation to see
  whether the eval function would ever choose differently, or (b) a
  hand-analysis of when "protect resources" vs "protect food" is
  correct given the rest of the ruleset (yellow-bank tiers, upcoming
  build/develop/pop costs) -- neither was attempted this pass, per the
  task's read-only/no-new-engine-feature scope.

MEASURED fact bearing on urgency: because the SAME function
(`food_or_resources`) is shared between self-play (training) and replay
(evaluation against real human logs), fixing it changes BOTH -- there is
no way to "fix it for replay only" the way the prior pass did with the
journal-side patch; a real engine fix (section 5) necessarily touches
self-play behavior too.

5. SKETCH OF A CORRECT FIX (NOT IMPLEMENTED -- per this task's scope)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

This mirrors, and largely just re-confirms, the sketch already written by
the prior pass (docs/REPLAY.md, ~lines 5114-5151), which I read and checked
against the current code rather than re-deriving from scratch:

  1. New `ChoiceKind` (or a `PlunderSplit`-shaped variant with an optional/
     self `victim`), opened by `resolve_count_targets`'s
     `food_and_or_resources` arm (events.rs ~1004-1040) instead of calling
     `food_or_resources` directly -- one `Pending::Choice` per targeted
     player, opened in `targets` order (matching "<Color> choses first"),
     offering every valid split of the block's total between food and
     resources (gain side: capped by blue-token availability, mirroring
     `economy::gain_resources`/`gain_food`'s existing caps; loss side:
     capped at the player's current holdings, floored at zero -- both
     already computed inside today's `food_or_resources`, just needs
     enumerating into options instead of picked deterministically).
  2. Reuse `interact::offer_plunder_split`'s existing split-enumeration
     shape (`ChoiceOption::Gain`) rather than writing a second one --
     structurally the same problem, self-directed instead of
     attacker-directed.
  3. A self-play default-answering policy for when the weighted eval
     doesn't have anything specific tuned for this new choice kind --
     `pending_moves` (legal.rs:222-223) already routes any `Pending::
     Choice` into ordinary scored legal moves, so this does NOT need a
     hardcoded default the way a naive implementation might assume: once
     the choice exists, the SAME weighted evaluation that already picks
     among PlunderSplit options would pick among these too. The "keep
     'resources first' as a safe default" language in the prior pass's
     sketch would only be needed if the choice were deliberately kept
     OUTSIDE the normal move-scoring path -- worth flagging as a design
     decision the next implementer must make explicitly, not inherit
     silently.
  4. `replay_common.rs`'s own `resolve_intervening`/`PrepareEvent` handling
     (already reading the journal's real split via
     `parse_produces_grant_line`/`prescan_produces_grants` per the prior
     pass's fix) would need to be re-pointed at draining the NEW
     `Pending::Choice` instead of post-hoc correcting `p.food`/
     `p.resources` after the deterministic call -- the replayer's
     bookkeeping and the engine's own choice-resolution would then agree
     structurally, not just numerically.

Files touched (sketch only, not attempted): `state.rs` (new `ChoiceKind`
variant), `events.rs` (`resolve_count_targets`/`apply_gains_block`, remove
the direct `food_or_resources` call for this one arm), `interact.rs` (new
choice-opening function alongside `offer_plunder_split`), `apply.rs`
(resolving the new choice kind once answered), `legal.rs`/`interact.rs`'s
`pending_moves` (enumerate the new option kind -- likely falls out of the
existing generic `Pending::Choice` handling for free, per point 3), and
`replay_common.rs` (repoint the journal-side correction at draining the
real choice instead of post-hoc patching).

WHAT COULD BREAK / INVALIDATION RISK:
- If the new choice is wired into the normal scored-legal-move path (as
  point 3 argues it naturally would be), self-play GAMEPLAY changes going
  forward: the weighted bot would start actually deciding this split based
  on board state instead of always doing "resources first" -- this is a
  strictly MORE faithful game, but it means newly generated self-play data
  is not directly comparable/mixable with anything generated before the
  fix (the two corpora reflect different rule sets at this one decision
  point). Existing trained weight vectors (`champion_2p_now.json` and
  siblings in this tree) were fit under a world where this decision does
  not exist for the bot to make -- they are not automatically WRONG (the
  decision point they were trained under is a strict subset of the real
  game), but they have zero learned skill at the new decision and would
  need further training exposure to it before being trusted at full
  strength. This does NOT retroactively invalidate past hill-climb
  generations' RELATIVE rankings (both/all competitors in any given climb
  round were equally blind to this decision), but it does mean absolute
  win-rate/skill estimates against anything OUTSIDE that closed self-play
  population (e.g. against real humans, or future bots that do model this
  choice) were measured under a simplified ruleset.
- The replayer-side fix (deea9a0/5154e08) would become partially redundant
  once the engine change lands -- risk of the two corrections fighting
  each other (the journal-side FIFO correction and a real engine
  `Pending::Choice` both trying to apply the same journal line) if not
  carefully sequenced; the prior pass's own "food-text-vs-stock-delta trap"
  section (docs/REPLAY.md ~5397+) is the relevant cautionary precedent for
  this exact family of double-correction bug.
- Any cached/serialized game-tree or training data that encodes
  `food_or_resources`'s deterministic outcome as ground truth (not checked
  this pass what, if anything, in this tree does that beyond the live
  champion JSON weight files already named above) would need
  re-validation; I did not find such a cache in this pass and did not go
  looking beyond the champion weight files already named, per scope.
