CANONICAL OPENINGS: HUMAN VS BOT (measurement pass only)
==========================================================
Scope: 2-player base-game (2015) BGO corpus only, 692 games / 1384 player-
games (the sub-corpus with a champion self-play comparison point). The
holdout at /private/tmp/bgoholdout/ was never read.

Definition of "the opening" used throughout: each player's own moves from
the start of the game through the end of round 3 (Age A plus the first
couple of Age I rounds, per game_state.round <= 3), described the way a
human would: which card they took and at what civil-action cost, what they
built/developed/upgraded first and what kind of thing it was, whether they
elected a leader, whether they increased population, and how many civil
actions they left unspent.

4. THE WHY -- top 3 divergences traced to code
------------------------------------------------------------------------

### #1: Humans build a Mine first; the bot builds a military unit first
(94.9pp gap on the same field -- the single biggest, cleanest divergence)

Mechanism, traced: a military unit card's printed strength is tagged as a
`WeightKey::Strength` yield of `YieldKind::Unit`
(`rust/src/bots/weighted/cards.rs:242-244`, `card_yields`). That yield is
priced through `feature_marginal` (`rust/src/bots/weighted/rivals.rs:849-859`),
which special-cases `WeightKey::Strength` to call `strength_marginal`
(`rivals.rs:737-748`). `strength_marginal` sums FOUR weights from the frozen
vector: `strength` (-7.152), `strength_rel` (+17.231), plus a phase blend of
`strength_rel_early` (-0.195) and `strength_rel_late` (+5.606) weighted by
lateness (`rivals.rs:745-747`; weight values confirmed directly from
`/private/tmp/rowdig/frozen_champion_2p.json`; defaults at
`rust/src/bots/weighted/weights.rs:513,648,649`). Even at lateness≈0 (the
literal opening), this nets to roughly +9.9 -- a strongly POSITIVE marginal
value on any move that raises the player's own strength, in the opening,
before any war is remotely relevant. A cheap early Warriors-to-Swordsmen
upgrade (once Swordsmen is developed -- and Swordsmen is already the bot's
single most common first-develop tech at 41.8%, from the pre-existing
`first_develop_card` census) scores this large positive delta essentially
for free, every game, while Farm/Mine production yields are priced through
the ordinary flat per-worker weights (`FoodRate`/`ResourceRate`,
`cards.rs:232-233`) with no comparable early-game amplification. Confidence:
high on the pricing-channel mechanism (every citation above is a direct
read, not inference); medium on "this is THE reason it wins the comparison
against Mine specifically" -- did not diff the two candidate moves' raw
scores side-by-side in a real position, which would close the loop
completely (out of this measurement pass's scope; flagged for the
force-play half).

### #2: Humans elect a leader by round 3 83.9% of the time; the bot does it
46.2% of the time (and takes one FIRST only 7.75% vs. humans' 26.9%)

Mechanism, traced: `card_potential_core` (`rust/src/bots/weighted/cards.rs:1733`)
gives FIVE card kinds a dedicated, always-live per-type board-pricing gate
before falling through to a generic path: unit techs (`UnitTechCredit`,
:1745), levelled techs (`TechBoardCredit`, :1749), governments
(`GovBoardCredit`, :1752), actions (`ActionBoardCredit`, :1757), and --
notably -- WONDERS get their own dedicated gate too (`WonderBoardCredit`,
:1778, confirmed nonzero at 0.1123 in the frozen vector). Leaders get NO
such dedicated gate: `CardType::Leader` is not one of the `kind ==` branches
in that function at all. A leader instead falls through to the generic
fallback (`cards.rs:1808-1815`): `credit_board = card_board_credit (0.0) +
card_board_leader`. `card_board_leader` is the field poisoned by the
"leadersign" bug (raw value -15.003, confirmed in the frozen JSON); the
newly-landed `dominance_repair` gate (`eval.rs:934`, applied at load time,
`eval.rs:1083`) neutralizes it to exactly `-card_board_credit = 0.0`
(confirmed via the repair's own unit test, `eval.rs:1683-1732`, which states
explicitly that a repaired leader lands at "0.0 (unpriced), not because the
fix under-corrected" -- the repair's job was only to stop the SIGN
INVERSION, not to restore genuine value). With `credit_board == 0.0` AND
`card_board_credit == 0.0`, `card_potential_core` takes its
static-per-card-table early return (`cards.rs:1811-1815`), which the same
test comment confirms carries "no printed static price" for a leader. Net
effect: the fix that already landed in this clone stopped leaders from
pricing NEGATIVE (the acute form of the bug), but leaders still price at
essentially ZERO board value through this channel, structurally unlike
wonders, governments, actions and techs, which each kept (or already had) a
genuine dedicated positive gate. That is exactly consistent with what this
pass measures: bot leader uptake is way down from human, but not literally
zero -- residual value leaks in only through row-scarcity
(`row_last_copy`), the same channel every other card gets, with no
leader-specific credit on top. Confidence: high -- every step is a direct
code read plus a confirmed value from the actual frozen file used in this
run's bot self-play.

### #3: The bot takes a Wonder first 72.75% of the time vs. humans' 32.9%
(the "vice versa" case -- bot over-plays what humans do more moderately)

Mechanism, traced: this is the mirror image of #2's missing gate -- wonders
DO have the dedicated gate (`WonderBoardCredit`, `cards.rs:1778`, value
0.1123, nonzero and positive in the frozen vector), which fires BEFORE the
generic fallback and prices a row wonder's genuine long-run board benefit
via `board_yields::board_yields`/`sum_board_triples` (`cards.rs:1779-1780`).
This is the composite, already-weight-priced `card_potential` the
project's own facts note is then MULTIPLIED AGAIN by `row_last_copy`'s
row-scarcity term (`rust/src/bots/weighted/row.rs:459-494`, `total += val *
gone` at :491) -- so a wonder's card_potential, already boosted by its own
dedicated gate, gets a second multiplicative boost from sitting in a
scarce/about-to-disappear row slot, which a first-round Age A row often is
(only 6-7 slots, cleared fast in 2p). Humans do value wonders in the opening
(32.9%, and it's a roughly-average-win-rate convention, not a losing one:
49.8%) -- the gap here isn't "bot values something worthless," it's that the
SAME genuinely-positive signal is applied through two multiplicative,
uncapped channels for wonders specifically (dedicated gate x row-scarcity),
while leaders (finding #2) get neither. Confidence: high on the mechanism
existing; medium on the exact multiplicative-double-count framing, since
this reuses the project's own already-established `row_last_copy`
dominance finding (from the task brief) rather than re-deriving the
"double-counting" claim from scratch in this pass.
