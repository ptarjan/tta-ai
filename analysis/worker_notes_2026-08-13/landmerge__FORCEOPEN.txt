0. HEADLINE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
YES -- forcing the mined human openings onto the champion wins it more
games, for all three arms tested, and the win is NOT explained by a
biased harness (the mandatory control landed exactly on the null):

  arm                    n     win rate    95% CI          p (vs 50%)
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------
  MilitaryFirst (control) 1000  50.1%       [49.8, 50.4]    0.56  (null, as required)
  MineFirst               3000  55.2%       [53.5, 56.9]    <0.0001  SIGNIFICANT
  LeaderByRoundThree      3000  52.0%       [50.3, 53.7]    0.019    SIGNIFICANT
  MineFirstAndLeader      3000  53.7%       [52.0, 55.4]    <0.0001  SIGNIFICANT

Detection limit: at n=3000 paired games the half-width is ~1.7pp (tighter
than the ~5-6pp the brief estimates for n=300, because these runs are 10x
larger -- see §3 for why that extra scale was worth spending). All three
real-arm point estimates clear their own interval; none of this is a
sub-detection-limit 2-3pp read being over-interpreted.


1. THE MECHANISM (built once, reused across all four arms)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
New module `rust/src/opening_force.rs` (`OpeningPolicy`, `OpeningTracker`,
`pick_with_optional_force`), plus one small surgical seam in the existing
engine: `rust/src/bots/greedy.rs`'s `Bot::pick` was split into `Bot::pick`
(unchanged) and a new `Bot::pick_from(&mut self, state, moves: &[Move])`
that runs the SAME per-kind dispatch over a caller-supplied move list
instead of generating one internally.

`OpeningTracker::restrict` narrows the REAL legal-move list down to
whichever human-opening category is under test -- ALWAYS a subset of the
real legal moves, so this can never manufacture an illegal move -- and
hands that subset to `pick_from`, which lets the champion's own
UNMODIFIED evaluator/search pick among the narrowed candidates exactly as
it always picks among the full list. `bots::weighted::eval` itself is
never touched. If nothing in the narrowed subset is legal this decision,
`restrict` returns the list COMPLETELY UNCHANGED and records a
fallthrough (see below for exactly when that counts).

Policies (`OpeningPolicy`, exhaustively matched, no wildcard on the enum):
  - Unforced             -- the control seat's own unconstrained choice.
  - MineFirst            -- force first build/develop/upgrade to be
                             Mine-kind (in practice this is Bronze, the
                             only Mine-kind card affordable this early).
  - LeaderByRoundThree   -- force a leader taken-then-played by round 3:
                             a two-step pursuit (Take a Leader-kind row
                             card if none in hand yet, else PlayLeader the
                             moment it's legal -- `PlayLeader` is checked
                             FIRST so a seat holding a leader plays it
                             rather than hoarding a second one).
  - MineFirstAndLeader   -- both goals pursued independently; when a
                             single decision offers legal moves toward
                             BOTH at once, their forced candidate sets are
                             UNIONED, never intersected (two disjoint move
                             families intersect to nothing, which would
                             silently un-force everything).
  - MilitaryFirst        -- force first build to be a military unit: the
                             bot's OWN current habit (97.8% unforced, per
                             OPENINGS.txt), made explicit as the mandatory
                             control.

Categories match /private/tmp/openings/rust/src/bin/humanopenings.rs's
`build_kind` exactly (Mine -> CardType::Mine; Military -> Infantry/
Cavalry/Artillery/Air; `Upgrade{to,..}` keys on `to`, never `from`,
matching that file's own extraction) and its `took_leader` field (driven
by `Move::PlayLeader`).

Fallthrough semantics, precisely (per the brief: "if the preferred move is
not legal or not affordable this turn, fall through ... and record that
it fell through"): at each decision in the forced window (round <= 3,
goal unmet), if the legal list has NOTHING in the goal's move family at
all (e.g. every legal move is Take/Pop/EndTurn for a build goal), that is
NOT a fallthrough -- it's simply not this goal's moment, no counter
moves, list passes through untouched. If the decision DOES offer moves in
the goal's family but NONE hits the target category, THAT is a
fallthrough: the counter increments and the entire original legal list is
handed back, unrestricted, for this one decision. This keeps the
fallthrough rate a small, meaningful signal rather than being swamped by
the majority of a turn's decisions that were never relevant to the goal.

"Achieved" caveat, stated precisely (so the number isn't over-read): it
means "a move in the target category was actually played by round <= 3",
via `OpeningTracker::observe` (which is hard-gated to `state.round <= 3`
-- confirmed by a dedicated regression test, see next section, after a
real bug where an unforced late-game build was silently counting). It is
NOT strictly "this was the literal first build of the game" -- if an
earlier fallthrough let a different build happen first and the target was
only reached on a later round<=3 decision, "achieved" is still true. For
every arm actually forcing that goal this pass, the fallthrough rate was
low (0-7.4%, see §2), so in practice "achieved" and "was first" coincide
almost always; this is flagged for precision, not because it changed a
conclusion.

Once a goal is satisfied, `OpeningTracker::opening_complete()`
short-circuits `restrict` to a pure pass-through for the rest of the
game -- forcing is a one-shot nudge at the start of the opening, never a
standing constraint.

Root cause: `legal::action_moves` has a hard §1.9 rule -- round 1 offers
ONLY Take/EndTurn, no Build/Develop/Upgrade/PlayLeader is ever legal there
-- so these fixtures needed `state.round = 2`, and the leader-take
assertion needed relaxing from exact-equality (the deal at that seed
happened to also drop a second leader at slot 1). Fixed; re-run green
(12/12, then 13/13 once the round-3 `observe` gate regression test below
was added).

A second real bug was caught the same way, AFTER the first 12 were green:
a smoke run showed `leader achieved 60%`/`military achieved 100%` under
the MineFirst arm even though neither goal was being forced -- `observe`
was being called for a seat's WHOLE game (every round, not just the
forced window), so a mine/leader/military that happened to show up in
round 6 of ordinary play was silently counting as "the human opening was
achieved". Added `observe_ignores_a_mine_build_that_happens_after_round_3`
(confirmed it would have caught the bug: without the `state.round > 3`
guard added to `OpeningTracker::observe`, `achieved_mine()` comes back
`true` for a round-4 build) and gated `observe` on `state.round <= 3`.
Re-run: 13/13 green. Final smoke check after the fix (n=20,
`--policy mine-first`) showed sane, non-inflated numbers: mine achieved
100/20 (forced, as expected), leader 12/20, military 13/20 -- consistent
with ordinary round<=3 bot behaviour, not "eventually true for every long
game".

`bin/forceopen.rs`'s own 5 tests cover argument parsing (`--policy`
required, `--games` rounds to whole 2p deals, every documented policy
name round-trips through `parse_policy`) and the `GoalTally::
fallthrough_rate` zero-division guard.

Full suite after landing: `cargo test --profile difftest` -- 1498 passed,
0 failed, across every binary in the clone (not just the new files).


3. THE RUNS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
All four use `--seed 0 --threads 2`, the same champion vector both seats,
2p only (the corpus's largest bucket and the only one with a champion
self-play comparison point, per OPENINGS.txt). `--threads 2` throughput
was ~23-29 games/s -- 1000 games took ~42s, 3000 took ~105-120s, so
scaling past the brief's 300-game floor to 3000 (10x) for MineFirst,
LeaderByRoundThree and MineFirstAndLeader cost about 6 minutes total wall
time and meaningfully sharpened the read (half-width ~1.7pp instead of
~5-6pp) -- worth it since the first-pass n=1000 numbers were suggestive
but inconclusive (MineFirst n=1000: 52.4% +/- 2.8%, p=0.0955) and the
question the owner actually asked deserves a real answer, not a shrug.

### MineFirstAndLeader -- n=3000 (n=1000 first pass: 51.5% +/- 3.1%, p=0.3183)
    win rate     53.7%  +/- 1.7   (null 50.0%)   p = 0.0000
                 [naive +/- 1.8, rho -0.06, deff 0.95, 1500 deals]
    mine      achieved 100.0% (3000/3000)  forced 3034  fallthrough 0    rate 0.0%
    leader    achieved 100.0% (3000/3000)  forced 8966  fallthrough 480  rate 5.1%
    military  achieved  66.3% (1990/3000)  forced 0     fallthrough 0    rate 0.0%
    verdict: the forced seat wins MORE (interval clear above 50%)
Both sub-goals fully achieved (100%/100%), fallthrough low on both. The
combined effect (+3.7pp) sits BETWEEN LeaderByRoundThree alone (+2.0pp)
and MineFirst alone (+5.2pp) -- notably LESS than MineFirst alone, not
more. The two forced habits do not simply add. Read: forcing a leader
election on top of the mine-first line likely competes for the same early
civil actions/resources that would otherwise go toward compounding the
Mine's economic lead, so the combination trades away some of MineFirst's
own edge in exchange for the smaller, independent Leader edge. This is a
real, interesting interaction, not noise -- both CIs are tight (+/-1.7pp)
and clearly separated in direction (combined 53.7% sits comfortably below
MineFirst's 55.2% and its own lower CI bound of 53.5%... actually
overlaps at the boundary, so "combined is a bit lower than Mine alone" is
the honest, not over-claimed, read here -- not "combined is worse than
Mine alone with full confidence", since [52.0,55.4] and [53.5,56.9]
overlap. Directionally suggestive, not itself a proven ordering.)


4. INTERPRETATION -- which of the two stories is this?
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
The brief poses two possible readings for a forced-opening result:
(a) the evaluator misprices the early game and this is a concrete,
    landable target, or
(b) the human convention is just convention (or the champion's own later
    play is co-adapted to ITS OWN opening and gets stranded by a foreign
    one), so forcing it shouldn't be expected to help.

This pass's result is (a), for all three arms, and it lines up exactly
with the mechanisms OPENINGS.txt already traced in the bot's own weight
vector -- this experiment did not need to invent a new explanation, it
confirmed the ones already on file actually cost real games:
  - MineFirst's effect (the largest, +5.2pp) matches OPENINGS.txt finding
    #1: `strength_marginal` prices ANY move that raises the player's own
    military strength positively even at lateness~=0 (the literal
    opening), while Farm/Mine production yields get no comparable
    amplification -- so the champion's default "build military first"
    habit was measurably leaving equity on the table, not merely
    differing from human convention by taste.
  - LeaderByRoundThree's effect (smaller but real, +2.0pp) matches finding
    #2: leaders get no dedicated `card_potential_core` pricing gate the
    way techs/governments/actions/wonders do, and land at ~0 board value
    through the generic fallback even after `dominance_repair` -- so the
    champion under-values leaders relative to their real worth, and
    forcing one in gains a little.
  - The combined arm being LESS than MineFirst alone (not more) is the
    one place this pass earns a genuine caveat rather than a clean
    confirmation: the two mispricings are not independent contributions
    to add up -- pursuing both spends some of the same early civil-action
    budget, so this is not "force everything human and win the most",
    it's "MineFirst is the strongest single lever measured here".

No claim is made here about human-like play being strong in general
(that is a separate, already-measured fact: the searchless human-
imitation bot wins only ~2% of its games -- imitation accuracy measures
the DATA, never playing strength). What this pass measured is narrower
and sharper: specifically forcing the champion's OWN evaluator to spend
its OWN search/choice on the mine-kind and leader-kind moves it already
undervalues wins more, because the mispricing was already sitting in the
weight vector, independently confirmed by direct code reads in the mining
pass and now confirmed IN PLAY here.


5. WHAT WAS NOT CHASED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
- The third mined divergence (bot over-takes Wonder first, 72.75% vs
  humans' 32.9%) was not run as a forced arm -- the brief's mandatory set
  is MineFirst, LeaderByRoundThree, MineFirstAndLeader plus the
  MilitaryFirst control; a WonderFirst/AvoidWonder arm was out of scope
  for this pass to stay inside budget, and is a natural next experiment
  given MineFirst's result here (the row-scarcity double-count mechanism
  OPENINGS.txt traced for Wonders is architecturally similar to the
  Mine/Military mispricing this pass just confirmed costs real games).
- 3p/4p and any non-2p corpus were not touched, matching the mining
  pass's own scope note (2p is the only bucket with a champion self-play
  comparison point).
- The MineFirstAndLeader-vs-MineFirst-alone ordering (§3's caveat) is
  flagged, not formally tested head-to-head with its own paired
  comparison -- both are independently significant against the 50% null,
  and their CIs overlap, so "combined is worse than Mine alone" is a
  directional read from this pass's numbers, not itself a p-valued claim.
