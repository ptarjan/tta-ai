# Forced-Population Arena, 2p: champion vs forced-population-build variant

**Date:** 2026-08-30
**Champion:** `experiments/rust_champion_2p.json`, gen 128800 (frozen to `/tmp/arenapop_champ_2p.json`)
**Games:** 2500, seeds 1..2500, 2 threads, 820.9s (3.0 games/s)
**Replication:** an independent n=500 run (seeds 1..500, same champion, same binary
lineage) landed on the same conclusions before an instrumentation bug in the FIRST
draft of this report's reading was caught and fixed -- see "WHAT WENT WRONG IN THE
FIRST DRAFT" below. Its numbers are quoted where they corroborate n=2500.
**Null:** for a DECIDED game (see (b)), 50% (symmetric seats, same weights -- the
only difference is the forcing)

## Method

`openerprobe --arena-pop`, built as the population mirror of the existing
`--arena-food` (`analysis/forced_food_arena_2026-08-29.txt`): two independent 2p
replays per seed, both seats playing the SAME weights.

- **Forced side:** seat 0 takes a legal population-increase move (`Move::Pop` or
  `Move::PopFree`) at every own-turn decision point in rounds 10-17 inclusive where
  one is legal; the bot's own top-ranked pick everywhere else (including rounds
  1-9 and 18+). Outside r10-17 the forced and champion replays are identical.
- **Champion side:** no seat forces; both seats play the bot's own picks throughout.
- Both sides track seat 0. A game is a WIN for the forced side if its final culture
  (== final score; `game::scores` reads `p.culture` directly after `finish_game`
  writes the end-game bonus into it, so there is no separate score field) strictly
  exceeds the champion side's; a TIE is exact equality; anything else is a loss.
- Pop-action counting reuses behavcensus's definition exactly: one increment per
  executed `Move::Pop`/`Move::PopFree`, whether or not it was forced -- a free pop
  counts as 1, never a CA debit.
- Implementation: `rust/src/bin/openerprobe.rs`, `is_pop_move`, `arena_play_pop`,
  `arena_game_pop`, `ArenaPop`, `print_forced_pop_arena`.

## (a) VALIDITY CHECK FIRST: did the forcing actually move population ACTIONS?

| | champion (unforced) | forced | reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| cumulative pop actions/player by r17 | 6.033 | 6.248 | unforced baseline 5.945, human 8.089 |

Forcing raised pop actions **6.033 -> 6.248 (+0.215), 14.1% of the way** from the
unforced baseline to the human rate. (n=500 replication: 6.040 -> 6.288, +0.248,
16.0% of the way -- consistent.) This is a real, correctly-signed, but WEAK effect,
much smaller than the food arena's food-stock move (which was 3.3x, +4.125
absolute). **PASS on validity, but read the win-rate result knowing the lever only
partially engaged** -- see (c) for why.

The champion side's own 6.033 also reproduces the independent 5.945 baseline from
`bot_pop_actions_per_round_2026-08-29.txt` (a different 200-game run, different
champion generation) to within run-to-run noise, which is an unplanned but useful
sanity check that the harness is measuring what it says it measures.

## (b) win rate -- and why the naive 50%-of-all-games comparison is the WRONG test here

Because the engine is deterministic and both replays share a seed, any game where
forcing never finds a legal pop anywhere in r10-17 produces a forced trajectory that
is **byte-for-byte identical** to the champion trajectory -- an automatic, exact-culture
TIE. Ties are not "close games", they are games the lever never touched.

| | count | % of 2500 |
|---|---|---|
| wins | 580 | 23.2% |
| ties | 1348 | 53.9% |
| losses | 572 | 22.9% |
| games with zero legal pop anywhere in r10-17 (forcing never fired) | 623 | 24.9% |

**53.9% of games are ties**, and the majority of those (623 of 1348) are the
zero-legal-pop case exactly as predicted; the remaining 725 are games where forcing
fired at least once but the trajectories re-converged to an exact culture tie by
game end (a real, if surprising, phenomenon this run's own numbers show rather than
assume away).

With over half the sample uninformative by construction, the raw win rate --
**580/2500 = 23.2%, Wilson 95% CI [21.6%, 24.9%]** -- answers a different question
than "does forcing help or hurt". Comparing it to a 50% null implicitly assumes
every game is decided, which most of these are not. The right null for "does
forcing help or hurt, GIVEN it fires" is wins == losses **among decided games only**:

**DECIDED-games win rate: 580/1152 = 50.3%, Wilson 95% CI [47.5%, 53.2%].**

**This interval CONTAINS 50%.** Once you condition on the lever actually having
fired, the forced side wins about as often as it loses. Mean margin when forced
wins (25.2 culture) and mean margin when forced loses (23.6 culture) are also
near-symmetric, which is what a null effect looks like on the margins too -- NOT
"wins big and loses small" (an earlier draft of this instrumentation read the
skew that direction; it was explaining the tie-fraction artefact, not a real
asymmetry, and that explanation has been removed from both this file and the
tool's own printed output).

## (c) THE MECHANISM: forcing pop roughly halves its own subsequent legality

This is the header result, not a footnote. Measured directly (not estimated):

| side | legal pop points | r10-17 own-turn decision points | legal fraction |
|---|---|---|---|
| champion (unforced) | 11,547 | 91,647 | **12.6%** |
| forced | 5,046 | 92,272 | **5.5%** |

On the UNFORCED side, pop is legal at only **12.6%** of the bot's own r10-17
decision points -- consistent with `pop_shortfall_decomposition_2026-08-29.txt`'s
finding that 87-92% of the r10-17 gap is "couldn't" (illegal), not "wouldn't"
(declined). **The r10-17 peel is primarily an affordability/civil-action-budget
failure, not a preference failure.**

Forcing a pop at every legal r10-17 opportunity **roughly halves the rate at which
the NEXT one is legal** (12.6% -> 5.5% for the forced seat, on its own already-forced
trajectory). Taking a pop spends the CA and food that would otherwise have bought
the next one, so 8 rounds of forcing buy far fewer additional actions than
8 x (the champion's own r10-17 rate) would suggest. **This compounding is why (a)'s
lift is only +0.215 out of a possible ~+2.1 gap to the human rate** -- the SAME
mechanism the food arena found (a food build costs a civil action that comes out of
population), mirrored: a pop action costs a civil action (and food) that comes out
of the SAME budget the next pop action needs.

### mean final culture / score

| side | mean culture |
|---|---|
| champion | 188.550 |
| forced | 189.000 |
| **forced − champion** | **+0.450 (~0.2%)** |

Consistent with (b): a small, near-zero difference is exactly what a decided-game
wash produces once ties (which contribute 0 either way) are folded into the mean
over 2500 games.

### end-of-round-12 and end-of-round-17 population (both sides)

| metric | champion | forced | forced − champion |
|---|---|---|---|
| pop total, r12 | 15.015 | 14.950 | **−0.064** |
| pop total, r17 | 13.575 | 13.529 | **−0.046** |

**This snapshot is NOT a proxy for pop actions taken, and its near-zero (slightly
negative) delta does not mean forcing failed to raise population** -- an earlier
reading of this exact number made that error and it is corrected here. The
snapshot is `yellow_bank + workers_free + yellow_granted`
(`economy::increase_population`): a normal `Move::Pop`/`Move::PopFree` moves ONE
token from `yellow_bank` to `workers_free` -- **the sum is invariant to it**. The
sum only rises when a pop is granted past an already-empty bank (the
`yellow_granted` path), and it **drops on its own by 2 at every age transition
after the first** (`game::advance_age`, Sec 12.2.4: "two unborn population are
removed from every supply"), independent of anything either side does. (a)'s
action COUNT is the correct instrument for "did forcing raise population
actions"; this snapshot answers a different question (total tokens ever granted)
and the small deltas here are noise from where each side's trajectory happens to
sit relative to an age boundary.

## VERDICT

**The decided-games CI [47.5%, 53.2%] contains 50%. This is a WASH, not a loss and
not a win.** Forcing population, when it engages, neither helps nor hurts the
forced side's final culture in a way this sample can distinguish from chance. That
result is attached to a validity check that only weakly passed (+0.215 of a
possible +2.1 actions, 14.1% of the human gap) and to a legality mechanism that
explains why: forcing pop roughly halves the rate at which the next pop is legal,
so an "always force" policy runs into the same affordability wall the unforced bot
already sits behind, just delayed by one action.

## WHAT WENT WRONG IN THE FIRST DRAFT (kept for the record, not archaeology)

The first pass at this arena read the raw all-games win rate (23.2%, CI entirely
below 50%) as "the forced side loses" -- exactly the food arena's headline shape --
without checking what fraction of games were ties. It also read the near-zero
`yellow_bank + workers_free + yellow_granted` delta as "forcing failed to raise
population" and a `+1.272` mean-culture gap at n=500 as a possible scoring bug. All
three were re-derived from the data itself before this file was written: the
win/tie/loss breakdown was added directly to the tool (`ArenaPop::ties`,
`::no_divergence_games`, `::win_margin_sum`, `::loss_margin_sum`, and a
decided-games Wilson CI in `print_forced_pop_arena`) so the correct headline prints
by default on any future run, not just this one; the population-total caveat is
printed inline; and the n=500-vs-n=2500 comparison itself resolved the mean-culture
question as noise (+1.272 -> +0.450, no bug).

## What this does and does not license

- This measures the value of **ALWAYS forcing** population in r10-17, not of
  **sometimes** forcing it -- the same caveat the food arena carried, and it is
  weaker evidence than "the food lever is closed": that closed on a decisive loss.
  This closes (if it closes anything) on a **wash inside a weak validity check**,
  which is a much softer signal. It does NOT prove population is a lever worth
  pulling with a smarter (conditional) forcing rule, nor does it prove the
  opposite -- a wash under blunt, unconditional forcing is uninformative about a
  targeted policy.
- Per the pre-registered protocol, **no milder forcing rule and no different round
  window were tried after seeing this result**, and none should be: the discipline
  that closed the food lever on a loss applies just as much to a wash -- shopping
  for a variant that wins is the failure mode this protocol exists to prevent,
  regardless of which side of 50% the first honest result lands on.
- The compounding legality mechanism in (c) is the one finding here that
  generalises past "always forcing": **the r10-17 shortfall is dominated by
  affordability (couldn't), not preference (wouldn't), and pop actions compete
  with EACH OTHER for the same civil-action/food budget, not just with other move
  types.** That is consistent with, and sharpens, `food_gates_population_2026-08-28.txt`
  and `pop_shortfall_decomposition_2026-08-29.txt`'s existing "couldn't dominates"
  finding: population is not just gated by food, it is gated by its OWN prior
  purchases.
- Read together with the food arena: forcing EITHER food or population into the
  r3-9/r10-17 windows fails to help (food loses outright; population washes). The
  live hypothesis both tests were built to attack -- that the bot's civil-action
  budget, not any single line item on it, is the binding constraint -- has now
  survived two independent tests rather than one. Neither food nor population is a
  free lever; the budget itself is what would need to move.

## Reproduction

```
cp experiments/rust_champion_2p.json /tmp/arenapop_champ_2p.json   # gen 128800
cd rust && cargo build --release --bin openerprobe
./target/release/openerprobe --players 2 --games 2500 --seed 1 --threads 2 \
    --arena-pop --weights /tmp/arenapop_champ_2p.json
```

Deterministic given the frozen champion; 2 threads chosen over the requested ~5 to
avoid resource contention with the 3 live climb processes on this machine (a first
attempt at 5 threads aborted with no output, consistent with memory pressure --
swap was 83% full and load average 8-12 on 6 cores at the time).
