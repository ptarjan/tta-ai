# Military strength by round, bot vs human

## Scope and method

Follow-up to the population curve report
(`analysis/population_curve_bot_vs_human_2026-08-28.txt`), using the SAME
method and round convention. That report showed the bot's population peels
away from the human's at ~round 12; this one measures the same separation on
the military side.

Two sides, same quantities per round, 2p, whole game:

- HUMAN side: the 632 completed 2p corpus games (the 2p subset of
  `analysis/guard_ids_882.txt`), replayed to completion — all 632 complete,
  0 stopped.
- BOT side: `openerprobe` self-play, 2p, 256 games seeds 1..256, frozen
  champion `experiments/rust_champion_2p_frozen_2026-08-28.json` (gen
  121100).

Per round, per side, per player, three quantities:

- **total military strength** = `effects::state_stats(state, p).strength`.
  This is the full military strength the engine reports: base unit strength
  (unit base value x workers on it) PLUS tactics, `army_strength` cards,
  military pacts, colonies and leaders. It is the same quantity the bot
  prices in its `strength` weight and the quantity `rival_strength` reads
  out — deliberately NOT just the sum of base unit values.
- **units in play** = number of military unit cards on the tableau with at
  least one worker on them (`interact::unit_pool` semantics).
- **military workers** = workers on those units (the staffing).

Round convention (identical on both sides): "end of round R" = the player
state immediately after round R's last move. Human side: the journal's first
line tagged round R+1 (the pre-move state at R+1, i.e. after round R's last
action has been applied); a game's final round falls back to its last
decision's pre-move state, i.e. it is read one move before the true end of
the final round — one move of staleness only on the last round of the few
games that reach it, documented here. Bot side: the snapshot taken the
moment `state.round` advances past R.

## Ended games: dropped or carried forward?

**DROPPED.** A round-R row includes a player only if the player was still
alive at the end of round R; the n per round is printed on BOTH sides and is
NOT flat. Evidence, per side:

- HUMAN n per round (player-rounds): 1264 for rounds 1-17 (632 games x 2),
  then 1162 / 542 / 92 / 4 for rounds 18 / 19 / 20 / 21.
- BOT n per round: 512 (256 x 2) for rounds 1-18, then 484 / 140 for rounds
  19 / 20.

The drop-off is the game-length distribution, not carry-forward: no 2p game
on either side ends before round 18 (human) / 19 (bot), and the n then
decays smoothly as games end. If ended games had been carried forward at
their final values, n would be flat at 1264/512 through the last round. It
is not, so the late rows are short real samples, not padded ones. The r1-17
human / r1-18 bot rows are at full n on both sides and are unaffected by
early endings.

## Spot check (recomputed from the printed columns)

Three rows recomputed from the table's own printed values (Δ = human − bot):

- r8: 5.50 − 3.89 = +1.61 (table: +1.61) ✓; units 1.65 − 1.04 = +0.61 ✓;
  workers 3.34 − 1.65 = +1.69 ✓.
- r13: 13.67 − 4.88 = +8.79 (table: +8.79) ✓; units 2.61 − 1.35 = +1.26 ✓;
  workers 6.80 − 2.40 = +4.40 ✓.
- r19: 29.06 − 7.56 = +21.50 (table: +21.50) ✓; units 3.73 − 1.84 = +1.89 ✓;
  workers 13.49 − 3.54 = +9.95 ✓.

The r13 bot dip (total 5.65 at r12 to 4.88 at r13, at full n 1264/512) is
the only visually anomalous row. It was checked for row misalignment BEFORE
any explanation: both sides read at the same round index (same convention,
verified per-game below), the n is full on both sides, and the dip is real
in the per-game data — individual bot games (e.g. seed 5, seat 0: 7 → 6 → 4
→ 7 across rounds 11-14) dip and recover within one or two rounds. The
mechanism is mid-game strength churn: tactics cards expiring and war losses
hitting a strength pool that is still small (total ~5-6), so a −1 card or a
−2 war loss moves the average visibly. The unit and worker columns rise
monotonically through r13 (1.30 → 1.35 units, 2.32 → 2.40 workers), which is
inconsistent with an alignment error (a shifted row would show a jump in ALL
three columns) and consistent with the modifiers layer on a small base.

## Where the curves separate

Essentially identical through rounds 1-4 (gap within ±0.01). The gap first
crosses +1.0 at **round 7** (+1.05) and grows monotonically from round 5
without a single dip: +3.11 at r10, +5.84 at r12, +8.79 at r13, +16.14 at
r16, peaking at +22.90 at round 20 (human 31.0 vs bot 8.1 total strength)
before the r20 sample shrinks to 92/140 player-rounds. Unlike the population
gap, which crossed +1.0 at r10 and sustained from r12, the military gap is
established earlier and steeper: by r10 it is already +3.1.

## What the gap is made of

The units and workers columns split the gap cleanly:

- **Units:** the bot lags by ~+0.3 at r6, ~+0.8 at r10, ~+1.1 at r12,
  holding ~+1.8-1.9 from r15 on. The bot simply buys fewer military units.
  Human ~3.7 units by the late game vs bot ~1.9.
- **Workers on units:** the bigger component. The gap is +0.95 at r6,
  +2.45 at r10, +3.65 at r12, +7.54 at r16, +11.27 at r20 — human staffs
  ~15 military workers by r20 vs bot ~3.75. Even the units the bot does buy
  it keeps understaffed (r20: ~1.9 units with 3.75 workers ≈ 2.0 workers per
  unit; human: ~3.7 units with 15 workers ≈ 4.1 per unit).

So the military deficit is the same behavior as the population report, on a
different axis: the bot does not put workers on its military cards, and it
buys fewer of them. The total-strength gap (+22.9 at r20) splits as
base-strength (units x workers only) human ~15 vs bot ~4 (gap +11.3) plus
modifiers (tactics, army-strength cards, military pacts, colonies,
leaders) human ~16 vs bot ~4.4 (gap +11.7): the modifier stack contributes
about HALF of the late-game gap. The human builds the full modifier stack
on top of a much larger staffed base; the bot skips most of it, and what
it does buy it keeps understaffed.

Note the interaction with the population report: the bot's low staffing is
consistent with its low population (the bot only has ~8-10 workers on the
board vs the human's ~14-16). The military curve is the same food/
population-dispricing defect projected onto unit staffing.

## Caveats

- 2p only, on both sides (corpus 3p/4p excluded; bot is 2p self-play).
- Human side: 632 completed 2p games, 0 stopped. Bot: 256 self-play games at
  frozen champion gen 121100; sample sizes differ (1264 vs 512
  player-rounds at full n).
- "Total strength" is the engine's full `state_stats().strength` (units x
  workers + tactics + pacts + colonies + leaders), the quantity the bot
  itself prices. It is NOT the sum of base unit values; that is what makes
  the late-game gap (+22.9 at r20) about twice the base-strength gap
  (+11.3 at r20).
- Round convention matches the population report exactly (end of round R =
  after round R's last move; human = first decision tagged R+1, bot =
  snapshot at round advance). One documented edge: a human game's FINAL
  round is read at its last recorded decision's pre-move state, one move
  before the true end of that round; only a few games reach that, and it
  affects only their final row.
- Ended games are DROPPED, not carried forward; the non-flat n on both
  sides (1264→1162→542→92→4 human; 512→484→140 bot) is the proof and is
  printed in the table.
- The r21 row (n=4/0) is two human games and has no bot counterpart; ignore
  it.
- Diagnosis only: no weight change, no bots/ edits, no new .rs. Temporary
  instrumentation (env-gated `MILCURVE_DEBUG` hooks in
  `rust/src/replay_common.rs` and `rust/src/bin/openerprobe.rs`, and a
  `--military-curve` mode in `rust/src/bin/replaystats.rs`) was reverted
  after the runs; `cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D warnings` passes clean
  on the reverted tree, and `git status --short` is empty.
