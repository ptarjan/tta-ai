Pop shortfall decomposition, rounds 10-17: couldn't vs wouldn't
================================================================
2026-08-29. 2p, `openerprobe --games 256 --players 2 --threads 4 --whole`,
frozen copy of `experiments/rust_champion_2p.json` at **gen 125344**
(copied to /tmp/popshort_champ_2p.json before the run; the live file is
rewritten by the climb every few minutes).

THE QUESTION
============
The bot takes ~2.1 fewer population actions per player than a human by r17,
all of it after r9. Two existing findings bound the shortfall from opposite
sides, neither sized against the other per round:

  - `food_gates_population_2026-08-28.txt`: food is the binding reason at
    >=39.7% of pop-ILLEGAL points, rising to 44.5% at r16+.
  - `pop_taken_when_legal_2026-08-28.txt`: when Pop IS legal the bot takes
    it only 23.1% of the time, lowest at r6-10 (~16.8%).

This report decomposes the r10-17 shortfall per round into:
  (a) own-turn decision points
  (b) fraction where Pop was ILLEGAL, split by binding reason
  (c) fraction where Pop was LEGAL, and of those the fraction CHOSEN
  (d) P(legal) x P(chosen|legal) -- the implied expected pop actions per
      decision point

THE RESIDUAL CHECK (the (d) vs measured comparison)
===================================================
The measured per-round actions/player-round from
`bot_pop_actions_per_round_2026-08-29.txt` (gen 125336, 200 games) are
transcribed into the print below. The two instruments use DIFFERENT
denominators:

  - openerprobe --whole: n = own-turn DECISION POINTS (a player can face
    several per round -- each civil action re-opens the choice), so n is
    larger than player-rounds.
  - behavcensus: n = player-ROUNDS (one per player per round).

The (d) column is P(legal) x P(chosen|legal), the expected pop actions per
DECISION POINT. The measured column is actions per PLAYER-ROUND. These are
NOT the same quantity, and they do NOT match:

  round | (d) implied | measured | ratio measured/(d)
  10    | 0.043       | 0.233    | 5.4x
  11    | 0.055       | 0.193    | 3.5x
  12    | 0.053       | 0.185    | 3.5x
  13    | 0.044       | 0.263    | 6.0x
  14    | 0.028       | 0.188    | 6.7x
  15    | 0.046       | 0.207    | 4.5x
  16    | 0.054       | 0.273    | 5.1x
  17    | 0.065       | 0.305    | 4.7x

(d) is 3.5-6.7x LOWER than the measured rate in every round. Both are
monotone in the same direction (r14 is the trough in both). The ratio is
not constant, so this is not a simple denominator rescaling.

DIAGNOSIS: WHICH INSTRUMENT IS WRONG?
=====================================
The residual is large and systematic. The most likely explanation is that
the two instruments measure different things and neither is "wrong" in the
simpler sense:

1. behavcensus counts EXECUTED pop actions (game::step applied a Move::Pop
   or Move::PopFree). A player who spends 3 CA in one round can pop up to
   3 times in that round (if food and CA permit), so actions/player-round
   can exceed 1. openerprobe's (d) is the probability that the NEXT
   decision point is a pop -- it can never exceed 1, and it is diluted by
   all the non-pop decisions in between pops.

2. openerprobe's (d) is per DECISION POINT, not per round. If a player
   faces ~4-5 own-turn decisions per round (one per CA spent), then
   actions/player-round ~= (d) x (decisions per round). The measured
   (a) column shows ~2300-2500 decision points per round across 512
   player-rounds (256 games x 2 players), i.e. ~4.5-4.9 decisions per
   player-round. 0.05 x 4.7 ~= 0.235, which is close to the measured
   0.233 at r10. The ratio is not constant because decisions-per-round
   varies (a player with more CA faces more decisions).

CONCLUSION: neither instrument is wrong. (d) is the per-decision-point
probability; the measured rate is per-player-round. The correct comparison
is (d) x (decisions per player-round) vs measured. The (a) column gives
the per-round decision count; dividing by the player-round count (512 for
256 games x 2 players, minus any cap-hit games -- none in this run) gives
decisions per player-round. The product (d) x (a/512) should approximate
the measured rate. It does NOT reproduce it exactly because (d) is a
probability and the actual pop count per round is a Poisson-like count
with mean (d) x decisions, but the two should be within a factor of ~1.5
for a fair comparison. The 3.5-6.7x ratio above is the RAW ratio without
this correction.

KEY FINDINGS
============
1. The shortfall is DOMINATED by legality, not preference. 92.7% of the
   per-decision-point gap is "couldn't" (Pop illegal), not "wouldn't"
   (legal but declined). This is a stronger statement than the existing
   food-gates finding (39.7% of pop-ILLEGAL points are food-bound) because
   it sizes the ILLEGAL fraction itself: 87.6-92.3% of own-turn decision
   points in r10-17 have no legal Pop move at all.

2. Of the "couldn't", food is the dominant binding reason: 73.3% of
   illegal points are food-unaffordable, 26.5% are no-CA. This is
   consistent with the food-gates analysis (39.7% of pop-illegal points
   are food-bound over the WHOLE game, rising to 44.5% at r16+). The
   r10-17 window is food-heavier because the pop price rises with bank
   size and food income does not keep pace.

3. The "wouldn't" component is real but small: even when Pop is legal,
   the bot only takes it 39.5% of the time (vs 23.1% whole-game from
   pop_taken_when_legal_2026-08-28.txt -- the r10-17 window is higher
   because the bot is more pop-aggressive in mid-game). The 60.5%
   decline rate at legal points is the "preference" component, and it
   accounts for only 7.3% of the total gap.

4. The per-round shape: % illegal is HIGHEST at r14 (92.3%) and r13
   (88.8%), and P(chosen|legal) is LOWEST at r10 (34.8%) and r14 (36.3%).
   The r14 trough in (d) (0.028) is driven by the highest illegality, not
   the lowest preference. The r17 peak in P(chosen|legal) (52.8%) is the
   highest of the window -- the bot is most willing to pop when it can in
   late mid-game.

5. The measured per-round actions/player-round (0.185-0.305) is 3.5-6.7x
   the per-decision-point probability (0.028-0.065) because a player
   faces ~4.5-4.9 own-turn decisions per round. The two instruments are
   measuring different denominators and are NOT in conflict -- see the
   residual check above.

REVIEWER CORRECTION 2026-08-29: THE 92.7/7.3 SPLIT DOES NOT RANK THE LEVERS
================================================================================

The decomposition above is correct as arithmetic and the residual diagnosis is
right. The HEADLINE is the problem: "92.7% couldn't, 7.3% wouldn't" is a count
of how often each reason OCCURS, and it reads as "preference is only 7.3% of
the problem, so fixing the eval's taste for Pop is not worth it". That does not
follow, and it is false.

A reason count is not headroom. What decides which lever is worth pulling is
how far each one can move the outcome, so put both counterfactuals against the
human rate. Using (a)/512 as decisions per player-round and holding everything
else fixed:

  round  bot now   preference->100%   food-blocks->legal   human
   10     0.197        0.566                1.231          0.332
   11     0.237        0.574                1.387          0.381
   12     0.240        0.588                1.471          0.460
   13     0.199        0.500                1.418          0.479
   14     0.135        0.371                1.394          0.521
   15     0.217        0.559                1.414          0.542
   16     0.246        0.588                1.517          0.672

BOTH levers overshoot the human rate. The lever that is "only 7.3% of the
reasons" is on its own enough to reach the human rate in five of seven rounds.
So the split ranks nothing, and the useful question is not which reason is
commoner but what P(chosen | legal) would have to be:

  round   current   needed to match human   reachable?
   10      34.8%           58.6%             yes
   11      41.2%           66.4%             yes
   12      40.9%           78.2%             yes
   13      39.8%           95.8%             yes (only just)
   14      36.3%          140.4%             NO -- impossible
   15      38.8%           97.0%             yes (only just)
   16      41.9%          114.3%             NO -- impossible

THAT is the real result, and it is a split by ROUND, not by reason:
  - r10-r12: preference alone closes the gap, and comfortably (59-78% against
    a current 41%). Legality is not what is binding there.
  - r13-r16: it does not. At r14 and r16 no preference change whatever is
    enough, because Pop is illegal at 92.3% / 87.2% of points. Only supply --
    food, and the CA to spend on it -- can move those rounds.

SECOND CORRECTION: LEGALITY IS ENDOGENOUS, SO "COULDN'T" IS NOT AN ALIBI.
"Food unaffordable" at round 12 is not a fact of the rules imposed on the bot;
it is the consequence of choices the same eval made at rounds 3-9. The Farm is
legal at 1405 mid-game decision points and chosen ZERO times
(`farm_never_outranks_mine`), so the food stock that makes Pop unaffordable at
r12 is itself a preference outcome. Likewise "no CA left" (26.5% of the
illegal points) is the consequence of having spent those CA on takes.

A decomposition into "illegal" and "declined" therefore cannot separate "the
rules blocked me" from "I chose earlier not to be able to". Both columns are
the eval. The 92.7% is best read as the SIZE of the downstream consequence of
the upstream preference defect, not as evidence that preference is a minor
term.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT CHANGE: the per-round table, the reason split within the
illegal points (food 73.3% / no-CA 26.5%), the r14 illegality peak, and the
r17 preference peak are all measurements and all stand. Only the headline's
ranking is withdrawn.
