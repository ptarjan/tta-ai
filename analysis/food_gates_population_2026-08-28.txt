# Is food gating population growth? (diagnosis only, no weight change)

## Scope and method

Question from the coordinator: is food actually the binding constraint on
population growth? If so, food's real value is invisible to the eval, which
is why the climb prices `food_rate` negative.

Measured with `openerprobe` at 2p, 256 games (seeds 1..256), frozen champion
`experiments/rust_champion_2p_frozen_2026-08-28.json` (gen 121100), over the
**whole game** (the `play_one` loop was extended from `round <= 3` to
`!state.game_over`). At every own-turn civil-phase decision point the
probe asks `legal::legal_moves` whether any Pop-kind move is legal; if not,
`classify_pop_illegal` (openerprobe.rs:456) names the single binding reason.

Total: **70,922 own-turn decision points** across 256 games.

## The four binding reasons (whole game)

Pop is legal at only **11,371 / 70,922 (16.0%)** of decision points.
It is illegal at **59,551 (84.0%)**. Of those:

| reason                                   | count   | % of illegal |
|------------------------------------------|---------|--------------|
| round 1 (take-only, SS1.9)               | 1,128   | 1.9%         |
| yellow bank empty (no worker to place)   | 0       | 0.0%         |
| no civil action left                     | 34,790  | 58.4%        |
| **food unaffordable**                    | **23,633** | **39.7%** |
| already at max                           | 0       | 0.0%         |

Note on "already at max": TTA has no population maximum other than the
yellow bank itself, so that category is not a real gate and never fires.

CORRECTION (coordinator, verified in source): the claim that
`increase_population` "always succeeds once the food price is paid, even
when the bank is at 0" is WRONG. `economy::pop_cost_base` (economy.rs:47)
returns `None` at `yellow_bank == 0`, and `economy.rs:1530`
(`increase_population_rejects_short_food_or_empty_bank`) is the guard test.
The engine is correct; the 0.0% is simply because a 2p player's bank is
never empty at a decision point. This does not change any number here.

CAVEAT ON THE ORDERING (coordinator): the classifier names ONE binding
reason, so the split depends on the order the checks run in, and the
probe was reverted so the order can no longer be read. If food is checked
last (as the table's order suggests), the 23,633 are the points where food
is the ONLY remaining reason, and points where CA was also exhausted were
charged to CA. Under either ordering, food is short at **at least** 39.7%
of pop-illegal points — the figure is a floor, not a ceiling.

So the real competition is **civil action (58.4%) vs food (39.7%)** for
being the binding constraint, with food a substantial second.

## At the food-binding points: food stock and FoodRate

At the 23,633 points where food is the single binding reason:

- **food stock (p.food)**: min=0, p25=0, median=1, p75=2, max=6, mean=1.25
- **FoodRate (features.rs:713, NET of consumption)**: min=-3, p25=-1,
  median=0, p75=1, max=5, mean=+0.019
- **72.8%** (17,197/23,633) have **FoodRate <= 0** — at the margin the
  player's food does not even grow.

The food stock is tiny (median 1) because the pop price is 4-5 food at the
bank sizes typical in mid-game (bank 9-12 → price 4, bank 5-8 → price 5).
The player is almost always 3-4 food short of the price. The negative
FoodRate at 72.8% of these points means that even if the player did not
grow population, food would not accumulate on its own — the food deficit is
structural, not a one-round timing issue.

The food-gating fraction **rises monotonically** from ~32% in rounds 2-3 to
~45% in round 16+. The late-game rise is the key signal: as the game
progresses, the yellow bank grows (more workers placed), the pop price
stays at 4-5 (the bank is in the 9-16 range), and the player's food stock
does not keep pace — so food becomes the binding constraint more and more
often. The early-game floor (~32%) is set by the pop price being 5-7 when
the bank is small (1-8), but the bank grows fast, so the price drops to 4-5
and stays there.

## Why the eval prices food_rate negative — the mechanism

The eval's food-related weights in the frozen champion are:

- `food_rate` = **-0.199** (negative)
- `food_stock` = +0.855
- `food_gap` = **-0.274** (negative)
- `food_surplus` = **-0.181** (negative)
- `food_stock_needed` = -0.057
- `pop_cost` = -0.037
- `happy_margin_after_next_pop` = -0.066

The climb learned to **disprice food** because, over the whole game, food
gating population is real (39.7% of pop-illegal points) but the eval has no
feature that says "food is the gate on population." The features it does
have are:

1. **FoodRate** (net of consumption): the climb sees that at food-binding
   points the rate is ~0 or negative, and that food does not accumulate.
   It prices the rate negative because the marginal food is not
   converting into population — it is being consumed.

2. **FoodGap** (needs.food - food_have, floored at 0): `needs.food` is the
   pop price (economy.rs:189, `pop_food_cost` with no one-time discount).
   The gap is positive whenever the player is short of the pop price —
   which is 39.7% of the time. The climb prices the gap negative because
   a large food gap does not mean "food is valuable"; it means "population
   is gated," and the bot cannot act on it (it cannot build farms faster
   than the climb wants, and the gap persists).

3. **FoodStock / FoodSurplus**: the climb prices surplus negative because
   surplus food is food that is not being used for population — it is
   "dead" food. A player with food surplus is not growing population,
   which is the outcome the climb is trying to maximize.

The net effect: the climb has **no feature that measures the value of food
as a population gate**, so it prices every food-related feature in the
direction that is empirically correlated with the climb's objective
(maximizing score, not population). Food's real value — its role in
unlocking population, which unlocks production, which unlocks score — is
invisible to the eval.

## Diagnosis

Food **is** gating population growth at a substantial fraction of decision
points (39.7% of pop-illegal, 16.0% of all points). But the eval does not
see it as a gate: it sees food as a cost that does not pay back in
population. The climb therefore prices food features negative, which
further suppresses food accumulation, which further gates population — a
self-reinforcing loop.

The eval would need a feature that measures **food's marginal value as a
population enabler** — e.g., "if I had X more food, would I be able to
grow population this turn, and what is the score value of that population?"
— to see food's real value. Without such a feature, the climb will
continue to disprice food.

## Caveats

- 2p only. In 3p/4p the yellow bank dynamics differ and the food-gating
  fraction may be lower (more players share the bank, but the pop price
  table is the same).
- The frozen champion is gen 121100; the live champion may have moved.
- This is a diagnosis, not a weight change. The fix (if desired) would be
  a new feature, not a weight tweak.
