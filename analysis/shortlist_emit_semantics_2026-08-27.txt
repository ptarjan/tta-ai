EMIT-SITE SEMANTICS FOR THE CORRECTED SIX
==========================================
2026-08-27. Read-only pass over the six keys the audit (bf0479f) corrected
from the shortlist: strength_rel_early, free_workers, best_farm, best_mine,
auction_bid, rival_culture_rate. For each: what the feature PHYSICALLY holds
at its emit site, and whether a "a bigger number is never a downside" rules
citation is even POSSIBLE for that quantity. No citation was looked up. No
sign_intent proposed. No weights.rs edit.

Keys in the audit's corrected order: core tier first, then two-armed.


1. strength_rel_early
----------------------
PHYSICS: A phase-blend weight, NOT a feature. registry.rs:399 documents it as
"phase blend off StrengthRel": the early-phase partner of the ONE remaining
PHASE_KEYS member (weights.rs:1188-1225, `WeightKey::early()` panics on any
key but StrengthRel). The underlying feature is StrengthRel, set at
features.rs:932 to `rel` where (features.rs:680-682) `strength` =
f64::from(s.strength) + g(GainFeature::Strength) and `rel` = strength -
rival_str. So the quantity is: my projected strength MINUS the maximum
projected strength over all non-resigned rivals (rival_str is the max over
rivals of s.strength, computed in rivals.rs via effects::compute per rival).
UNITS: a signed strength difference (units of "strength", the same unit as a
single Infantry). NOT clamped, NOT floored — the full signed value is
emitted (the sibling StrengthLead at features.rs:934 is the clamped/floored
variant: `rel.clamp(0.0, STRENGTH_LEAD_CAP)` with STRENGTH_LEAD_CAP = 6.0 at
features.rs:86; StrengthRel itself is the raw difference). Rival-relative:
yes, by construction — it is the difference, not my strength in isolation.
The `_early` suffix is a weight, not a feature: it is blended into evaluate
as (1 - lateness) * w[strength_rel_early] (weights.rs:1188-1192), so the
"feature" it multiplies is the same StrengthRel value.

CITATION: needs-a-rules-read. The quantity is a rival-relative difference
that can be negative (I am behind) or positive (I lead). A positive value
(leading) is not unambiguously good: leading in strength makes me the target
of attacks and wars (the sibling keys StrengthLead and StrengthDeficit exist
precisely to price the two directions separately). A negative value (trailing)
is not unambiguously bad: a small deficit is survivable and a large deficit is
a war I cannot win. "More strength lead is never a downside" is not a rule
that holds in TTA — leading is a target. "Trailing is never a downside" is
also not a rule — a large deficit loses wars. The citation would have to say
something about WHEN leading vs trailing matters (phase, rival composition,
wonder threat), which is a rules read, not a one-line sign.


2. free_workers
---------------
PHYSICS: The raw count of my unassigned workers in reserve, PLUS a
pending-gain adjustment. features.rs:840:
  f.set(WeightKey::FreeWorkers, f64::from(p.workers_free) + g(GainFeature::FreeWorkers));
`p.workers_free` is a u8 (state.rs:497) — the number of workers in my
reserve that are not assigned to any tableau slot. UNITS: a non-negative
integer count (0 to my total worker count, which is at most ~10-12 in a full
game). FLOORED at 0 by the type (u8 cannot be negative); no upper clamp at
the emit site (the u8 type bounds it). OWN-BOARD: yes, reads only my
`p.workers_free`. The `g(GainFeature::FreeWorkers)` term adds any
pending-gain that would increase free workers (e.g. a bonus card that
grants a worker), so the emitted value is the projected count after pending
effects, not just the current raw count.

CITATION: needs-a-rules-read. More free workers is not unambiguously good:
idle workers are workers that are not producing. A large free_workers count
means I have not assigned workers to production, which is a sign of a
stalled economy. However, free workers are also the raw material for
assigning to new tableau slots (farms, mines, units), so a positive count is
necessary to do anything. The quantity is a resource with an opportunity
cost: more is better only up to the point where I have nothing to assign
them to. A "more is never worse" citation would have to address the
opportunity cost of idling workers, which is a game-strategy read, not a
card-data read. The 2p-opener finding (EndTurn 48.6%, idle worker rewarded
at +5.1) is independent evidence that the fit is pricing this as "reward
idling", which is a sign that the citation should be about WHEN idling is
good, not whether more is ever worse.


3. best_farm
------------
PHYSICS: The maximum level of any Farm card in my tableau. features.rs:971:
  f.set(WeightKey::BestFarm, f64::from(best.farm));
`best.farm` is computed in `sweep_tableau` (features.rs:188-235): for each
tech in my tableau, if the kind is CardType::Farm and its level exceeds
`best.farm`, update it. UNITS: a non-negative integer level (0 = no farm,
1 = Level 1 Farm, 2 = Level 2 Farm, 3 = Level 3 Farm). FLOORED at 0 by the
type (u8, initialized to 0 in BestTypes::default()). No upper clamp at the
emit site (u8 type bounds it, but the max Farm level in TTA is 3, so the
effective range is 0-3). OWN-BOARD: yes, reads only my tableau.

CITATION: needs-a-rules-read. A higher best_farm level means I have a more
productive farm (Level 3 Farm produces 3 food per worker vs Level 1's 1).
More is better in the sense that a higher-level farm is more productive.
However, the quantity is a MAX over my tableau — it does not capture how
many farms I have, only the best one. A player with one Level 3 Farm and no
other farms has best_farm = 3, same as a player with one Level 3 Farm and
two Level 1 Farms. The quantity is a proxy for "I have invested in
production" but it is lossy. A "more is never worse" citation would have to
say that having a higher-level farm is never a disadvantage, which is
plausible (a Level 3 Farm is strictly better than a Level 1 Farm) but the
quantity's lossiness (max, not sum) means it does not capture the full
economy. The citation is about the level, not the count, so "a Level 3 Farm
is never worse than a Level 1 Farm" is a defensible rules statement. But the
feature's sign (negative at 2p, positive at 4p) is about the WEIGHT, not the
quantity — the quantity itself is always "more productive is better". The
citation question is whether the weight's sign can be justified by a rules
reason, which is a different question from whether the quantity's direction
is unambiguous.


4. best_mine
------------
PHYSICS: The maximum level of any Mine card in my tableau. features.rs:972:
  f.set(WeightKey::BestMine, f64::from(best.mine));
Identical structure to best_farm: `best.mine` is the max level over
CardType::Mine in my tableau, computed in the same `sweep_tableau` pass
(features.rs:200-235). UNITS: a non-negative integer level (0 = no mine,
1-3 = Level 1-3 Mine). FLOORED at 0 (u8, BestTypes::default()). No upper
clamp at emit site. OWN-BOARD: yes.

CITATION: needs-a-rules-read. Same structure as best_farm. A higher-level
mine produces more resources per worker (Level 3 Mine: 3 resources per
worker vs Level 1's 1). "A Level 3 Mine is never worse than a Level 1 Mine"
is a defensible rules statement. The same lossiness applies (max, not sum).
The quantity's direction is unambiguous (more productive is better); the
weight's sign is a separate question.


5. auction_bid
--------------
PHYSICS: My committed bid in the current auction, discounted by the
probability I am still the high bidder. features.rs:966:
  f.set(WeightKey::AuctionBid, dc.auction_bid);
`dc.auction_bid` is computed in `deferred_credit` (rivals.rs:405, 464, 483).
Two cases:
- Pending::Auction (rivals.rs:459-467): if I am the current high bidder
  (auc.high == Some(idx)), `out.auction_bid += share * auc.bid as f64` where
  `share = 1.0 / (1.0 + rivals_still_bidding)` — the fraction of the bid
  that is "committed" given that N rivals are still active. `auc.bid` is
  the current bid amount in resources.
- Pending::Colonize (rivals.rs:479-487): if the auction has resolved and I
  am the winner (c.player == idx), `out.auction_bid += c.bid as f64` with
  share = 1.0 (the full bid, no discount).
UNITS: a non-negative resource amount (the bid is in resources; `auc.bid` is
an i32 resource count). FLOORED at 0 by construction (bids are non-negative;
the sum of non-negative terms). No upper clamp at the emit site. Rival-
relative: PARTIALLY — the discount factor `share` depends on how many rivals
are still bidding (a rival-relative quantity), but the base amount `auc.bid`
is the absolute bid level. OWN-BOARD + RIVAL-RELATIVE: the committed
fraction is my board's commitment, but its size depends on the rival
landscape.

CITATION: needs-a-rules-read. A higher auction_bid means I have committed
more resources to winning the auction. This is a cost: I lose those
resources regardless of whether I win (the bid is forfeited to the bank or
the loser pool, per TTA auction rules). "A higher bid is never a downside"
is FALSE — a higher bid costs more resources. However, a higher bid also
means I am more likely to win a valuable card, so there is a benefit side.
The quantity is a cost with an expected-benefit tradeoff, not a monotone
good thing. A "more is never worse" citation is IMPOSSIBLE for this
quantity: the bid is a resource expenditure, and more expenditure is
inherently a cost. The weight's sign (negative at 2p, positive at 4p) is
about whether the fit is pricing the bid as a cost (negative weight =
avoiding high bids) or as a commitment signal (positive weight =
rewarding high bids), which is a strategy question, not a rules question.
The rules citation would have to say "bidding more is always good" which is
flatly contradicted by the fact that the bid is a resource cost.


6. rival_culture_rate
---------------------
PHYSICS: The maximum culture production rate over all non-resigned rivals,
adjusted for pending pact effects. features.rs:1109:
  f.set(WeightKey::RivalCultureRate, rival_culture_rate);
Computed in two stages:
- Base: `rival_culture_rate` is initialized to `f64::from(ctx.rival_culture_rate)`
  (features.rs:654), where `ctx.rival_culture_rate` is computed in
  `rivals::rival_context` (rivals.rs:658) as `best_rate` — the maximum of
  `s.culture` over all non-resigned, non-self players (rivals.rs:636-642:
  `best_rate = best_rate.max(s.culture)` where `s = effects::compute(state, q)`).
- Pact adjustment: for each rival, if a pending pact would raise that rival's
  culture rate (via `dc.partner_gains[pi].get(GainFeature::CultureRate)`),
  the max is recomputed: `rival_culture_rate = rival_culture_rate.max(f64::from(base_culture) + pg.get(GainFeature::CultureRate))`
  (features.rs:670-676).
UNITS: a non-negative integer rate (culture per turn; `s.culture` is the
projected culture production from `effects::compute`, which is an i32
accumulated from card effects). FLOORED at 0 by initialization
(`best_rate = 0` at rivals.rs:630, and the max over non-negative rates). No
upper clamp at the emit site. RIVAL-RELATIVE: YES — this is entirely a
rival-board quantity; it reads no own-board state. It is the maximum culture
rate among my opponents, not a difference.

CITATION: needs-a-rules-read. A higher rival_culture_rate means my opponents
are producing more culture, which is a threat to my culture lead. "A higher
rival culture rate is never a downside" is FALSE — it means my rivals are
catching up or pulling ahead in culture, which threatens my culture lead
and my ability to score on culture-heavy wonders/events. However, a higher
rival culture rate is also a signal that the game is progressing and that
culture production is active, which is not inherently bad. The quantity is a
threat metric: more is worse (rivals are stronger in culture). A "more is
never worse" citation is IMPOSSIBLE for this quantity: the quantity is a
rival's strength, and more rival strength is a threat. The weight's sign
(positive at 2p, negative at 3p/4p) is about whether the fit is pricing
rival culture as a threat to defend against (negative weight = penalizing
high rival culture) or as a signal of a healthy game state (positive weight
= rewarding high rival culture), which is a strategy question. The rules
citation would have to say "rivals producing more culture is never bad for
me" which is contradicted by the basic dynamic of a race game.
