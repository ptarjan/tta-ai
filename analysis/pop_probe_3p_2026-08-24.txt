POP PROBE, 3 players, 2026-08-24
=================================

QUESTION (task framing): `analysis/farm_build_probe_3p_2026-08-24.txt` found the
champion reaches a legal `Move::Build` on a developed Farm only 1.9% of the
time it is SEEN, 88.3% of the failures being `p.workers_free == 0`. This probe
asks WHY the champion has zero free workers, distinguishing two structurally
different causes:
  (a) it never GROWS the population (`Move::Pop` illegal, or legal-but-
      routinely-outranked), vs.
  (b) it grows normally and OVER-COMMITS (workers produced are immediately
      consumed by other actions, so the free pool never accumulates).

METHOD
------
Extended `rust/src/bin/takeprobe.rs` (the ONLY file modified) with a `--pop`
mode, reusing the same self-play harness and four-stage discipline (SEEN ->
LEGAL -> SCORED -> RANK) the `--build`/Mine-TAKE probes already use, plus a
worker-pool balance tracker.

SEEN DEFINITION (stated explicitly, per this task's own instruction)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
`farm_build_probe_3p_2026-08-24.txt` (caveat 2) hit a trap: its SEEN was
"the Farm tech is developed" -- state that itself depends on how much a given
weight vector chooses to develop Farm techs, which made the champion's SEEN
count 8.6x smaller than the human vector's and confounded the LEGAL-rate
comparison.

This probe's SEEN is defined WITHOUT any champion-specific state:

    SEEN := state.phase == Phase::Actions
            && state.pending.is_empty()
            && state.round > 1

This is exactly `legal.rs::action_moves`'s own precondition for possibly
generating a pop move: `action_moves` returns immediately on round 1
("legal.rs:425-427, `§1.9: taking cards is the only legal action`"),
before it ever reaches the pop block. Every decision point counted here is
one where `action_moves` COULD structurally have pushed a `Move::Pop`/
`Move::PopFree`, regardless of what either vector's own policy chose to do,
develop, or build beforehand. Round-1 decision points (where a pop is never
structurally possible, by construction of the code) are correctly excluded
from SEEN, not miscounted as illegal-for-some-reason.

Residual confound (see CAVEATS): the TOTAL number of SEEN decision points
still depends on how many turns a given weight vector's own games run
(different vectors play different-length trajectories) -- this is much
smaller than the 8.6x farm-build gap (130271 vs 110829 here, a 1.18x ratio)
because the definition no longer depends on a champion-specific gate, but it
is not exactly zero and is called out below rather than compared silently.

Four stages, same semantics as the Mine-TAKE/Farm-BUILD probes:
  1. SEEN    -- as defined above.
  2. LEGAL   -- `Move::Pop{full:_}` or `Move::PopFree` present in
                `legal::legal_moves`' output at that decision point.
  3. SCORED  -- survives into `candidate_features`'s own candidate set
                (structurally identical to LEGAL for a plain WeightedBot,
                confirmed again here, not assumed -- see prior probes' own
                doc comments).
  4. RANK    -- 1-based rank and score gap to the winner, via the same
                `candidate_features`/`dot` argmax re-derivation the other
                probes use. When more than one pop-family move is legal at
                once (rare -- `legal.rs`'s own doc comment on `Pop{full:
                None}`/`PopFree` coexisting), the BEST-ranked pop-family
                candidate is reported, since the question is "is pop
                competitive," not "which specific variant."

ILLEGALITY CLASSIFIER
----------------------
For every SEEN-but-not-LEGAL occurrence, `classify_pop_illegal` replays
`legal.rs:432-517`'s own checks IN THE SAME ORDER and with the SAME
short-circuit semantics the real `&&`/`if let Some` uses:

  1. EmptyYellowBank    -- `economy::pop_cost` returns `None`
                            (`p.yellow_bank == 0`, the Population Bank is
                            empty). `action_moves` never even enters its
                            `if let Some(cost) = pop_cost(...)` block in this
                            case, so NEITHER the CA nor the food check below
                            is ever reached -- checked first here too.
  2. NoCivilAction      -- no spare civil action AND no live Civil-Life-style
                            CA-free grant (`costs::spare_ca(p) >= 1 ||
                            costs::civil_life_ca_free(...)` false). This is
                            the LEFT half of `legal.rs`'s `&&`.
  3. InsufficientFood   -- a civil action is available, but `p.food` falls
                            short of `economy::pop_cost`'s result. The RIGHT
                            half of the same `&&`, only reached (in the real
                            code) once the CA half already passed.
  4. Other              -- residual bucket; firing at all would mean this
                            classifier's replay of `legal.rs` diverged from
                            the real move generator. It read exactly 0 in
                            both 600-game runs below -- the classifier never
                            diverged.

WORKER-POOL BALANCE (settles cause (b) directly)
--------------------------------------------------
Read straight off `apply.rs`'s own effect on `p.workers_free` (confirmed by
reading the handlers, not guessed):
  - `h_pop`/`h_pop_free` (`Move::Pop`/`Move::PopFree`) call
    `economy::increase_population`, which always does `p.workers_free += 1`
    -- an ENTER.
  - `h_destroy` (`Move::Destroy`) does `p.workers_free += 1` (`apply.rs:893`)
    -- a worker returns to the pool -- also an ENTER.
  - `do_build` (`Move::Build`, civil OR unit) does `p.workers_free -= 1`
    (`apply.rs:881`) -- the only LEAVE.
  - `do_upgrade` (`Move::Upgrade`) moves a worker from the low card to the
    high card (`apply.rs:923-924`) -- net ZERO on `workers_free`, never
    touched.
  - `h_develop` (`Move::Develop`) inserts the new tech at zero workers
    (confirmed by `apply.rs`'s own test name) -- never touches
    `workers_free` either.

This CORRECTS this task's own working hypothesis in its prompt ("takes/
develops/upgrades" consuming the pool): reading the actual handlers, Develop
and Upgrade never touch `p.workers_free` at all. Only Build does. The balance
below counts exactly ENTER = successful Pop/PopFree/Destroy, LEAVE =
successful Build, tallied per round from the move the bot actually applied
(independent of whether a pop was itself SEEN that turn -- a round-1 Build
still moves the pool even though pop is never SEEN there).

VERDICT: (a) vs (b)
--------------------
This is NOT primarily cause (a). Under the champion vector, `Move::Pop` clears
LEGAL 26.7% of the time it is SEEN -- 14x higher than the Farm build's 1.9%
LEGAL rate from the prior probe -- and when it IS legal, it is genuinely
competitive: mean rank 4.34 among all candidates, and it wins outright 27.6%
of the time. That is not "illegal" and it is not "routinely outranked" in the
way the Farm build was (where another Build won 96.2% of the time a Farm
build was scored). Pop is a move the champion is both able to make and
willing to make, reasonably often.

This IS cause (b): over-commitment. The worker-pool balance is net NEGATIVE
in 18 of 21 rounds under the champion (total -3656 across 600 games), and the
deficit is not concentrated in one phase -- it holds from round 4 straight
through round 19, the entire midgame-to-lategame span the Farm-build probe
found `NoFreeWorker` dominating (round 5 onward). Every worker the champion
grows into the pool via Pop is, on average, consumed by Build faster than
Pop replenishes it. The human vector, by contrast, nets POSITIVE overall
(+237) and swings decisively positive from round 13 onward, which is exactly
the window in which its Farm-build LEGAL rate (25.2%, prior probe) so
outpaced the champion's (1.9%): a policy that lets its worker pool
accumulate a standing surplus has free workers on hand when a Farm (or any
other) build becomes available; a policy whose pool is perpetually near-zero
or negative-pressured does not, regardless of how well it values Pop itself
when Pop is on the table.

A secondary, real contributor for BOTH vectors: `InsufficientFood` is the
dominant illegality reason (69.3% champion, 79.8% human) -- neither vector is
mostly blocked by civil-action scarcity or an empty population bank. This
ties back to the already-established finding that champion food production
declines over the game (2.00 -> ~1.10) while human food production climbs
(2.00 -> ~5.35): the champion's Pop-legality rate is itself constrained by
its own collapsing food economy, on top of (and likely contributing to) the
worker-pool over-commitment above. This is a plausible causal chain --
declining food -> fewer legal Pops -> a thinner worker pool that Build still
drains at the same rate -> more decision points at workers_free==0 -- but
this probe measures the Pop funnel and the balance, not the food-production
mechanism itself, and does not prove the causal direction between them (see
CAVEATS).

CAVEATS
-------

1. SEEN-count denominators still differ between vectors (130271 champion vs
   110829 human, a 1.18x ratio) even though SEEN's definition no longer
   depends on any champion-specific gate (see the SEEN DEFINITION section
   above). This residual gap is the champion and human vector playing
   different-length games (different numbers of action-phase turns across
   600 self-play games each), not a definitional artifact -- flagged here
   per this task's own instruction, rather than comparing the LEGAL/mean-
   rank/balance numbers as if the two funnels ran over identical decision-
   point sets. It is far smaller than the 8.6x gap the farm-build probe's
   SEEN definition produced, but it is not zero.

2. The champion and human-vector self-play games are NOT the same
   trajectories -- each vector plays its own game (own seeds' outcomes
   diverge as soon as a single decision differs), so the two runs' round-by-
   round tables are not literally comparable turn-for-turn, only in
   aggregate shape. Round 22-23 rows in particular have very small samples
   (most games have ended) and should not be over-read.

3. The worker-pool balance is a SIMPLE per-round net (enters minus leaves,
   summed over every player-game in the run) -- it is not a running
   STOCK/level of `p.workers_free` over time, and it does not distinguish
   "always near zero" from "swings widely but averages near zero." A
   negative net in a round means Build outpaced Pop/PopFree/Destroy THAT
   round, aggregated across every player and every game; it does not by
   itself prove `p.workers_free` was 0 at every decision point in that round
   (though the Farm-build probe's own direct measurement of
   `p.workers_free == 0` at 88.3% of illegal-build occurrences is independent
   corroborating evidence that it usually was, for the champion specifically).

4. This task's own working hypothesis named Develop and Upgrade as possible
   worker-consuming actions alongside Build. Reading `apply.rs`'s actual
   handlers (`h_develop`, `do_upgrade`) directly, in this same investigation,
   found that only `Move::Build` ever changes `p.workers_free` --
   `h_develop` inserts a new tech at zero workers and `do_upgrade` moves an
   existing on-card worker from the low card to the high card, net zero on
   the free pool. The worker-pool balance in this report reflects that
   correction (ENTER = Pop/PopFree/Destroy, LEAVE = Build only), not the
   task prompt's original three-way guess; stated here so the discrepancy is
   visible rather than silently resolved.

5. The illegality classifier replays `legal.rs`'s checks; it does not call
   `legal.rs` itself, so a future change to the pop block's checks/order (or
   a new gate added there) would silently desync this classifier from the
   real legality path until this probe is updated to match. `Other` reading
   exactly 0 in both runs is the check that they agree today, not a
   guarantee they will keep agreeing after a `legal.rs` change.

6. The causal link drawn in the VERDICT section between declining champion
   food production and the Pop-legality/worker-balance findings here is
   plausible and consistent with both this probe's numbers and the prior
   Farm-build probe's own background section, but it is not independently
   proven by this probe -- this probe measures the Pop funnel and the worker
   balance, not the food-production mechanism (what produces/consumes food
   turn to turn). A dedicated food-production probe would be needed to
   settle that link on its own terms.

7. Per this task's own instruction, this file reports what was measured and
   does not propose a weight change or code fix.

COORDINATOR NOTES 2026-08-24: THE (a)-vs-(b) VERDICT ABOVE IS TOO STRONG
================================================================================

The report concludes "cause (b), not (a) -- the champion is neither illegally
blocked from Pop nor routinely outranking it away". Its own numbers contradict
the second half of that sentence.

Pop wins outright 27.6% of the occurrences on which it is SCORED. The
complement is the finding: **something else beats a legal, scored Pop 72.4% of
the time.** That is routinely outranking it away, by any reading. Mean rank
4.34 with a mean gap of 52.66 evaluator units says the same thing -- Pop is
competitive, not chosen.

Set the two vectors side by side over the same 600 games and the difference is
symmetric, and it is a difference in POLICY, not in what the engine permits:

    ENTERS (Pop/PopFree/Destroy)      champion 11649     human 13483
    LEAVES (Build)                    champion 15305     human 13246
    net                               champion  -3656    human   +237
    Pop chosen, as a fraction
      of the times it is scored       champion  27.6%    human   89.3%

The champion builds 15.5% MORE and pops 13.6% LESS. The human vector holds its
pool flat; the champion spends down its starting endowment (-3656 over 1800
player-games is -2.03 each, i.e. the initial free workers, drained and never
rebuilt) and then runs at a floor of zero for the rest of the game. That floor
is what the Farm-build probe measured from the other side as NoFreeWorker 88.3%.

One corroborating detail the report has but does not use: EmptyYellowBank is
3.3% of the human vector's illegal pops and 0.0% (nine occurrences in 600
games) of the champion's. The human vector pops hard enough to occasionally
exhaust the population bank. The champion never comes near it.

SO THE VERDICT IS BOTH, WITH (a) THE PART THAT IS ACTIONABLE:
  - (b) is real: Build does drain the pool and Develop/Upgrade genuinely never
    touch it (that handler correction is right and is worth keeping).
  - (a) is ALSO real and was mis-scored: Pop reaches the evaluator constantly
    and loses the argmax nearly three times out of four. InsufficientFood
    (69.3%) gates the other side, and food is itself downstream of the builds
    the bot is making instead.

CAVEAT ON THE 89.3%. The human vector's Pop clears LEGAL only 11.1% of the
time versus the champion's 26.7%, so its high chosen-fraction is partly a
selection effect: it reaches the decision less often and in more clear-cut
spots. The ABSOLUTE counts (11649 vs 13483 enters) do not suffer from that and
point the same way, which is why the verdict rests on them.
