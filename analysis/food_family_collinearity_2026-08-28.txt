2. WHAT EACH SEPARATELY PRICES (net vs gross)
---------------------------------------------
FoodRate (features.rs:713): `s.food + g(FoodRate) - consumption`.
NET surplus / famine margin: gross production (s.food) plus pending food
gains the trial state cannot show, MINUS the consumption bill read off
the projected yellow bank. It is the per-turn food balance, not gross
production. Test at features.rs:1327-1344 fixes its meaning: a bigger
population must lower food_rate by exactly the consumption it owes.

FoodStock (features.rs:736): `p.food + g(FoodStock)`. GROSS stock on the
board plus pending food gains. Not net of anything -- the raw available
food before this turn's production is added.

FoodGap (features.rs:1172): `(needs.food - food_have).max(0.0)`, where
food_have = FoodStock (features.rs:1171) and needs.food is the
population-increase food price (marginal_needs, features.rs:308-317).
The SHORTFALL against the pop threshold: "how much food short of being
able to grow". It is net of the pop threshold, not of consumption; the
features.rs:738-751 comment states explicitly that this is a DIFFERENT
rulebook threshold from FamineDeficit's.

FoodSurplus (features.rs:1173): the mirror hinge, `(food_have -
needs.food).max(0.0)`. Food ABOVE the pop threshold. Same threshold,
opposite side; together with FoodGap it is the two-coordinate gap/surplus
idiom (weights.rs doc on that block).

FamineDeficit (features.rs:752): `(-(FoodStock + FoodRate)).max(0.0)`.
Net: by construction FoodStock + FoodRate = p.food + pending + s.food +
pending - consumption = available food this turn MINUS consumption, so
this is the RULE'S OWN famine shortfall (RULES_SPEC 6.6 step 3d,
economy.rs:1057-1065): surplus reads 0.0, deficit reads the exact
missing count (4 culture lost per missing food). It is the only key
that prices the famine cliff itself, and it is a strict FUNCTION of
FoodStock and FoodRate -- which is the collinearity that matters for
this question (section 3).

PopCost (features.rs:838): `needs.food`, the raw population-increase food
price (the same number FoodGap/FoodSurplus threshold against). GROSS
rulebook constant for this player (discount/bank-adjusted, but not net
of any stock). It prices "how expensive the next population is".

Uprising (features.rs:891-894): `uprising_production_at_stake =
s.science + s.culture + s.food + s.resources`, set only when
discontent > free workers. s.food enters as ONE of four additive terms
scaling the production at stake if the player's workforce revolts (the
entire production phase is forfeited, features.rs:880-890). It prices
food only as its share of total production, inside a gated event term.

RivalFoodStock (features.rs:1158): the strongest rival's gross food
stock (rivals.rs:110-121, max over rivals of q.food). Not the evaluated
player's food at all -- listed for completeness of "every food reader".

3. THE COLLINEARITY THAT MATTERS FOR THE FLOOR
---------------------------------------------
The queued floor acts on FoodRate. Which neighbours could absorb a
negative that the floor removes?

(a) FamineDeficit is a STRICT FUNCTION of (FoodStock, FoodRate):
FamineDeficit = max(0, -FoodStock - FoodRate). It carries no
independent information. If FoodRate is floored at 0 and the fitter
still needs to price "food is bad here", it can do so through
FamineDeficit's weight -- but only in positions where FoodStock +
FoodRate < 0 (the famine zone). In the SURPLUS zone (sum >= 0)
FamineDeficit is identically 0 and cannot carry anything. So the
famine-deficit channel is available as a migration sink ONLY in the
famine zone; elsewhere it is structurally silent.

(b) FoodStock is the other additive member of the same sum. A floor on
FoodRate leaves FoodStock free to go negative and absorb the "food is a
liability" price for every position, famine or not. This is the wide
sink: unlike FamineDeficit it is live in every food state. If the
negative migrates anywhere, this is the most likely home.

(c) FoodGap / FoodSurplus are net of the POP threshold (needs.food), a
different rulebook fact from consumption. They can carry "food relative
to my pop plan" but not "food relative to my consumption bill". A
migration here would change what is being said (pop-readiness, not
survival), so a floor-induced move onto them would be a genuine
re-pricing, not a silent substitution.

(d) PopCost is a constant per player (no state dependence in the
decision), so it cannot absorb a position-varying negative -- it can
only shift the whole pop axis.

(e) Uprising's s.food term is gated on discontent > free workers and
diluted across four resources; it is not a meaningful sink for a
general food-negative.

4. ANSWER TO THE QUESTION
-------------------------
Would the NonNegative floor on FoodRate fix anything, or would the
negative migrate to a collinear neighbour?

The structural answer: the only strict-function neighbour is
FamineDeficit, and it is silent outside the famine zone, so it cannot
absorb a general food-negative. The wide sink is FoodStock, and the
live evidence shows it already sits LARGE POSITIVE on both converged
arms (+0.94 / +46.46) -- the family's "food as asset" price lives there
and is stable. The negative is concentrated in food_rate itself
(-0.184 / -0.214), with no collinear neighbour carrying a comparable
negative (all others are within 0.27 of zero except the small
famine_deficit).

So the floor would NOT be defeated by silent migration: there is no
live collinear channel through which "food is bad" is currently being
expressed. If the floor holds, the negative has nowhere to go except
FoodStock (a genuine re-pricing that would show up as food_stock going
negative -- watchable at the next champion read) or the pop-threshold
hinges (a change of meaning, also watchable). If instead the fitter
re-asserts the negative through one of those, the floor did not fix a
defect -- it met a real preference, and the experiment will show that
directly.

What the floor WOULD fix, if the negative is a defect: the farm-vs-mine
gap. The gap was -0.761 at 2p, and the food-rate half of it was
food_rate -0.184 vs resource_rate +0.402. Flooring food_rate at 0
removes ~0.59 of the farm's penalty immediately (the rate term alone),
which is most of the -0.761 gap. That is the mechanism by which the
queued experiment would move the farm's rank, and it does not depend on
any neighbour migrating.

Caveat carried from the farm-vs-mine file: food_rate is NET of
consumption, so part of its negativity may be a real opening fact (low
population, idle food stock). The concentration evidence above says the
family has NOT re-expressed that fact anywhere else, so the floor's
experiment is the right instrument to separate "real opening fact" from
"fitter artifact" -- but this file, per the job, makes no sign_intent
proposal.
