Card rot, cause split: why was a wasted card never legal?
=========================================================
Date: 2026-08-24
Tool: rust/src/bin/behavcensus.rs, "Zero playable-turns follow-up" section
Run:  ./target/release/behavcensus --games 200 --players 3 \
        --weights /private/tmp/opencensus/champ3p.json --threads 5

WHAT THIS ANSWERS
-----------------
analysis/card_rot_3p_2026-08-24.txt (commit ff05c2d) found that 63.6% early /
61.3% late of the civil cards the bot never plays were never LEGAL to play
again after the take. This file asks why, and splits every blocked decision
point into exactly one of three buckets, all attributed from the SAME
legal::legal_moves result the playable-turns count already computes -- no
second call, no re-derived affordability check:

  "no civil action"            -- nothing in hand was legal because the player
                                  had no action left to spend. An action-budget
                                  miss; the hand may have been perfectly
                                  affordable.
  "nothing affordable"         -- an action WAS available and still nothing in
                                  hand could be paid for. A true production
                                  shortfall.
  "something else developable" -- some OTHER civil card in hand WAS legal, so
                                  this specific card lost the competition. A
                                  card-selection defect.

PLAYED cards are the control: the same three buckets over the blocked turns
they sat through before finally winning one.

RESULT
------
Per-turn share of ALL blocked decision points:

                       no civil action   nothing affordable   something else
  EARLY, PLAYED              32.6%              54.4%             13.0%
  EARLY, NEVER-played        16.6%              65.6%             17.8%
  LATE,  PLAYED              33.4%              53.4%             13.2%
  LATE,  NEVER-played        16.6%              65.8%             17.6%

Absolute means, blocked decision points per card:

                       no civil action   nothing affordable   something else
  EARLY, PLAYED               3.11               5.17              1.23
  EARLY, NEVER-played         4.63              18.35              4.97
  LATE,  PLAYED               3.07               4.89              1.21
  LATE,  NEVER-played         4.82              19.14              5.10

THE ANSWER: A PRODUCTION SHORTFALL
----------------------------------
Two thirds of a wasted card's blocked decision points are turns at which the
player HAD a civil action to spend and could not afford a single card in hand.
The median wasted card sits through 17 early / 18 late such decision points.
The played control sits through 3, and 5.17 on the mean against 18.35 -- a
3.5x gap on the one bucket that means "too poor", against a 1.4x gap on the
one that means "wrong card".

The defect is the civilization's production, not the take gate's choice of
card and not the action budget.

THE CIVIL-ACTION CONFOUND IS DEAD
---------------------------------
The previous version of this file could not separate "too poor" from "out of
actions", and flagged the poverty count as an upper bound. It is now measured
and the confound is small and points the WRONG way: only 16.6% of a wasted
card's blocked turns are action exhaustion, against 32.6% for a played card.
Played cards resolve fast, so their blocked turns concentrate in busy,
action-starved turns; wasted cards accumulate long stretches of plain poverty.
Removing the exhaustion bucket entirely makes the poverty gap wider, not
narrower.

DO NOT LEAD WITH THE "AT LEAST ONE TURN" SHARE
----------------------------------------------
The report also prints that 75.5% early / 77.1% late of wasted cards had at
least one turn where something else in hand was developable. That is a count
of CARDS over a population that sits through ~28 blocked decision points, so
nearly anything lands in it. The per-turn share above is the comparable
quantity: 17.8% against 13.0%. Real, but secondary.

CONSISTENT WITH THE HUMAN MIRROR
--------------------------------
analysis/card_fate_human_2026-08-24.txt: humans take 26.92 civil cards per
player-game at 3p and play 78.3%; the bot takes 17.49 and plays 61.0%. A bot
that takes FEWER cards and still cannot afford them does not have a selection
problem at the take gate -- it has less civilization behind the take.

WHAT THIS STILL DOES NOT SAY
----------------------------
"Nothing affordable" is a state, not a cause. It has at least two:
  - the bot under-builds production (too few farms/mines, or it upgrades too
    late), so its per-turn income never reaches what its hand costs; versus
  - its income is fine but its blue tokens are committed, so the resources
    exist on paper and not in the bank.
The discriminating follow-up is a production-curve census against the human
corpus: resources and food PRODUCED per round by round number, bot vs human,
at 3p. That is a diagnostic mirror only.

DO NOT FIT WEIGHTS TO THE HUMAN NUMBERS.
