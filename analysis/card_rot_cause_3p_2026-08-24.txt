Card rot, cause split: poverty or a card-selection loss?
========================================================
Date: 2026-08-24
Tool: rust/src/bin/behavcensus.rs, "Zero playable-turns follow-up" section
Run:  ./target/release/behavcensus --games 200 --players 3 \
        --weights /private/tmp/opencensus/champ3p.json --threads 5

WHAT THIS ANSWERS
-----------------
analysis/card_rot_3p_2026-08-24.txt (commit ff05c2d) found that 63.6% early /
61.3% late of the civil cards the bot never plays were never LEGAL to play
again after the take. That file closed by naming the next discriminator: at
each decision point where this card was not legal, was ANY other civil card in
hand legal?

Two buckets, attributed from the SAME legal::legal_moves result the previous
section already computes -- no second call, no re-derived affordability check:
  "nothing developable"        -- no civil card in this player's hand was legal
  "something else developable" -- some OTHER civil card in hand WAS legal

PLAYED cards are the control: the same two buckets over the blocked turns they
sat through before finally winning one.

RESULT
------
EARLY (942 never-played zero-playable-turn cards, 1914 played controls):
  nothing developable,        PLAYED:      median=5  mean=8.28
  something else developable, PLAYED:      median=0  mean=1.23
  nothing developable,        NEVER:       median=22 mean=22.99
  something else developable, NEVER:       median=3  mean=4.97
  231 (24.5%) blocked by poverty on EVERY turn; 711 (75.5%) had >=1 turn where
  something else in hand was developable.

LATE (830 never-played zero-playable-turn cards, 1712 played controls):
  nothing developable,        PLAYED:      median=5  mean=7.96
  something else developable, PLAYED:      median=0  mean=1.21
  nothing developable,        NEVER:       median=23 mean=23.96
  something else developable, NEVER:       median=3  mean=5.10
  190 (22.9%) blocked by poverty on EVERY turn; 640 (77.1%) had >=1 turn where
  something else in hand was developable.

THE ANSWER IS ECONOMY-SIDE, NOT CARD-SELECTION-SIDE
---------------------------------------------------
Do NOT read the 75.5% / 77.1% as the headline. It is an "at least one"
statistic over a population that sits through ~28 blocked decision points; a
card that is the odd-one-out even once lands in it. The comparable quantity is
the per-turn share of blocked turns at which another card WAS developable:

                       EARLY            LATE
  PLAYED (control)     12.9%            13.2%
  NEVER-played zero    17.8%            17.6%

A never-played card is the odd-one-out on 17.8% of its blocked turns against
12.9% for a card that eventually got played. That enrichment is real but it is
1.4x, not the 6x the "at least one" framing suggests.

What actually separates the two populations is DURATION in a hand that can
build nothing at all:
  blocked decision points, total   PLAYED 9.5   NEVER-played zero 28.0  (early)
                                   PLAYED 9.2   NEVER-played zero 29.1  (late)

Roughly 82% of a wasted card's blocked decision points are turns at which NO
civil card in the hand was legal. The bot is not mainly taking the wrong card
out of an affordable field. It is spending the large majority of its decision
points unable to build ANYTHING it owns. One in four wasted cards (24.5% /
22.9%) never once shared a decision point with a developable card.

This is consistent with the human mirror in
analysis/card_fate_human_2026-08-24.txt: humans take 26.92 civil cards per
player-game and play 78.3% of them; the bot takes 17.49 and plays 61.0%.
Humans take MORE and cash a HIGHER fraction. A bot that takes fewer cards and
still cannot afford them does not have a selection problem at the take gate --
it has less civilization behind the take.

THE CONFOUND, AND WHY THE RATIO IS THE TRUSTWORTHY NUMBER
---------------------------------------------------------
"Nothing developable" absorbs every decision point at which the player has no
civil actions left, because no Develop is legal then for any card. That
inflates the poverty bucket. It inflates it in BOTH populations at the same
per-turn rate, which is why the 17.8%-vs-12.9% comparison survives it and the
absolute "22.99 poverty turns" figure must be read as an UPPER BOUND on
resource poverty.

THE NEXT DISCRIMINATOR
----------------------
Split "nothing developable" into:
  - no civil action left (an action-budget effect: the hand is affordable, the
    player simply has nothing left to spend this turn), versus
  - civil action available but nothing in hand affordable (a true production
    shortfall).

Until that split exists, "the bot is too poor" and "the bot runs out of civil
actions before it runs out of cards" are the same number here, and they have
opposite fixes. Do not touch a weight on the strength of this file alone.

DO NOT FIT WEIGHTS TO THE HUMAN NUMBERS. The human census is a diagnostic
mirror only.
