PATTERN COMPARISON: 4P VS 2P MECHANISM

2-PLAYER (ESTABLISHED MECHANISM):
  - Take share collapsed: 32.1% → 12.8% (19.3pp loss, 60% of prior share)
  - EndTurn share rose: 32.9% → 47.0% (+14.1pp)
  - Root cause: bot's end_turn_bias parameter RELAXED (became less negative)
  - Interpretation: bot stopped valuing Takes and started valuing EndTurns

4-PLAYER (THIS MEASUREMENT):
  - Take share collapsed: 34.8% → 22.0% (12.8pp loss, 37% of prior share)
  - EndTurn share rose: 24.3% → 31.7% (+7.4pp)
  - BUT 4p's end_turn_bias TIGHTENED (became MORE negative): -29.494 → -37.336
  - Interpretation: This parameter change OPPOSES EndTurn rise

VERDICT ON PATTERN:
The 4-player decline SHARES the 2-player core pattern (Take collapse) but the
MECHANISM IS DIFFERENT. In 2p, the bot fundamentally stopped taking cards because
end_turn_bias relaxed (the scoring function punished Takes less, or rewarded EndTurns
more). In 4p, end_turn_bias TIGHTENED (became more negative), yet Takes still
collapsed and EndTurns still rose. Instead of pure Take→EndTurn substitution, 4p
also shifted to alternative high-value moves: Mine, Urban, and Pop. This suggests
the 4p decline was not driven solely by end_turn_bias, but also by degradation in
the scoring or evaluation of Take moves themselves, or an elevation of alternative
build actions (Mine/Urban/Pop).

DETAILED FINDINGS

1. CORE SIMILARITY: Both 2p and 4p show Take collapse as the headline.
   - 2p: -19.3pp, EndTurn compensation +14.1pp
   - 4p: -12.8pp, EndTurn compensation only +7.4pp

2. MECHANISM DIFFERENCE:
   - 2p: end_turn_bias RELAXED → bot downvalued Takes
   - 4p: end_turn_bias TIGHTENED → yet Takes still collapsed

   This contradiction means 4p's Take collapse is NOT explained by end_turn_bias
   alone. The bot is choosing Mine (1.7%→7.2%), Urban (1.0%→5.3%), and Pop
   (11.3%→17.0%) over Takes, even though it's trying harder to avoid EndTurns.

   Possible root causes in 4p:
   - Degraded Take move evaluation (some parameter downweighting Takes)
   - Elevated alternative build move scores (Mine/Urban/Pop became more attractive)
   - Changed opening strategy that deprioritizes early card draws in 4-player games
   - Some tuning that decoupled Takes from the end_turn_bias mechanism

3. ROUND CONCENTRATION: Rounds 2 and 3 suffered the largest CA loss (30% each).
   This is where the opener's decision quality regarding action point use declined.
   Round 1 held relatively stable (-7.3%), suggesting early-game strategy remained
   consistent but mid-to-late opener decisions degraded.

4. MILITARY BUILDS ALSO DECLINED: Build Military fell from 12.6% → 6.8% (-5.8pp),
   while Mine/Urban rose significantly. This is not visible in the 2p pattern and
   suggests a shift in the value function for build types, not just Takes.

5. UNSPENT CA JUMPED SHARPLY: In postclimb, turns ending with >=1 unspent CA rose
   from 18.1% → 55.9% (3x increase). This is evidence the bot is making worse
   decisions about when to spend its allocation, or is running out of feasible
   moves to spend CA on. The per-round unspent data shows this is especially acute
   in Rounds 2 and 3 (r2: 0.366→1.456, r3: 0.544→1.613).

================================================================================

CONCLUSION

The 4-player opener decline is NOT a simple consequence of the end_turn_bias
tightening (which would be consistent with the parameter change). Instead, 4p
appears to have experienced a mixed rebalancing: the bot both stopped taking
cards AND shifted to alternative build moves (Mine/Urban/Pop), while barely
increasing EndTurns relative to 2p's pattern. The accumulation of unspent CA
in Rounds 2-3 suggests the bot is struggling to find high-value moves in the
mid-to-late opener when facing 4 players, possibly because the presence of more
players constrains the board state or card availability in ways that devalue
Takes specifically.
