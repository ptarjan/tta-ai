What ln 124 is
--------------

CORRECTION (2026-08-25, from the coordinator's post-mortem): an earlier version
of this file said the two lines referenced "the same row slot (slot 1)" and
treated that as evidence of a duplicate. That was an ARTIFACT, not evidence.
Replayer::ground_row_slot resolves a take by forcing the first ungrounded slot
whose costs::take_cost matches the journal's CA charge, and take_cost is PURELY
POSITIONAL (slots 0-4 cost 1, 5-8 cost 2, 9-12 cost 3) with zero card-identity
information; an ungrounded slot's card identity is documented fictional filler
("never validated against anything", only its EXISTENCE is real). So two
adjacent cost-1 takes both resolve to "slot 1" simply because the greedy
lowest-index-first scan finds cost-1 slots interchangeable. Do NOT carry the
"same slot" observation forward as a fact -- the duplicate is established by
the byte-identical text + 3s gap + the engine's hand running one card high,
not by slot identity.

  pre-ln123:  hand_civil = [Rich Land (I), Patriotism (I), Iron, Irrigation]  (4, limit 4)

               takes Urban Growth, hand -> 5
  apply ln124: TAKE REJECT HandFull (gate_have=2, limit 4) -> hand_full override

played or built (no journal line for it), and the "discovers Iron" line is the
one line that actually removes it. Everything downstream of ln 124 is this

The scattered +2/+3/+5 pattern in the wider corpus is the same single
root cause: a BGO client-side double-log of a TAKE line, applied twice
because the replayer has no take-dedup. The drift is not a clean
whole-game offset (it is +1 at ln 124, then normal engine removals);
it is one spurious card inserted at ln 124 that persists (here) until
the next "discovers X" / hand-removal line removes it.

CURE STATUS (2026-08-25, coordinator post-mortem): the cure is NOT available.
A row-slot-occupancy guard was built and empirically falsified before it
landed -- ground_row_slot's cost-based grounding makes adjacent identical
takes indistinguishable from two genuine takes of different physical copies
(many action cards have many copies, so two byte-identical adjacent takes are
the norm, not the exception: 281 such occurrences across 251 of 1011
journals, see analysis/adjacent_identical_takes_2026-08-25.txt). A
text-equality or slot-occupancy dedup would corrupt a quarter of the corpus.
The finding stands as a known, unfixable replayer limitation: the hand drift
in these games is the double-logged take being applied twice, and there is no
safe signal to suppress it.
