THE OPENER FOOD DEFECT IS A VALUATION, NOT A LEGALITY PROBLEM
=============================================================
2026-08-27. Follows analysis/opener_recheck_2026-08-27.txt (c47e3df), which
re-measured the four opener baselines after two weeks of leaf-eval feature work
and found the food-production defect completely unmoved: production still sits
flat at exactly 2.00 through rounds 2-3 and then declines, at every player count.

This file localizes it.


THE MEASUREMENT
---------------
rust/target/release/openerprobe --games 60 --players 3 --seed 0 --threads 2
  --weights experiments/rust_champion_3p.json      (LIVE, gen 65552)
1851 own-turn decision points in rounds 1-3.

Chosen move by kind:
    EndTurn      540 (29.2%)
    Take         426 (23.0%)
    Pop          310 (16.7%)
    Build Mine   180 ( 9.7%)
    Build Urban  155 ( 8.4%)
    Build Farm     0 ( 0.0%)     <-- zero, in 60 games

Rank of the best LEGAL move of each build-shaped kind (rank 1 = actual pick):
    Farm   legal 359/1851   min=3  p25=3  median=4  p75=5  mean=4.47
    Mine   legal 359/1851   min=1  p25=1  median=1  p75=3  mean=2.25
    Urban  legal 156/1851   min=1  p25=1  median=1  p75=1  mean=1.01

Farm and Mine are legal at EXACTLY the same 359 decision points -- the same free
worker, the same resources, the same civil action. At none of those 359 is a farm
ranked in the top two. The minimum rank a farm ever achieves is 3.

Pop (increase population) is legal at 808/1851 (43.7%) and ranks median 2, so the
population path is open as well; the bot takes it 310 times. Workers are being
produced and then placed somewhere other than a farm.

So the bot is not BLOCKED from farming. It prices farming last, every time.


IT IS ZERO AT ALL THREE PLAYER COUNTS
-------------------------------------
Same tool, same 60 games, same seed, live champion of each count:

              decisions   Build Farm   Build Mine   Build Urban   Pop   EndTurn
    2p            741        0 (0.0%)     1 (0.1%)    94 (12.7%)   6.5%   48.6%
    3p           1851        0 (0.0%)   180 (9.7%)   155 ( 8.4%)  16.7%   29.2%
    4p           2432        0 (0.0%)   228 (9.4%)   131 ( 5.4%)  12.8%   29.6%

Zero farms in 5024 opener decision points across 180 games. This is not a 3p
quirk.

2p is a separate and worse failure: it builds essentially NOTHING (one mine in
741 decisions) and ends its turn on 48.6% of them. That is the "end turn beats
spending" defect resurfacing at 2p specifically, on a lineage whose free_workers
weight is +6.31 -- i.e. holding an idle worker is REWARDED, so nothing is ever
worth the worker.


THE WEIGHTS SAY THE SAME THING -- BUT NOT THE SIMPLE VERSION
------------------------------------------------------------
Live champions, gens 2p 116992 / 3p 65552 / 4p 21459:

                    2p         3p         4p
    food_rate      -1.0516    -0.0657    +0.6461
    resource_rate  +0.2144    +7.8257    +4.5434
    food_stock     +0.7428   +40.4226    +0.0271
    best_farm      -0.1870    -1.1984   +37.7806
    best_mine      -0.0481   +19.5980    -1.2100

food_rate is fitted NEGATIVE at 2p and 3p: the eval is actively penalized for
owning more food production. best_farm is negative at 2p and 3p as well.

The three lineages do not merely differ in size, they disagree in SIGN on
best_farm and best_mine, and by two orders of magnitude on food_stock. That is
the signature of a collinear family whose fitted signs flip on noise, not of
three lineages that have each discovered a different real strategy.

IMPORTANT REFINEMENT, and it weakens the simple story: at 4p, best_farm is
+37.78 and food_rate is +0.65 -- both POSITIVE -- and the bot still builds zero
farms in 2432 decisions. So "the food weights are negative" cannot be the whole
explanation. Two distinct mechanisms are visible, one per lineage:

  4p: food is priced positively but resource_rate (+4.54) is seven times
      food_rate (+0.65), and free_workers is NEGATIVE (-8.86) so a worker
      wants to be spent. The farm loses the head-to-head against the mine on
      every board, which is exactly the "min rank 3, never top two" shape.

  2p: food_rate is negative (-1.05) AND free_workers is positive (+6.31), so an
      idle worker is worth more than anything it could be spent on. Nothing gets
      built at all; EndTurn wins 48.6% of decisions.

A NonNegative sign_intent on food_rate addresses the 2p mechanism directly and
the 4p one not at all. The 4p mechanism is a RELATIVE pricing failure between
two production channels, which is the coarse-feature problem: one scalar per
card type cannot say "a farm is worth more when my food rate is low".


THE STRUCTURAL FACT BEHIND IT
-----------------------------
FoodRate, ResourceRate, BestFarm, BestMine, FoodStock and ProdWorkers have NO
sign_intent -- none of them appears in weights.rs's sign_intent match, so all six
fall through to Free. Nothing in the code prevents food production from being
priced as a liability.

By the rules it cannot be one. Checked against RULES_SPEC §6.1/§6.2/§6.6 and
cross-checked on the open web (UltraBoardGames' rules pages and the BGG food-
consumption thread), because an earlier draft of this paragraph had the rule
slightly wrong:

  - Corruption is levied on the BLUE BANK, not on stock (§6.2, RULES_SPEC:174):
    it is a function of how many blue tokens remain unspent, so it penalizes not
    BUILDING, and it is indifferent to how much food you hold.
  - Food is the corruption SHORTFALL BUFFER (§6.6 step 3b, RULES_SPEC:192):
    "pay resources = corruption; shortfall paid in food". So holding more food
    strictly helps when corruption exceeds your resources.
  - Food consumption (§6.6 step 3d) charges food = the consumption number, and
    a shortfall costs 4 CULTURE per missing food. More food production strictly
    reduces exposure to that.
  - Nothing anywhere charges a player for holding or producing MORE food.

So the correct statement is not "food has no corruption analogue" — it is that
food is on the RECEIVING end of corruption, never the paying end. Owning a
higher food RATE is never worse than owning a lower one, holding workers fixed;
the opportunity cost of the worker sitting on a farm is carried by the other
production terms, not by food_rate itself. A negative food_rate is a fit the
rules forbid.


WHAT NOT TO DO
--------------
Do not hand-reprice food_rate. That is already on the dead list, and the
three-lineage disagreement above is exactly why: a hand-set value in a collinear
family is re-fitted away within a few hundred generations.

Do not land anything from this file while the clamp-ceiling A/B is live. That
experiment (f942741, judged 2026-08-30) is running on all three arms right now
and a weight-space constraint landed mid-flight would contaminate it.


THE CANDIDATE INTERVENTION, QUEUED
----------------------------------
A rules-derived NonNegative sign_intent on FoodRate. That is a constraint rather
than a value, which is the form the codebase already uses for ~100 keys, and it
is the form that survives re-fitting.

It carries a real cost and must have its own A/B: per
analysis/zero_writer_census_2026-08-27.txt, dominance_repair snaps a wrong-sign
weight to exactly 0.0 on every load and every mutation, so a sign_intent is the
one decision the climb cannot undo. If the intent is wrong, food_rate is pinned
at 0.0 rather than merely mispriced. Before landing it, check whether the signal
would simply migrate to food_stock or food_gap, which are collinear with it.

Open question this does NOT answer: whether best_mine/best_farm need the same
treatment, and whether the real defect is that one scalar per card TYPE cannot
express "a farm is worth more when my food rate is low" -- the coarse-feature
problem. The rank data above (farm never above rank 3, at 359 different board
states) is consistent with a feature that cannot condition on need.
